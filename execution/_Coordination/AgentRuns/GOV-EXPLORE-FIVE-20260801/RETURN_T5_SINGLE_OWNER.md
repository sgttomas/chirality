## Plurality seams that already exist

1. **B2 is per-root, and loops are quasi-sovereign already.** "one accountable owner **per root**" (§9.2); D-8/D-11: "Each loop's stricter local merge discipline remains controlling until that loop adopts or acknowledges the policy **under its own instruments**" (`docs/PRD_ROOT.md` §5.3 D-8; `AGENTS.md` change-notice rule: "the receiving loop adopts, amends, or declines under its own instruments and cadence"). Each loop has its own decision register (`projects/chirality-piping/.../\_DECISIONS/D-*`, app-dev `D-APP-*`), its own receipt contract (piping D-44, app-dev D-APP-57), its own LOOP_INIT.
2. **TM PRD is structurally multi-owner.** §6.1 table has a per-loop "Triage owner" column ("Loop owner + instruments"); §19.3 says "triage owner**s**"; §7.1's trigger list already names plural decision principals: "a decision on alternatives needed from the owner, **leadership, client, or third party**". Finding F-1 disposition: "owners are loops, not roles."
3. **The allowlist is a table, not a constant.** `human_actors.md` schema (canonical name / role / aliases / git identity / email / **Effective** date) supports N rows and dated occupancy; its own Maintenance clause contemplates "additions and removals". `harness_common.py` `match()` handles any number of actors.
4. **Capacity separability is doctrine.** `docs/PRD_ROOT.md` §2.3: "Four distinct capacities may be held by four different people... At the accepted basis all four capacities happen to be held by one person; **that is a fact about the present instantiation, not a property of the product.**" Explicitly: engineer-of-record ≠ root owner (`docs/DIRECTIVE.md` §3.2 + §2.3 "a professional practitioner does not thereby obtain root-governance authority").
5. **Attribution fields already name actors**: `FramedBy`, `RuledBy`/`AdoptedBy`/`HumanRuling`, `ResponsibleParty` (D-GOV-27 assigns "Ryan Tufts" as ResponsibleParty — a field that could name anyone), K-ROLE-2 run attribution, RASCI Assignment (`TM PRD §6.3: "Assignment — R/S/C/I parties"`).

**Could two humans own two loops today without doctrine change?** Almost. K-AUTH-1/2, K-BIND-1, K-MERGE-1 need no edit. What breaks first, in order: (1) `human_actors.md` row 1's **role text** — "sole author of binding rulings" is falsified by the second row itself; (2) `docs/PRD_ROOT.md` §9.2's effect column "Matches the live registry exactly... one registered actor" — a D-14 class currency failure the moment the row lands; (3) the bare `owner` alias becomes ambiguous, and refuse-rather-than-guess turns every new "ruled by the owner" attribution into an exit-2 refusal; (4) merge identity — the repo is `github.com/sgttomas/chirality`, a personal account; NOT FOUND: any doctrine governing GitHub account/collaborator topology.

## Break analysis: partners, then organization

**Partners (2–5):**
- **Conflicting rulings on shared surfaces (root `docs/`).** K-CONFLICT-1 routes conflicts to "human adjudication" — no rule says *which* human. The supersede-never-edit convention (D-2) gives pure temporal precedence: any owner can supersede any ruling at any time. NOT FOUND: any precedence, quorum, or partition rule — RD-2 expressly declined the two options (A2, A3) that would have created one.
- **Merge authentication.** Semantic approval today = in-session verbatim direction + the `sgttomas`-authenticated CLI. With two owners the merge actor no longer discriminates who approved; receipts attribute "the owner" generically. D-GOV-30 PACKET.md:90 already flags the degenerate case as a disclosure item: "Author == merge actor on 20/20 PRs."
- **K-AUTH-2 races.** Two approvals on branches touching the same file: the first merge changes content under the second, voiding it. Mechanically fail-safe — but *merge execution order* then adjudicates between owners, making whoever runs merges a de facto precedence authority with no doctrinal basis.
- **Divided falsification clocks.** "Unobserved falsifiers" (OBJ-4) is only meaningful if someone is obligated to observe; with the sweep obligation owner-refused (TM §14), plurality converts a self-binding choice into a diffusion-of-responsibility hole — each partner may assume the other is watching, and the record cannot tell.

**Organization (owner-as-role):**
- The verbatim-ruling convention collapses: a committee does not emit in-session verbatim tokens; the owner token grammar presumes a single utterance. You would need minuted resolutions plus a designated executor — precisely RD-2's declined option A3.
- Attribution is trust-based, not cryptographic. NOT FOUND: any GPG/signed-commit/signature requirement anywhere in CONTRACT.md, SPEC.md, PRD_ROOT.md, or D-GOV-04. The owner himself conceded this in the RD-2 verbatim: "attribution can be done more securely that way" (PEC interface + database) — expressly not current scope, and any database attribution layer collides with D-GOV-01 (files are sole authority; database never citable) "unless a future ruling consciously amends D-GOV-01" (`docs/PRD_ROOT.md` §9.2 reconciliation).
- Occupancy change: `human_actors.md` has an Effective column (the only succession-shaped mechanism found), but rulings are personally attributed ("Ryan Tufts, in-session") and do not transfer; who appoints/removes owners is itself owner-curated — a role-occupied owner needs a governance-of-governance instrument that does not exist.

## The minimal second-owner amendment

To give one loop (piping) an engineer-owner while root stays with the founder:

1. **`docs/governance_harness/human_actors.md`** — add a row (an owner edit published by CHANGE; the file's own Maintenance clause already permits this). Simultaneously amend row 1's role text ("sole author of binding rulings") and **scope the aliases** (`owner` → retire or bind to root; new actor gets `piping-owner`), or refuse-rather-than-guess breaks on the first ambiguous attribution.
2. **One `D-GOV-*` record** (terminal-artifact rule, D-1) granting the new actor ruling jurisdiction scoped to `projects/chirality-piping/**` loop instruments — piping D-* records, brief adoptions, piping-lane merge direction — with an exclusion list copied from the D-49/D-52 nondelegable taxonomy plus: root `docs/`, `AGENTS.md`, `agents/`, `skills/`, and `human_actors.md` itself.
3. **A superseding clarification of RD-2** (D-13: the PRD is amended only by a SHA-bound superseding instrument). Substantively small — A1 governs the *root* and a per-working-root reading of B2 ("one accountable owner per root") makes a piping owner conformant — but §9.2's "Matches the live registry exactly... one registered actor" sentence goes stale on step 1, which D-14 classes as a currency failure routed to the owner anyway.
4. **No K-* invariant changes.** K-AUTH-1/2, K-BIND-1, K-MERGE-1, K-TM-3 are already actor-neutral or per-loop.
5. **One unavoidable new mechanism:** merge-channel identity. Either the piping owner gets their own authenticated GitHub actor (collaborator on `sgttomas/chirality` — territory no doctrine currently governs) with their git identity in their allowlist row, or their approvals ride the founder's channel and attribution stays semantic-only. If their write jurisdiction is to be *enforced* rather than reviewed, branch protection/CODEOWNERS on root `docs/` would be the first mechanism in the system that bounds a human.

## The one hard problem (the thing no small amendment fixes)

**The doctrine has no concept of a bounded human.** Every containment mechanism in the system — K-WRITE-1/2, ScopePath, sealed briefs, the D-49/D-52 nondelegable lists — bounds *agents*; the human is the unbounded terminus every rule resolves to: BLOCK override is human-only (D-GOV-02), a validator "may never mechanically reject content the owner has adopted or ruled — the validator is defective... never the ruled text" (D-GOV-17), and supersede-never-edit grants the owner temporal supremacy over every prior ruling. With one human this is coherent: the unbounded party and the accountable party are the same person, so "the one gate you cannot cross" needs no lock — only a keeper. Admit a second owner and, for the first time, the system needs rules that bind a human's authority *within* it — jurisdiction fences, precedence between conflicting human rulings, and authentication of which human acted — and its own axioms resist each one: D-GOV-17 forbids a validator from blocking an owner ruling even when it trespasses the *other* owner's jurisdiction; in-session verbatim transcription cannot prove who spoke; and the token grammar carries no identity. The minimal amendment above works only because it partitions jurisdiction so the two humans never share a surface; the moment they do, the missing piece is not a rule but an inversion of the system's one-way trust gradient — humans bound agents, nothing bounds humans. The owner's own ruled answer locates the fix in an authentication substrate (RD-2's "PEC interface and a database... not current scope") that would itself pressure D-GOV-01's file-native-authority axiom — meaning the honest cost of true multi-owner operation is a constitutional amendment, and the record already knows it.
