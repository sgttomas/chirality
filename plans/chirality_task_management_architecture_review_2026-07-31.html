Chirality Program · architecture review · task-management lens · 2026-07-31

The program writes every concern down, and obligates almost no one to read one back

Four sealed reviewer lenses and one deterministic dry-run examined the whole program architecture from the perspective of the proposed Task Management module, then turned the same instruments on the module's own PRD candidate. The architecture's gap is real but narrower and differently shaped than the candidate assumed — and the candidate itself takes nine critical breaks that require a structural revision, recorded here before any adoption act.

01 · Verdict

Loss is by artifact class, not by session — and the failed class is the one the candidate proposed to extend

The candidate PRD's thesis said task-relevant issues "die between sessions." Live-state tracing falsifies that as a general claim and confirms it as a class-specific one. Concerns that enter a decision-register row or a receipt Parked lanes: field get dispositioned — 269 of 275 register rows across five registers carry a filled ruling, usually with a verbatim owner token and a bound SHA, and deferred rulings are re-raised through the owner-ruled residual-row convention until closed. Concerns that enter a standalone CSV finding register or the routed-notice ledger essentially never close: 0/12, 0/9, 13/15 open, 29/30 unacknowledged, and one register that is structurally uncloseable because it has no status column.

The instruction layer explains why: across 22 concern-generating mechanisms, emission is enforced and consumption is not. Only five gates in the whole program require anyone to read accumulated concerns, and none of them reads _run_records/, prior audit snapshots, inbound notices, immutable research packets, or the decomposition open-issues list after Gate 6. Because governed outputs are immutable snapshots (K-SNAP-1), their Status/HumanRuling columns can never advance in place; the only mutable git-tracked open-work surface is the optional ## Remaining section — and nothing connects the birth sites to it.

Against this, the candidate PRD proposed a program-tier register + scanner. The adversarial lens showed the program already ran that experiment: the notice-status ledger is precisely a git-tracked CSV+MD register of open concerns with provenance and SHA binding — and after three days it sat at 29/30 open, never advanced, already stale against three newer notices. A register nobody is obligated to read reproduces the pathology it cites as motivation. What actually closes items in this program, on the evidence, is adoption into a loop's own instruments — the surfaces each loop re-reads at every entry by its own ruling.

The owner's two directions of record this session fence the product to exactly that shape:

"The task manager shouldn't be dispatching tasks for working in deliverables. Those should be discovered via the development loop. But as matters arise during the course of those development loops that can't or shouldn't be resolved in that session need to have a means of being identified and worked on later."
"Matters get resolved directly or by updating the content of the deliverables and the appropriate surfaces the development loop touches."

Verdict: the product survives, restructured. It is not a new coordination plane, not a work queue, and not a tree-wide scanner. It is a per-loop disposition ledger for session-boundary residue, seeded from the measured backlog, bound into each loop's entry instruments by that loop's own ruling, with the program-level register carried by the root loop. Section 08 records the full amendment list applied to the PRD candidate.

03 · Concern genesis (Reviewer A)

Twenty-two birth mechanisms, five reading gates

The instruction layer requires concerns to be recorded at every boundary — K-PROV-1, K-INVENT-1, K-CONFLICT-1, K-CLAIM-1 make the recording itself an invariant. It obligates later reading at almost none of them. The five gates that do read: REVIEW Gates 4–5 (its own findings; zero TBD on CRITICAL/MAJOR), the CHECKING-entry warranted-empty ## Remaining check, EQUATION_AUDIT's mechanical flagged == 0 close, and DBM_PUBLISHER's open-item surfacing scan plus disposition-before-pointer-update — the broadest cross-mechanism look in the program, confined to publication runs. The root loop's session-entry instruction to enumerate parked lanes is the only session-start look, root-only, and register-less.

A disposition ledger with a named triage owner is that instrument, by the contract's own description.

06 · Fan-in without averaging

Where the lenses agreed, added, and could not see

AGREED (independently converged)

  • The gap is real and specific: no mutable, obligated-to-be-read disposition surface exists for concerns that outlive a session; the invariant catalog itself names an unbuilt "human triage queue" (K-STALE-2).
  • Registers filed beside the flow fail (29/30, 0/12, 0/9); items adopted into loop instruments close (269/275). Any viable design binds the ledger into loop entry by loop ruling.
  • The tier model must collapse to loops. Loops are the real operating units with real cadence instruments; packages and tiers have no standing occupants.
  • Tree-wide free-text scanning is unviable; structured, closure-capable surfaces are the only day-one candidate sources, and the backlog they carry (~115 items) is tractable.
  • No new tool root, no agent-file fan-out, no runtime-contract change is needed for a first release — and each of those paths is fenced by an instrument the candidate had not priced.

ADD — one lens strengthened another

  • From B to the thesis: the observed re-litigation case (DEL-01-01 C001→C003) happened with the earlier record found and cited — record insufficiency, not record loss. Closure quality, not just closure existence, is in scope.
  • From B to the design: the owner refused a standing notice-sweep amendment (Receipt 89, "I explicitly do not want this") — hooks must be adopted per loop, and per-run steering remains the lawful mechanism for the uncommon case.
  • From C to the design: PEC PRD §16-1 reserves register structuring at source to the owner; a TM register decides it by construction, so the PRD must present that decision to the owner explicitly.
  • From the dry-run to D: the derivative-snapshot double-count (notice ledger copied verbatim into an _Evaluation/ terminal package) requires a canonical-vs-copy dedup rule no lens had named.

STANDING_DIVERGENCE

None material. Reviewer A's framing ("the two layers are not connected by any instruction") and Reviewer B's framing ("two classes of artifact carry no obligation to be revisited") describe the same defect at different altitudes. D's F-2 (loops are re-derivation-first, so a register is a derivative claim agents are told to distrust) is answered by making the register authoritative for exactly one thing — Action Item existence and disposition — and derivative for everything it cites.

SHARED_BLIND_SPOT_RISK

  • No lens assessed whether the owner's actual working practice would sustain register triage cadence — the decisive variable, unmeasurable from paper.
  • All lenses read the same frozen basis; drift since (e.g. D-GOV-31 notices) was sampled, not swept.
  • The three-source professional corpus was treated as fixed input; no lens challenged whether the nine-domain framing itself fits a one-person program.

07 · What changes in the proposal

The amendment list applied to the PRD candidate (Revision 2)

  1. Registers move to the loops. One register per development loop, living beside that loop's existing coordination surfaces (_Coordination/_TaskManagement/), owned by the loop's owner-plus-instruments; the root loop's register is the program register. No package or deliverable registers — deliverable association is a row field. No new SPEC §1.2 tool root is minted. (answers F-1, F-3, F-4, F-27; OBJ-6; C's placement scoring)
  2. Adoption is per loop, by that loop's own ruling — exactly as the single-surface fences require and the notice doctrine already models: the TM PRD charters the standard and the program register; each other loop adopts, amends, or declines by its own decision record. (F-APP-5/F-PIP-5; Receipt 89)
  3. The counterweight to 29/30 is loop-entry binding, not a checklist line in agent files. A loop that adopts a register amends its own LOOP_INIT/workplan (one loop-owned file, one ruling) so session entry reads open rows and session close records dispositions — the same mechanism that gives receipts their closure discipline. K-TM-4 is re-scoped: no act outside a loop's own adopted instruments may require a TM read or write. The root loop's existing parked-lanes entry step becomes the first binding. (F-19, F-20, F-21)
  4. Elevation is re-specified as linked rows, not a move: the lower register's owner marks the row ELEVATED and routes the ordinary parent-ward notice; the higher register's owner opens a linked row citing it. Cross-register writes by one actor are prohibited, matching K-WRITE-1. (F-5)
  5. The scanner is re-scoped to structured, closure-capable surfaces only: decision-register non-ruled rows, notice files vs. ledger state, FINDINGS.csv, Review_Findings.csv open rows, HOLD registers, handoff blockers, packet open-question/amendment-candidate rows, TBD registers, slate items, receipt parked-lanes. Free-text token scanning survives only as an explicit per-document mode. TBD is a signal only in disposition-bearing columns. The exclusion set is rebuilt from measured sources and gains a canonical-vs-derivative-copy dedup rule. (F-7..F-11; dry-run RF-A/RF-B)
  6. Layer 2 is re-grounded: the return-contract amendment is withdrawn (no such contract exists; returns are runtime-owned and gated behind DEL-02-06). The governed capture path for out-of-brief observations is the loop receipt's Parked lanes: field — already contractual in receipt validators — plus an optional inline marker grammar. (F-28; A's M14)
  7. Identifier scheme changes: marker becomes TM-CANDIDATE:; row IDs become TM-<LOOP>-<seq>, program-unique by loop namespace. (F-25; OI-013 collision)
  8. Schema is repaired: RegisterSchemaVersion as column 1 per the v3.1 precedent; typed NoticeRef and ScaRef; EvidenceSha binding closure evidence to bytes per K-AUTH-2/HINGE-3; EvidenceQuote; single authoritative CSV with the MD file explicitly a non-authoritative summary in the _DEPENDENCIES.md-over-CSV pattern; a closure-capable-schema invariant (every register must be able to record its own closure). (F-13, F-14, F-16, F-17, F-18; B's TP-EXPORT-006)
  9. Coverage duties extend by name to HOLD registers, evaluation findings, review reports and their ranked recommendations (the 07-28 review's §09 actions and §08 held-open questions become seed rows of the program register), and the K-STALE-2 triage-queue vacancy is claimed explicitly as the instrument being built. (F-22, F-23, F-24; A's M10)
  10. New product duty — closure echo: views must show, as labeled display, when an item's source record still reads open after disposition (the "originating register never learns" pattern), without ever writing to the source. (B)
  11. Boundary statements added: against A-B-012 (a disposition ledger is not a work-discovery surface — with the owner's direction quoted as the controlling distinction); against PEC §16-1 (adopting the register decides structure-at-source, presented as an explicit owner decision); against K-STALE-1 (transitive staleness stays with the fenced dependency surfaces; only K-STALE-2 semantics transfer). (C; F-15)
  12. Stage A shrinks to one loop and zero agent-file changes: the register standard, the root-loop program register seeded with the measured backlog (~115 items), taskmgmt scan/validate over structured surfaces, and the root LOOP_INIT amendment — all by owner ruling in the root loop's own instruments. Other loops, the App projection, and any runtime route come later, each by its own gate. (F-6; §17 rebuild)

08 · Risk and recommended sequence

Where this design is most likely to fail on contact, and the smallest governed actions

RankRisk
1Triage cadence is still human. The design binds reading to loop entry, but nothing starts a loop session. If the owner does not open loops, rows age — the register makes that visible and measurable; it cannot make it false. This is the residual, honestly stated.
2Seed-backlog triage is itself a session of work. ~115 items, many stale, some already resolved downstream. If the first triage session is deferred, the register opens at 0% closure — indistinguishable from the notice ledger it replaces.
3Per-loop adoption may stall at one loop, exactly as the notice channel did. The design tolerates this (each loop's residue simply stays in its receipts), but cross-loop items then live only in the program register.
4Discipline drift in receipts. The Parked lanes: capture path works where practiced (pec: 68 mentions) and is nearly unused elsewhere (app-dev: 1). The register does not fix uneven receipt discipline; it only harvests what receipts record.
5The App projection and PEC seam invite scope creep back toward the coordination plane. The §16-1 presentation and the admitted-source framing must stay decisions of record, not drift.

Smallest governed actions, in order

  1. Owner reviews PRD Revision 2 and, if satisfied, adopts by explicit act: the standard, the K-TM rows, the root-loop register home under execution/_Coordination/, and the root LOOP_INIT amendment — one loop, one ruling.
  2. Seed the program register from the measured backlog and run the first triage session; dispositions land as ordinary owner rulings.
  3. Route the standard as coordination notices to the other loops; each adopts, amends, or declines by its own decision record on its own cadence.
  4. Only after two loops run registers through real sessions: consider SOFTWARE_DECOMP for the service tooling, the App admitted-source projection via App scope change, and any PEC reconciliation — each by its own gate.

How findings become governed work — unchanged: observation → evidence-linked finding → challenge → fan-in → human disposition → owning instrument or workflow → accepted change → affected-scope rerun. Nothing in this report enters product scope without that route.
