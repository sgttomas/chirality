## 1. Owner-stated theses and intents (the durable payload)

Recorded as close to the owner's words as fidelity allows; each item
was stated directly by the owner in this engagement.

1. Build-time vs run-time governance is a deliberate asymmetry.
   Operational Piping will NOT be approvals-heavy: agents will have
   near-parity autonomy with human users except at certain prescribed
   workflow gates. The heavy governance exists for the BUILD, because
   autonomously building the application under a single accountable
   human requires it.

2. The single-human development model is chosen, not forced. It is
   "not necessary, but it's been sufficient and a practical
   limitation" — and it carries a real advantage: the owner holds all
   context personally, so inter-human coordination overhead is absent,
   which is "not to be underestimated."

3. Alignment is maintained by presence, not prose. The owner keeps
   design alignment through direct involvement in development loops;
   is "not confident that writing about it achieves anything"; and
   knows no tighter control mechanism for now.

4. Owner background (stated for the record): autodidact mechanical
   engineer; 25 years into career; entered the Canadian market in the
   ascendant early-2000s period of megaproject design/construction;
   full project lifecycle experience within the first 10 years;
   positions held up to Director level. Zero coding experience and
   zero knowledge of software engineering principles. Self-taught in
   statistical analysis, graph theory, category theory, epistemology,
   ontology, and systems engineering prior to this program. First
   Chirality prototype ~2025-08 ("almost exactly 1 year ago"); the
   last 7 months were the development window for all aspects of the
   present program, which began as separate projects.

5. Paradigm claim: "everyone else is porting software culture into
   agents and that's the wrong paradigm because we already have the
   model for organizing and coordinating agency at scale and
   complexity and professional rigor and accountability" — i.e., the
   professional/capital-projects delivery model.

6. Release strategy: the owner cannot supply human accountability for
   the code itself and will not publish publicly-relied-upon software
   without it. Solution: publish open source once the paradigm is
   established. "My product is as someone who can use the tool to do
   the thing, not the one trying to sell the thing." The owner
   anticipates others building products and charging premiums on the
   open base, and accepts that.

7. Bedrock thesis: "The practice of harnessing agents with
   capabilities far beyond humans, but lacking the accountability of
   human judgment, and doing so at scale, reliably, is my bedrock for
   the future of professional work."

8. Epistemological core (stated as the raison d'etre of Chirality):
   the accountability scaling problem is not being properly
   addressed, is not widely understood, and is subject to many
   misconceptions and misconstruals. Sharpest form: "I don't think
   that professionals have a strong grasp on what their knowledge
   actually is and how it is grounded and why."

## 2. Dialogue-developed elaborations (dated model induction)

Derived 2026-08-01 by the orchestrating model in dialogue; several
were affirmed by the owner in passing ("It's both"; "This is the
core...") but the formulations are the model's. Supersession: re-derive
from Section 1 with a current model; that derivation supersedes this
list.

1. Exchange rate: governance at build time purchases autonomy at run
   time. Constraints enforced structurally during construction are
   approvals that never need to exist during operation (the
   absent-enum pattern as the exemplar).

2. Asymmetry underlying the bedrock thesis: capability scales with
   compute; accountability does not scale at all (per-human,
   non-transferable, non-parallelizable). As capability becomes
   abundant, accountability becomes the binding constraint, and
   professional work re-centers on the scarce factor.

3. Accountability is a social relation (exposure to consequence), not
   a capability; therefore no degree of model improvement retires the
   thesis. Caveat recorded: synthetic accountability (insurance
   pools, certification, legal personhood) is institutionally
   possible, but underwriting would demand exactly the evidence
   infrastructure this program builds — the evidence layer wins on
   both branches.

4. The single-human model is a coordination arbitrage: n(n-1)/2 lossy
   human channels replaced by hub-and-spoke briefs; the doctrine is a
   compiled replacement for organizational communication, and the
   model scales WITH capability (the hub delegates more; the residual
   human function converges on warrant-bearing plus novel rulings).

5. On release: open source provides inspectability, not
   accountability — the precondition for others to take
   responsibility. Engineering software's actual reliance mechanism
   has never been vendor warrant (commercial CAE licenses disclaim
   fitness); it is validation evidence plus the using professional's
   stamp. An evidence-native tool can exceed the industry's real
   standard, which is a disclaimer.

6. No license protects the epistemics: a fork can restore forbidden
   enum members in an afternoon. The boundary survives only if the
   profession comes to expect it (reviewers/insurers/owners asking
   for acceptance records and claims discipline). Paradigm
   establishment therefore runs through practiced, stamped work with
   public evidence trails — the release date is downstream of the
   practice, not of the software.

7. Double-deficit framing of the accountability scaling problem: the
   machine lacks accountability; the professional lacks articulacy
   about their own knowledge grounding. Model-centric framings
   (hallucination, alignment, evals) see only the first deficit.
   Tacit, institutionally-grounded knowledge was transmissible by
   apprenticeship but is not delegable to agents: you cannot gate
   what you cannot state.

8. Prediction: decomposing a profession's knowledge for delegation
   will find what this program's test-estate audit found in code —
   dead clauses with vanished failure-bases, cargo-cult practice on
   unexecuted paths, and silent load-bearing judgments. Delegation to
   agents is the first force strong enough to compel the professions'
   self-audit; the decomposition machinery is the instrument built
   ahead of the compulsion.
