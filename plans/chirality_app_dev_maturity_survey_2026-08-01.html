Chirality App (app-dev) Maturity Survey

2026-08-01 · basis main@12df406b5 · read-only survey by a sealed bounded agent (69 file reads across frontend source, tests, CI workflows, decision and hold registers, scope-change and horizon records), fanned in by the supervising root session · derivative, non-governing (see footer) · companion to plans/chirality_piping_solver_maturity_survey_2026-07-31.html; the pair gives the two application loops a comparable evidence basis
Verdict, two axes. As an engineered desktop client, the App is mature: ~46k LOC of product TypeScript behind 1,022 test cases, a real Unix-socket daemon client seam whose API routes construct no engine, three engine adapters with the D-APP-40 interruption taxonomy implemented and conformance-tested, and a CI gate that boots the actual daemon and fails closed on evidence summaries. As a relied-upon release surface, it is not mature: reliance is released across every entry path while the UI/API semantic-parity instrument does not exist, no e2e layer of any kind exists, six historical relations remain UNKNOWN, the PRD body contradicts its own §17, and zero of 53 deliverables has ever left IN_PROGRESS. This is the exact inversion of the Piping posture (capability-deep, reliance-withheld): here reliance ran ahead of assurance.

6 · Synthesis — the two application loops, side by side (supervising session)

Piping and App now hold mirrored postures. Piping is capability-deep and reliance-withheld: a validated-in-substance mechanics kernel whose every tolerance and reliance claim is deliberately TBD under an explicit hold. App is reliance-released and assurance-thin: a well-engineered client whose formal permission to be relied upon ran ahead of the instrument that would witness it. The program's 07-28 review ranked this inversion its #2 risk; this survey confirms all three prongs byte-for-byte. The two missing objects are the same species — Piping's tolerance policy and App's parity instrument are both the undefined meaning of “matches” — which suggests any future product-basis or rebalancing act should treat them as one design question instantiated twice, not two backlogs.
