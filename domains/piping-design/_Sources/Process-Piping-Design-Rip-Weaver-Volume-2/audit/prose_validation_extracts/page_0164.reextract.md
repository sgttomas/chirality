Step 4: Determine value of *L*, the total length of line.

*L* = 6 + 10 + 2 + 8 + 6 + 2 + 4
 = 38'

Step 5: Solve the formula which must be equal to or less than 0.03 or full stress analysis is needed.

$$\frac{DE}{(L-U)^2} \leq 0.03$$

$$\frac{6 \times 0.54}{(38-20)^2} = \frac{3.24}{18^2}$$

$$= \frac{3.24}{324}$$

$$= 0.01 \leq 0.03$$

So the configuration is satisfactory.

When piping connects to equipment nozzles which expand and contract due to temperature, the nozzle movements must be considered and added to expansion (*E*) calculations in the direction they occur.

Referring to Figure 5-9, should anchor point *A*₁ become an equipment nozzle and expand upward 0.375″ and in direction *Z* toward *A*₂ by 2″, the basic formula must be modified. Expansion must be figured for net lengths of *X*, *Y* and *Z* and anchor movements applied.

Step 1: Calculate expansion in direction *X*:

Σ*X* = 12 × 0.027″ = 0.324″

Since there is no anchor movement in the direction *X*:

Σ*X* = 0.324″ + 0
 = 0.324″

Step 2: Calculate expansion in direction *Y*:

Σ*Y* = 2 × 0.027″
 = 0.054″

Since *A*₁ is moving upward 0.375″:

Σ*Y* = 0.054″ − 0.375″
 = −0.321″

So use 0.321″ as this becomes the net anchor movement.

Step 3: Calculate expansion in direction *Z*:

Σ*Z* = 16 × 0.027″
 = 0.432″

Since *A*₁ is moving 2″ in the direction *Z*:

Σ*Z* = 0.432″ + 2″
 = 2.432″

Step 4: Calculate expansion in direction *U*:

$$E = \sqrt{\Sigma X^2 + \Sigma Y^2 + \Sigma Z^2}$$
$$= \sqrt{0.324^2 + 0.321^2 + 2.432^2}$$
$$= \sqrt{6.023}$$
$$= 2.45″$$

Step 5: Solve the basic formula using the new value for *E*:

$$\frac{DE}{(L-U)^2} \leq 0.03$$

$$\frac{6 \times 2.45}{(38-20)^2} = \frac{14.7}{324}$$

$$= 0.045″$$

which is larger than 0.03, so a stress analysis is required.
