$$V = \frac{f_y d_y}{2L} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d_y}{L}\right)\frac{d_y}{L} \cdot y + \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{d_y}{L}\right)^2 K_{LY} - (K_Y) y$$

$$\text{where } K_Y = \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{d_y}{L}\right)^2 K_s \tag{7.14}$$

where *L* is the bellow effective length, which is the free natural length adjusted by the axial deformation, if any. $K_\theta$ is the rotational spring rate due to the *y* lateral displacement. $K_Y$ is the lateral spring rate. The lateral-rotational spring rate, $K_{y\theta}$, is inversely proportional to the bellow effective length, and the lateral spring rate, $K_Y$, is inversely proportional to the square of the bellow effective length. Because the potential axial deformation can constitute a significant portion of the bellow length, it needs to be included in the calculation of spring rates. To be on the safe side, the potential minimum length, after subtracting the potential axial deformation, should be used.

The spring rates as given by Eqs. (7.10), (7.11), (7.13), and (7.14) are functions of the axial spring rate, $K_s$ effective bellow length, *L*, and bellow pitch diameter, $d_b$. Once $K_s$, *L*, and $d_b$ are known, the spring rates of all directions and thus the stiffness matrix of the bellow element can be constructed.

The *EJMA rotational spring rate*, the one given by the manufacturer, implies that there is an accompanying free lateral movement, $\Delta$, at the end as shown in Fig. 7.5(a). This free lateral displacement, sometimes called the *relief displacement*, can be calculated by

$$\Delta = z \sin\theta = R\tan(\theta/2)\sin\theta = \frac{L}{\theta}\tan(\theta/2)\sin\theta \approx \frac{1}{2}L\theta \tag{7.15}$$
