To get a better idea of the method, a check on the nature of these dimensionless stress parameters is necessary. Because the limiting value of these dimensionless parameters is 1.0, these parameters correspond to a stress value of $1.5S_c$ — that is, 110% of the basic allowable stress at the operating temperature. Furthermore, the tank shell hoop stress at the nozzle location is assumed to be $1.0S_c$, as is usually the case. The stress due to $M_c$, for example, can be determined from the dimensionless parameters by setting

$$\frac{\lambda}{aK_c\left(\frac{M_c}{F_p}\right)} \cdot \frac{a\sqrt{Rt}}{a} \cdot \frac{M_c}{Y_c} \cdot \frac{1}{1} = 1.0 = \frac{S_{Mc}}{1.1S_c} = \frac{S_{Mc}}{1.1pRt}$$

or

$$S_{Mc} = \left(\frac{1.1}{Y_c}\right) \frac{\sqrt{R}}{t^{3/2}} \left(\frac{M_c}{2\pi p}\right)$$

(8.31)

Similarly, for $M_C$ and $F_b$, we have

$$S_{MC} = \left(\frac{1.1}{Y_C}\right) \frac{\sqrt{R}}{t^{3/2}} \left(\frac{M_C}{2\pi p}\right)$$

(8.32)

$$S_p = \left(\frac{1.1}{Y_b}\right) \frac{\sqrt{R}}{t^{3/2}} \left(\frac{F_b}{2\pi a}\right)$$

(8.33)

The above stress formulas are in exactly the same form as given by Kellogg's choking model discussed in Section 6.8.2. $(1/Y_c)$, $(1/Y_C)$, and $(1/Y_b)$, times 1.1 are the stress coefficients for $M_c$, $M_{\phi c}$, and $F_b$, respectively. $Y_b$, $Y_C$, and $Y_c$ are called allowable coefficients whose values are given in Fig. 8.25.
