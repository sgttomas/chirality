where $f_{cs} = f_c \cos \theta_i$ and $f_{ss} = -f_s \sin \theta_i$ are components for the sine function and cosine function, respectively. The load vector becomes two vectors: one with the sin or function and the other with the cos or function as follows:

$$\{F(t)\} = \{F_s\} \sin \omega t + \{F_c\} \cos \omega t \tag{13.67}$$

The above equation has two scalar functions, sin $\omega t$ and cos $\omega t$, instead of the matrix function $[\sin(\omega t - \theta)]$. $\{F_s\}$ and $\{F_c\}$ are force amplitude vectors for sine and cosine functions, respectively.

The analysis is then performed by the superposition of two forcing vectors. Each forcing vector is analyzed separately, then combined. From Eq. (13.14) we have the relation for the *i*th mode as

$$\ddot{q}_i + 2\zeta_i \omega_{n_i} \dot{q}_i + \omega_{n_i}^2 q_i = \{\Phi\}^T \{F_s\} \sin \omega t + \{\Phi\}^T \{F_c\} \cos \omega t$$

or

$$\ddot{q}_i + 2\zeta_i \omega_{n_i} \dot{q}_i + \omega_{n_i}^2 q_i = F_{si} \sin \omega t + F_{ci} \cos \omega t, \quad i = 1, N \tag{13.68}$$

where $\omega_{n_i}$ is the natural circular frequency of the *i*th natural mode. The above has decoupled the system into *N* SDOF systems, each in the form of Eqs. (12.33) and (12.34), with normalized unit effective mass. The solution for the steady-state forced vibration of Eq. (13.68) is given by Eqs. (12.36) and (12.37), with $k/M = k = \omega^2$ for a unit effective mass. That is,

$$q_i = \frac{1}{\omega_{n_i}^2 \sqrt{(1-\beta_i^2)^2 + (2\zeta_i \beta_i)^2}} \left[ F_{si} \sin(\omega t - \alpha_i) + F_{ci} \cos(\omega t - \alpha_i) \right]$$

and

$$\alpha_i = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2\zeta_i \beta_i}{1 - \beta_i^2}\right) \tag{13.69}$$

where $\beta_i = \omega / \omega_{n_i}$. Expanding $\sin(\omega t - \alpha_i)$ and $\cos(\omega t - \alpha_i)$ and collecting terms, we have

$$q_i = [q_{si} \sin \omega t + q_{ci} \cos \omega t] \tag{13.70}$$

where

$$q_{si} = \frac{F_{si} \cos \alpha_i - F_{ci} \sin \alpha_i}{\omega_{n_i}^2 \sqrt{(1 - \beta_i^2)^2 + (2\zeta_i \beta_i)^2}}, \qquad q_{ci} = \frac{F_{ci} \cos \alpha_i - F_{si} \sin \alpha_i}{\omega_{n_i}^2 \sqrt{(1 - \beta_i^2)^2 + (2\zeta_i \beta_i)^2}}$$

Equation (13.70) represents a sinusoidal curve as shown in Fig. 12.9(a). That is,

$$q_i = \xi_{qi} \sin(\omega t + \theta_{qi})$$

where $\xi_{qi}$ and $\theta_{qi}$ are the resultant coordinate amplitude and phase angle for the *i*th mode, respectively. They are calculated by

$$\xi_{qi} = \sqrt{(q_{si})^2 + (q_{ci})^2}, \qquad \theta_{qi} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{q_{ci}}{q_{si}}\right) \tag{13.71}$$

Engineers are often tempted to directly use the resultant coordinate value given above to obtain the modal displacement amplitude. However, this will lead to an unpredictable analysis because the resultant given in the preceding equation has lost the sign, which is needed to properly combine with other modes. The proper approach is to maintain two sets of results, one with sin $\omega t$ and the other with cos $\omega t$.
