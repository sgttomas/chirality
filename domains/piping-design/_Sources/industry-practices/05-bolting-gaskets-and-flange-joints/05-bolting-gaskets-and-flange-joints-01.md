## INTRODUCTION

This standard defines the requirements for assembly and tightening of gasketed, bolted flange connections in order to consistently achieve flange joint integrity for leak-free operation.

This document applies to any BP Cherry Point employees or contractors involved in bolt torqueing.

In this document, the following words have specific meanings:

- **'Shall'** is used where a provision is mandatory.
- **'Should'** is used where a provision is preferred.
- **'May'** is used where alternatives are equally acceptable.

## SECTION 1: GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

### 1.1 Joints

The following joints shall be torqued based on gasket, flange, and equipment type:

1.1.1 Gasket type

a) Ring type joint (RTJ) – All

b) Kammpro – 2-inch and larger

c) Spiral wound (flex gaskets) – 6-inch and larger

d) Corrugated metal gasket (CMG) – 14-inch and larger

1.1.2 Flange type

a) Isolation kit

b) Fiberglass

c) Lap joint

d) High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) – per manufacturer recommendations

e) Flanged joints with cap screws (see *Section 1.6: Cap Screws*)

1.1.3 Equipment Type

a) Heat exchanger bonnet, channel, shell, floating head and tail flanges only. All other exchanger connections to be torqued only if required under this section (*Section 1.1: Joints*).

b) Reactor flanges, regardless of gasket size and type

c) Heater flanges (i.e., thermowell, inlet and outlet headers, crossover piping), regardless of gasket size and type

d) Tower and vessel flanges shall be torqued as required in this section (*Section 1.1: Joints*).

e) Rotating equipment nozzle flanges, regardless of gasket size and type

1.1.4 Other

a) Double gasketed joints in hot hydrogen service (>500°F)

b) As specified by Reliability Engineering

The requirements and torque values herein do not apply to equipment that is not specifically listed (e.g., valve bonnets, rotating equipment case bolting, non-metallic lined piped, equipment anchor bolting, sight glasses). Equipment types that are not listed here should be tightened

## 1.3 Bolting

### 1.3.1 Common bolting

Common bolting includes:

- A193 Gr B7
- A193 Gr B16
- A193 Gr B7M

For other materials or grades, see a Reliability Engineer.

### 1.3.2 Bolting installation

Bolting shall be installed according to the following:

- With markings facing outward and all in the same direction in order to facilitate post-assembly inspection.
- A193 Gr B16 bolting used in place of A193 Gr B7 bolting when design temperatures are greater than 750°F.
- Bolts shall be visually checked prior to use for any physical damage to the shank or threads which would interfere with the bolt assembly or performance.

### 1.3.3 Reuse of bolting

Reuse of bolting is acceptable provided the following:

- The stud is free of mechanical and corrosion damage.
- The joint was lubricated during previous assemblies.
- Previous torque values did not equal or exceed 60% of bolt yield.
- Nuts shall run freely by hand past where they come to rest after tightening before new lubricant is applied.
- Bolting was not previously installed on an exchanger floating head.

Replace bolting if there are any signs of damage, or if nuts will not run freely by hand. Clean the bolt threads if they have been previously used. Cleaning should be done with a standard steel wire hand brush or straight thin wire rotary brush.

When bolts are reused they should be installed back in the same orientation so that the same threads are being worked. If one bolt in the joint is replaced, all of them should be replaced unless approved by Reliability Engineering. This helps insure consistency in applied torque and bolt loading.

---

## 1.4 Nuts

The common grades of heavy hex nuts are:

- A194 Gr 2H to be used with B7 studs
- A194 Gr 7 to be used with B16 studs.

Nuts and studs shall not be interchanged; for example if Gr B7M bolting is used, the nuts used shall be Gr 2HM.

When Gr 2HM fine thread nuts are used, regular height nuts are acceptable.

## 1.5 Washers

When a typical flange joint is to be torqued, two hardened steel washers shall be installed on one end of the stud bolt only, under the nut on which the torque wrench is placed per sketch in Figure 5. The washers are to be circular, non-galvanized and manufactured from 4130/4140 alloy material to ASTM F436 dimensions and hardness requirements. The special F436 washers are available in the Storehouse.

Washers for flange isolation kits are specified in *Section 3.1: Isolation Flange Kit Assembly Procedure* (in this document).

Washers shall not be reused.

## 1.6 Cap Screws

Cap screws shall adhere to the following requirements:

- When required for flanged joints (e.g., butterfly valve, orbit valve), cap screws shall be a heavy hex cap screw in the material and grade stamped on the end of head. A heavy hex cap screw shall also have a 1/32-inch raised landing under the head.
- Cap screws shall never be used for alignment; doing so may damage the tapped hole.
- Cap screws shall be threaded in by hand after all other studs have been brought up snug and the flange is properly aligned. If they cannot be threaded in by hand consult with a Reliability Engineer.
- When opening a flanged joint, cap screws shall be the first to come out.
- Flanged joints that use cap screws shall be torqued to a value that does not damage the threads in the valve body. See Table 1: Torque Summary for appropriate torque.

## 1.7 Lubricant/Anti-Seize

Lubricant/Anti-Seize for bolts, nuts, and washers shall be used on all joints. The torque values in tables 11, 12 and 13 in this standard are based on the properties of Loctite C5-A and may require adjusting if a substitute lubricant is used.

Lubricant/Anti-Seize shall never be applied to gaskets.

If there are abnormal ambient temperatures, consult with a Reliability Engineer on the appropriate torque value.

### 1.7.1 General external bolting applications (-20°F to 1800°F)

Bolt thread anti-seize for general use shall be copper-based Loctite C5-A (Storehouse free bin) or an approved alternate.

**DO NOT USE** C5-A for:

- Temperatures above 1800°F
- Flange isolation kit bolting
- Pure oxygen

---

- Oxygen-rich services
- Chlorine
- Other strong oxidizing services

1.7.2 Flange isolation kits (-65°F to 2600°F)

Bolt thread anti-seize for flange isolation kits shall be non-conductive anti-seize Jet-Lube ECF (Storehouse #2845028904) or an approved alternate. Jet-Lube ECF may also be used for general high temperature applications above 1800°F.

**DO NOT USE** Jet-Lube ECF for:

- Pure oxygen
- Oxygen-rich services
- Strong inorganic and organic acids
- Copper reactive agents
- Chlorine
- Other strong oxidizing services

1.7.3 Oxygen and aggressive chemicals (-65°F to 450°F)

Bolt thread anti-seize for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services shall be PCTFE/PTFE based paste, Hernon Drpistop 950 (Storehouse #2845095050) or an approved alternate.

**DO NOT USE** Hernon Dripstop 950 (Storehouse #2845095050) for general applications since it is very expensive.

For service temperatures above 450°F, contact Reliability Engineering.

1.7.4 Equipment internal bolting

Lubricant/anti-seize used on equipment internal bolting (e.g., floating heads) shall be compatible with the process service conditions and materials. Verify these conditions with a Reliability Engineer prior to using a lubricant/anti-seize.

For exchanger internal bolting (e.g., floating heads, bellows flange) use Castrol Paradene 32 on threads and nuts with torque values from tables 14, 15, and 16.

**DO NOT USE** when equipment is in oxygen-rich services.

1.7.5 Special applications

Moly B or Nickel based lubricants shall only be used as directed by Reliability Engineering.

## 1.8 Flanges

Flanges should be identified by the class, size and material stamped on the outside surface. The gasket sealing surface on a flange shall be free of defects (per ASME PCC-1) that jeopardize the integrity of the joint. Concern for surface finish or defects should be directed to a Reliability Engineer.

Flanges shall adhere to the following requirements:

- When cleaning a flange face, tool room supplied thin straight wire cups or wheels are used.
- Wheels (arbor brush style) should have 0.008-inch or less SS wire.
- Cups should have 0.010-inch or less SS wire.

---

- Brass arbor type (0.014-inch max. wire) shall not be used on flanges that are in ammonia service.

**NEVER USE thicker wire or twisted or braided wire cups/brushes – THEY WILL DAMAGE THE MACHINED FLANGE FACE.**

## 1.9 Gaskets

Gaskets shall be new and undamaged.

Refinery piping specifications designate the gasket material and rating to be used for specific service conditions. This information can be found in the Piping Material Specification (POPV/STP 205), available on BP Cherry Point's SharePoint site. Typical gasket selection per type and line class can be found below. However, some flanged joints in the field may have been upgraded to a gasket that is different than indicated in the tables. If a gasket found in the field does not match what is specified, verify its replacement with a Reliability Engineer.

Use full face gaskets for flat face flanges.

Verify gasket design with a Reliability Engineer for operating temperatures above 850°F.

### 1.9.1 Gasket types

Gaskets are classified in four categories: flat, spiral wound, Kammpro and RTJ.

#### 1.9.1.1 Flat gaskets

CMG type gaskets are used for most 150# and 300# pipe classes up to the 650°F temperature limit.

Special gaskets are still needed in oxygen, acid, lube oil, potable water, chemicals, and other corrosive services and seal flush systems (e.g., Top CHEM 2000, Teflon, rubber).

#### 1.9.1.2 Spiral wound gaskets

Spiral wound (flex type) gaskets with APX2 Graphite are used in some 150#, 300#, and most 600# and higher applications up to the 850°F temperature limit. Specially designed spiral wound gaskets with mica shielding can be used at temperatures above 850°F.

Spiral wound gaskets are in stock for use around existing equipment where thinner flat gaskets may induce pipe strain and may affect machinery alignment.

Spiral wound gaskets should never be used on slip-on flanges and the inner ring should never protrude into the flange bore.

#### 1.9.1.3 Kammpro gaskets

Kammpro gaskets are used on exchangers as specified, in lube oil and seal oil systems, and other special cases.

See a Reliability Engineer prior to reconditioning a Kammpro gasket for the proper technique and cover thickness.

#### 1.9.1.4 Ring joint gaskets

Ring joint gaskets (i.e., RTJs) are effective for high temperature and high pressure. These types of ring gaskets are made of steel, monel, stainless steel, or other metals.

Ring joint gaskets are available in both oval and octagonal shape. Cherry Point requires oval ring gaskets. The different sizes and pressure ratings are designated by an "R" number (see tables in *Section 4: Size Tables for Stud, Wrench, and RT Gaskets* (in this document)).

---

Impact guns shall never be used with ring joint gaskets, except for loosening studs. If the ring looks octagonal, it is worn out. In general, RTJs should not be reused unless directed to do so by Reliability Engineering.

## 2.2 Tightening Procedure

2.2.1 Review *Section 3: Specific Flange and Equipment Considerations* (in this document) for special flange and equipment considerations.

2.2.2 Confirm that stud lengths and diameters are correct for the flange type, size, and class per the tables in *Section 4: Size Tables for Stud, Wrench, and RTJ Gaskets* (in this document).

2.2.3 Liberally apply the lubricant to the stud over the length of the nut engagement, the nut faces, and the washer faces. Nuts should be run by hand past the point of final seating to assure there are no burrs or other obstruction. Remove any burrs on the flange or stud with a file.

2.2.4 Install all studs and nuts finger tight, along with washers (where required for torquing). Studs and nuts shall be assembled to have equal extension outside of the nuts on both sides of the flange joint. When flanges are made up, a minimum of one thread to a maximum of four threads shall be exposed past the nut.

When faced with the choice between adding a hardened washer or having extra threads showing, BP prefers that hardened washers are used.

2.2.5 Number the flange according to the appropriate bolt pattern in Appendix A: Bolt Patterns (in this document).

2.2.6 Ensure that the bolts are correctly aligned and lubricated before tightening. Studs should be tightened using the star pattern. Verify the gap around the joint during the tightening process.

2.2.7 If personnel are in fresh air during flange joint makeup, it is required that the procedure defined in this section (*Section 2.2: Tightening Procedure*) be followed, and that all studs be installed and tightened sequentially per the bolt pattern.

2.2.8 When torquing is required, tightening shall be performed with the following five pass procedure and relax torque requirements:

a) 1st Pass: 30% of final torque value applied according to Appendix A: Bolt Patterns

b) 2nd Pass: 60% of final torque value applied according to Appendix A: Bolt Patterns

c) 3rd Pass: 100% of final torque value applied according to Appendix A: Bolt Patterns

d) 4th Pass: 100% of final torque value applied in a circular pattern

## SECTION 3: SPECIFIC FLANGE AND EQUIPMENT CONSIDERATIONS

### 3.1 Isolation Flange Kit Assembly Procedure

3.1.1 Assemble the flange isolation kit as shown in Figure 6: Sketch of Assembled Isolation Flange. Use a torque wrench, NEVER use an impact wrench.

3.1.2 Use the torque settings from the tables in *Section 2: Flange Joint Assembly Requirements* (in this document). Unless otherwise directed, use the torque for 50% bolt yield (T).

3.1.3 Use the tightening procedure specified in *Section 2.2: Tightening Procedure* (in this document). Continue in a circular pattern, reducing the torque value on each subsequent bolt by 10% until there is no nut movement.

3.1.4 Use non-conductive anti-seize, as specified in *Section 1.7.2: Flange isolation kits (-65°F to 2600°F)* (in this document).

3.1.5 The conductivity between each individual stud and flange should be checked using a multimeter.

3.1.6 Use only the specified gaskets. CMG, spiral wound gaskets, or any other gaskets shall NOT be used.

3.1.7 Isolation kits, including gaskets, washers, and bolt sleeves are storehouse stock for most class 150 utility services in pipe sizes 12-inch and smaller. Separate washer sets and sleeves are also available in storehouse stock. For more information see *STP 205 Piping Material Specification (POPV) General Notes, Section 18: Flange Electrical Isolation*.

3.1.8 Materials (shown in Figure 6: Sketch of Assembled Isolation Flange) are limited to service temperatures up to 392°F. See a Reliability Engineer for higher temperatures.

Do not bolt a flat face cast iron flange up to a raised face flange. Use full face gaskets on flat face flanges.

## 3.5 HDPE Flanges

Bolting for HDPE flanges shall be tightened according to the patterns and torques recommended by the flange manufacturer for the specified gasket. Following the same bolting pattern recommended by the flange manufacturer, flange bolts shall be re-torqued two additional times to the final value, at least one hour after the first tightening, and again at least four hours after the second tightening.

- Do not replace fine thread with coarse thread bolts. Existing floating heads in sour service that were retrofitted with B7M fine thread bolts shall maintain the use of B7M fine thread bolts.

## 3.8 Fin Fan Plugs

### 3.8.1 Installation of fin fan plugs

Install fin fan plugs according to the following specifications:

- Visually inspect the gasket seating surfaces on header box for marks and scratches prior to plug installation. If abnormalities exist, do not install plug and promptly report issue to Reliability Engineering.
- Unless necessary, fin fan plugs should NOT be reused and gaskets shall NEVER be reused.
- Where carbon steel plugs are installed on carbon steel headers, gaskets shall be non-grafoiled soft iron, Lamons LP1 Kammpro, or LP1 Integral Plug style gaskets with Sigraflex Grade B flexible graphite.
- When installing Kammpro style gaskets with graphite, use extra care not to damage the graphite facing. Thread the new Kammpro gasket onto a new clean fin fan plug. Do not push the gasket on. Use only one gasket per plug.

---

- The fin fan header box threads shall be well lubricated using LOCTITE C5-A or approved equal. Other lubricants may require adjusting the torque value.

- Do not lubricate the fin fan plug or fin fan header box face. Excessive lubricant should not be pushed inside of the header box.

- Do not use lubricants from an aerosol can.

- If the plug cannot be threaded all the way in by hand, then the threads shall be chased using a proper tap. Contact the BP Cherry Point Machine Shop if the threads need to be tapped. Only tap threads when necessary; otherwise, threaded connections may become sloppy.

- Center gasket while hand tightening fin fan plug into header box.

- Fin fan plugs shall be torqued according to the size, material, and type of gasket being used, per Tables 3 and 4 of this document. Note that the size is the nominal threaded part of the plug, not the head.

- All plugs shall be relax torqued after a four-hour relaxation period.

- Impact guns shall never be used to remove or install fin fan plugs.

# SECTION 5: TORQUE VALUES

The following tables (Tables 11 to 16) contain torque values based on the noted bolt diameter, bolt material, % bolt yield strength and nut factor.

Torque = KDFp/12, where K = Nut factor, D = bolt diameter and Fp = bolt preload, or stress x effective thread stress area.

There is no longer a "new" or "used" bolting torque value. The provided torque value shall be used when tightening the joint at ambient conditions (40°F to 100°F) on new or used bolting.

See a Reliability Engineer for other materials, flanges not specified in this standard, or for unique bolting applications (e.g., tightening a joint online, warm joint temperatures, etc.).
