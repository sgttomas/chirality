# Flanges, Gaskets, and Bolting

**Refining Engineering Practices**

## 1.0 SCOPE

**1.1** This Practice covers requirements governing the selection of flanges, gaskets, and bolting for metallic piping systems. In addition, this Practice also covers supplemental requirements for flanges, gaskets, and bolting for pressure vessels, heat exchangers and tankage. The requirements in this Practice form the basis of the flange, gasket and bolting requirements contained in REP 5-2-3.

**1.2** When modifications to flanges, gaskets, and bolting on existing equipment are made, they shall meet the requirement of this Practice and shall be made in accordance with REP 7-3-3.

**1.3** Any deviation from this Practice must be approved by the procedure described in REP 1-1-3.

**1.4** An asterisk (*) indicates that a decision by the Owner's Engineer or Owner is required, or that additional information is to be furnished by the Purchaser.

**1.5** A revision bar indicates all changes made to this Revision.

## 3.0 DEFINITIONS

**3.1** Aggressive Environmental Service (AES) – Process services which result in material degradation such as cracking, scaling, blistering, and severe pitting and/or corrosion. Examples of such services are hydrogen sulfide, cyanides, caustic, amine, and hydrofluoric acid. AES process fluids are defined in REP 10–2–1.

**3.2** Category D Fluid Service – A fluid service, as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which all of the following apply.

- The fluid handled is non-flammable, non-toxic, and not damaging to human tissues as defined in paragraph 300.2 of ASME B31.3.
- The design gauge pressure does not exceed 150 psi.
- The design temperature is from -20°F through 366°F.

**3.3** High Temperature Hydrogen Service – For the definition of high temperature hydrogen service, see REP 10–2–1.

**3.4** Hydrogen Service – For the definition of hydrogen service, see REP 10–2–1.

**3.5** Inspector – A Phillips 66 appointed engineer or inspector.

## 4.0 FLANGES

**4.1** (*) All flanges shall be in accordance with the applicable industry standards shown in Table 1. Permissible flange materials shall be in accordance with REP 5-2-1 and REP 7-1-1D, as applicable. The design of flanges not shown in Table 1 shall be subject to the approval of the Owner's Engineer.

**4.2** Design limits, facing and gasket requirements for piping, nozzle and manways flanges shall be in accordance with Table 2. Design limits, facing and gasket requirements for services not covered in Table 1shall be subject to the approval of the Owner's Engineer.

**4.3** Pressure-temperature ratings for flanged joints shall be per the applicable flange standard; see Table 1.

**4.4** (*) The temperature reduction permitted by ASME B31.3 and REP 5-1-1 for uninsulated flanges shall not be used unless approved by the Owner's Engineer.

**4.5** ASME B16.5 class 400 flanges shall not be used.

**4.6** (*) Flange facing finish shall be per the applicable flange standard; see Table 1. Unless otherwise specified by the Owner's Engineer, the gasket contact surface for flanges to be used with spiral wound gaskets shall have a finish within the range of 125 μ inch to 250 μ inch arithmetic average (Ra).

**4.7** (*) Proprietary piping components such as PLIDCO® flanges, Weld-Ends®, and Garlock® or Reftane® connections shall not be used without approval of Owner's Engineer. When the use of such devices is approved, they shall be designed in accordance with the ASME Code, Section VIII, ASME B31.1, or ASME B31.3 and shall be applied in accordance with the manufacturer's recommendations and additional instructions (if any) from the Owner's Engineer.

**4.8** (*) The use of slip-on flanges shall be subject to the following limitations:

1. Subject to approval by the Owner's Engineer, they shall be used only as piping flanges; weld neck flanges or long weld necks shall be used for all pressure vessels and heat exchangers per REP 7-1-1D.
2. They shall not be used above 500°F or for ASME B16.5 Class 600 and higher pressure ratings.
3. They shall not be used in erosive service or corrosive service where the specified corrosion allowance exceeds 1/8-inch.
4. They shall not be used in cyclic service.
5. They shall not be used in lethal service as defined by the ASME Code.

---

6. They shall not be used in carbon steel or low alloy steel aggressive environmental service (AES) with the exclusion of lean amine, fresh amine, caustic, carbonate, where they may be used subject to Phillips 66 Materials/Corrosion Engineer approval. They may be used in high alloy AES other than hydrogen subject to Phillips 66 Materials/Corrosion Engineer approval.

7. They shall not be welded directly to welding fittings.

**4.9** The use of lap joint flanges shall be subject to the following limitations:

1. They shall be used only as piping flanges.

2. They shall not be used above 750°F or for ASME B16.5 Class 600 or higher pressure ratings.

3. They shall not be used where the combined longitudinal stress in the pipe where attached to the lap-joint stub-end, resulting from pressure, weight, and thermal expansion, exceeds the ASME B31.3 allowable stress at the pipe design temperature.

4. Lap joint stub ends shall conform to ASME B16.9 dimensions and ASME B31.3. Class WP ASTM wrought fittings shall be required. The stub end thickness shall be in accordance with the requirements of Table 3.

5. Lap joint backing flanges shall be forged carbon steel. Slip-on flanges shall not be permitted for use as backing flanges in lap joints.

6. Lap joint stub ends shall have a flatness tolerance of 0.0035 inch over the entire circumference. Front and back faces shall be parallel.

7. The flange may be of material different from that of the pipe provided galvanic corrosion will not occur (e.g., carbon steel flanges may be used on lap-joint stub-ended stainless steel pipe in aboveground service).

8. The flange shall not be used with ring joint gaskets.

**4.10** (\*) Suitability of flanged joints for services under bending loads shall be confirmed by calculations when the pipe diameter exceeds NPS 12 and the pipe design temperature exceeds 650°F, and for all temperatures when the diameter exceeds NPS 24. The calculation procedures used for this analysis shall be subject to the approval of the Owner's Engineer. Potential calculation procedures that still require Owner's Engineer approval are the Kellogg and ASME Section III, Article NB-33658 methods.

**4.11** (\*) Electrically insulated flanged joints (joints that will not conduct electricity across the flange connection) shall not be used without approval of Owner's Engineer. If approved for use, electrically insulated flange joints shall comply with the following:

1. Electrically insulated joints shall be used to isolate lines heated by electric impedance heating systems.

2. These joints shall be considered for the following:

   a) Cathodically protected systems.

   b) Stray current protection for pipelines used to load or unload barges, tankers, tank cars, or tank trucks when the fluids have closed cup flash points less than 130°F or that are at a temperature above or within 15°F of their flash points.

3. The joint assembly shall be designed to use a central insulating gasket, insulating bolt sleeves and washers, and steel bolt back-up washers. The central gasket shall be made from a material with nonconductive binders and impregnates.

---

4. A gasket shall be used on both sides of the central isolating gasket to provide a seal due to the typically poor seal ability of the central isolating gasket. These gaskets shall be full-face flexible graphite sheet gaskets per Table 8.

**4.12** (\*) Threaded steel flanges shall not be used without approval of the Owner's Engineer.

**4.13** Cast iron flanges shall be permitted only if furnished as integral flanges on cast iron piping or equipment.

**4.14** (\*) Aluminum alloy flanges shall not be used without approval of the Owner's Engineer. If approved, they shall be either wrought or forged.

**4.15** If flanges are to be insulated, they shall comply with the requirements of BEP 11-3-6.

**4.16** Unless otherwise specified by the Purchaser, flanges shall be furnished to match the size, bore, thickness and pressure rating of the attached pipe. End preparation of flanges shall conform to the following standards:

1. Weld neck flanges: ASME B16.25, Figure 2A or 3A as applicable.
2. Socket weld flanges: ASME B16.11.
3. Threaded flanges: ASME B1.20.1.

## 5.0 GASKETS

**5.1** The selection of gasket type for piping systems shall be based on the requirements given in Table 2. Gasket selection for service conditions not covered in Table 2 shall be subject to the approval of the Owner's Engineer.

**5.2** (\*) The selection of gasket type for pressure vessels and the exchangers shall be approved by the Owner's Engineer.

**5.3** Requirements for spiral wound gaskets shall be as covered in Table 4 and shall be color-coded per ASME B16.20.

**5.4** Ring joint gaskets shall be in accordance with ASME B16.20 and shall meet the following requirements.

**5.4.1** Permissible ring joint materials shall be as shown in Table 5.

**5.4.2** Soft iron or low carbon content steel oval ring gaskets are satisfactory for carbon steel piping with a maximum corrosion allowance of 0.050 inch. Oval ring gaskets of a material other than soft iron or low carbon content steel shall be used if the corrosion allowance is greater than 0.050 inch. The ring material selected shall have a predicted corrosion rate of less than 0.010 inch/year.

**5.4.3** (\*) The hardness of the ring gasket shall be lower than that of the flange. Where it is not possible to obtain this hardness differential, the material selection shall be approved by the Owner's Engineer.

**5.5** Sheet gaskets shall be in accordance with the following requirements:

1. (\*) Service limitations for sheet gaskets shall be as given in Table 2 and Table 6. Sheet gaskets may be used in hydrocarbon, steam, and boiler feed water if approved by the Owner's Engineer.
2. Sheet gaskets shall have a flat ring shape with dimensions in accordance with ASME B16.21.
3. Thickness and material requirements for sheet gaskets shall be as covered in Table 6.

## 6.0 BOLTING

**6.1** (*) Except where stress corrosion cracking (SCC) is a consideration, bolting shall be used as specified in Table 10. In environments that SCC of low-alloy or carbon steel bolting, the bolting shall be as shown in Table 11. The use of bolting not specified in Table 10 and Table 11 shall require the approval of the Owner's Engineer. Note that bolting materials used for pressure vessel and heat exchanger components shall be in accordance with the ASME Code, Section II.

**6.2** (*) All flange bolting, except in water service, shall be of the stud bolt type with continuous full length threads. Bolt lengths shall be calculated and specified in accordance with ASME B16.5. When approved by the Owner's Engineer, cap screws may be used for flange connections when through stud bolt clearance is not available.

**6.3** Bolting for nonferrous flanges and for nonmetallic flanges having a metal temperature between -20°F and 800°F shall be ASTM A193 Grade B7 if galvanic corrosion is not an expected problem.

**6.4** (*) Carbon steel bolting conforming to ASTM A307 Grade B may be considered for Category D fluid service. Use of such bolting shall require approval of the Owner's Engineer.

**6.5** Bolts with either zinc or cadmium plating shall not be used in hydrocarbon or toxic service or in any service above 400°F.

**6.6** Bolting for marine environments shall be coated with ceramic-filled fluorocarbon (Stand-Cote® SC-1). Other manufacturers of coated bolting shall provide evidence that bolts and nuts are equivalent to Stand-Cote SC-1. Evidence shall include laboratory evaluation (at manufacturer's expense before delivery, if accepted) of randomly selected bolts.

**6.7** (*) Coatings for flange bolting shall not be specified except when approved by Owner's Engineer for highly corrosive areas such as cooling towers and docks.

**6.8** (*) Bolt selection for bimetallic flanges shall be approved by Owner's Engineer.

**6.9** If austenitic bolting is to be used with ferritic flanges operating above 400°F, the flanges shall not be insulated. Low yield strength bolting (minimum specified yield strength < 30 ksi) shall not be used for Class 400 and higher flanged joints nor for flanged joints using metallic gaskets, unless calculations indicate adequate strength to maintain joint tightness (see ASME B31.3, paragraph 309.2.1).

**6.10** Unless otherwise specified by the Purchaser, stud bolts shall be threaded full length and each bolt shall be supplied with two semi-finished heavy hex nuts. In addition, bolt assemblies over 1-1/2 inch in diameter shall be furnished with hardened washers.
