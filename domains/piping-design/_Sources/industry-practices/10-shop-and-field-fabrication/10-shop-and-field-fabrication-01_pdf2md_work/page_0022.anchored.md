- 24.1.2 Test pressures, as determined by the requirements of this standard, shall be shown on the line summary, rating sheets , and isometric drawings for all piping. These shall be submitted to BP CHP well in advance of construction for review. No test pressure shall be revised without the approval of the BP CHP Reliability Engineer. Any component that is inspected by other means, in lieu of pressure testing, shall be as directed or approved by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

- 24.1.3 System testing through equipment is generally not acceptable if the test pressure would be limited by the equipment to levels below those specified herein. This requirement can only be waived by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

- 24.1.4 Testing procedures for firewater piping shall be in accordance with NFPA, as a minimum. The test pressure of above ground threaded fire water piping shall at least meet the requirements of NFPA (200 psig or 50 psig above operating pressure, whichever is greater).

- 24.1.5 Potable water piping shall be sanitized per BP CHP Safety & Health Policy #730, after pressure testing and before placing lines in service.

## 24.2 Test Temperature and Fluids

- 24.2.1 For pipe wall thicknesses 1" and smaller for all material types, the minimum metal temperature during pressure testing shall be 40°F. For pipe wall thicknesses greater than 1", the minimum metal temperature during pressure testing shall be determined per the applicable ASME piping code. If testing is necessary under ambient conditions colder than the minimum specified test temperature, a heated water source may be used to maintain the minimum required metal temperature, or a method of recirculation of heated glycol mixture or similar fluid may be submitted for BP CHP approval. When the ambient temperature is below the specified minimum metal temperature during pressure testing, the pipe metal temperature shall be monitored to assure that it does not fall below the minimum requirement.

- 24.2.2 The use of salt or brackish water is not allowed. Test water shall be free of sediment and corrosive substances. Hydrostatic testing of austenitic stainless steels shall be done with potable quality water containing less than 50 ppm chlorides. Alternate test fluids may be used with BP CHP approval.
