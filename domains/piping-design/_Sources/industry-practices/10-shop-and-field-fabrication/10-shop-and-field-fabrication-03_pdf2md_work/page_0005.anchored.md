Bending radii shall be indicated on the isometric drawing. The bending radius, R₀, shall be 1.5 × D₀ or 5 × D₀ (where D₀ is the nominal diameter of the pipe) and depends on the minimum remaining wall thickness, maximum deformation allowed, available space and available bending machines. Other bend radii shall be subject to the approval of the Principal.

If the pipe contains a longitudinal weld, this weld should be located in the neutral zone of the bend. If the pipe will be installed horizontally, the longitudinal weld shall be located on the top of the pipe. If one spool is bent in various planes, the longitudinal weld shall be 45° from the top of the pipe.

Pipe bending shall be carried out in pipe bending machines or presses using formers. Tolerances for thinning, flattening and wrinkling are specified in (2.6.2).

The Manufacturer shall submit bending, heat treatment and non-destructive examination procedures to the Principal for acceptance. These procedures shall, as a minimum, including the following:

- operating sequence;
- forming process and machinery used;
- production parameters which shall at least cover:
  - width of heated band;
  - heating rate and method;
  - bending temperature range;
  - cooling rate, cooling method and coolant flow rate;
  - production bending speed;
  - method of temperature measurement and recording;
  - bend dimensions and tolerance;
  - non-destructive examination procedures;
  - post forming;
  - fabrication procedure qualification;
  - production control procedure;
  - calibration of equipment and tolerances.

Three types of bending processes are identified:

1. **Cold bending**, which is defined as bending at a temperature well below the final heat treatment temperature of both ferritic and austenitic materials. Cold bending shall not be used for piping in sodium hydroxide service.

2. **Hot bending**, which is defined as bending at a specified elevated temperature. Heat shall be applied uniformly before bending. Local heating by manually operated gas torches shall not be applied. Water cooling at any time during bending shall not be applied.

3. **High Frequency Induction Bending (HFIB)**, which is a hot bending process using machines through which the pipe is passed through a coil in a static system, successively, a narrow band of pipe material is rapidly heated to the required bending temperature using the high frequency induction method. The adjacent pipe is kept cool by water and/or air jets, thus providing the necessary support for the heated area without the need for mandrels. It shall be ensured that the bending temperature is uniform over the complete circumference of the bend. An induction ring with a square profile should be used. For low alloy steel this method is generally attractive only for special applications (e.g. large diameters, large wall thicknesses) where welding elbows are not readily available.
