If adjustments must be made for satisfactory fitup, but provision has not been allowed for fitup field welds, the alternative is to cut the spool as needed to achieve proper fitup; this may be expensive.

A minimum distance between butt welds of 6 times the pipe wall thickness, or 2 inches, whichever is greater, is recommended.

Before bolting up flanged connections, flange faces must be parallel within 1/16 inch per foot measured across any diameter; flange bolt holes must have less than 1/8 inch maximum offset. This conforms to ASME/ANSI B31.3, Section 335.1.1(c).

## 640 Pressure Testing of Piping Systems

### 641 General

Proof testing of completed piping systems is required by ASME/ANSI B31.3 and Company Practice. Small pressure vessels and similar equipment are often included in the pressure test systems even though such equipment has already been strength tested to Code requirements in the shop (except for the cases of field fabrication, alterations, and repairs). Proof testing of completed systems tests the assembled system for strength and leakage.

ASME/ANSI B31.3 Section 345 covers testing of piping. Code testing of equipment, if performed in conjunction with field testing of piping systems, must also meet the requirements of the appropriate specifications and code (ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section VIII, UG-99 to UG-101, for example, cover testing of pressure vessels). Guidelines for field testing are given in the *Pressure Vessel, Fired Heater and Waste Heat Recovery*, and *Heat Exchanger and Cooling Tower Manuals*.

A piping system should be tested:

- Before initial operation

- After alterations or repairs that affect its strength. Alternatives such as increased nondestructive examination may be substituted after appropriate approval

- When rerated for higher pressure or temperature service, such that previous tests do not satisfy Code requirements for the rerated conditions

Hydrostatic testing is done with a liquid, normally water. See Section 642. Service testing of low-pressure (not exceeding 150 psig) utility lines with their normal operating fluid (air, water, steam) is permitted. See Section 643. Pneumatic testing with air or inert gas may be necessary for large lines designed for gas or vapor service where the weight of water would overstress the piping or pipe supports, or for refractory-lined piping. See Section 644.
