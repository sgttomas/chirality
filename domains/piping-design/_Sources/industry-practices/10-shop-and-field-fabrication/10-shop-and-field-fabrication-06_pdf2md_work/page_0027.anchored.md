- Piping for oil mist lubrication distribution systems should be flushed in accordance with Standard Drawing GF-G1005 in the *General Machinery Manual*

- Piping for central lube oil distribution systems should be designed so that they can be adequately cleaned prior to startup. Pockets in which dirt can collect should be avoided. Lines should be air-blown to clean and dry them, and then flushed with lube oil. Flushing continues at velocities higher than design oil distribution velocities until there is no dirt pickup on the filters

- Fuel oil and fuel naphtha systems with burner openings of less than 0.125 inch should be cleaned as described in Model Specification PIM-MS-2411

- As protection, systems cleaned by these special procedures should be filled with oil, dry air, or nitrogen immediately after cleaning, if they are not put directly into service

- High-velocity steam-blowing and thermal cycling of the piping will remove weld spatter, mill scale and debris. This removal prevents damage during operation to control valve seats and steam turbine blades by solid particle impingement. Operations personnel are normally involved in this procedure as well as the construction forces (before turnover)

Many systems can be satisfactorily water-washed by plant pumps. To drain water at the locations necessary to get debris out of the lines, valves may need to be dropped out, blinds removed, or temporary spools and hoses installed. Utility system headers should have valves at their ends to permit adequate initial cleanup when put into service.

The following guidelines may apply to water washing:

- Water velocity should be somewhat higher than the design flowrate. Velocities from 8 to 12 feet/second are suggested for lines up to 10 inches in diameter. Larger lines should use the highest velocity possible. Compressed air may be injected into the water while flushing to increase the turbulence and velocity

- Low chloride water (less than 100 ppm) should be used to flush stainless steel piping

- Vessels and columns containing internals, and heat exchangers should be excluded from the flushing operation, and temporary bypasses installed

- Vessels or columns included in a flushing circuit should be inspected, and any debris accumulated during the flush removed

- The direction of flushing water flow should be from the upstream side to the downstream side of any check valve or stop check in the system

Lines that cannot be water-washed should be air-blown, if small enough for air-blowing to be effective in removing solids that could plug or damage exchangers or control valves. Typically, air blowing is also done on the following piping systems:

- Instrument and utility air lines
