## Temperature

If the operating temperature of the line is above 200°F then the weld has effectively been preheated and, as long as the weld is above 200°F, the chance of weld cracking is very low. Some risk of cracking still exists in sour or hydrogen service when the weld cools to ambient during a shutdown. For all wall thicknesses except "very thin" (defined previously), the operating temperature of the line has no effect on whether burn-through will occur.

## 673 Controllable Parameters

### Welding Procedure

**Low-hydrogen Electrodes.** Low-hydrogen electrodes (Exx16 or Exx18) are superior to cellulosic electrodes (Exx10, Exx13, Exx24, etc.) for hot tap and repair welding for two reasons:

- They have a lower risk of weld cracking
- They are less penetrating and therefore reduce the risk of burn-through

**Temper Bead Welding Sequence.** The most practical way of avoiding both burn-throughs and weld cracks is by using either 3/32 or 1/8-inch diameter electrodes with a temper bead weld pass sequence (see Figure 600-8). The basic principle of the temper bead technique is to avoid completing the weld (cap pass) on the base metal of the pipe. The temper bead technique is effective in reducing the weld HAZ hardness and can significantly reduce the weld cracking risk.

The temper bead technique is often not well understood by welders and craft foremen, but the need to do it conscientiously should be emphasized.

**Longitudinal Welds on Full Encirclement Sleeves.** The two halves of a full encirclement sleeve should be joined using a butt weld rather than lap welded with a butt strap. This longitudinal butt weld should be a full penetration weld. Fusing the weld to the main run pipe is acceptable. When a backing strip is not used, penetration of the first pass into the run pipe is usually negligible and is not of concern. For very thin wall thicknesses, use a backing strip to avoid fusing to the main run and to minimize the risk of burn-through.
