Chemical cleaning is normally done by a specialist contractor using proven methods, experienced personnel, and equipment designed for the purpose. Chemical cleaning typically involves several steps:

1. The metal is degreased with a hot alkaline solution. Oil or grease prevent the acid from contacting the rust and mill scale, so they must be washed out before the actual scale removal can begin.

2. A warm solution of inhibited acid is circulated for several hours to dissolve the rust and scale. Acids commonly used include hydrochloric and citric.

3. The acid solution is dumped, the system is rinsed with water and final traces of acid are neutralized with a dilute alkali such as sodium carbonate (soda ash).

4. The final step is a passivation treatment, usually involving either phosphoric acid or a phosphate. Freshly cleaned steel will rerust rapidly. To prevent this, the passivation treatment forms an iron phosphate surface layer on the metal that inhibits the rerusting. Because water will destroy the passive film and cause rusting, the piping should not be rinsed with water after being passivated.

Chelating agents are often used in place of acids for rust removal. They function by chemically complexing the rust rather than actually dissolving it. Chelating agents work more slowly and are generally less effective in removing heavy rust scale. However, they are easier to use because they save several steps in the cleaning procedure.

Before using a chelating agent the piping is degreased. It is then filled with an acidic chelating agent, which is circulated. Next alkali, usually ammonia, is added to the solution to bring the pH to near-neutral. Circulation is continued. This final circulation accomplishes neutralization and passivation in one step. It isn't necessary to follow it with the phosphate passivation from step 4 for acid cleaning. A similar technique uses citric acid and ammonia, and also eliminates the need for subsequent passivation.

Which of the two procedures to use is usually decided by the chemical cleaning contractor. This is a safe approach when using a contractor with an established local reputation, because our cleaning specifications are basically performance specs.

A conventional acid-cleaning procedure is typified by use of a strong acid, such as hydrochloric, for the cleaning, with phosphoric acid or a phosphate for passivation. Chelating can be recognized by the trade name of the chelating agent; "Vertan" is the most common.
