## 677 Considerations for Other Materials

**High Strength Line Pipe (API 5L X56 & above).** These steels are covered in the *Pipeline Manual*, Section 700.

**Low Alloy Steel.** Because postweld heat treatment (PWHT) is required, ambient temperature hot taps of steels with P-numbers 4 and 5 should not be considered. For an explanation of P-numbers see ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section IX, QW-420. If the line is operating at above 300°F, a hot tap may be considered without PWHT with limited risk. The weld will become susceptible to hydrogen cracking if the line is allowed to cool below 300°F prior to PWHT. If this happens the weld should be replaced and heat treated or undergo thorough ultrasonic examination before and after heat treatment.

**Austenitic Stainless Steels (P-number 8).** Only "L" grade (304L, 316L, etc.) or stabilized (321 or 347) stainless steels should be welded. Caution is advised when welding on stainless steels in environments that may contain chlorides. Particular care is needed on insulated line. Prior to welding, insulation and any deposits should be removed and the line inspected both visually and via dye penetrant. Chlorides can cause stress corrosion cracking of stainless steels at temperatures above 150°F.

Unlike carbon and low alloy steels, stainless steels are not subject to hydrogen cracking at welds. Therefore, reducing the flow is not necessary. Flow is recommended for making hot taps on very thin wall. Preheat and temper bead welding of stainless steel are not required.

**Other Materials.** Other materials such as aluminum, copper alloys, and cast iron may also be hot tapped, but may require special procedures. Consult CRTC's Materials and Equipment Engineering group.
