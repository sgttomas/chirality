**Weld Cracking.** Decreasing the flow rate for liquids and for low pressure and low velocity gases will decrease the probability of weld cracks. This is because the slower weld cooling rate will result in lower hardness welds and less susceptibility to delayed hydrogen cracking. This benefit will decrease as the pipe wall thickness increases.

Cracking probability is independent of the fluid heat capacity in thick wall pipes. Decreasing or stopping the flow is desirable for intermediate wall thicknesses, and recommended for thin wall thicknesses. Flow should be maintained for very thin wall thicknesses.

- **Liquids:** The heat removal is independent of pressure for liquids, but is substantial even if the fluid is not flowing. The fluid properties (density, specific heat, viscosity, and latent heat of vaporization) determine the effectiveness of the fluid as a means of removing heat during welding on a pressurized line
