Failure to properly route small piping will result in costly rework.

## 652 Small Piping Connections Subject to Vibration or in High-Pressure Service

Small piping connections such as vents, drains, instrument connections, auxiliary piping, etc., subject to vibration at equipment or on larger piping must be designed to prevent failure. Construction forces must ensure that the design purpose is achieved.

Vibrations can arise from:

- Cyclic pressures from reciprocating compressors and pumps
- High frequency vibrations at centrifugal pumps and compressors, and high-energy dissipation at control valves and relief valves

To avoid fatigue failure, designs for these connections should have:

- Short, heavy-wall pipe nipples, or equivalent, with bridge welding between the first valve and the equipment or larger piping; see Standard Drawing GD-L1057. Minimum recommended connection size is NPS 1; NPS ¾ is the minimum accepted by some organizations
- Sturdy, lightweight root valves, as appropriate for the service: see Standard Drawing GD-L1057. For instrument connections, suggested valves include Kerotest 110N or 200N, Dragon 10F, or reduced port Rockwell (Edward) Univalve
- Adequate bracing of the root valve and downstream piping or instruments

## 653 Socket Welding and Seal Welding of Fittings and Valves

Heat produced by socket welding can generate thermal expansion stresses that may cause cracking in the weld. As a general practice, there should be a nominal 1/16-inch clearance between the end of the pipe and the bottom of the fitting recess, as shown in ASME/ANSI B31.3 Figures 328.5.2 B and C. This is achieved by pulling the pipe back 1/16 inch after contacting the bottom of the recess. This procedure is a prudent precautionary measure for carbon steel piping. For stainless and alloy materials, and for lines in high temperature service (over 800°F), it is critical.

Heat produced by socket welding or seal welding at connections to valves may distort seating faces so that it is difficult to get tight valve shutoff in service. To minimize this risk, gate and globe valves should be closed and then backed open a half turn for welding. Prevent overheating of valve packing, gaskets and seal rings if weld heat input is high or if special materials for packing, gaskets or seal rings are not suitable for usual service maximums (650°F to 800°F); see manufacturer's catalog and installation instructions.
