- **Heat Exchangers.** Shop code test pressures should be obtained from the manufacturer's data reports. Also, exchangers are usually retested after maintenance and retested if corroded. Hydrostatic testing of a heat exchanger whose design differential pressure is less than the pressure rating of either side must take into account the allowable differential pressure. Upon releasing the test pressure from such a system, the pressure on one side of the tube sheet drops faster than on the other side. If the resulting differential pressure becomes large enough, the tube sheet or another common part may fail. The pressure must be released slowly enough to avoid a large momentary differential pressure

- **Steam Service Coils.** Steam service coils that qualify as boiler code piping should be hydrostatically tested in accordance with the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code and ASME/ANSI B31.1, Power Piping.

- **Equipment Supports.** Adequacy of equipment supports, including column and vessel skirts, should be checked if designs are not based on water-filled contents

- **Mechanical Equipment.** It is not good practice to hydrotest through pumps, compressors, and other mechanical equipment.

## 643 Service Testing of Low Pressure Utility Systems

Utility piping within ASME/ANSI B31.3 Category D fluid service (nonflammable, nontoxic fluid not exceeding 150 psig, with a temperature from -20°F to 366°F) may be tested with the service fluid. Pressure should be gradually increased in steps, holding the pressure at each step long enough to equalize piping strains. For piping in gas or vapor service, a preliminary check for leaks should be made at 15 psig for lines NPS 14 and larger, and not more than 25 psig for other lines.

Utility piping designed for pressures over 150 psig, including nominal 150-lb steam lines, must be hydrostatically tested as described in Section 642.

## 644 Pneumatic Testing

Pneumatic testing involves the hazard of a sudden release of the energy stored in the compressed gas. It should be avoided except where conditions do not allow hydrostatic testing, for instance, large relief lines that cannot withstand loading when water-filled, and refractory-lined piping. A testing specification with safety procedures should be prepared for each line to be pneumatically tested. Company practice requires approval by the designated level of local operating management, as well as by the engineering manager for the project.

To minimize the chance of brittle failure during a pneumatic test, the testing should only be made well above the metal ductile-brittle transition temperature. Pneumatic testing should not be done on nonmetallic piping systems.

The procedure for pneumatic testing should have the following provisions:

- A test pressure limit of 110% of design pressure
