Acceptable welding processes, discussed in the *Welding Manual*, are:

- Shielded metal arc welding (SMAW)

- Gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW)

- Gas metal arc welding (GMAW), interrupted arc. Interrupted arc GMAW is restricted to butt welds and is not used for weldolets and other attachments. Root passes may be made in any direction, but fill and cap passes are not permissible in the downhill direction below the two o'clock position.

- Flux cored arc welding (FCAW). Both gas-shielded and self-shielded electrodes are permitted. The types of self-shielded electrodes are restricted.

For the gas-shielded arc welding processes, the wind velocities in the weld area should not exceed 5 mph. Wind screens and enclosures should be used to shield the weld area if necessary.

Contractors must qualify their welding procedures except where they are provided Company qualified procedures. Qualification of welders by an organization other than the organization doing the welding is not acceptable.

## Seal Welding

Seal welding of threaded joints eliminates the stress risers caused by exposed threads. Secondarily, it eliminates leakage at threaded joints. Seal welding is normally used in piping services involving cyclic loads (vibrational or thermal) or flammable or dangerous fluids.

Seal welding should cover all exposed threads with weld metal and blend smoothly into the pipe (see Figure 600-1). Threaded joints must be made up dry, without thread compound or Teflon tape, and with proper thread engagement.

## Bridge Welding

In severe cyclic service valves at branch connections should be made with short nipples or male ends and the seal weld built up to the full hub diameter of the valve for the full length of the nipple. See Figure 600-2 for typical bridge welding details.

## Galvanized Pipe

Galvanized pipe should not be welded unless the galvanizing is ground off. The zinc from the galvanizing is a contaminant and if picked up by the weld can result in poor quality. Stainless steel welds are particularly sensitive to zinc and even minute pickup in the weld can result in severe cracking.

## Minimum Weld Spacing

The minimum spacing between circumferential weld joints should be 2 inches or 6 times the wall thickness of the pipe, whichever is greater.
