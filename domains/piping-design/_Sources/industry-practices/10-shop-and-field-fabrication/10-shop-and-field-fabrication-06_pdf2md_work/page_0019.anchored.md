- A pressure relief device set no higher than the test pressure plus either 10% of the test pressure or 50 psi, whichever is less

- Test fluid consisting of air or inert gas

- Using soap solution or equivalent liquid, a preliminary examination for leaks at 25 psig or one-half the test pressure, whichever is lower, of all weld seams, joints and connections

- Subsequently, the pressure is gradually increased in steps until the test pressure is reached. Pressure is held at each step long enough to equalize piping strains

- Pressure shall be reduced from the test pressure slowly to design pressure before examining for leakage at weld seams, joints and connections

To avoid creating an explosive hydrocarbon-air mixture, air must not be used as a test media for a system that has been in hydrocarbon service without first properly cleaning and purging the system in order.

Because static buildup in solid CO₂ particles in the discharge jet is a source of ignition, carbon dioxide from a high-pressure source should not be discharged into a flammable atmosphere.

If steam or other condensible fluid is used as the test fluid, precautions shall be taken to avoid condensation before system depressurization. Steam tests should only be performed on equipment verified as adequate for vacuum.

Large relief systems designed to operate at low pressures—less than 15 psig—are often tested for leaks with air or steam. Testing must conform with the above precautions and restrictions.

## 645 Corrosion or Contamination by Test Fluids

Clean potable water is normally specified as the test fluid. Precautions include:

- For chrome-nickel 300 Series austenitic stainless steel, chloride content of the water should be less than 100 ppm to avoid chloride stress corrosion cracking. For 300 Series stainless steels other than 321 and 347 that have been in service above 700°F, the test fluid should be a 1½% soda ash solution to prevent polythionic stress corrosion of the sensitized steel

- For carbon steel piping in 98% sulfuric acid service, where severe corrosion would result from diluting the concentrated acid with any water left in the piping, diesel oil, kerosene or another clean hydrocarbon with a flash point above 120°F can be used. Extreme caution should be exercised to prevent spills or fires when using hydrocarbons as the test fluid.
