Notes: 1. With stud stresses as recommended above, bearing stress on some ring joint gaskets is higher than desirable. Such loadings may cause more problems from deformation of the ring and groove than might result from leaks from reduced loadings. If such conditions are found, they should be reported.
2. Spiral wound gaskets have a centering ring somewhat thinner than the uncompressed gasket. This ring acts as a stop to prevent overcompression and damage to the gasket. After initial bolt up, the joint should be inspected to see if the flange faces are closed uniformly against the ring. If not, apply additional loads up to the 1/3 added recommendation. If still not closed, engineering review is suggested.

- (1) The pipefitter or boilermaker should be alert to flange distortion and bolting elongation. Upon evidence of each, he should stop the operation and obtain advice. For flange standards not included, engineering review is recommended unless suitable loadings are known. If these recommendations are not satisfactory, any alternative procedures developed should be recorded. This table does not apply to special bolt-up procedures prepared for particular closures.
- (2) For flange materials not listed (excluding cast iron), initial bolt stress (with A193 studs) should be equal to 30,000 psi times the ratio of the pressure rating at 100°F to the pressure rating for carbon steel (for the same flange) at 100°F. It should not exceed 30,000 psi without engineering review.
- (3) For conversion of bolting stress to torque, see Figure 600-6.
- (4) ASME/ANSI B16.5 includes cast or forged steel flanges from preceding standard ASA-B16.e.
- (5) For Class 150 flanges of Types 304, 304L, or 316L stainless steel: the flange should be carefully observed for evidence of distortion. For distortion or other problems, engineering assistance should be obtained. Appropriate gasket loads should be considered.
- (6) If bolting is other than A193, engineering review is recommended unless suitable loadings are known from previous experience.
- (7) If leaks develop, these stresses should be increased by up to 1/3 (without engineering review) if there is not evidence of flange distortion or concern over damage to the gasket or flange seat.
- (8) Some Class 150 piping classifications permit mild steel bolts for flange diameters through 16" with up to 1" bolts with minimum yield strength of 30,000 psi. Thus the initial bolting stress has been reduced to 24,000 psi. Further tightening should be limited without engineering review) to 30,000 psi and stopped if there is indication of bolt yielding. If the nut makes up 2 or 3 facets without a clear decrease in flange gap, the bolt is yielding.
- (9) Cast iron flanges require full face gaskets and mild steel bolts. If further tightening is required, initial stresses may be increased by 1/3 (without engineering review). Be careful not to yield the bolts (Note 9).
