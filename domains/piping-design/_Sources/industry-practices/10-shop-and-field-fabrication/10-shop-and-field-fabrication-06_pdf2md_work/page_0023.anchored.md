If carbon steel flanges are in alignment but the faces are not parallel, and if a run of line sufficient for deflection exists on either side of the flanges, the first bolts to be tightened should be on the side of the flange where the greatest opening exists. When flanges are drawn up parallel, all bolts can be tightened to the same approximate degree of tension, and then gradually increased around the bolt circle until all are tight.

If the flanges cannot be drawn parallel without severely straining the bolts, the pipe, if carbon steel, may be heated to a temperature sufficient for bending. At this temperature bolt the flanges together as outlined above, then, if the piping class requires, PWHT to between 1100°F and 1200°F. After the pipe has been allowed to cool, unbolt the flanges for inspection and if strain still exists repeat the procedure. This procedure should not be used for alloy or stainless steels without advice from Company metallurgy specialists.

If lines cannot be lined up without excessive strain or by heating the pipe, the piping must be cut and rewelded so that flange buildup is possible without strain. Flange misalignment at machinery must always be corrected to avoid strain on the machinery. (Piping design should provide adequate flexibility to prevent excessive forces on equipment under high temperature operating conditions.)

Standard length wrenches should be used. If hammer wrenches are used, ten-pound hammers are the heaviest permissible.

Good practice for tightening bolted flanges is to draw up progressively on bolts in the 12 o'clock, 6 o'clock, 9 o'clock, and 3 o'clock positions, progressing in this fashion around the flange until all bolts are brought up snugly to ensure sufficient compression to seat the gasket. The larger the flange the more important this procedure becomes. When using stud bolts, ensure all threads on each nut are fully engaged.

Flanged joints in high-pressure or high-temperature services often require precision loading of studs, and special tools must be used. Torque wrenches are available with various torque ranges (foot-pounds). In some cases bolt tensioners are used to stretch the bolts or studs to obtain required bolt tension, such as Biach Tensioners. Use of an extensometer, such as the ultrasonic Maintenance Monitor Model 633, is recommended for accurate control.

For more complete guidelines on bolting up large piping flanges and equipment flanges, see the *Heat Exchanger and Cooling Tower Manual* Sections 532 and 1000, which also discuss bolting equipment usage and development of bolting procedures.
