**Single-cut Boring Machine.** This machine is usually employed for installing ½ to 2-inch pipe branches. The machine consists of an ordinary drill or diamond point bit attached to a shaft run through a packing gland. While it is normally hand-operated, power may be supplied by an electric or air drill. Feed may be manual or automatic. A disadvantage of the boring machine is that cuttings fall into the line.

**Pilot Drill and Shell Cutter.** This type of machine may be operated manually or by power. Suppliers include Mueller Co., T. D. Williamson, Inc., and A.P. Smith Manufacturing Co. The Mueller machine can handle two-inch to 12-inch taps in any size pipe and is the most commonly used. Because it permits removal of the coupon, it has the advantage of leaving a minimum of cuttings in the line. Some locations require verification of coupon removal by, for instance, hanging the coupon on the valve or attaching it to the line.

**Taps Larger Than 12 Inches.** Special equipment for making taps larger than 12 inches can be furnished by most of the manufacturers.
