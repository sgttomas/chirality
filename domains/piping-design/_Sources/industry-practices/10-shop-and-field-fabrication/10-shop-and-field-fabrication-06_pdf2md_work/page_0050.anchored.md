Some coatings (e.g., coal tar enamel, Pritec, Extrucoat, and most tape wraps) become soft and are more easily damaged when warmed by the sun. During storage thermal expansion can cause significant damage to the pipe coating at the temporary supports. This will probably be an area of difficulty with the contractor. Contractors consider it less costly to repair the pipe coating than to avoid damage. The Company prefers to minimize the damage and subsequent repairs in order to have the best possible quality coating.

## 684 Long Term Storage of Pipe

Pipe that will be stored for more than six months should receive additional attention, as follows.

### Uncoated Pipe

To avoid pitting corrosion, uncoated pipe should be racked together so that no pipe surface directly touches any adjacent steel surface (pipe, support, stanchion, etc.). Rubber, plastic sheet, plastic pipe, and rope are often used. This separation material should be checked to avoid corrosion underneath the separation material. For long-term storage absorbent materials such as wood and fiberboard should be avoided. Grease and plastic pipe caps should be considered.

**Avoiding Pitting Corrosion.** Except in very dry climates, water absorption should be a primary consideration for selection of the separation material. Corrosion pitting of the pipe under supports, at stanchions or chocks, and under rope used to separate the pipes can be a significant problem. The corrosion pits occur at locations where the steel surface is wet for extended periods. The pits can become so deep that even abrasive blasting cannot clean the pipe sufficiently for corrosion coating application.

Supports covered with plastic are generally good, except where the plastic is punctured (consider double sheets) or where the water collects in folds in the plastic sheet. Avoid using wooden supports and chocks. If used, hard woods, which absorb less water, are preferred over softer woods.

Pitting under ropes can be minimized by using plastic rope or PVC tubing, which retains little or no water.

**Storage Location.** For long-term storage, uncoated pipe should be stored away from coastal areas subject to salt spray. In salt spray areas, heavy corrosion scales can form that are difficult to remove, particularly for over-the-ditch cleaning machines. Soil underneath the stacks can be treated to prevent plants from growing.

### Coated Pipe

In addition to the precautions for short-term storage of coated pipe, the recommendations of the pipe coating manufacturer concerning ultraviolet damage of the coating should be followed. The most susceptible coatings are polyethylene, polyethylene (Pritec, Mapec, & Extrucoat) and tape wraps with polyethylene backing. Tarpaulin coverings may be required. Only the top layers of the stack are exposed to damage by ultraviolet light.
