- Large off-plot blowdown, flare and relief lines, and any other vapor service lines that are not designed to support a full hydrotest load

- Lines upstream of catalyst-packed vessels

- Large diameter water lines in which water flush velocity is insufficient to properly clean the system

- Halon system lines

The most effective means of air blowing is the surge method, in which pressurized air is stored in vessels and piping upstream of the controlling point of the blow. This air is then released as quickly as possible into the section of piping to be blown. This method requires quick-opening valves and takes full advantage of the large pressure differential between the pressurized and atmospheric sections of a piping system.

In addition to being very loud, high velocity air releases may contain hazardous flying debris. Piping should be secured so that the jet thrust generated during blowing is not hazardous to personnel or equipment. Prior to air blowing, control valves, globe and check valves, flow meters, orifice plates, transmitters and other instruments or equipment that may either be damaged by air blowing or interfere with the performance of the air blow should be removed, isolated or bypassed. Pipe and vessels that have not been pressure-tested should not be used to store pressurized air.

Strainers should be provided at pump and machinery inlets and ahead of other equipment (vessels, etc.) from which removal of debris accumulated during cleanup is not practical. Permanent process strainers with properly sized mesh for line cleanup can be utilized, and temporary strainers should be installed at other locations. The maximum particle size that a particular machinery item can safely handle without damage should be considered when specifying screen mesh size. Strainers are described in Section 290.

After cleanup, ferrous piping systems that will not promptly be put in service should be properly protected against corrosion. Methods should be suited to the particular pipe material (including valve packing and elastomers), operating service, and length of time before going into service. These methods include:

- Water fill (oxygen scavenger and/or biocide is rarely used)
- Dry air or nitrogen fill
- Oil fill

Corrosion specialists in the Materials and Equipment Engineering Unit of CRTC can advise on corrosion protection of piping and connected equipment awaiting entry into service.
