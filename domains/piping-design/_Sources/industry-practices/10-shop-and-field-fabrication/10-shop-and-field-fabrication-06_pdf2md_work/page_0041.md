## Full Port Gate Valves

Full port straight-through gate valves are normally used for hot taps. The Company normally purchases reduced port valves so care should be taken either to obtain the full port straight-through style or confirm that the cutting machine will not damage the valve seats. Occasionally, the lugs on the valve seat rings will jam the cutter and may require grinding off. Failure to make these checks could result in damage to the valve or machine, which would make removal under pressure impossible.

## Hydrotesting

To avoid pressure testing the entire system, the branch connection should be hydrotested prior to cutting into the main line. Although desirable, hydrotesting the hot tap connection is not commonly done unless required by the operating code.

**Hydrotest Pressure.** Hot tap connections should be hydrotested at the pressure required in the appropriate code, typically 1.25 or 1.5 times the design pressure of the line or maximum rated operating pressure. To prevent temperature-induced pressure increase, the test pressure should be released as soon as possible after completion of the test and the connection vented.

**Test Media.** Water may be used to test any line operating under 200°F. At temperatures greater than 200°F, oil, glycerine, or commercially available products (e.g., Dowtherm), may be used, but the problem of disposal should be considered. In some instances, the pipe contents can be bled off the line and used to pressure test the fitting. If air or inert gas is used, the pressure should be limited to 1.1 times the design pressure.

**Collapse.** When testing full encirclement fittings such as stopple fittings (covered by Section 676) confirm that the test pressure will not collapse the main run line. The collapse pressure of the main run may be calculated using the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section VIII, Division 1, UG-28. Note that the ASME/ANSI piping and pipeline codes do not contain design details for externally pressurized pipes.

**Brittle Fracture.** When the hydrotest fluid temperature is below 70°F, brittle fracture during hydrotesting becomes a possibility. Equipment should not be pressure tested when the hydrotest fluid temperature is below the minimum pressurizing temperature specified by ASME Section VIII, Figure UCS 66; see also Section 400 of the *Pressure Vessel Manual*.

## Hot Tap Machines

**Packing and Gaskets.** Refer to Standard Drawing GC-L99771 to confirm that the packing and gasketing are adequate for the proposed hot tap.

**Special Precaution.** The connection (nipple and flange) to be installed must be the correct length for the hot tapping equipment (a table of lengths should be included in the hot tap tool kit). The cutter stem should be marked to indicate points at which the cut will be completed and at which the cutter is retracted past the valve seats.
