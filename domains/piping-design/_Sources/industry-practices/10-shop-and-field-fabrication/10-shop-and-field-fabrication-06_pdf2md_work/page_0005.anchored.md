## 613 Equipment and Manifold Modules

Modularizing complete equipment assemblies or complex piping manifolds may be economical, particularly when labor costs at the field site are very high or where extreme weather conditions make field erection difficult. Examples are offshore and arctic projects. Also, packaged units such as refrigeration equipment, compressors, fired heaters, and metering and pressure control manifolds are available from vendors as standardized packages. The modules must be within transportable limits of size and weight, structurally designed for lifting, and able to support loads with minimal deflection and stressing of the components. Often temporary provisions are needed to avoid damage in shipment such as supports to limit vibration of instruments and small piping, guards to protect delicate items, and rust-preventive coatings or fluids to prevent corrosion of machinery parts.

Design and specifications for module piping and equipment may be done by the Company, or by the fabricator/erector to Company performance specifications, with either minimal or detailed piping specifications supplied by the Company. Procurement of the module is handled by Company Purchasing. Company-engineered equipment is often procured by the Company and shipped to the module fabricator/erector.

As with fabricated spools, union jobsite labor may refuse to erect modules from a nonunion shop.

### 621 Welding Procedure and Welder Qualifications

#### Specifications

Welding procedures and welders are required by ASME/ANSI B31.3 to be qualified per ASME Section IX. Additional Company requirements for welding of pipe are contained in Specifications PIM-MS-2505, 4770, 4772.
