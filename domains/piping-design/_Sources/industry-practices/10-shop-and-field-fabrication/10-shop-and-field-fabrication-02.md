## 1.0 GENERAL

### 1.1 Scope

This specification is to establish the quality of work procedures to be followed in the fabrication, installation and testing of all process and utility piping systems. Requirements herein shall govern field and shop fabrication.

### 1.2 Intent

The intent of this specification is to provide for a complete package in accordance with standard practice. This is a general specification, and therefore subject to variations in materials, design and construction, provided that the requirements of standard practice are met. Prior approval shall be obtained from the OWNER before making any change to these specifications.

### 1.3 Conflicting Requirements

Should a conflict arise between this specification and other specifications or government regulations, the Vendor shall contact the OWNER and a decision reached in writing before any work is done.

### 1.4 Vendor Responsibility

The Vendor shall be responsible for conformance to governing codes, OWNER's specifications, drawings, legal and contractual requirements. In the event of non-compliance with the foregoing, the OWNER may correct any deficiencies, and the cost of such repairs shall be charged to the Vendor.

## 2.0 CODE REQUIREMENTS

### 2.1 Codes and Standards

The following codes and standards shall apply:

- Provincial regulations governing the design, fabrication and testing of boilers, pressure vessels, and related facilities. In cases of conflict, the most rigid shall apply.
- American Standard Code for Pressure Piping, ANSI B31.1 or B31.3
- American standard Code for Steel Pipe Flanges and Flanged Fittings, ANSI B16.5
- ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section VIII and IX
- CSA Standard for Oil and Gas Pipeline Systems, Z-662.
- All completed piping, both bent and welded, shall conform to the fabrication and dimensional tolerances specified in the Standard of Pipe Fabrication Institute and the American Standards Association.

**3.0 FABRICATION**

3.1 **General**

3.1.1 The following piping shall be shop fabricated unless otherwise approved by OWNER.

- Welded alloy piping
- Welded non-ferrous piping
- Welded carbon steel piping requiring heat treatment
- Above ground piping NPS 6 and larger

3.1.2 All other piping including closure welds of shop welded piping may be field fabricated unless otherwise directed by OWNER.

3.1.3 Vendor is responsible to insure that dimensions agree with the piping drawings.

3.1.4 Vendor must submit to OWNER the mill test reports of certifications required by applicable codes or other requirements.

3.2 **Shop Fabrication for Spools**

3.2.1 Vendor shall keep a running record of the status of his work. At the end of each workweek fabricator shall report all changes to status record since the previous report. The record shall show:

- Plant, area, drawing and spool number
- Date drawings received, their revision numbers, and their priority, if assigned
- Dates shop details started and released
- Dates material committed and available
- List of material committed
- Dates fabrication started and finished
- Dates spools released and shipped
- Dates last spool for each area shipped

3.2.2 Vendor shall make spool sheets, if required, for all piping.

3.2.3 The spool sheets must show the spool number, materials, heat (MTR) numbers, dimensions, fabrication details, applicable codes and procedures.

3.2.4 A number consisting of the line number, assembly letter and piece number shall identify each pipe spool. Assembly letters and piece numbers shall be assigned alphabetically and numerically in direction of flow.

3.2.5 Spool sheets must identify all equipment and lines connecting to that spool.

3.2.6 The isometric drawing must be shown as reference.

3.2.7 Vendor shall provide reproducible copies of spool sheets.

3.2.8 Spool sheets shall show the limits of field run piping and locations of field welds required between spools.

3.2.9 Shop fabricated piping shall include all couplings, fittings, etc. required for connection of field fabricated branch connections.

3.2.10 Other size stubs or branches shall extend to the nearest flange or will be extended in the required direction as far as possible (limited only to shipping requirements).

## 3.3 Fabrication Details

3.3.1 All welding must be carried out in accordance with a suitable registered welding procedure by a competent certified welder who has demonstrated ability to weld to the procedure. This ability may be demonstrated from past performance using the procedure or one fully radiographed test weld.

3.3.2 Where not otherwise noted, flange boltholes shall straddle principal centrelines of equipment and piping.

3.3.3 Pipe and fittings shall be accurately aligned at weld joints. Concentricity of adjacent parts and alignment of internal surfaces shall be within 1.6 mm. Where parts with internal diameters differing by more than 1.6 mm are joined concentrically, the inside of the smaller bore shall be tapered to match the larger bore within 1.6 mm.

3.3.4 All threaded connections shall be gauge-checked or chased after welding or heat treating. Openings for thermowells and other inserts shall be drilled through the connection and be free from obstruction.

3.3.5 Templates shall be used in laying out headers, laterals, and other irregular details to insure accurate cutting and proper fit-up.

3.3.6 Machine cut bevels to form the welding groove are preferred, but smooth, clean, slag-free flame cut bevels are acceptable.

3.3.7 In fitting up details preparatory to final welding, spacers shall be used while tack welding the pipe and connections in position so that a proper gap is made for full penetration of welds.

3.3.8 Flange faces shall be in a plane perpendicular to the axis of the pipe to which they are welded.

3.3.9 Tack welding should be performed to the same procedure as the final weld. Only small tack welds which penetrate to the bottom of the welding groove may be used and may become a part of the finished weld. Tack welds lacking penetration are not acceptable and must be removed. Each weld shall be cleaned of all scale, slag, flux and other foreign matter before additional welding beads are applied.

3.3.10 In general, permanently welded-in back-up rings shall not be used. If back-up rings should be necessary or desirable, they may be used only upon specific approval by OWNER.

3.3.11 Galvanized piping shall not be bent or welded. Galvanized piping required to be bent or welded shall be galvanized after fabrication.

3.3.12 Completed bends shall have smooth surfaces and shall be free of flat spots and corrugations.

3.3.13 Spools not too small for painted numbers shall also be identified by numbers painted or stenciled in characters at least 2 inches high. Painted numbers shall be located on spools, and repeated as necessary, to permit easy identification of any spool with turning or lifting.

3.3.14 Openings in pipe spools shall be securely closed and flange faces and threaded connections protected with suitable plugs, flange covers and thread protectors. Small diameter branches shall be adequately braced or otherwise protected to prevent damage in handling, shipping and storage.

3.3.15 Teflon tape shall be used as thread sealant on all tapered threads unless operating temperatures prohibit use. OWNER's approval is required for thread sealant for such conditions. Joints to be seal welded shall not be coated with thread sealant.

4.0 FIELD INSTALLATION

4.1 General

4.1.1 All pipe trenches or ditches shall be excavated and backfilled in accordance with OWNER's Specification GS 4.1 Earth Work and OHSA Regulations.

4.1.2 The piping is to be run true to vertical, horizontal and the axis of the building in such a way that it is pleasant to the eye unless the type of installation demands a slope on the pipe in which case the slope is to remain even along the length of the pipe.

4.1.3 The assembly of piping shall be performed so as to impose the least possible mechanical stress.

4.1.4 All piping shall be installed in a neat workmanlike manner, properly insulated, guided and supported.

4.1.5 Suitable points for venting and draining of liner shall be provided.

## 4.2 Installation Details

4.2.1 No threaded connections shall be seal welded unless required by Piping Material Specifications. Seal welds shall cover exposed pipe threads completely.

4.2.2 Where seal welding is required, no pipe thread lubricant or sealant shall be used.

4.2.3 For stainless steel pipe threads thin Teflon tape shall be used on male threads as a lubricant. For other pipe and flanged-bolt threads a lubricant equal to Crane Anti-Seize thread compound shall be used on male threads. Threaded piping in corrosive or sour service is permitted as per OWNER's Specification GS 2.1 Piping Design shall not be back welded, socket welded joints are required.

## 4.3 Bending

4.3.1 Cold bends to a radius of five times the nominal pipe diameter or greater may be made without subsequent stress relieving.

4.3.2 Cold bending to a radius of less than five times the nominal pipe diameter shall not be permitted in the field.

4.3.3 Hot bending shall not be permitted in the field.

## 4.4 Underground Piping

Underground piping shall be carefully laid to grade and depth as specified on the drawings. Where possible underground piping is to be laid so that it will drain to one end of the pipe with traps.

## 4.5 Supports

4.5.1 Piping and equipment shall be adequately supported, rigid enough to prevent excessive vibration, and anchored sufficiently to prevent undue strains on the equipment served to the complete satisfaction of the OWNER.

4.5.2 Hangers and supports shall be made of non-combustible material and capable of sustaining the load to which subjected. Wood or wire shall not be used for permanent installations.

4.5.3 Where piping crosses supports and is tied down, rubbing pads fabricated as shown on the Drawings, or as approved by OWNER, shall be placed on the support to provide a rubbing surface for the pipe.

## 4.6 Coated Pipe

Coated pipe shall be handled with wide rubber or canvas belts without forward or backward scuffing motion. Rope of any kind, hooks, chains or cables shall not be used.

## 4.7 Final Installation

4.7.1 Piping spools shall be dry, thoroughly cleaned and free from any foreign material before installation.

4.7.2 Any obstructions or foreign matter remaining in the pipe after installation thereof shall be removed at the expense of the Vendor.

4.7.3 Before shipping the inside of all compressor suction piping shall be pickled to SSPC-SP-8 to remove rust and scale and a suction screen installed.

4.7.4 Lube-oil piping shall be pickled, neutralized and given a protective coating.

4.7.5 Cleaning, purging, etc. shall be under supervision of OWNER. Vendor shall provide men as required to disconnect, dismantle, and clean all equipment, pipe, valves, fittings, etc. On gas or airlines where it is impossible to ensure complete elimination of moisture, methanol is to be fed into pipe to absorb the moisture and then re-purged with air or nitrogen.

## 5.0 INSPECTION

### 5.1 General

Vendor's facilities, work and records shall be accessible at all reasonable times to OWNER's authorized inspectors. Such inspectors may reject any materials, procedures or work which, in his opinion alone, fail to meet the requirements of applicable codes, specifications, procedures, drawings or purchase orders.

### 5.2 Specific Requirements

5.2.1 Alloy and non-ferrous piping, carbon steel piping requiring heat treatment or mill certification, and carbon steel piping so designated on piping drawings are subject to full inspection. Other carbon steel piping is subject to spot inspection.

5.2.2 Vendor's quality control shall include 10 percent radiographic examination of the welds by each welder. OWNER may require examination of a greater proportion of welding if, in his opinion, this is justified to insure satisfactory workmanship. The cost of radiographic examination for quality control shall be borne by the Vendor.

5.2.3 Procedures for radiographing girth welds shall be such that conclusive images of the entire welds are available. Welds of a size or type preventing conclusive radiographic images such as branch connections, shall have magnetic particle or dye penetrant examination.

5.2.4 Where examination of any type shows a weld fails to meet code requirements, examination shall be made of two more welds made by the same welder. If either of the additional welds fails to satisfy the coe, OWNER may reject work by the Welder. Repairs to defective or rejected work and costs of above additional examinations shall be at Vendor's expense.

## 6.0 TESTING

### 6.1 General

**6.1.1** Vendor shall test all piping to the complete satisfaction of OWNER before placing in service. A testing procedure and method of draining all lines shall be submitted in writing to the OWNER for approval. Each system shall be tested to the pressure and for the time period specified by OWNER and in strict accordance with the following procedures. The piping shall be carefully examined for leaks and/or distortion. Vendor shall be responsible for rectifying all defects in workmanship and materials disclosed by the test. OWNER shall compensate Vendor for all extra labour in rectifying defects due to faulty material supplied by OWNER.

**6.1.2** Vendor shall supply all materials, equipment and labour required to successfully test all piping systems.

**6.1.3** Under no circumstances shall any test be carried out unless approved and witnessed by the OWNER.

**6.1.4** If testing is conducted with water as the test medium, when the temperature may fall below the freezing point, some form of antifreeze must be used to prevent ice forming during the testing.

**6.1.5** In no case will natural gas be permitted as a test medium, unless approved by OWNER.

**6.1.6** All liquid used for hydrostatic testing shall be removed from piping and lines dried out in accordance with service requirements and to the satisfaction of OWNER.

**6.1.7** Testing records shall be kept by Vendor and turned over to the OWNER upon completion.

### 6.2 Code Requirements

Process and utility pressure piping shall be tested as required by the applicable code and described herein.

### 6.3 Pressure Piping Defined

Pressure piping includes all piping designed to convey or contain process or utility fluids, at either a positive or a negative internal pressure. Open-ended vent, drain and like piping operating at essentially atmospheric pressure is not included.

### 6.4 Boiler Code Piping

Piping governed by Section I, Power Boilers, of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code shall be tested hydrostatically as required by Paragraphs P-329 and P-300 of that code, at 1 1/2 times maximum allowable boiler working pressure.

### 6.5 Process Piping

Pressure piping governed by the Process Piping Code, ANSI B31.3, shall be tested as required by Section 337 of that code.

---

6.6 **CSA Z-662 Code Piping**

Pressure piping governed by CSA Z-662 Oil & Gas Pipeline Systems codes shall be tested as provided in Section 8.

6.7 **Test Procedures**

6.7.1 **Hydrostatic Test**

Except where specified otherwise, Process Piping shall be tested hydrostatically at 1 1/2 times the piping design pressure adjusted for design temperature, as specified in Chapter IV of ANSI B31.3 or Section 8 of CSA Z662. Unless otherwise noted, test pressures in line designation tables are the required hydrostatic test pressures.

6.7.2 **Pneumatic Test (Refinery Code)**

a) Where approved by OWNER, piping shall be tested pneumatically at 110% of piping design pressure (not adjusted for temperature) as specified in Section 337 of ANSI B31.3.

b) **Caution:** Pressures intended for hydrostatic test shall not be used for pneumatic tests. Where necessary to change from hydrostatic to pneumatic test, test pressure shall be recalculated.

6.7.3 **Air Test**

Ductwork and piping for which hydraulic testing is impractical, will be air tested. Air tests will be specifically noted in line designation tables.

6.7.4 **Visual Examination**

No pressure test is required of open-ended vent, drain, and line piping. Such piping shall be checked thoroughly by visual examination after installation.

6.7.5 **Flanged, Screwed and Socket Weld Closing Connections**

Flanged connections at points where blinds are used during pressure tests do not require separate tests after test blinds are removed. Screwed and socket weld connections used for pressure tests are excepted from separate tests. Such connections shall be inspected thoroughly after make-up to assure tightness.

6.7.6 **Closure Welds**

Where practical, pressure tests of piping shall include all closure welds between different parts of the system. Where it is not practical to include a closure weld in the pressure test, the weld shall be examined completely with radiographic procedures as per B31.3 345.2.3(c).
