## SECTION 1: PURPOSE

This standard establishes the minimum requirements for the fabrication, inspection and testing of piping systems for BP Cherry Point Refinery referred to as BP CHP in this standard.

## SECTION 2: SCOPE

2.1 This standard applies to shop and field fabrication of all process and utility piping including piping supplied with packaged equipment or modules, and shall be used along with the applicable job specific piping specifications and drawings.

2.2 When terms such as "authorized", "approved" or "accepted" are used in this standard, it means that the intended action will be by BP CHP Reliability Engineering, unless otherwise specified.

2.3 Where jurisdictional authorities have requirements exceeding or in conflict with this standard, such requirements shall supersede this standard and BP CHP Reliability Engineering should be notified of the conflict.

## SECTION 3: APPLICABLE CODES, STANDARDS, AND REFERENCES

3.2 The limits of piping governed by codes other than ASME B31.3 shall be indicated on the piping isometrics. Piping covered by other codes shall be fabricated in accordance with those codes and this standard.

## SECTION 4: PIPING COMPONENTS AND MATERIALS / METALLURGY

4.1 Piping components, materials and metallurgy shall be in accordance with the contract documents and the specified piping classes in STP 205. Any conflicts between the contract documents and STP 205 shall be reported to the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

4.2 Materials which the fabricator is responsible to furnish will be defined in the contract documents. Material descriptions provided in the contract documents shall be used along with STP 208 to purchase any piping components specified for purchase by the fabricator.

## SECTION 5: CONFLICTS AND DEVIATIONS

5.1 Any conflicts between the requirements of this standard, related specifications, codes, standards, purchase orders, drawings, and data sheets shall be referred to the BP CHP Reliability Engineer for clarification before proceeding with the fabrication of the affected parts.

5.2 In the event that any of the designs, specifications, or other criteria furnished by BP CHP to the fabricator under this order shall prevent fabricator from conforming to his obligations to meet the codes and standards as defined above, fabricator is required to notify BP CHP prior to proceeding.

5.3 Any deviation from this standard shall be submitted in writing to BP CHP for authorization. Deviation is not permitted unless prior approval in writing from BP CHP Reliability Engineering is obtained.

## SECTION 6: SUBSTITUTIONS AND CHANGES

6.1 Piping components and materials, including thicker wall materials, shall not be substituted without prior written authorization. When "minimum wall" or "guaranteed minimum wall" piping is specified in the contract documents, the fabricator shall advise BP CHP Reliability Engineering of the nominal wall thickness prior to procurement and fabrication.

6.2 Alternative configurations to the piping arrangements on the drawings shall not be furnished without prior written authorization.

## SECTION 7: CONTROL OF OWNER-FURNISHED MATERIALS (OFM)

7.1 The fabricator shall submit his procedure for receipt, control, and issuance of OFM to BP CHP within two weeks after purchase order award for review and approval by the BP CHP Project Manager.

---

7.2. The procedure shall include a plan on segregation and tracking of OFM to ensure correct metallurgy and traceability of the completed spools.

## SECTION 8: DEFINITIONS

8.1 The terms "shop fabricated" and "field fabricated" piping shall be defined as follows:

   a) Shop Fabricated - fabrication performed at a "vendor" shop remote from the job site.

   b) Field Fabricated - fabrication performed at the job site either in the field shop or at the installation site area. Where necessary, piping to be fabricated in the field shop may be identified in the drawings.

## SECTION 9: DRAWINGS

9.1 The pipe fabricator shall be responsible for the accuracy of their pipe spool drawings, and shall maintain them current. Each spool shall be detailed on a separate sheet.

9.2 The field fabricator shall maintain drawings current and updated as to any field changes that may be required and shall provide BP CHP with as-built drawings upon completion of the job. Field changes shall be authorized by BP CHP in writing before proceeding.

9.3 Piping isometrics shall show all components for welding and assembly.

9.4 Requirements for field fabricated portions of shop fabricated lines should be indicated on the drawings. The shop fabricator shall not supply or fabricate piping that is so noted.

9.5 The fabricator shall indicate on the shop drawings the flange orientation as defined in section 16.2.

9.6 Drawing Dimensional Tolerances

   9.6.1 Dimensions on drawings are in feet and inches. Only inches are shown when dimensions are less than 1'-0". Feet and inches are shown when dimensions are 1'-0" and larger.

   9.6.2 All dimensions shall be field verified for accuracy prior to fabrication. When field verification is not possible a "field fit up joint" should be used.

9.7 Where cold spring (CS) or pre-spring (PS) is required, the dimension shown on the piping isometric includes the required amount subtracted or added. The piping shall be fabricated exactly to the dimension shown. Use of cold spring or pre-spring in the design requires approval by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

---

9.8 Reducers are concentric unless otherwise noted.

9.9 Dimensions locating lateral pipets are to the intersecting point on the outside of the pipe.

9.10 All vent and drain locations, including hydrotest vents and drains, shall be shown and identified on the isometric drawings.

## SECTION 10: FABRICATION DIMENSIONAL TOLERANCES

10.1 The tolerances for fabrication dimensions in this section apply to piping in-line items, branch connections and continuation of lines to other drawings.

10.2 These tolerances may be exceeded to a maximum of 1/2 inch for vent and drain connection locations, dummy support lengths, temperature and pressure tap locations, providing the deviation does not affect an in-line item.

10.3 Tolerances for general dimensions such as face-to-face, face-to-end, end-to-end, face- to-center, end-to-center, and center-to-center shall be as follows:

- 10.3.1 ±1/8 inch for NPS 10 and smaller;
- 10.3.2 ±3/16 inch for NPS 12 through 36;
- 10.3.3 ±3/16 inch for NPS 38 and larger with additional ±1/32 inch for each 12 inch size increment over 36 inches;
- 10.3.4 When closer tolerances are required, they shall be noted on the piping isometrics. Closer and more stringent tolerances will be required for all rotating equipment.

10.4 The tolerance for inclination of flange face from true in any direction is 3/64 inch per foot.

10.5 Rotation of flange bolt holes shall not exceed 1/16 inch from two-hole.

10.6 Fabrication tolerances not specified here shall comply with PFI Standard ES3.

## SECTION 13: THREADED JOINTS

13.1 General

13.1.1 Where threaded fittings or pipe nipples are welded to pipe spools, the threads shall be checked for fit and roundness following stress-relieving. If threads are not in acceptable condition, chase threads with a tap or die. Threads shall then be cleaned with cutting oil and a suitable solvent.

13.1.2 Threaded pipe joints shall be assembled with the use of thread sealant as specified below, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering. For applications that do not fit the categories below, consult with the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

13.2 Metal Pipe Thread Sealant

13.2.1 General Applications & Potable Water (-65°F to 300°F)

Metal pipe thread sealant for general applications shall be PTFE based paste, Loctite 565 (Storehouse free bin) or an approved alternate. For potable water applications use Loctite 565 in 250 ml or 1 liter containers (not stocked – order as needed), which are NSF-61 certified. Use with Loctite 7649 activator (highly flammable, Storehouse #3363076490) for cold applications below 40°F or for inactive metals (galvanized steel, plated fittings, stainless steel, titanium, Inconel, aluminum). Use Loctite 567 for metals prone to galling and for temperatures from 301°F to 400°F. DO NOT USE Loctite 565 for temperatures above 300°F or for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services.

---

### 13.2.2 Metals Prone To Galling & Potable Water (-65°F to 400°F)

Metal pipe thread sealant for metals prone to galling shall be PTFE based paste, Loctite 567 (Storehouse #3370005670) or an approved alternate. For potable water applications use Loctite 567 in 250 ml or 1 liter containers (not stocked – order as needed), which are NSF-61 certified. Use with Loctite 7649 activator (highly flammable, Storehouse #3363076490) for cold applications below 40°F or for inactive metals (galvanized steel, plated fittings, stainless steel, titanium, Inconel, aluminum). DO NOT USE Loctite 567 for temperatures above 400°F or for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services.

### 13.2.3 High Temperature Applications (401°F to 1175°F)

Metal pipe thread sealant for high temperature applications up to 1175°F for 2" & smaller pipe shall be graphite based paste, Grafoil GTS (Storehouse #28510-46049) or an approved alternate. DO NOT USE Grafoil GTS for temperatures above 1175°F, for pipe larger than 2", or for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services.

### 13.2.4 Extreme Temperature Applications (1176°F to 1800°F)

Metal pipe thread sealant for extreme temperature applications up to 1800°F shall be copper based paste, Jomar Hi-Temp Anti-Seize Lube/Thread Seal (Storehouse #28450260000) or an approved alternate. DO NOT USE Jomar Hi-Temp Anti-Seize Lube/Thread Seal for temperatures above 1800°F, or for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services.

### 13.2.5 Oxygen & Aggressive Chemicals

Metal pipe thread sealant for pure oxygen, oxygen-rich services, chlorine, or other strong oxidizing services, shall be PTFE tape or Hernon Dripstop 950 paste, if service temperature allows. See the following paragraphs for the sealant material specifications. For service temperatures above 500°F contact Reliability Engineering.

a) PTFE tape (-212°F to 500°F) shall be industrial grade, ½" width, 3.5 mil thickness, Dixon TT-150 (Storehouse free bin) or an approved alternate. Wrap the external threads with 2 or 3 layers of tape. DO NOT USE PTFE tape for general applications, unless approved by Reliability Engineering.

b) Hernon Dripstop 950 (-65°F to 450°F, Storehouse #28450950500) is a non-hardening PCTFE/PTFE based paste sealant for harsh environments. DO NOT USE Dripstop 950 for general applications since it is very expensive.

---

13.3 Plastic Pipe Thread Sealant

13.3.1 Thread Sealing Requirements

Thread sealant shall be used for all threaded thermoplastic pipe joints and shall be chemically compatible with the fluid service and the pipe material. Acceptable sealants are non-hardening paste certified for thermoplastic piping, or PTFE tape. Sealant material specifications are in the following paragraphs. Paste sealant is preferred over PTFE tape, since overtightening is more likely to occur with use of tape. Non-lubricating or low-lubricity (non-PTFE) pastes should be used to prevent over tightening the threaded joints. Thermoplastic pipe threads need sealant but do not need lubrication. Tighten joints finger tight (not hand tight) plus 1½ turns to 2 turns maximum with strap wrench or other non-damaging tool. Note that the upper temperature limits for the sealants below may exceed the limits of the piping material. Typical maximum temperatures for plastic pipe material are 210°F for CPVC and 140°F for PVC.

13.3.2 Paste Sealant (-50°F to 400°F)

Paste sealant for plastic pipe threads shall be non-hardening non-PTFE paste. Spears Blue 75 (Storehouse #2870075020) or an approved alternate. The maximum useage temperature may be limited by gaseous service. For gaseous fluids verify the maximum allowable temperature with Spears.

13.3.3 Tape Sealant (-212°F to 500°F)

Tape sealant for plastic pipe threads shall be industrial grade PTFE tape, ½" width, 3.5 mil thickness, Dixon TTA50 (Storehouse free bin) or an approved alternate. Wrap the external threads with 2 or 3 layers of tape.

13.3.4 Paste Sealant For Harsh Environments (-65°F to 450°F)

Paste sealant for plastic pipe threads in harsh environments shall be non-hardening PCTFE/PTFE based paste, Hernon Dripstop 950 (Storehouse #2845095050) or an approved alternate. DO NOT USE Dripstop 950 for general applications since it is very expensive.

## SECTION 14: FIELD WELDS

14.1 Drawings shall indicate all field welds (FW) required for erection and shipping, and will also indicate shop fabrication and field fabrication requirements. See paragraph 16.4 for positioning of circumferential and longitudinal weld joints.

14.2 All field welds shall be located so as to minimize field welding.

---

14.3 Unless otherwise shown or approved, field welds shall be buttweld or socketweld type. Stub-in or miter weld joints are not allowed.

14.4 Unless otherwise specified, end connections of shop fabricated spools shall be made as follows.

- 14.4.1 Where field welding is required to join the ends of a fabricated pipe spool to pipe or piping components, the pipe spool shall be furnished with the ends beveled for field welding and fabricated to the drawing dimensions.

- 14.4.2 Where field fit-up is required, the end of one of the spool pieces at the fit-up field weld shall be furnished with a plain end 4 inches longer than the dimension indicated on the piping isometric drawings. The adjacent spool piece shall have a beveled end, and shall be fabricated to the dimension indicated.

- 14.4.3 Austenitic stainless steel pipe shall be beveled using mechanical devices unless otherwise approved by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer. Flame cutting may be acceptable, subject to prior approval specified above.

- 14.4.4 Seal welding of threaded fittings shall be minimized and generally limited to pipe plugs in vents and bleeds used for initial hydrostatic testing. Threaded joints which are specified to be seal welded shall be assembled without teflon tape, liquid teflon, or other thread compounds. No threads shall be left exposed but shall be covered with weld metal. See standard drawing LA-000-0100-0200-017.

14.5 For tie-in welds to existing piping in alkaline services, such as caustic, catacarb, and amine, the existing piping to be welded shall be neutralized prior to welding per STP 42.

## SECTION 15: SHIPPING LENGTHS

Pipe spool dimensions shall not exceed 40 feet by 8 feet by 8 feet to facilitate shipping.

## SECTION 16: FABRICATION

Where taper boring has not been called for on the piping isometric, and the internal surface misalignment of piping components to be joined by welding exceeds 1/16" for components 24" and smaller, or 1/8" for components 26" and larger, rotate the pipe and/or fittings to reduce misalignment to acceptable tolerances if possible. If that is not successful, one of the following procedures shall be used to ensure the adjoining internal surfaces are approximately flush.

16.1.2 Backweld the root pass.

16.3.4 One hundred (100) percent visual inspection of the weld shall be performed on both OD and ID of the weld. To facilitate visual inspection of the inside weld, inspection shall be done before welding any piping component to the branch fitting. When direct visual inspection, as defined in ASME Section V, Article 9, Paragraph T-952 cannot be performed, a remote visual inspection in accordance with Paragraph T-953 of ASME Section V shall be performed.

## 16.4 Circumferential and Longitudinal Welds

### 16.4.1

Adjacent sections of welded pipe that are joined by buttwelding shall have the longitudinal weld seams positioned so that they are at least 30° apart. The longitudinal weld joints are not to contact or be positioned directly on the pipe supports.

### 16.4.2

The fabricator shall position the longitudinal weld seam away from the top and bottom of pipe, and away from any branch connection fitting locations such as Pipets and Olets so they will not be positioned on a weld seam.

### 16.4.3

Circumferential buttweld joints shall not be positioned directly on pipe supports unless the crown of the weld has been ground flush with the pipe O.D.

## 16.5 Slip-on Flanges

### 16.5.1

For all services, slip-on flanges shall be double-welded (front and back) as shown in ASME B31.3, Figure 328.5.2B - Typical Detail (1). Flanges shall be positioned so that the distance from the flange face to the pipe end provides 1/8 inch clearance from the front fillet weld to the flange face. The weld shall be applied in such a manner that refacing of the flanges is not necessary.

**Note:** Double-welding for all services exceeds the requirements of B31.3, but is required by BP CHP to reduce corrosion potential and provide a stronger flange-to-pipe joint.

## 16.6 Socket Weld Joints

In accordance with ASME B31.3 or B31.1 as applicable, socket weld joints shall incorporate a gap between the bottom of the socket and the end of the pipe to accommodate thermal expansion. Use a GAP-A-LET (or approved equivalent) socket weld contraction ring to ensure proper gap. The pipe end shall be inserted into the socket a minimum of 1/4". A minimum of two weld passes are required, and the minimum fillet weld size shall conform to the applicable ASME piping code. See standard drawing LA-000-0100-0200-018.

## 16.7 Meter Runs and Orifice Flanges

### 16.7.1

Pipe ends and bevels at the flanges shall be machine cut, and be perpendicular to the axis of the pipe. Welds at the flange shall be ground smooth and flush inside.

---

16.7.2  The sections of pipe to which the orifice flanges are attached may be standard mill run pipe, but shall be choice, selected pieces, and shall be round.

16.8 Stub-ins.

Stub-ins shall not be used without specific written approval from the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

16.9 Reinforcing Pads

16.9.1  Reinforcing pads for branch connections and supports shall be installed only where called for on the piping isometrics, and added as a subsequent fabrication step after weld inspection and examination of the pressure weld, but prior to any post weld heat treatment. Basic material shall be the same as the pipe material unless otherwise authorized by BP CHP. Isometrics will specify size and pad thickness. Each pad, or each piece if the pad is made from more than one piece, shall have a 1/4" telltale (weep) hole drilled and tapped (NPT) prior to installation of the pad. The telltale hole shall be located at the lowest practical point on the pad for drainage. Telltale holes shall be packed with heavy grease after pressure testing.

16.9.2  Reinforcing pads required for field installation shall be fabricated by the shop and shipped loose.

16.10 Non-pressure Attachments

16.10.1  Non-pressure attachments shall be installed in accordance with detail drawings as referenced on the piping isometrics. The material for non-pressure attachments welded directly to pressure piping shall be of the same material or have the same nominal chemical composition as the pressure piping. Exceptions shall be approved in writing by BP CHP Reliability Engineering, and be indicated on the drawings.

16.10.2  Pipe insulation shoes should be welded in the field, except on lines that are required to be postweld heat treated. Pipe insulation shoes which are field welded to pipe shall be centered over pipe supports in the cold position. Shoes should preferably be located and attached after the line is completely installed, but may be welded to pipe prior to installation, provided they are centered over pipe supports when the pipe is installed.

16.10.3  Non-pressure attachments shall not be installed by the shop fabricator unless they are dimensionally located on the piping isometrics. Postweld heat treated lines shall have non-pressure attachments installed by the shop prior to postweld heat treatment.

## 16.11 Bolted Flange Joints & Torquing

**16.11.1** Orifice flanges shall be purchased and installed without jacking bolts/screws. When orifice flanges are received from the supplier with jacking bolts/screws, the bolts/screws shall be removed from the flanges before installation.

**16.11.2** Flanged joint assembly, stud bolt lengths, and bolt tightening shall be in accordance with BP CHP STP 50. The types of flanged joints and gaskets which require bolt torquing are also specified in STP 50, along with bolting procedures and torque values.

**16.11.3** For flanged joints where the bolts are specified to be tightened with a torque tool, install sets of two lubricated hardened washers under each nut at only one end of the studs, on the side of the joint where the torque tool will be applied. The hardened washers shall be as specified in STP 50. Stud bolt lengths shall be increased to accommodate the thickness of the two washers. Washer thicknesses will vary based on stud diameter.

**16.11.4** Bolting shall be assembled with a thread lubricant as specified in STP 50. The lubricant shall be applied to the threads, washers, and metal-contacting faces of the nuts.

**16.11.5** For flanged joint situations where the stud bolt tables in STP 50 are not applicable, the length of stud bolts shall be designed for two threads to protrude through the bolt-up nuts at each end. In accordance with ASTM A193, stud lengths are measured from first thread to first thread, and do not include the unthreaded points on the ends. The specified stud length may be rounded to the next higher commercial length, as long as the maximum thread protrusion criteria is not exceeded.

**16.11.6** The acceptance criteria for installed stud bolts is a minimum protrusion of one full thread through the nuts, and a maximum protrusion of four threads. Stud bolts shall be installed centered in the flange joint with nearly equal lengths protruding at both ends. Longer studs with additional thread protrusion is allowed where bolt tensioning is specified or approved by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

## A4 POSTWELD HEAT TREATMENT

A4.1 Postweld heat treatment shall be achieved by locally heating both welds simultaneously, using the electrical resistance method, or Halogen Quartz lamps.

A4.2 Postweld heat treating, including heating rate, and cooling rate, shall be in accordance with ASME B31.3 and STP 42. Holding time shall be 1/2 hour at temperature.

A4.3 Valve bodies and welds shall be insulated as shown in Figure 211-2, with at least 1" (24.4 mm) thick "Kaowool" or other insulation material approved by BP CHP. Insulation should not only be as thick as is practicable to reduce heat loss, but should also extend along the pipe, on both sides of the valve, a minimum of 6" to provide a gradual temperature gradient in both directions when electric heating coils are used. Special applications will be used when Halogen Quartz lamps are the heat source.

A4.4 Do not insulate upper (bonnet) portion of the valve.

A4.5 Valves shall be closed completely, then backed-off two full handwheel revolutions during the postweld heat treatment cycle. If short, open-ended piping is involved, the pipe ends shall be sealed to prevent heat loss by convection.

A4.6 Thermocouple locations, heating elements and insulation requirements are shown in Figure 211-2.

## APPENDIX C: FIELD INSTALLATION OF VALVES AND OTHER PIPING COMPONENTS

### C1 GENERAL VALVE INSTALLATION REQUIREMENTS

C1.1 Visually inspect the valve for damage such as bent shafts, broken handle or handwheel, and damaged flange faces. Inspect the inside of the valve and remove any foreign material.

C1.2 Inspect the valve stems for any stem protection during painting at the factory or for shipping and remove it. If the stem was inadvertently painted, it shall be cleaned before installing the valve.

C1.3 Some valves such as globe valves, check valves, butterfly valves, Orbit valves, and metal seated ball valves, have directional flow arrows. These valves shall be installed accordingly. Disregarding this will result in valve failure and leakage.

### C2 SOFT SEATED VALVES

C2.1 Where soft seated valves will be installed, piping shall be thoroughly cleaned, flushed, and drained before the valves are installed. Debris in the line (i.e., metal chips, weld rod, weld slug, grit, sand) can damage the soft seats. Soft seated valves that had been welded in the line (vents, drains, in-line) shall be in the wide open position during the flushing operation. The hydrotest for these lines may be conducted at this time, and the flanged soft seated valve installed after the hydrotest.

### C3 BUTTERFLY VALVES

C3.1 Ensure that the interior of mating pipe and flanges are free of any obstructions such as weld rods and weld slugs before installing the valve. When the valve flange facing is lined with a resilient material, the mating pipe flange shall be separated sufficiently so as not to damage the resilient facing. Do not force or wedge the valve into position.

C3.2 Rubber lined butterfly valves shall be installed with the disc in the open position prior to tightening the bolts. After the bolts are tight, check the operation of the valve through a complete open and close cycle to ensure that the disc has not been jammed during the installation.

C3.3 Triple offset butterfly valves shall be installed per the manufacturer's instructions with the valve oriented to have the higher pressure on the shaft side of the disc when in the closed position. Triple offset butterfly valves are designed to be leak tight in both directions at the

## APPENDIX D: WELDING SOCKETWELD BALL VALVES WITHOUT DISASSEMBLY

**D1 SCOPE**

D1.1 This appendix defines the procedure for welding socketweld ball valves in a piping system without disassembling the valve. This procedure will prevent damage to the teflon seats and seals on the valve from the heat of welding.

D1.2 This procedure applies to teflon seated ball valves in sizes 3/4", 1" and 1-1/2" with socketweld ends that are manufactured by Jamesbury, Model No. 4C or 4D.

**D2 WELDING PROCEDURE**

D2.1 Do not disassemble the ball valves.

D2.2 Welding must be done using the GTAW or SMAW welding process, using 3/32" diameter weld rod.

D2.3 The temperature of any part of the valve body between passes must be cooled to 200°F before welding the next pass. A 200°F tempstick must be used to check the temperature and must not melt.

D2.4 The last pass must be cooled, by blowing with air, as soon as welding is complete to 200°F maximum.

D2.5 The temperature of the valve body must be checked immediately after each pass with a 400°F tempstick. If the temperature exceeds 400°F, the teflon seat may have been damaged. The valve shall be disassembled and visually examined. If damaged, it shall be replaced.

**Note:** Care shall be used to assure all valve parts are clean before re-assembling.

D2.6 After the valve has cooled off, check the packing nuts for tightness. Packing nut is to be hand tight, then one half turn with a wrench. All valve handles and tags must be secured on the valve.
