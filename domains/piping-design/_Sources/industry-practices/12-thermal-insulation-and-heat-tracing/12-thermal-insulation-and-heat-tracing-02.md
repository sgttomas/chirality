## 1. SCOPE

1.1. This specification covers the general requirements for materials and application for external thermal insulation to piping, vessels and equipment operating at temperatures from -265° F through 1200° F.

1.2. Insulation shall be required where necessary to:

- Conserve heat.
- Maintain stabilized process temperatures during atmospheric temperature changes.
- Prevent burning of personnel if surface temperature in normal operation is 140° F and over.
- Avoid condensation and sweating.
- Prevent freezing.
- Conserve refrigeration.
- Fireproofing.
- Noise control.

1.3. Definitions:

- The "**Owner**" means Suncor Energy Inc.
- The "**Supplier**" means the manufacturer, fabricator, vendor or contractor that supplies the materials or services.
- The "**Buyer**" means Suncor Energy Inc. or its designated agent.

## 2. GENERAL

2.1. Line Designation Tables shall indicate insulation type, operating temperature and insulation thickness for piping. Piping Isometric Drawings will repeat this information plus identify the insulation material. Equipment Drawings shall indicate insulation thickness and material for each equipment item requiring insulation. These thicknesses will reflect any special process control requirement and shall take precedence over thicknesses shown elsewhere. As traced piping will require oversizing of insulation, the O.D. of the material must be determined accordingly.

2.2. Operating temperatures of this specification refer to the internal fluid or material temperature of the process system.

2.3. Extent of insulation shall be as noted on P&ID's, Mechanical and Utility System Flow Diagrams, Line Designation Tables and/or equipment or module drawings. Thickness of insulation for piping, vessels and equipment shall be determined by reference to operating temperatures and to the applicable Insulation Thickness Charts. Insulation thickness shall not be changed when heat tracing is applied unless otherwise noted on drawings. The greater of insulation thickness on the line designation tables or the thickness charts in this specification shall be used.

2.4. Insulation shall not be applied to piping, vessels and equipment before completion of pressure testing.

2.5. Studs, pins, clips, or other attachments used for securement of insulation shall not be welded to stress-relieved vessels (including tanks) or piping without subsequent stress relief except as specifically permitted in the notes in Table

---

UCS-56 of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code; furthermore no attachments shall be welded to vessels or piping that have been stress relieved for process reasons without subsequent stress relief.

2.6. Expansion and contraction joints shall be provided below vessel insulation support rings and in piping as required. Contraction joints on cold piping shall be at flanged joints if possible.

2.7. Nozzles, valves, fittings, piping specialties items, etc. shall be considered a part of the piping and shall be insulated in accordance with the requirements of the attached piping.

2.8. Insulation Limitations

2.8.1 The following items shall not be insulated unless otherwise noted on the drawings, or herein:

- Piping or Vessel components designed on the basis of reduced metal temperature resulting from heat loss to atmosphere.
- Expansion and rotating joints.
- Steam Traps.
- Unions.
- Overflow lines from tanks or drains.
- Nameplates and code inspection markings on equipment and vessels.

2.8.2 Pipe Flanges

2.8.2.1 Pipe and valve flanges shall be insulated up to and including 650° F operating temperature in both traced and untraced insulated systems. At 651° F and above pipe and valve flanges shall not be insulated.

2.8.2.2 For the purpose of personnel protection, pipe and valve flanges operating at 651° F and above shall be caged with 2.5 lb/sq. yard expanded carbon steel metal lath on the upper 180° portion of the flange.

2.9. Ultrasonic testing ports shall be provided with removable and weather proof plugs.

2.10. All materials shall be new and shall conform to the regulation of the Federal, Provincial and Local Occupational Safety and Health Act. Materials such as insulation blocks, molded pipe covering, and weatherproofing shall be delivered to the jobsite in factory sealed cartons, bags, packages and/or containers.

2.11. All insulation material shall be non corrosive, and shall be suitably inhibited for application to stainless steel surfaces (Ref. 2.12).

2.12. All insulation materials which are used for insulation of austenitic stainless steel piping and equipment shall meet the requirements of ASTM Standard C795, latest edition.

2.13. Austenitic stainless steel piping, vessels and equipment in low and intermediate temperature services shall be painted for protection against chlorides leaching out of insulation.

- For up to 250° F use Epoxy Phenolic,
- For 250° F to 800° F use Chloride-free Heat Resistant Silicone based paint such as Thermalox 70 or approved equal.

---

For more details see Painting Standard STD 0214.

NOTE: Thermalox 70 is not covered by Suncor Standard 0214, but it is an acceptable product

2.14. Insulation materials must be kept dry and shall not be applied while wet. If the materials become wet after application, they shall be allowed to dry before proceeding with application of metal jacket or weatherproofing compounds. Calcium Silicate type insulation that becomes wet after application must be disposed of unless the normal operating temperature of the line exceeds 200° F. Special care shall be taken to keep aluminum sheets dry prior to application in order to prevent water stain between sheets.

2.15. Materials containing asbestos shall not be acceptable.

2.16. Materials specified or referenced by manufacturer and designation are not intended to exclude equal or better items offered by other manufacturers. Products of reputable manufacturers and of comparable type, quality and other physical characteristics shall be submitted for Suncor's approval.

2.17. Care shall be taken to avoid contact between dissimilar materials which might cause galvanic corrosion. (Concerns shall be directed to the Suncor corrosion engineer.)

2.18. The following are general requirements regarding on site insulation of piping and equipment.

2.18.1. On drains and vents, leave the pipe cap or flange and valve bonnets exposed.

2.18.2. With the aid of Operations and Maintenance, identify flanges, exchanger heads, PSVs, etc. which require custom-made blankets or insulation boxes. These flanges, heads, PSVs, etc. are typically in high maintenance areas. Custom-made blankets shall have properties equal to or better than insulation box cover per Dwg 0A-N-0013.

2.18.3. On insulation on sewer drains shall be cut back by a minimum of 3" to allow viewing of the drain pipe.

2.19. For all insulation materials, current MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet) shall be provided at the time insulation material is delivered to site.

## 3. HOT INSULATION, TYPE HC–HEAT CONSERVATION (70°F THROUGH 1200°F)

### 3.1. Hot Insulation Classes

On P&IDs, Mechanical and Utility System Flow Diagrams, LDT's, Iso's, and Equipment Drawings these areas shall be coded HC.

### 3.2. Insulating Materials

#### 3.2.1.
In areas where due to traffic or maintenance work the insulation may get damaged, the insulation shall be calcium silicate per ASTM C533 Type 1.

#### 3.2.2.
In areas where the insulation must also provide fire protection, a dual layer of CALCIUM SILICATE with all joints staggered must be installed. For installation details see Note in 7.2.2.

---

3.2.3. For temperatures from 70° F to 350° F, the insulation may be mineral fiber, complying with ASTM Standard C547, Type II, except as required for 3.2.1, and 3.2.2.

Acceptable manufacturers are:

- Roxul Inc.
- Fibrex Inc.
- IIG (Industrial Insulation Group, LLC)
- Suncor approved equal.

3.2.4. Mineral fiber shall be preformed, scored or in block form.

3.2.5. For temperatures between 351°F and 1200°F, and as required for 3.2.1 and 3.2.2, the insulation shall be CALCIUM SILICATE per ASTM C533, Type 1 with the exception of Cokers which shall be insulated with double layer 8 lb/ft3 density blanket, each layer faced with 20 gauge Type 304SS. Hex wire mesh on one side and 2.5 lb/sq yd., Type 304SS lath on other side.

Acceptable manufacturers are:

- IIG Themo 12 Gold
- Suncor Approved Equal

3.2.6. For Temperatures over 1000°F the first layer of insulation shall be high temperature, needled, E-glass mineral fiber per ASTM Standard C1086 with a density of 11 lbs./ft3. The second layer shall be CALCIUM SILICATE.

Acceptable manufacturers of needled E-Glass are:

- BGF, PPG and JPS*

3.2.7. For lube oil piping operating at 250° F and over, insulation shall be cellular glass. At an operating temperature of 249° F and below, mineral fiber shall be used per ASTM C547, Type II.

Acceptable manufacturers are:

- Pittsburgh Corning
- Roxul Inc. (Mineral Fiber)
- Fibrex Inc. (Mineral Fiber)
- IIG (Mineral Fiber)
- Suncor approved equal

3.2.8. Thickness of insulation for untraced pipe shall be in accordance with 3.3 Chart A, or as noted otherwise on the line designation tables or on the equipment data sheets.

For electric and steam traced insulated lines, the insulation thicknesses shall be in accordance with 3.4 Chart "A" and 3.4 Chart "B".

3.2.9. For operating temperatures less than 1200° F the insulation for elbows may be molded perlite.

Acceptable manufacturers are:

---

\* BGF = Burlington Glass Fibers
PPG = Pittsburgh Plate Glass
JPS = J.P. Stevens

---

- IIG - Sproule WR-1200
- Howred Corp.- Goodtemp
- Approved Suncor equal

3.2.10 Alternative insulation material for austenitic stainless steel

3.2.10.1 Perlite insulating material may be used in lieu of other insulation materials per ASTM Standard C610, latest edition.

## 6. INSULATION FOR TRACED LINES, TYPE HC (HOT COVER)

### 6.1. General

6.1.1. Steam traced lines shall be insulated like HOT INSULATION per section 3.0. The insulation thickness shall be the higher of Table 3.3 in this standard or in Suncor Energy Standard 0209. When process operating conditions require even greater thickness it shall be specified on the Piping Isometric drawings.

6.1.2. Steam traced lines shall use oversized insulation on pipe of all sizes. The void area created by the oversize insulation shall be packed every 20 feet with "tempmat" insulation to eliminate air flow in the gap.

6.1.3. Where tracer tubing jumps over valves, flanges, etc., the tracer shall be spirally wrapped with high temperature fiberglass tape 2" wide by 1/8" thick, lapped a minimum of ½". Tape shall be secured at both ends with 16 gauge Type 304 stainless steel wire. Tape shall be weather-proofed with a brush coat of mastic.

## 7. APPLICATION - PIPING INSULATION

### 7.1. General

7.1.1. Piping shall be insulated as coded on P&ID's, Mechanical and Utility Flow Diagrams, Pipe Line Designation Tables, and Piping Isometric Drawings.

7.1.2. Unless otherwise noted, insulation shall be stopped at flange or union connections to permit "breaking" the line without damage to the insulation. Each break shall be "beveled" and weatherproofed.

Some corrosive services require insulated flanges, these will be identified in the LDT's. (Refer to paragraph 2.8.2).

7.1.3. Insulation shall be stopped short of each flange at sufficient distance to permit removal of bolts without damage to the insulation.

7.1.4. Horizontal piping elevated on shoes will have the insulation continued over the shoes to minimize heat loss.

7.1.5. All holes, cracks, and seams and interruptions in the vapor barrier coating shall be repaired and sealed with vapor barrier mastic. See 7.4.13.

### 7.2. *Hot Piping 70° F Through 600° F*

7.2.1. A single layer insulation and insulation on traced piping shall be applied by the staggered joint method, except when using hinge type insulation, with all joints tightly butted together and securely wired, in accordance with detail Standard Drawing 0A-N-0004.

7.2.2. Double layered insulation may be installed with hinged-type insulation, with the ends tightly butted together and securely wired with 18 gauge stainless steel wire up to 12" insulation OD; and 16 gauge stainless steel wire greater than 12" insulation OD. Longitudinal and butt joints shall be staggered between layers when applying in double layer method.

Note: In high fire risk areas where the insulation serves as fire proofing, both layers of the insulation shall be held in place with 1/2" wide bands on 9" centres. Wire is not acceptable.

---

7.2.3. When bands are used they shall be tightened with a mechanical tightening tool and secured with 304 stainless steel wing type seals.

7.2.4. All fittings, bends and valves, when required, shall be covered with mitered sectional insulation, securely wired or banded in place, to a thickness equal to that on adjacent piping.

7.2.5. All voids around valves and fittings shall be filled and smoothed with flexible insulation equivalent to the adjoining insulation, or insulation cement. Insulating and finishing cement shall be composed of mineral fiber pellets and insulating fillers, blended with hydraulic setting binders and corrosion inhibitors. Recommended products: Pro-Tec-T-Kote conforming to ASTM C-449 or Suncor approved equal.

7.2.6. Fittings on piping under 3" shall be covered with insulating cement, mitered sectional insulation, or filled with flexible insulation equivalent to the adjoining pipe insulation.

## 7.3. Hot Piping 601° F Through 1200° F

7.3.1. Insulation shall be applied in single or double layer construction as defined by Chart "A" within limits of available thickness, with expansion joints.

7.3.2. Insulation shall be applied by the broken joint method. For multiple layer construction, the second layer shall be applied so that both circumferential and longitudinal joints are staggered.

7.3.3. All joints shall be butted together.

7.3.4. All voids shall be filled and smoothed with insulation cement. Insulating and finishing cement shall be composed of mineral fiber pellets and insulating fillers, blended with hydraulic setting binders and corrosion inhibitors. Recommended products: Thermal-I-Kote 1-FW by Pro-Tec-T-Kote or Suncor approved equal.

7.3.5. Insulation expansion joints shall be provided on 21 feet maximum centers. Joints shall be 1¾" wide, packed with mineral wool. When applying double layer pipe insulation, the expansion joint on the outer layers shall be offset 12" from the joint on the inner layer.

7.3.6. On multiple layer construction, each layer shall be wired or banded separately.

## 7.4 Cold Piping Below 70° F Through -265° F

7.4.1. Sectional insulation (semi-circular form) shall be used for all pipe sizes when available.

7.4.2. All end joints shall be staggered by starting with alternate half and full length legs. Longitudinal joints shall be on top and bottom of the pipe.

7.4.3. The insulation shall be applied with sealer (Pittseal 444N, Chi-Prd CP-22/24 by Childers) on all joints of single and outer layers of double layer installations. Sealer shall be applied to both sides of adjoining joint. Cut off any squeezed-out sealant flush with surface.

7.4.4. Single layer shall be secured with stainless steel bands. On multiple layers the inner layers shall be secured with glass fiber reinforced filament tape, and the outer layers with stainless steel bands.

7.4.5. Bore of insulation in contact with pipe shall be coated with Pittsburgh Corning anti-abrasive compound 2A (factory applied).

7.4.6. After the final insulation is applied, all unfilled joints and chipped edges shall be packed up with sealer, or filled with pieces of perlite/glass cut to fit and cemented in, so as to leave a smooth and even surface.

7.4.7. All voids around fittings, valves, flanges, etc., shall be filled with perlite, 3 lb density loose fill insulation conforming to ASTM C-549.

7.4.8. Polyisocyanurate shall be used for inner layer on combination type cold insulation as follows.

7.4.8.1. Section insulation (semi-circular form) shall be used for all pipe 24" and smaller. Segmental form shall be used for pipe sizes above 24"

7.4.8.2. All end joints shall be staggered by starting with alternate half and full length legs. Longitudinal joints shall be on top and bottom of pipe.

7.4.8.3. Insulation shall be secured with ¾" wide glass fiber reinforced tape with 100% overlap.

---

7.4.9. Insulation securement shall be applied as follows:

7.4.9.1. On the inner layer the tape shall be placed 3" back from all end joints on piping where insulated outside diameter is under 12" and 4 ½" from all end joints on pipe where insulated outside diameter is 12" and over. Tape shall be installed with 100% overlap.

7.4.9.2. On single and outer layer the band spacing shall be 9".

7.4.9.3. Steel bands shall be tightened with a mechanical tightening tool.

7.4.10. Care shall be taken that the single or outer layer of Foamglas is not cracked when secured with stainless steel bands. Cracked or broken Foamglas shall be replaced.

7.4.11. Insulation at fittings shall not be of less thickness than the insulation on adjacent piping. Fitting insulation (covers) shall be held together and applied with sealer (Pittseal 444N or Chil-Pruf CP-22/24). All fitting covers shall be made in sections so as to be readily applied and shall fit with a minimum of void spaces. All insulation on welded fittings shall fit snugly to the contour of the fitting and shall be applied in the same manner and with the same materials as specified for pipe.

7.4.12. Fitting insulation shall be applied with sealer on all joints and each segment banded in place.

7.4.13. Insulation for piping, fittings, valves and flanges shall be finished with Pittsburgh Corning Pittcote 300 or Chil-Pruf CP-22/24 vapour barrier coating. Apply a wet coat of 4-5 gal/100 ft2. Total wet thickness should be 3/32" - 1/8" thick, giving a dry film thickness of 1/16". No fabric membrane is required.

N = Non-Flammable

## 7.5. *Weatherproofing Application*

7.5.1. For general process and offplot areas, the jacketing material shall be aluminum type 3003 or 5005, 0.016" thick, with stucco embossed finish, and with polykraft or poly-surlyn moisture barrier.

For high fire risk process areas or acid or caustic handling areas, the jacketing material shall be 304 stainless steel 0.010" thick, stucco embossed with polykraft or poly-surlyn moisture barrier and will be identified on piping isometric or module drawings.

All jacketing material shall be supplied with factory applied integral flame resistant moisture barrier such as Childers "Polykraft" or Suncor approved equal.

7.5.2. Straight portions of insulated piping shall have the basic insulation covered with metal jacketing, with all joints lapped 2" and arranged on horizontal center line with the lap turned down in order to shed water. All seams shall have a 2" hem on the overlapping edge. Elbows, transitions, etc., shall be installed using slip joint, lockformed or similar method. On horizontal lines, the pipe jacketing shall overlap the fitting covers.

The jacketing shall be secured with stainless steel bands on maximum centres of 12" using a mechanical tightening tool and secured with stainless steel wing seals.

---

7.5.3. Fittings shall be weatherproofed with prefabricated metal covers with weatherproof seams.

7.5.4. A watertight metal bevel ring shall be installed where pipe insulation terminates.

7.5.5. Where required, all possible sources of moisture penetration shall be carefully sealed with a continuous bead of flashing compound Pittseal 444N or Chil-Pruf CP-22/24 or Suncor approved equal.

7.5.6. On vertical piping, "S" clips shall be used at circumferential joints. Two (2) clips shall be used for piping 12" and under, three (3) clips for piping over 12", per joint.

7.5.7. The jackets shall not be installed in contact with bare carbon steel.

7.5.8. The moisture barrier must be hole free and continuous before application. Bare aluminum or stainless steel shall not be installed in contact with calcium silicate or other alkaline insulations.

7.5.9. The moisture barrier shall isolate the jacketing from the insulation. Any damaged moisture barrier on the jacket shall be patched before the jacket is applied, or the sheet shall be rejected.

## 8. APPLICATION - VESSELS AND EQUIPMENT

### 8.1. General

8.1.1. Vessels and equipment shall be insulated only when indicated on the Equipment List.

8.1.2. Nameplates, 'A' number stampings, code inspection plates, and weephole nipples shall not be insulated unless otherwise specified. Suitable flashing shall be applied at each junction of insulated and uninsulated surfaces, to keep moisture from wetting the insulation.

8.1.3. Vessel manways, inspection holes and sample connections, etc., shall be insulated with removable covers on insulated vessels.

8.1.4. Flanges on exchangers, channels and channel covers shall not be insulated. (Refer to 7.3.12.) Insulation shall be stopped short of flanges at sufficient distance to allow bolt removal without damage to insulation. Insulation at these points shall be bevelled and weatherproofed with metal bevels.

8.1.5. Vertical vessels which have a significant difference in operating temperature from top to bottom may have the lower half of the vessel insulated for the bottom operating temperature and upper half insulated for the vessel's overall average operating temperature. Applicable thickness shall be as indicated on the Equipment drawings, or Equipment List.

8.1.6. For vessels and tubular equipment 3 feet diameter and smaller, preformed or segmented pipe insulation shall be used where applicable.

8.1.7. Insulation supports for vertical vessels shall be in accordance with Standard Drawing 0A-N-0008.

## ATTACHMENT 1 - ACOUSTICAL PIPE LAGGING SPECIFICATIONS

### Functional Pipe Lagging

An effective acoustical pipe lagging system should perform four functions:

1. *Absorption* of airborne sound
2. Reduction in *transmittance* of airborne sound
3. *Damping* (absorption) of structure-borne (vibration induced) sound
4. *Decoupling* (reduction in transmittance) of structure-borne sound.

A suitable design would incorporate all four acoustical functions. The individual components of such a system would perform the following acoustical functions:

- mineral wool or fiberglass insulation will provide sound absorption, decouple the outer jacketing from the pipe wall, and provide a small degree of damping for the pipe wall
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing will provide a reduction in sound transmittance, and the adhered vinyl will damp out vibratory energy that would otherwise result in the aluminum outer layer becoming a re-radiator of sound
- the septum layer (when used) provides additional mass for additional reduction in sound transmittance.

The acoustical pipe lagging shall be installed in such a way as to meet the intent described above.

### Lagging Systems

Four different pipe lagging systems are specified herein. These are as follows:

**System "A"** shall consist of:

- 1" mineral wool insulation
- impregnated vinyl septum
- 1 1/2" mineral wool insulation
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing;

**Systems "B1" and "B2"** shall consist of:

- 2" mineral wool insulation
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing;

**System "C"** shall consist of:

- 1" mineral wool or fiberglass insulation
- aluminum jacketing.

The differences between Systems B1 and B2 are as follows:

- valves in System B1 are to be treated, as indicated under Treatment Locations
- valves in System B2 do not require treatment, even when piping on each side of the valve is treated.

## Corrosion Protection

Above ground piping exposed to the outdoor elements may already be painted, coated or otherwise treated with corrosion inhibiting protection. Piping inside compressor buildings, to a lesser extent, also requires consideration. With the addition of acoustical pipe lagging, two concerns are typically raised: 1) the piping will be more prone to corrosive buildup when contained within a porous insulation system; and 2) the piping cannot be easily inspected for corrosive buildup when covered. To mitigate the potentially adverse effects of corrosion, Bantrel / Suncor at their option, may wish to supplement the acoustical design with an implementation plan for additional corrosion inhibiting protection treatments. While not specifically preferred, recommended nor specified herein, such corrosion inhibiting protection treatments could include the following:

- priming and painting with a urethane paint system

- priming and application of a corrosion inhibiting tapewrap, such as first an elastomeric adhesive, a rubber and synthetic resin, or a bituminous rubber compound, and second a polyethylene film backing

- utilizing pre-coated pipe.

## Thermal/Acoustical Lagging Systems

In cases where the pipe lagging needs to provide thermal insulating characteristics as well as provide noise control, the following general comments are made:

---

- For cold pipes where Foamglas insulation is desirable, the Foamglas must be placed on first and conventionally sealed, it need not be jacketed, and then the full acoustical pipe lagging system should be installed over the Foamglas. Due to the thermal insulating characteristics that the acoustical pipe lagging's mineral wool provides, the otherwise required Foamglas thickness may be able to be reduced.

- For hot pipes where calcium silicate insulation is desirable, a substitution to mineral wool should be evaluated. Otherwise the calcium silicate must be placed on first, it need not be jacketed, and then the full acoustical pipe lagging system should be installed over the calcium silicate. Due to the thermal insulating characteristics that the acoustical pipe lagging's mineral wool provides, the otherwise required calcium silicate thickness may be able to be reduced.

- For hot pipes where mineral wool insulation is desirable, the acoustical pipe lagging system may provide all of the necessary requirements. If necessary the thickness of the mineral wool may be able to be increased upon review of the noise reduction characteristics required, but it should never be decreased.

- For hot pipes where only personnel protection is required, any of the acoustical pipe lagging systems should suffice.

- Vapor barriers of various types may be used when required, the specific applications of which need to be assessed.

## Installation Specifications

The following are required installation practices for the insulation, septum and jacketing.

### Insulation:

- the pipes must be clean and dry before treatment
- all pipewall corrosion protection, cold service Foamglas vapour barriers, etc. shall be in place prior to acoustical material installation
- butt-joint all longitudinal and circumferential insulation joints
- fill all voids with loose insulation, insulating adhesives or insulating cements should not be used
- for vertical piping, exceeding 6m (20 ft.) in height, the contractor shall either install or have installed some acceptable form of mechanical support fixtures to the pipe or equipment to support the insulation (and jacketing)
- when adjacent insulation sections are butt-jointed together, their rotational alignment shall be such that longitudinal seams are staggered (not lining up to each other) circumferentially by at least 45°.
- all insulation should be banded according to standard banding practices, using either ½" x 0.020", ¾" x 0.015", or ¾" x 0.020" Type 304 stainless steel banding along with stainless steel wing seals, on not more than 12" centres.

### Septum (System A only):

- longitudinal and circumferential edges of the septum should overlap adjacent septum layers by 2" (no hems or hugs)
- a vinyl adhesive, and/or an approved tape and/or banding may be used (optional) to assist in holding the impregnated vinyl septum together.

### Jacketing:

- the insulation must be free of any moisture prior to jacketing
- longitudinal edges of the jacketing should have a 1" hem (folded in), or alternatively a 15° hug edge, and the hemmed edge or hug edge should overlap the mating flat edge by 2"
- jacketing panel seams shall be staggered from previously installed insulation section seams (not lining up to each other), both circumferentially by at least 30° and longitudinally by at least 6"
- on horizontal outdoor piping, longitudinal edges of adjacent jacketing panels should be located at (roughly) either the 4 o'clock or 8 o'clock positions.

---

- longitudinal edges of adjacent jacketing panels at the same position (o'clock) should not line up exactly, they should miss-align by alternating (rotationally) 1" back and forth

- circumferential edges of the jacketing should overlap the previously installed jacketing panel by 2"

- on vertical outdoor piping, the circumferential edge of the higher jacketing panel shall overlap the circumferential edge of the lower jacketing panel by 2"

- all overlapped longitudinal and circumferential edges of the external jacketing should be continuously caulked

- all jacketing segments between beads ("lobster-back" crimped joints between segments) of mitered (gored) elbows for piping 16" or greater shall have at least two band straps applied, one at the center of each segment, and one right alongside the bead on the overlap side

- for Systems A and B1 only, all beads of mitered elbows shall be visually inspected after installation and banding of the jacketed elbows for any gaps whatsoever between adjacent segments, and any gaps found shall be caulked to seal

- all aluminum jacketing surfaces to be caulked shall first be wiped dry with an alcohol based solvent, to remove the commonly found oil film from the aluminum

- all caulking installed shall be a continuous bead, placed on the under layer of all hem or hug overlaps, so as not to expose any of the caulking

- all caulking shall consist of an aluminum colored or clear silicone elastomer sealant, such as Dow Corning Silastic 732 RTV or 999A Adhesive/Sealant, or approved equal

- for vertical piping exceeding 6m (20 ft.) in height, where the insulation was mechanically supported from the pipe wall, the contractor shall also prepare "S clips" from ¾" x 0.020" stainless steel banding with a functional depth of 3" and insert these into the top edge of each vertical section of jacketing in quadrant positions to receive and support each succeeding tier of jacketing

- all jacketing should be banded according to standard banding practices, using only ¾" x 0.020" Type 304 stainless steel banding along with stainless steel wing seals, on not more than 9" centres; sheet metal screws or pop-rivets should not be used, except by approval

- banding shall be installed in such a way to prevent "fish-mouthing" openings that separate the caulking.

## ATTACHMENT 2 - ACOUSTICAL BLANKET SPECIFICATIONS

### FUNCTIONAL ACOUSTICAL BLANKET

To reduce the amount of sound radiation from the casing of non-uniform shaped components, such as for valves, valve flanges, compressor casing or pump, flanges, and/or where quick access and reusable lagging are desirable, treatment with an **"Acoustical Blanket"** is recommended. This Acoustical Blanket contains a massive but limp barrier material to stop transmission of airborne sound, and a fluffy insulation layer to absorb airborne sound. The barrier material is placed a couple of inches away from the casing of the sound radiating components; with the insulation layer placed between the component casing and the barrier. Another thinner layer of insulation is placed on the outside of the barrier, only to re-conform to the natural curvature of the components and hide creases in the flat barrier material. A fire rated, oil resistant, tear resistant fabric is placed over both sides of the insulation/barrier/insulation product for durability and protection purposes.

The recommended composite material for the Acoustical Blanket therefore consists of a layer of limp impregnated vinyl noise barrier material, sandwiched between two layers of a high density E-glass insulation and a low density fiberglass insulation, and covered on each side with a Teflon coated fiberglass fabric. The entire blanket is stitched together, giving it a "quilted" appearance. An insulation contractor would custom fit and install a removable enclosure out of the Acoustical Blanket material, producing a tight fitting enclosure for the components.

The word Acoustical Blanket is used within these Specifications, whereas "removable covers" are synonymously referred to by insulation contractors in the industry. Regardless of the terms used, specialty acoustical materials must be used to achieve the desired noise control performance; these Specifications are not about thermally insulating.

### CONTOUR FIT ACOUSTICAL BLANKET COMPOSITION AND MATERIAL SPECIFICATIONS

The Acoustical Blankets should consist of the following (from inside working outwards):

**TYPE I**

A. Teflon coated fiberglass cloth

B. Scrim cloth

## CORROSION PROTECTION

Above ground equipment exposed to the outdoor elements may already be painted, coated or otherwise treated with corrosion inhibiting protection. Equipment inside any buildings, to a lesser extent, also requires consideration. With the addition of Acoustical Blankets, two concerns are typically raised: 1) the equipment will be more prone to corrosive buildup when contained within a porous insulation system; and 2) the equipment cannot be easily inspected for corrosive buildup

---

when covered. To mitigate the potentially adverse effects of corrosion, Bantrel / Suncor at their option, may wish to supplement the acoustical design with an implementation plan for additional corrosion inhibiting protection treatments. While not specifically preferred, recommended nor specified herein, such corrosion inhibiting protection treatments could include the following:

- priming and painting with a urethane paint system, compatible with the operating temperature of the equipment
- priming and application of a corrosion inhibiting tapewrap, such as first an elastomeric adhesive, a rubber and synthetic resin, or a bituminous rubber compound, and second a polyethylene film backing, compatible with the operating temperature of the equipment
- utilizing pre-coated materials.

## INSTALLATION SPECIFICATIONS

The following are required installation practices:

- the equipment must be clean and dry before treatment
- the blanket enclosure must be designed and fabricated by the contractor in such a way that it is a removable and reusable blanket enclosure
- all edges of the blanket material must be folded over and either sewn, stapled, or otherwise sealed so as to protect the edge of the blanket
- the blanket material must be oriented such that the side of the blanket material with the 2-inches (50 mm) of E-glass insulation becomes the inside of the blanket enclosure in contact with the components
- the various segments of the blanket enclosure that require attachment at an overlap should be attached together by sewing, utilizing velcro strips, common sense fasteners, eyelet and wire fasteners, or by other means upon approval
- where one layer of the blanket overlaps another layer of the blanket, the overlap must be at least 4-inches (100 mm); the use of attachment techniques along the entire length of every overlapped edge is not required, as long as the contractor ensures that no acoustical leaks are occurring
- where the blanket and the pipe lagging jacketing from site piping meet, the blanket should flash the jacketing by a minimum of 4-inches (100 mm); this overlap should be banded but not caulked
- any protrusions such as by shafts, small piping, support structure members, gauges, pressure/temperature transmitters, etc. do not require treatment by the blanket; however sufficient overlap or other means to achieve a proper seal without acoustical leaks, using appropriate attachment techniques and/or banding, is required for all protrusions
- the blanket shall include any access flaps to allow for access to service or observation points, as requested by Bantrel / Suncor; all access flaps must have sufficient overlap with appropriate attachment techniques
- the entire casing of the component treated is to be covered

## SPECIFICATIONS FOR REMOVEABLE INSULATION BLANKET

### 1. Scope

This Specification covers the general requirement and technical specification for removable blanket material and insulation for the remaining piping and fittings operating at the temperature from 140 °F through 1200 °F.

Suncor Standard 0208 – Thermal Insulation of Piping, vessels and Equipment does not cover much about the insulation blanket and insulation material inside.

### 2. Application Requirement

The Removable Insulation Blankets in this specification shall be used for the purposes of heat conservation, process stabilization and personnel protection.

### 3. Typical Application

The following pipe and fittings areas shall be covered with removable insulation blankets:

- Instrumentation (PSV's, PVC's PT etc)
- Vessels
- Exchangers
- Valve and flanged assemblies with isolation blinds
- Flanged piping spools
- Orifice flanges
- Other applications, not specified

### 4. General

4.1 The blanket material shall be Fire retarding and may serve as fire proofing for certain applications.

4.2 The blanket material shall be weather proof, waterproof and chemical resistance.

4.3 In the application of Stainless Steel, all the insulation material shall be non corrosive, whether wet or dry.

4.4 All the material shall be new and shall confirm to the regulation of the Federal, Provincial and Local Occupational Safety and Health Act. The Suncor Industrial hygiene department, prior to implementation, should approve the MSDS.

4.5 Material containing asbestos shall not be acceptable.

4.6 As per Suncor Standards 0208, the chloride content of insulating material shall not exceed 50 PPM.

4.7 Use of insulation materials other than those stated in this specification requires Engineer approval.

4.8 Blanket shall be securely fixed on the valves and fittings to avoid any air pocket or exposed area.

---

[Suncor logo]

OIL SANDS PROJECTS ENGINEERING

4.9 Blanket loose fitting on the valves or pipefitting is unacceptable, which cause a heat loss due to air passage between blanket and applied surface.

## 5. Design Criteria

5.1 Blankets on flanged valves and equipment shall be designed to cover adjacent mating flanges and overlap line insulation by a minimum of 50 mm.
5.2 Blankets on valves shall be designed to cover the valve body and the bonnet flange of the valve.
5.3 Blankets for pressure gauges include block and bleed valve.
5.4 Blankets for PSV's shall include the outlet flange. Blanket shall cover entire spring chamber.
5.5 Supplier/ Manufacturers will do all dimensional measurement of each equipment and assume responsibility for fit.
5.6 Manufacturer will maintain the documentation of items furnished, which includes dimensional tractability.

## 6. Material Specification

### 6.1 Blanket Jacket Material

#### 6.1.1 Outside Jacket Cover

Teflon Coated Fabric EJ 1650,

- Weight = 16.5 OZ/ Sqyd ± 10%
- Temperature Range – upto 550°F
- Base Fabric = Fiberglass / Satin weave
- Coating = Grey Fluorocarbon

This material has severe chemical and temperature exposure capabilities and it is flame retardant.

#### 6.1.2 Inside Jacket Cover

*1. For temperature range of 140°F to 550°F*
Use the same Teflon Coated Fabric EJ 1650 for the temperature range up to 550°F

*2. For temperature range of 550°F to 1200°F*
**TECSTAR 18**

- Weight = 18 OZ/Sq yd
- Thickness = 0.030"
- Temperature range = 550°F to 1200°F
- Base Fabric and Weave = Fiberglass / Plain

OIL SANDS PROJECTS ENGINEERING

This material is recommended to use in high temperature applications as an inside jacket material, use Stainless Steel Mesh – T304.011 (Gauge 11) wherever applicable for reinforcement of multilayer insulation material.

## 6.2 Insulation Material

### 7.2.1 BGF MATT

BGF (BGF- MATT) is the common material to be used for removable insulation covers.

- Temperature range = 1200 °F
- Insulation material is asbestos free, non-respirable E-Glass fibers, non-flammable, non-flame spread/ non-smoke generated.

Thickness of the insulation depends upon the temperature range and pipe size. The thickness chart is attached in Appendix I

## 7. Fire Proofing Application

### UC 100-95 Silica Cloth

- Weight = 36 OZ/sq yd
- Temperature resistance
- Continuous use = 1800 °F
- Melt temperature = 3100 °F
- Linear Shrinkage = 10% @ 1800 °F

This is not 100% fire proofing material, but provides high heat resistance and thermal protection. This material shall be use for flangeless check valves and at high temperature application > 1200°F.

## 8. Construction

- 8.1 Blankets shall be sewn construction for temperatures up to 1200 °C.
- 8.2 Blankets up to 1" thick shall be inside double seam construction, with the liner and jacket material sewn together to form the insulation pocket.
- 8.3 All blankets up to 1" thick must be double stitched. First stitch will be performed with cloth layers sewn together inside out. Blanket will then be turned right side out and top stitched around all penetrations and around the perimeter of the insulation pocket to create a separation between the insulation pocket and the perimeter flaps.
- 8.4 All drawcord and closure flaps, required inside the perimeter of the blanket shall be attached inside the seam, between the liner and jacket layers of the blanket.
- 8.5 Blankets over 1" thick shall be constructed using boxed corners and gussets in order to maintain consistent thickness across the entire area of the blanket.

---

- 8.6 Perimeter flaps shall be formed as an extension of the outer jacket, and shall not be attached to the blanket as separate pieces.
- 8.7 All drawcord and closure flaps, required inside the perimeter of the blanket shall be attached inside the seam, between the liner and jacket layers of the blanket.
- 8.8 No external fasteners shall be used to prevent shifting of the insulation. The insulation shall be mechanically fastened in place internally.

## 9. Accessories

Use standards accessories, fasteners, D-rings, Knitted mesh, Nomex/ Stainless Steel thread, Hook & loop for insulation blanket.

**Sewing Material**

- For temperature up to 550 °F, sewing material ALPHA MARITEX D-24 Teflon coated twisted fiberglass thread shall be used or equal
- For temperature range from 551 °F to 1200 °F, sewing material ST-304 (304 SS) thread shall be used.

## 10. Identification Tag

Stainless Steel 304 or aluminum tag shall be used. Stainless Steel tag is preferred in corrosive application. The tag dimensions shall be 2"x2.5". The corner of the tag should be chamfered by 1/8". Tag shall be riveted with a piece of same Teflon coated EJ 1050 fabric material, of 2.5"x3.5" size and sewed with the blanket cover. All blankets shall be fitted with an identification Tag clearly marked with the following information:

- Equipment #                        e.g. VGA1003#12
- Equipment size and type            e.g. 6" 300# Gate Valve
- Plant # / ISO #                    e.g. 52 / 52P3031-1510
- Co-ordinates and Elevation         e.g. E 200', N350' and EL 135'
- Max operating temperature          e.g. 300 °F
- Manufacturer's identification #    e.g. As per manufacturer standards
