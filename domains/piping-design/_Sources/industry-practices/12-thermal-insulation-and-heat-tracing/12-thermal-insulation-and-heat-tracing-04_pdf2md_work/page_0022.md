**Revision Memo**
**N-6 rev. 10**
July, 2001

1. Reformatted write-up based from the table of contents.

2. Modified Table 3 increased Mineral Fiber's temperature service up to 454°C (850°F).

3. Mineral Fiber insulation can be used for areas subject to mechanical abuse or piping adjacent to pumps, heat exchangers, control valves that requires the removal of insulation.

4. Deleted fibrous glass material, use Mineral Fiber or calcium silicate as insulation material for heat conservation or personnel protection.

5. The insulation thickness in the current N6 rev9a spec were checked/updated in two representative cases using hand calculations based on Kern (Process Heat Transfer) and the Richardson PC heat loss program. Using the current official cost of energy of $2.95/Mbtu versus $1.83/Mbtu in the current N6 spec it was found that increasing insulation thickness based on the current material and labour costs exceeded a 5 year payout. It was decided to leave the recommended thickness for heat conservation unchanged

6. Added table 5 and 6 for heat traced insulation thicknesses.

7. The higher insulation thickness will be used for lines with dual service for insulation (i.e. heat conservation based on normal operating temperature or that required for heat tracing based on the hold temperature).

8. Oversized insulation will be used for all heat traced piping.

9. Stainless steel jackets with 0.020 inch thickness will be used where insulation must be removed or where insulation is in high traffic areas.

10. The insulation materials for austenitic stainless steel shall be limited to a maximum chloride content of 10 ppm.

11. Added Paragraph 4.4 to use perlite insulation on all Austenitic 321 stainless steel piping.

12. Modified N-3 to UE-1 N-3, project specific specification.

13. Changed 3" to 4" in para. 6.5.3 to be consistent with UE-1 N-3.

14. Added para. 6.6.10 to use calcium silicate Tables 4 & 6 for perlite insulation thicknesses.

15. Changed 2" to 1" in para. 6.8.1 to be consistent with UE-1 L-3.
