## 1.0 SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose and Usage

1.1.1 This specification covers the material and application requirements for insulation of hot piping and equipment. Equipment includes vessels, heat exchangers, turbines, pumps, other, machinery and small hot tank diameters up to 7.6m (25 ft).

1.1.2 This specification is intended for use by suppliers and installers of insulation.

## 2.0 GENERAL

### 2.1 Interpretation

All conflicts between requirements of this specification and any related standard or code, purchase order shall be referred to the Purchaser for clarification before proceeding with purchase or installation of the affected part.

### 2.2 Trade Name

Trade names, as used in this specification, are included solely for the purpose of indicating the quality of material required, and are not intended to exclude equal or better items offered by other Manufacturers. Comparable products may be submitted for approval by the Purchaser.

### 2.3 Insulation Type and Thickness

The following insulation parameters shall be specified for each item of equipment or pipespool on documents accompanying this specification. The governing documents shall be:

a) For piping: Line Designation Tables (LDTs) and Piping Isometrics

b) For equipment: Equipment Insulation Schedules

The above documents shall show:

a) Insulation thickness

b) Insulation type (heat conservation, personnel protection, acoustic, etc.)

c) Insulation system (molded calcium silicate, mineral fiber, etc.)

d) Insulation finish (aluminum or stainless steel jacketing etc.)

If equipment or piping is not to be insulated, the Equipment Insulation Schedule or LDTs and piping isometrics shall specify 'NA' as the Insulation Code.

---

## 2.4 Proposal Information

The following data as applicable shall be submitted to Purchaser for approval:

a) All product specifications, including but not limited to insulation material, cements, mastics, caulking material, jacketing, banding, fasteners, etc.

b) Material storage requirements, including shelf life, storage temperatures and any special safety requirements for handling and storing of insulation materials or components.

c) Installation details of insulation expansion joints, breather springs, etc.

d) Repair procedures for damaged areas or insulation and/or jacketing or weatherproofing.

e) Inspection and testing procedures.

## 4.0 MATERIALS

### 4.1 General

4.1.1 All materials which do not meet the requirements of the specification and for which no exemption has been granted in writing, will be rejected by the Purchaser. Approval for substitutions shall be given only after Seller has clearly demonstrated that all requirements of this specification are satisfied by the subject material. Substitute material shall not be shipped to project site prior to written approval.

---

4.1.2 The recommendations of the insulation, weather barrier manufacturers, etc., shall be followed in addition to the requirements of this specification. All conflicts with this specification shall be submitted in writing to Purchaser for resolution.

Where manufacturer's instructions are given in permission terms, e.g., "should" and "recommended", these shall be considered mandatory.

4.1.3 Insulation materials and accessories shall be new, undamaged and shall preferably be of domestic origin. Factory or manufacturer's "rejects" or "seconds" will not be acceptable for use under this specification.

4.1.4 Insulation materials including cements, coverings, etc., shall not contain asbestos.

4.1.5 No type of insulating material shall crack, sag, delaminate, or show evidence of flaming, glowing, smoldering, or smoking when tested in accordance with ASTM C 411 at the temperature on which the insulation requirements are based. In addition, rigid insulation material shall not warp more than 6 mm (1/4") over 915 mm (36") test length.

4.1.6 All insulating materials, including cements, mastics and adhesives shall have a flame spread classification of not more than 25 per ASTM E 84.

## 4.2 Insulation Materials for Austenitic Stainless Steel

4.2.1 In cases where the piping or equipment to be insulated is constructed of austenitic stainless steel materials, limitations shall be placed on the chloride content of the insulation materials. This includes all wicking-type insulations, including calcium silicate, fiberglass and mineral fibre and shall also include any cements or mastics in contact with the stainless steel.

4.2.2 Water leaching and chemical tests shall be performed by the Seller, on three samples from each type of material in contact with the stainless steel piping or equipment, in accordance with ASTM C 871.

4.2.3 The leached products shall be tested for SCC effects on austenitic stainless steel according to ASTM C 692. The Seller shall provide a report of compliance which shows that the samples conform to this specification prior to supply of any insulation.

---

4.2.4 Acceptance of the insulating materials chemical composition and their effects on SCC of the stainless steel shall be per ASTM C 795.

## 4.3 Insulation Materials for Piping

4.3.1 Preformed rigid piping insulation shall be furnished in standard lengths with ends cut square. Dimensions shall conform to ASTM C 585. Rigid block insulation shall be furnished true and square in compliance with ASTM C 550.

4.3.2 Insulation for piping shall be sectional, segmental, beveled lag, or scored block.

4.3.3 Insulation for fittings, flanges, and valves may be field fabricated, shop fabricated, or molded. All insulation shall be in compliance with ASTM C 450. Insulation thickness shall match the adjacent insulation.

4.3.4 Materials of insulation for piping shall meet the requirements of the applicable ASTM specification.

- a) C 533 Calcium Silicate Block and Pipe Thermal Insulation

- b) C 547 Mineral Fiber Preformed Pipe Insulation
  Class 2: for Mineral Wool (8-10 lb/ft3)
  Class 2: for Fiberglass (6-8 lb/ft3)

- c) C 552 Cellular Glass Block and Pipe Thermal Insulation

4.3.5 The minimum compressive strength of calcium silicate insulation shall be 0.97 MPa (140 psi) to produce 5% compression as per ASTM C 165.

## 4.4 Insulation Materials for Equipment

4.4.1 Insulation for cylindrical shells of equipment shall be sectional or segmental pipe insulation, bevelled lags, scored block or rigid block.

4.4.2 Insulation for vessels, exchangers and tanks shall meet the requirements of the following ASTM Specifications:

- a) C 533 Calcium Silicate Block and Pipe Thermal Insulation

- b) C 612 Mineral Fiber Block and Board Thermal Insulation
  (Class 3 or Class 4)

- c) C 449 Mineral Fiber Hydraulic - Settling Thermal Insulating and Finishing Cement

4.4.3 Fibrous type insulation shall not be used over tanks or equipment handling hot bitumen where spillage or leakage onto the insulation is probable. Fibrous insulation shall not be used on equipment handling

---

materials subject to auto ignition. A non-wicking insulation shall be used in such service.

4.4.4 Insulation for stiffening rings on vertical vessels shall be "Thermal Insulation Wool" (AW) Types I or II, Fiberglass Canada Inc. or equal approved by Purchaser.

4.4.5 Insulation for pumps and other machinery shall be as detailed in paragraph 6.5.

4.4.6 All steam turbines shall be purchased with a thermal insulation and sound attenuation jacket, and no further insulation will be required to be installed in the field. Extent of insulation will be indicated in the Equipment Insulation Schedule.

The jacket and insulation shall be removable and reusable.

4.4.7 Refractory Blanket shall be ceramic fiber insulation suitable for applications up to a continuous temperature of 1260°C (2300°F). Standard blanket shall be 25 mm (1") thick and 6 or 8 lb/ft3 density unless otherwise stated. Refractory insulating blanket for continuous temperatures up to 1427°C (2600°F) shall be specifically ordered where operating conditions require its use.

## 4.5 Thermal Insulating Cement

Thermal Insulating Cement shall meet the requirements of ASTM C 449. Suitable materials include Fibrex "Superkote" or equal approved by Purchaser.

## 4.6 Mastic Sealer

### 4.6.1

Applied coats of asphaltic (mastic) or elastomeric (such as butyl rubber) materials shall comply with the following:

- a) A demonstrated resistance to solar radiation.
- b) Flexible at the lowest temperature to which they are exposed.
- c) Compatible with the insulation materials.
- d) Mastics shall be of the breathing emulsion type.

### 4.6.2

Reinforcing for applied coatings shall be as follows:

- a) Wire mesh shall be 25 mm (1") hexagonal woven wire poultry mesh, 0.7 mm (0.028") minimum wire diameters. Wire shall be stainless steel. The mesh shall be laced into place with wire at least 2 mm (0.08") diameter of same material.
- b) Rib Lath. 12.7 mm (1/2") mesh, held in place as in (a).
- c) Synthetic fiber fabric shall be 5 x 5 to 10 x 10 mesh.

## 4.7 Metal Jacketing

---

- 4.7.1 Aluminum sheet weather jacketing for piping and equipment shall be in compliance with ASTM B 209. Alloy and temper of aluminum sheet shall be 3003 - H14 with the exception of two piece, die-formed aluminum elbow covers which shall be 1100 - 0, dead soft.

- 4.7.2 Aluminum piping jackets shall have an exterior "stucco-embossed" finish and have a factory applied epoxy-polyester enamel coating. This coating shall have a minimum file thickness of .015 mm (0.6 mils). The enamel coating shall face the insulation. Sheet shall be 0.4 mm (0.016") thick.

- 4.7.3 Stainless steel weather jacketing for piping and equipment shall be in compliance with ASTM A 480, and shall be supplied in grade 304 (unless otherwise stated) tempered to soft anneal and a 2 "D" (dull) finish. The sheet thickness shall be 28 ga (0.0156"). Stainless steel jacketing requires no moisture barrier.

- 4.7.4 Metal jackets for vessels and column may be flat or corrugated. When the diameter of the vessel or column is up to 7.3 m (24 ft) the corrugations shall be on a 32 mm (1-1/4") pitch. When the diameter is larger than 7.3 m (24 ft), the corrugations shall be on 54 mm (2-1/2") pitch.

## 4.8 Bands, Seals and Breather Springs

- 4.8.1 Banding for piping and equipment shall be 0.5 mm (0.020") thick, 300 series stainless steel conforming with ASTM A 480. Banking shall have a smooth finish with edges free from burrs. Width of bands for piping shall be 12.7 mm (1/2") for lines 12" NPS to 24" NPS inclusive and 19 mm (3/4") for larger lines. Width of bands for equipment shall be 19 mm (3/4") or as indicated on the appropriate Standard Drawing.

- 4.8.2 Finger strap banding shall have fingers 50 mm (2") long by 19 mm (3/4") wide by 0.5 mm (0.020") thick and 230 mm (9") on center.

- 4.8.3 Seals shall be 300 series stainless steel, heavy duty, wing type conforming with ASTM A 167.

- 4.8.4 Breather Springs shall be 300 series stainless steel, with tensile strength of 1,900 MPa (275 psi), 100 mm (4") long, Techaloy, or equal approved by Purchaser.

---

## 4.9 Caulking Material

Caulking material shall be silicone, aluminum colored, Dow Corning 732 or equal approved by Purchaser. Caulking shall be applied on electric and steam traced lines at the beginning and end of the tracer where passed through insulation. Other caulking shall be applied as per the applicable Standard Drawings.

## 4.10 Tapes

4.10.1 Fastening tape shall be as a pressure sensitive glass filament tape.

4.10.2 Aluminum foil tape shall be an adhesive type, 5 mils thick with a transparent synthetic adhesive on one side, furnished in rolls 50 mm (2") wide.

## 4.11 Tie and Lacing Wire

Wire shall be fully annealed 300 series stainless steel conforming with ASTM A 580, 18 ga for lines up to 6" NPS, 16 ga for lines up to 10" NPS.

## 4.12 Fasteners

### 4.12.1 Stainless Steel Screws

Stainless steel screws shall be type 304 or 316 Type A, No. 8 by 1/2" long self tapping with Robertson or hex head. The spacing of fasteners shall be 150 mm (6").

### 4.12.2 Rivets

All blind rivets for aluminum jacketing shall be aluminum with steel mandrels. Rivets for stainless steel jacketing shall be stainless steel with steel mandrels. The spacing of rivets shall be 150 mm (6").

## 4.13 Adhesive and Sealers

### 4.13.1 Joint Sealer or Sheet Metal Lap Sealer

Joint sealer shall be metallic gray vapor barrier sealant suitable for use with all types of low and dual temperature insulations and remain flexible in service, will not crack or shrink during repeated cycles of high and low temperatures.

(Benjamin Foster, Foamseal 30-45, or equal approved by Purchaser)

---

#### 4.13.2 High Temperature Fabrication Adhesive

Fabrication adhesive shall be suitable for adhering dense high temperature insulation such as calcium silicate to itself and to nonporous surfaces. It shall be formulated for application with heating.

(Benjamin Foster 81-27 or equal approved by Purchaser)

## 4.14 Flexible Removable Insulation Covers

- **4.14.1** Where specified, flexible removable insulation covers shall be used for valves, fittings, manways, exchangers, pumps, etc., and shall be O'Brien Flexpak design or equal, approved by the Purchaser.

- **4.14.2** Flexible removable insulation covers are defined as prefabricated, custom fit over designed and fabricated specifically for the items requiring insulation.

- **4.14.3** Jacket material shall be teflon coated fiberglass type 1 (Chemfab g - Guard 14D 14 ounces per square yard) or equal.

- **4.14.4** Liner materials shall be as follows:

  Up to 500°F - same as 4.14.3 above.

  501°F to 1000°F - knitted stainless steel wire mesh, 0.011 inches diameter, 60 density mesh size (shall not be used in hydrocarbon, acid or hazardous services).

- **4.14.5** Insulation material and thickness shall be as follows:

  Operating temperature 250°F to 400°F Manville Spingles MTB or equal with stainless steel quilt pins and insulation thickness 1".

  Operating temperature 401°F to 1000°F Pittsburgh Corning Temp-Mat, or equal with stainless steel quilt pins, insulation thickness 1" up to 500°F 2" - 501°F to 1000°F.

- **4.14.6** Seams shall be outside bound seams with a pleated liner. No inside seams are permitted. Thread used in seam construction for services (excluding sulfuric acid) up to 750°F shall be Nomex 69 bonded. 300 series stainless steel hog rings shall be used in services above 750°F and also in caustic, hydrogen, acid or other hazardous services.

- **4.14.7** Fastener/closure method shall be 300 series stainless steel lacing hooks and 16 gauge stainless steel wire together with cinch belts (same as jacket material) and stainless steel D rings.

- **4.14.8** Wind flags shall have 3/16" solid braided 100% Nomex draw strings and shall extend 50 mm (2") over adjacent pipe jacket. Stainless steel

---

draw strings shall be used when the surface temperature of the item on which the closure is tightened is above 450°F.

4.14.9 All covers shall be designed and manufactured with a parting line at the low point of the cover. When design or clearance problems necessitate the parting line to be positioned in another location, a stainless steel 11 mm (7/16") I.D. weep tube/grommet should be installed at the low point of the cover.

4.14.10 Independent flange drains fabricated from 304 stainless steel with 5/8" O.D. drain tube(s) extending outside the cover shall be supplied and installed on flanges which are insulated with flexible removable covers.

4.14.11 The removable cover weight shall not exceed 65 pounds. Covers with finished weight exceeding 65 pounds shall be fabricated in two or more pieces.

4.14.12 A permanent stainless steel or aluminum identification tag shall be attached to each cover and shall be stamped with the following information:

- Equipment number or item tag number
- Size and brief description of item, i.e. 6" 300# Gate Valve
- Serial number of cover manufacturer.

## 5.0 STORAGE AND HANDLING

### 5.1 General

All materials shall be delivered in unopened standard commercial containers, bearing the manufacturers original label with the following information:

a) Name of manufacturer
b) Name of contents
c) Quantity of contents
d) Lot or batch number
e) A "caution" label giving safety instructions for handling and storing contents, including recommended storage temperature range.

---

## 5.2 Storage of Insulation Materials

Proper warehousing or other weather protected storage shall be provided for materials and accessory items. Cartons of insulation and accessories shall be stacked in such a manner that cartons in lower levels will not be compressed, damaging the material within those cartons. Containers shall be stacked in the manner recommended by the manufacturer.

## 5.3 Storage of Mastics

Plastic containers of mastic shall not be stacked over two high upon each other to prevent collapse and ultimate loss of material. Mastic shall be stored in protected areas so that temperature is never below 5°C (40°F).

## 5.4 Storage of Metallic Components

Aluminum sheets, or coils or other metals, shall be protected from rapid changes of temperature to prevent oxidation or other staining (discoloration) film forming on surface.

# 6.0 INSTALLATION OF INSULATION

## 6.1 General

6.1.1 All surfaces to be insulated shall be presented to the insulation installer free of all contaminants, including oil, grease, dirt, etc. Where surfaces require coating in accordance with painting Specifications X-1 they shall be applied by others and fully cured prior to application of insulation. All piping joints (including welded joints) and welded joints of field fabricated equipment shall be left unpainted and uninsulated until after completion of field pressure testing.

6.1.2 Austenitic stainless steel equipment surfaces that have been exposed to salt spray during the period of transportation, field storage, or construction shall be washed with water containing less than 500 ppm of chloride ions prior to application of the insulation. Syncrude shall determine the necessity for, and shall be, responsible for performing any required washing operations. Insulation contractor shall ensure that surfaces are completely dry before commencing installation of insulation.

6.1.3 Insulation and accessory materials, other than cements and mastics, shall be applied only if dry. Stored insulation that has become wet or damaged shall be replaced at the discretion of the Purchaser.

---

6.1.4 Cracks, voids, breaks, and improperly fitted insulation shall be refitted or replaced. Excessive filling of cracks, voids or holes with mastics or cements shall not be allowed.

6.1.5 Insulation that has been applied but not finished with weather barrier jackets or coating shall be protected from the weather or other sources of physical damage.

6.1.6 Structures, equipment, piping and other surfaces in the vicinity where insulation application is in progress shall be protected from dripping, splashing, or accidental application of coatings, mastics of cements.

6.1.7 All insulation jackets shall be installed butted together.

6.1.8 Each layer of insulation shall be installed with transverse and longitudinal joints staggered, and secured in place as specified.

6.1.9 Where studs, clips or pins are used for securing insulation, spacing shall be on approximately 460 mm (18") centers for blanket insulation. Block insulation shall be installed with no less than 2 studs (clips or pins) per block. Split pins shall be spread, bent over, and imbedded into the face of the insulation. Studs, clips or pins shall be supplied by the equipment manufacturer and under no circumstances shall the insulation contractor attach any such fasteners to equipment surfaces without the written consent of the Purchaser.

6.1.10 When dual layer insulation systems employing different insulation materials are used for combined thermal and noise control, the insulation for noise control shall be installed as the outer layer.

6.1.11 Insulation systems for equipment and piping shall permit access to parts requiring maintenance without requiring destruction of the entire insulation system.

6.1.12 Jackets shall be sealed by sandwiching a flexible adhesive between all laps and at all valves, fittings, tracers, etc. on piping, to prevent entrance of water for the following insulation systems:

a) On any equipment constructed of low melting alloys.
b) The insulation system is considered as fireproofing.
c) The insulation system is used for noise control.
d) Electrically heat traced (EHT) systems.
e) For shop installed insulation subject to exposed transportation to job site.

Insulating cements shall not be used for sealing purposes.

---

6.1.13 All projections from surfaces shall be insulated completely or to the maximum practicable extent. (For example: stairway clips shall be insulated to the attachment point of the stairhead; nozzles shall be insulated to the connective flange; sphere supports shall be insulated to the termination point of the fireproofing.)

6.1.14 All penetrations through the weather proofing jacket shall be sealed with a flexible caulking compound. For piping, penetrations shall, wherever possible, be in the lower 180° segment. Penetrations through the top shall be flashed and caulked.

6.1.15 For all insulation systems the finished insulation shall be neat in appearance. Vent valves, drain plugs, union, etc., shall be free of insulation to permit removal, unless indicated otherwise in tracing details.

6.1.16 A minimum clearance of 50 mm (2") between the outside surface of any insulation and adjacent equipment of structural members shall be maintained.

6.1.17 Double layer insulation with staggered joints is mandatory when insulation is used for fireproofing equipment and piping.

## 6.2 Items Not Insulated

Insulation systems designed for heat conservation shall have the insulation omitted from the following surfaces:

6.2.1 Piping or equipment components designed on the basis of reduced metal temperature resulting from heat loss to atmosphere.

6.2.2 Piping or equipment which are insulated internally.

6.2.3 Water side surfaces of coolers and condensers.

6.2.4 Cooling water piping except bypass piping which shall be traced and insulated.

6.2.5 Steam traps (except inverted bucket type), expansion joints, rotating joints.

6.2.6 Body flanges of heat exchangers.

6.2.7 Unless specified to be fireproofed, or insulated to afford process protection against the effects of ambient temperature (viscosity change, condensation, etc.), for steam service or for noise control, insulation shall also be omitted from the following:

- a) Pumps, turbines and compressors at operating temperatures below 120°C (250°F).

---

- b) Flanged fittings, unions.

- c) Lines which carry a fluid which is normally not flowing.

  - i) Snuffing steam lines beyond the last block valve.

  - ii) Blowdown lines from pressure relief devices except those on boilers.

- d) Vessels such as hot catalyst hoppers which are normally at ambient temperature, but which are heated above 65°C (150°F) for short periods during catalyst removal operations.

## 6.3 Insulation of Piping

**6.3.1** Each line shall be insulated as a single unit.

**6.3.2** Insulation for fittings, flanges, and valves, if required, shall be the same thickness as the insulation of the pipe.

**6.3.3** All traced lines shall be insulated. Valves and flanges shall be insulated as indicated on the piping isometrics.

**6.3.4** Flanges and valves shall be insulated with flexible removable insulation covers or with fixed or removable insulation and jacket in accordance with Standard Drawings N-524, N-526, and N-527, when so indicated on the piping isometrics. For piping up to 24" NPS, insulation shall be by means of independently removable insulation assemblies, (preformed). For sizes greater than 24" NPS, use block insulation, 300 mm (12") wide by 915 mm (36") long scored with three V-grooves.

**6.3.5** Insulation that is adjoining to uninsulated flanges shall be beveled back one bolt length to allow easy removal of stud bolts and nuts.

**6.3.6** Insulation system around flanged joints shall be provided with a 1/2" NPS drain or vent ("tell tale") tube. For hydrocarbon services, the flange periphery shall, in addition, be fitted with a leak band.

**6.3.7** Insulation on valves shall leave the packing gland accessible, unless otherwise indicated in tracing details.

**6.3.8** Seal pots, vent chambers and drip pots shall be insulated only if they are heat traced.

**6.3.9** Pipe fittings and bends up to 2" NPS shall be insulated by wrapping with ceramic fibre blanket (6 or 8 lb/ft3 density) and jacketing with preformed metal covers.

---

6.3.10 Fittings larger than 2" NPS shall be insulated with segments of pipe insulation, and voids packed with ceramic fiber. Alternatively, segmented premolded fittings may be used on an economical basis.

6.3.11 Pipe fittings up to 12" NPS shall be weatherproofed with two piece stamped metal covers. Fittings larger than 12" NPS shall be protected with interlocking gore elbows.

6.3.12 Tie wires or banding used for piping insulation shall be located on 230 mm (9") centers. Wires shall be properly tightened, have loose ends cut off and twisted ends bent down flush with the insulation.

6.3.13 Jacketing shall be overlapped a minimum of 76 mm (3") and positioned to shed water. To allow for thermal expansion and movement of jacket sections, screws shall not be used in the end laps of jackets.

6.3.14 Where continuous insulation of piping at support points is specified, the insulation shall be of the load bearing type capable of carrying the piping load.

## 6.4 Insulation of Equipment

6.4.1 No permanent insulation shall be provided for the following:

- a) Vessel manhole nozzles and covers
- b) Vessel nozzles and flanges
- c) Exchanger girth and piping flanges

A customized insulation system, easily removed and replaced shall be provided -- where specified on the Equipment Insulation Schedule; it shall extend a minimum of 50 mm (2") over adjacent jacketing.

6.4.2 Vessel equipment shall be covered with a weatherproofing jacket as specified in Section 4.7 and as indicated in the Equipment Insulation Schedule. Jacketing shall be held in place by banding spaced on 460 mm (18") centers maximum, and on vertical vessels the bands shall be supported by 'u' clips every 1100 mm (4 ft) with a minimum of 4 per band.

6.4.3 Corrugated sheets shall overlap by 76 mm (3") or two corrugations. If flat sheets are used the sheets shall overlap a minimum of 76 mm (3") and an expansion joint shall be provided as specified in Standard Drawing N-514.

---

- **6.4.4** For equipment less than 3 m (10 ft) diameter and operating at a temperature of 150°C (300°F) or less, and insulated with mineral wool, installation of breather springs is not required. All equipment greater than 3 m (10 ft) diameter or with an operating temperature in excess of 150°C (300°F) shall have breather springs installed. Quantity of breather springs to be used per band shall be such that no single spring is extended more than 50 mm (2") by the expansion of the equipment.

- **6.4.5** Insulation on vessel heads shall be installed as per details in Standard Drawings C-531, N-505, N-506, N-509, N-511, or N-515, as applicable.

- **6.4.6** Each layer of insulation on vessels shall be secured to the shell by stainless steel bands or studs and pins. The spacing of bands for single layer insulation shall be 300 mm (12"). The spacing for inner layers of multi-layer insulations shall be 460 mm (18") and 300 mm (12") for the outer layer. Where bands are impractical due to an irregular surface, 16 ga stainless steel wire shall be used at 230 mm (9") centers.

- **6.4.7** The Seller must advise the Purchaser as to what provisions have been made for both horizontal and vertical thermal expansion of tanks or vessels.

- **6.4.8** Insulation on tank roofs shall be a rigid type suitable for light foot traffic.

- **6.4.9** Edges of insulation openings such as around manholes, nozzles, cutouts for stud removal, pipe supports, and other attachments, shall be sealed to prevent water from entering. Metal covers shall be used if temperatures are beyond the usable ranges of weatherproofing coatings.

- **6.4.10** Equipment nameplates shall be insulated. Insulation surrounding these plates shall be sealed to prevent water from entering.

## 6.5 Insulation of Machinery and Other Irregular Surfaces

- **6.5.1** Irregularly shaped equipment to be insulated shall use either rigid type prefabricated sections or resilient type blanket insulation.

- **6.5.2** Insulation for pumps and turbines, where required, may be of the removable box type. The fabricated box shall have the interior surface lined with resilient type mineral fibre insulation.

- **6.5.3** The thickness of insulation on pumps, compressors and turbines including inlet and outlet flanges, shall be the same as that required for the inlet piping.

- **6.5.4** Steam ends of reciprocating pumps shall be insulated to permit the covers to be removed without damaging the insulation.

---

6.5.5 Steam traced piping shall be covered with oversized insulation.

6.5.6 Heat traced piping placed in sleeves under road crossings, or similar installations, shall have the insulation extend 300 mm (12") into the sleeve.

6.5.7 Acoustic insulation where required, shall be applied to avoid noise leakage from the major noise source or the noise transmission path. All openings in the insulation covering shall be completely sealed with loose insulation and/or insulating cement. In addition, all metallic pipe supports, support brackets, support plates, gussets, etc., that are connected to the major noise source or transmission pack shall also be acoustically insulated for a distance up to 3 m (10 ft) as required.

## 6.6 Insulation of Instruments

6.6.1 Insulation shall be installed in accordance with the requirements of Specification J-16.

## 6.7 Expansion Joints

Expansion joints shall be provided taking into account the following:

a) The expansion of the piping or equipment, based on the design temperature of the item.

b) The expansion, contraction, or shrinkage of the insulating material, based on the design temperature of the item.

c) The resilience of the insulation.

d) The method of supporting the insulation.

Seller's design of insulation expansion joints, including their location, and type of insulating weatherproofing material used shall be submitted to purchaser for approval.

## 6.8 Electrically Traced Equipment and Piping

6.8.1 Prior to the application of thermal insulation, the insulation contractor shall verify that the type, inside diameter and thickness of the insulation agrees with that shown on the electrical heat tracing isometric.

---

6.8.2 The insulation shall be protected against water intrusion during installation. Jacketing shall be installed immediately after the application of the insulation, and temporary weatherproofing shall be installed for protection if unjacketed insulation must be left overnight. Electrical tracing does not have the capacity to dry out wet insulation.

6.8.3 The insulation shall be applied in a manner so that the electrical heating cables do not become embedded in the insulation. Pre-grooved wraparound insulation shall not be used due to the problem of the heating cable becoming embedded in the grooves and causing cable burnout. Seams in the insulation shall be located as far as possible from the electric tracing cable, to help prevent the cables from becoming embedded in the seam. Extreme care shall be taken during the installation of insulation and weatherproofing, to avoid damage to the heating cable or cold leads. These are fragile, and any break in the outer cable sheath will result in cable failure.

6.8.4 Any problems with the heating cable as installed, which adversely affects the application of insulation and weatherproofing, shall be resolved with Purchaser before proceeding with the installation.

## 6.9 Application of Mastic Coatings

6.9.1 Elastomeric coatings shall be applied in at least two coats to achieve a total dry film thickness of .75 to 1.0 mm (30 to 40 mils). Successive coats shall be in contrasting color to the preceeding coat. Color of final coat to be specified by Purchaser.

6.9.2 Reinforcing fabric used in conjunction with the coating shall be applied without wrinkles, metal fasteners, e.g. staples, shall not be used to hold the fabric.

6.9.3 Immediately after application of the first coat of a multi-coat system and while still wet, a layer of reinforcing fabric shall be pressed smoothly into the coating. Fabric overlap shall be 25 to 50 mm (1 to 2"). After a minimum of 4 hours drying time, the second coating shall be applied.

Double layers of fabric shall be used at joints of attachments, or other configurations, requiring a smooth transition of the weatherproofing coatings.
