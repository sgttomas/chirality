- For cold pipes where Foamglas insulation is desirable, the Foamglas must be placed on first and conventionally sealed, it need not be jacketed, and then the full acoustical pipe lagging system should be installed over the Foamglas. Due to the thermal insulating characteristics that the acoustical pipe lagging's mineral wool provides, the otherwise required Foamglas thickness may be able to be reduced.

- For hot pipes where calcium silicate insulation is desirable, a substitution to mineral wool should be evaluated. Otherwise the calcium silicate must be placed on first, it need not be jacketed, and then the full acoustical pipe lagging system should be installed over the calcium silicate. Due to the thermal insulating characteristics that the acoustical pipe lagging's mineral wool provides, the otherwise required calcium silicate thickness may be able to be reduced.

- For hot pipes where mineral wool insulation is desirable, the acoustical pipe lagging system may provide all of the necessary requirements. If necessary the thickness of the mineral wool may be able to be increased upon review of the noise reduction characteristics required, but it should never be decreased.

- For hot pipes where only personnel protection is required, any of the acoustical pipe lagging systems should suffice.

- Vapor barriers of various types may be used when required, the specific applications of which need to be assessed.

## Resilient Pipe Shoes or Saddles

Resilient pipe shoes or resilient pipe saddles are required at various pipe support locations. The resilient pipe shoes or saddles shall be as provided by others. The locations where these are required are as follows:

- all indoor and all outdoor locations where System A is utilized

- all outdoor locations where System B1 is utilized.

The insulation, septum and jacketing shall be installed in such a way that it does not allow acoustical leakage where it meets the resilient pipe shoes or saddles, without altering the intended operation of the resilient pipe shoes or saddles.

## Treatment Locations

The pipes to be treated with acoustical pipe lagging are presented in Table A-2. A treated pipe includes all in-line elbows, the full through path of tees, valves, flanges, etc. When termination treatment of a pipe as listed on Table A-2 begins or ends at a flange, the flange does not require treatment; except when the treatment begins or ends at a valve or compressor flange, then that component's flange does not require treatment, unless otherwise indicated.

At some identified locations along treated pipes, the acoustical pipe lagging treatment may terminate, and acoustical blankets or removable valve covers may be used for easier accessibility. These locations are indicated on Table A-2, and are to be identified by Bantrel / Suncor.

Any time that the acoustical pipe lagging terminates, a suitable termination technique shall be utilized that both maintains the integrity of the system and provides a moisture and vapor barrier.

Branch lines are generally treated as follows:

- branch lines off of System A treated pipes that are 8" diameter or more are to be fully treated with System B1.
