## ATTACHMENT 1 - ACOUSTICAL PIPE LAGGING SPECIFICATIONS

### Functional Pipe Lagging

An effective acoustical pipe lagging system should perform four functions:

1. *Absorption* of airborne sound
2. Reduction in *transmittance* of airborne sound
3. *Damping* (absorption) of structure-borne (vibration induced) sound
4. *Decoupling* (reduction in transmittance) of structure-borne sound.

A suitable design would incorporate all four acoustical functions. The individual components of such a system would perform the following acoustical functions:

- mineral wool or fiberglass insulation will provide sound absorption, decouple the outer jacketing from the pipe wall, and provide a small degree of damping for the pipe wall
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing will provide a reduction in sound transmittance, and the adhered vinyl will damp out vibratory energy that would otherwise result in the aluminum outer layer becoming a re-radiator of sound
- the septum layer (when used) provides additional mass for additional reduction in sound transmittance.

The acoustical pipe lagging shall be installed in such a way as to meet the intent described above.

### Lagging Systems

Four different pipe lagging systems are specified herein. These are as follows:

**System "A"** shall consist of:

- 1" mineral wool insulation
- impregnated vinyl septum
- 1 1/2" mineral wool insulation
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing;

**Systems "B1" and "B2"** shall consist of:

- 2" mineral wool insulation
- vinyl / aluminum jacketing;

**System "C"** shall consist of:

- 1" mineral wool or fiberglass insulation
- aluminum jacketing.

The differences between Systems B1 and B2 are as follows:

- valves in System B1 are to be treated, as indicated under Treatment Locations
- valves in System B2 do not require treatment, even when piping on each side of the valve is treated.
