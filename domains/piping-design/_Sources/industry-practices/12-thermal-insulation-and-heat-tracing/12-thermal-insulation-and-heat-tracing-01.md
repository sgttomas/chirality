## SECTION 1: GENERAL

### 1.1 SCOPE

1.1.1 BP Cherry Point Refinery shall be hereinafter referred to as BP CHP.

1.1.2 This standard is intended to establish the minimum requirements of BP CHP for thermal insulating materials and their application on external surfaces of piping, vessels, storage tanks and equipment operating between -320°F and 1200°F. For applications above 1200°F the engineering contractor shall provide a proposal for insulating materials and installation methods for review and approval by BP CHP Reliability Engineering. The materials and methods of application described in this standard are for the conservation of heat, conservation of refrigeration, maintenance of operating temperature and protection of personnel.

1.1.3 The following applications of thermal insulation are not within the scope of this standard. Insulation for burners, furnaces, and similar equipment, where refractory or other fire-resistant materials are required, shall be in accordance with BP CHP STP 110. Insulation of buildings or structures for air conditioning or similar purposes shall be in accordance with the local building codes and any specific project design specifications.

1.1.4 Where materials are referred to by trade name in this standard, the use of such names is intended to describe the kind or quality of the material required. Equivalent materials from other manufacturers will be considered when submitted to BP CHP Reliability Engineering for approval.

### 1.2 PROPOSALS

1.2.1 The Contractor's proposal shall be in accordance with this standard.

1.2.2 At the Contractor's option, the basic proposal in accordance with this standard may be supplemented with an alternate proposal providing the following two conditions are met:

1.2.2.1 Significant price or completion date advantages are indicated, or a superior installation is offered at a justifiably higher price.

1.2.2.2 The alternate proposal includes a detailed list of all deviations from this standard.

1.2.3 Removable insulation covers shall be quoted as a separate item since they are often installed after plant start-up.

### 1.3 PREPARATION FOR INSULATION

1.3.1 No insulation shall be applied to any piping, vessels, tanks, or other processing equipment until the equipment has been inspected, tested, painted where applicable, and approved for the intended service. See STP 500 for requirements

---

for painting under insulation. Stainless steel surfaces specified to be insulated and operating above 0°F shall be painted per STP 500 System #6 before insulating, to protect from chloride induced stress corrosion cracking. Carbon steel and low alloy steel surfaces specified to be insulated and operating at 350°F and below (including intermittent operation), shall be painted per STP 500 System #1 before insulating. If there is a need to begin insulation work prior to completion of inspection and testing, written permission shall be obtained from BP CHP.

1.3.2 All surfaces to be insulated shall be cleaned by brushing off all dirt and wiping clean of oil, water, and other foreign matter before any insulation is installed.

1.3.3 All insulation shall be protected from moisture and weather before and during application. If applied insulation should get wet before it is jacketed, it shall be thoroughly dried by heating the line or by other suitable means before it is sealed with weatherproofing. All unjacketed insulation shall be covered with polyethylene sheets daily before leaving the job.

## 1.4 MATERIAL HANDLING, INSPECTION AND STORAGE

1.4.1 All materials shall be subject to inspection and approval by BP CHP.

1.4.2 Any material which has been damaged or improperly installed shall be removed and reinstalled at the Contractor's expense, unless the damage was caused by others.

1.4.3 Inspection and approval of materials and/or workmanship by BP CHP shall not be construed as relieving the Contractor of the Contractor's responsibilities.

1.4.4 Materials shall be delivered to the job site in the manufacturer's original sealed containers.

1.4.5 A storage area will be assigned to the Contractor. The Contractor shall adequately protect all stored materials from damage due to rain, freezing temperature, or any other cause.

1.4.6 Materials shall be prepared for use and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

1.4.7 The insulating Contractor shall guarantee all insulation furnished and installed by the Contractor against defective material and workmanship for a period of one (1) year from the date the equipment is placed in service and the insulating Contractor shall repair or replace promptly, without cost to the prime Contractor or BP CHP, any part or parts of the insulation that falls within said period due to defective material or workmanship. Insulating Contractor's responsibility shall not, however be held to exceed eighteen (18) months from date of completion of the Contractor's work under the contract.

## 1.5 GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

1.5.1 The minimum insulation thickness for all materials shall be 1.5", except as specified in 3.5.9 or unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

1.5.2 For unusual or questionable insulation applications, or for approval of alternate insulation materials not covered by this Standard, consult BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

1.5.3 Insulation thicknesses for economic heat conservation shall be based on the material used and the value of fuel gas. Consult with the BP CHP Energy Engineer to determine the current value of fuel gas, and use that fuel gas value and the specified insulation material to select the applicable insulation thickness table in Section 7 of this standard. The current value of fuel gas may be available on the BP CHP computer network at R:\CHPcomm\CI - Energy Conservation Team\utility calculator.xls.

1.5.4 Insulation thickness for conservation of refrigeration shall be as indicated in Table 3.

1.5.5 Freeze protection insulation shall be a minimum of 1.5" fiberglass for 2" and smaller water lines with continuous flow which are exposed to low ambient temperatures. This applies to bypass lines used to freeze protect cooling water lines. Any portion of water lines with no flow, such as dead legs or ends of headers, shall be heat traced and insulated. Water lines with low flow or intermittent flow shall be evaluated to determine if heat tracing is needed to adequately protect the line from freezing. These requirements do not apply to water lines covered by refinery freeze protection procedures for isolating and draining the lines during freezing weather.

**Note:** When selecting insulation for freeze protection, consider that periodic inspection for CUI may be warranted after the system is in operation. Consult with BP CHP Inspection to determine any requirements for periodic inspection, and specify these requirements in the design documentation for piping and insulation, including the piping line list.

1.5.6 Piping and equipment insulation shall be applied with all voids or open cracks filled with insulating cement, mastic, or caulking material appropriate for the service conditions and temperature.

1.5.7 The preferred weatherproofing on all irregular shaped items is aluminum jacketing. However, appropriate mastic coating with fiber reinforcing mesh or asphalt emulsion coating with fiber reinforcing mesh may be used as specified herein, or with approval of BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

1.5.8 Work shall be performed by skilled technicians in a professional manner.

---

1.5.9 The Contractor shall take adequate precautions to protect adjacent equipment, pavement, platforms, roadways, and other property of BP CHP from drippings, splattering, or other damage. Any damage resulting from failure of preventive measures shall be cleaned or repaired without undue delay at Contractor's expense.

1.5.10 The Contractor shall remove all excess materials, equipment, and debris remaining after completion of each day's work. Also, the job site shall be kept neat throughout the construction project.

1.5.11 Consideration must be given to process temperature and location of lines when determining insulation materials. Calcium silicate insulation should not be used on cold service lines or lines operating at temperatures 350°F and lower. Fiberglass and mineral wool insulation should not be used in areas where the lines may be walked on or stepped on. In low areas, such as lines going through fire banks, where they can be wetted by water or oil leakage, foam glass should be used exclusively. Foam glass should be used on wax lines or on lines in low temperature service.

## 1.6 SPECIAL APPLICATIONS

### 1.6.1 API Standard 521 Fire-Protection Insulation Systems

#### 1.6.1.1

Specific insulation systems and materials are required for applications where thermal insulation in accordance with this standard is intended to provide credit as a fire-protection insulation system in compliance with API Standard 521 for use in calculating PSV relief loads. API 521 compliant fire-protection insulation systems shall be designed and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions, and shall be documented on the P&ID and in the PSV file with design requirements and specific locations. These installations shall be prominently marked in the field as fire-protection insulation, to differentiate from other thermal insulation.

#### 1.6.1.2

Fire-protection insulation systems shall be tested and rated by the manufacturer or other approved independent testing agency to meet all of the following criteria during a fire:

- **1.6.1.2.1** Function effectively at a minimum temperature of 1660°F for a duration of at least 2 hours.

- **1.6.1.2.2** Retain the original insulation shape.

- **1.6.1.2.3** Retain the insulating value.

- **1.6.1.2.4** Retain most of the integrity of the insulation covering on the vessel or pipe.

---

1.6.1.2.5 Not be dislodged by high pressure fire fighting fluid streams.

1.6.1.3 Industrial Insulation Group (IIG) Thermo-12 Gold calcium silicate insulation installed in accordance with IIG technical bulletin TB004 is an acceptable API 521 fire-protection insulation system. Other proposed systems shall be submitted to BP CHP Reliability Engineering for approval. Installation of insulation and weatherproofing shall follow the same practices as specified in sections 3 through 5 of this standard except that jacketing and other hardware material shall be in accordance with paragraph 1.6.2 below. Aluminum jacketing is not acceptable for this type of application. Existing installations of calcium silicate or other insulation materials are not acceptable for API 521 fire-protection insulation.

## 1.6.2 Stainless Steel Jacket For Special Protection

1.6.2.1 In applications where insulation needs better protection for mechanical, fire, or corrosion reasons, and with approval of BP CHP Reliability Engineering, the insulation may be covered with minimum 0.010" thick, smooth finish, annealed ASTM A240 type 304 stainless steel jacketing, instead of corrugated aluminum. The stainless steel jacketing may be upgraded from type 304 to type 316 where needed. Bands and other installation hardware shall also be stainless steel to match the jacket material. The method of applying and securing the jacketing shall be the same as specified in the applicable weatherproofing paragraphs of sections 3 through 5 of this standard.

## SECTION 2: PERSONNEL PROTECTION

## 2.1 GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

2.1.1 Piping systems, equipment, and tanks, which operate with surface temperatures above 140°F either continuously or intermittently, shall be provided with a means of protecting personnel from all applicable hot surfaces. This applies to surfaces, which are less than 7 feet overhead or less than 3 feet horizontal distance from normal work areas, walkways, and edges of platforms, or surfaces which otherwise could be readily touched in the course of normal operating and maintenance duties. When insulation is needed for personnel protection in addition to heat conservation or temperature control, the insulation thickness shall meet the greatest applicable thickness requirement. Methods of protection shall be in accordance with the following requirements, which are dependent on the temperature of the hot surface.

2.1.1.1 For surface temperatures between 140°F and 350°F, the use of insulation for personnel protection is not allowed for new work, except for high alloy material surfaces which are not susceptible to CUI, such as Alloy 825. Consult with the BP CHP Corrosion

---

Engineer to determine other high alloy materials which are not susceptible to CUI. For personnel protection at temperatures 350°F and below, fabricated heat shields or Mascoat insulating coatings are acceptable options. Fabricated heat shields shall be in accordance with Appendix A. Mascoat coatings shall be designed and applied in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions, and shall be applied over a primer coating per STP 500 System #1.

2.1.1.2 For surface temperatures above 350°F, insulation shall normally be used for personnel protection unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering. Insulation materials and thicknesses shall be in accordance with the applicable Personnel Protection Table 1 series of this standard.

2.1.1.3 Where personnel protection is needed for flanges with operating temperatures above 500°F, fabricated heat shields shall be provided per Appendix A. See related information in 3.1.4.

2.1.1.4 Personnel protection shields are not required to be installed on process lines which normally operate at 140°F or below, but are steamed-out at temperatures above 140°F during maintenance outages. In such cases, personnel protection will be addressed during the steam-out portion of maintenance outages with restricted access, barriers, procedures, training, Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), or other means.

## SECTION 3: HOT THERMAL INSULATION

### 3.1 APPLICATION OF INSULATION ON HOT PIPING

3.1.1 Hot piping, operating above ambient temperature, shall be insulated with sectional molded pipe covering applied with all single layer joints tightly butted using staggered joint construction. Adequate provisions shall be made in design and application for expansion and contraction of the material being insulated. Pipe insulation greater than 2-1/2" thickness shall be applied in at least two layers on surfaces above 600°F. All multilayer insulation shall be applied with girth and longitudinal joints staggered such that all joints on the outer layer occur midway between those of the inner layer.

3.1.1.1 Hot piping insulation thicknesses should be specified and shown on the project piping line summary documents and the piping isometric drawings. The insulation thickness shall be based on the operating temperature of the piping to which it is attached, and shall be in accordance with the insulation thicknesses specified in Table 2 series of this standard.

---

3.1.1.2 Piping vent branch connections less than 12" long are not required to be insulated except for personnel protection. All piping drain connections (low points) shall be insulated to prevent freezing.

3.1.2 Each layer of insulation shall be separately wired in place with 16 gauge galvanized soft annealed iron or stainless steel wire on 12" centers, unless the insulation is supplied with a factory applied metal covering.

3.1.3 Expansion joints for single layer application shall be provided on straight run piping and spaced in accordance with chart below. ## 3.2 WEATHERPROOFING OF INSULATION ON HOT PIPING

3.2.1 After piping insulation has been installed in a satisfactory manner and upon approval by BP CHP, the insulation shall be weatherproofed.

3.2.2 Unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering or specified in this standard, pipe insulation shall be covered with 0.016" thick 3/16" corrugated aluminum jacketing with a factory attached moisture barrier. The longitudinal and circumferential joints shall overlap a minimum of 3" and be arranged to shed water. The aluminum jacketing shall be secured in place with 1/2" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel bands spaced at not greater than 12" centers. Bands shall be installed at all circumferential overlaps of the aluminum jacketing. The bands shall be painted blue to show asbestos-free insulation beneath. The cut end of the band at the clip shall be tapped flush against the jacket.

3.2.3 When Mineral Wool insulation is used, the insulation shall be externally protected with a double layer of aluminum jacket on exposed surfaces wherever compressive loads may be experienced, such as from personnel walking on the insulation. The inner layer of jacket shall be stucco-embossed aluminum and the outer layer of jacket shall be corrugated. For Mineral Wool insulation installed on horizontal piping, at least the top third of the outside surface of the insulation shall have a double layer jacket, unless compressive loads are not feasible.

3.2.4 Factory attached jacketing shall be installed with a 2" minimum width seal ring over each circumferential joint. A non-hardening and nonflammable sealer shall be applied between the seal ring and jacketing. Type 18-8 stainless steel bands, 1/2" x 0.020", spaced not greater than 12" centers shall be used to secure jacketing, starting at the seal ring on each circumferential joint. The seal ring shall face down or be self-draining.

3.2.5 All fittings and square cut ends at fittings shall be weatherproofed using 0.020" thick aluminum elbows, tee covers and end caps. Fittings 1-1/2" and smaller which are insulated per paragraph 3.1.7 may be weatherproofed with fiber reinforcing mesh and mastic.

3.2.6 Insulated valves with threaded ends or welded ends shall be weatherproofed with 0.016" aluminum jacketing on the valve body, and aluminum end caps or metallic gray mastic with fiber reinforcing mesh on the valve ends. When mastic with mesh is used it shall be applied in the following manner:

3.2.6.1 Trowel on metallic gray mastic to a thickness not to exceed 1/8".

3.2.6.2 Embed fiber reinforcing mesh into mastic and work to a wrinkle-free finish.

3.2.6.3 When surface is still tacky to the touch, finish with an additional 1/4" thick wet coat of metallic gray mastic. Laps in fiber reinforcing mesh shall be a minimum of 3".

---

3.2.7 Weatherproofing which encloses flanged connections shall be designed to be removed without damage to the insulation and weatherproofing.

3.2.8 Removable covers should be of a weatherproof material and allow for a tight enough fit to seal out water.

## 3.3 APPLICATION OF INSULATION ON HOT EQUIPMENT

3.3.1 This section covers the insulation of hot equipment, operating above ambient temperature, including vessels, machinery, and similar items, but excluding storage tanks which are addressed in section 1.

3.3.2 Vessels that are insulated internally shall not require external insulation.

3.3.3 Block insulation shall be applied with length of block parallel to greatest length of surface being covered. The base course shall be applied with alternate length blocks so that all successive end joints are staggered. Each course shall be banded before the succeeding course is applied. Blocks applied to curved surfaces shall be sized and/or kerfed or scored to limit any gap between surface and blocks to 3/4". Insulation shall be butted together, and all voids shall be filled with insulating cement. Block insulation greater than 2-1/2" thickness shall be applied in double layer construction for all fluid temperatures above 600°F. Joints of the second layer shall be staggered with those of the first. Blocks may also be attached by impaling them on pins welded to the equipment.

3.3.4 On fractionating towers, the thickness of insulation applied on the upper sections of the towers may be less than the insulation thickness at the base, in accordance with the expected decline in the process temperature towards the top.

3.3.5 The base row of insulation for vertical equipment shall rest on a solid metal ring, which is seal welded to the shell and sloped down to the outside for water drainage. A solid metal ring seal welded to the shell and sloped down to the outside shall also be used as applicable at the top of the insulation, 2" below the head to shell seam weld and at any shell transition sections, to prevent water intrusion behind the insulation. All intermediate insulation support rings shall be bolted or welded to clips that are welded to the shell, and shall have a minimum clearance of 0.375" between the shell and the inside edge of the support rings to provide a clear drainage path for any water that gets behind the insulation. The outside radius of intermediate support rings shall be at least 2" less than the outside radius of the insulation. Vertical spacing of insulation support rings shall not exceed 8 feet. Welding of insulation supports on new vessels should be done by the equipment fabricator. No welding shall be done on installed equipment without approval of BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

3.3.6 Equipment insulation shall be secured with type 18-8 stainless steel straps. For equipment 4 feet in diameter and smaller, use 1/2" x 0.020" straps. For equipment larger than 4 feet diameter and up through 8 feet diameter, use 3/4" x 0.020" straps with one 2" long or 4" long 300 series stainless steel flat

---

expansion spring. For equipment larger than 8 feet diameter use 3/4" x 0.020" straps with one 2" long or 4" long 300 series stainless steel flat expansion spring for every 25 feet of band. Strapping shall be spaced on 12" maximum centers.

3.3.7 Expansion Joints:

3.3.7.1 Expansion joints for vertical equipment shall be provided at every insulation support ring or stiffener ring. The expansion space below the support ring shall be packed with loose mineral wool, glass fiber, or ceramic fiber filler.

3.3.7.2 Expansion joints for horizontal equipment shall be provided by Contractor where required.

3.3.7.3 Details of design and location of all expansion joints shall be submitted to BP CHP for approval.

3.3.8 Skirts supporting insulated vertical vessels shall be partially insulated inside and outside with the same insulation system and thickness as the vessel shell. The insulation shall extend downward from the junction of the skirt and the shell a minimum distance of 18" or four times the thickness of the insulation, whichever is greater.

3.3.9 Insulation for top heads of vertical vessels shall be attached in the following way:

3.3.9.1 A 1/4" type 18-8 stainless steel rod or three strands of 16 gauge type 18-8 stainless steel shall serve as a cable float ring. On vessels with a center nozzle, the float ring shall have a maximum clearance of 6" between the float ring and outside diameter of the nozzle. If the vessel head has no center nozzle, the float ring shall be centered on the head and shall have a maximum diameter of one foot.

3.3.9.2 A 3/8" diameter 18-8 stainless steel tie ring supplied by vessel fabricator welded with clips to top side of weather shield angle, shall serve as anchor band.

3.3.9.3 Equipment head insulation shall be strapped down with 3/4" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel radial bands provided with clips at each end. One end of the radial band shall be fastened to the cable float ring and the other end shall be fastened to the anchor band. As an alternative, the insulation may be impaled on type 304 stainless steel pins welded to the equipment.

3.3.10 Insulation for horizontal vessel heads and bottom heads of vertical vessels shall be secured by lacing 18 gauge type 18-8 stainless steel wire through the nuts or studs on maximum 18" centers provided and installed by the vessel fabricator. Wire shall be spaced to secure each block or segment in place.

---

3.3.11 All vessel heads shall receive a 1/2" minimum thick coating of insulating cement finished to a smooth surface. The cement shall cover the flat bands. The cement shall then be coated with a metallic gray breathing type mastic sealer.

3.3.12 When shell sections of exchangers are specified to be insulated, clearance shall be provided at the body flanges to remove the stud bolts. When channel heads are specified to be insulated, use removable covers per section 3.5.

3.3.13 When specified by BP CHP to be insulated, steam ends of reciprocating pumps, steam turbines, and similar mechanical equipment shall be insulated with block insulation and insulating cement per thickness requirements in Table 1 series, unless the equipment is factory insulated. The manner of application shall be similar to that specified for valves and fittings in Section 3.1.7 of this standard. The joints in the blocks shall be beveled to insure tight joints and the surface shall be finished true and smooth with cement. The insulation shall be weatherproofed with one of the following methods: a metal jacket of suitable design; or a breathing type mastic coating with fiber reinforcing mesh; or breathing type asphalt emulsion coating with fiber reinforcing mesh. Weather barrier coatings shall be installed per the manufacturer's instructions.

3.3.14 When hot equipment or machinery is specified to be insulated, refer to section 3.5.1 of this standard for items which require removable insulation covers.

3.3.15 Code stampings, nameplates, and similar items shall remain visible after insulation is applied. Insulation surrounding these items shall be beveled away from the nameplates or markings when necessary.

## 3.4 WEATHERPROOFING OF INSULATION ON HOT EQUIPMENT

3.4.1 After equipment insulation has been applied in a satisfactory manner and upon approval by BP CHP, the insulation shall be weatherproofed.

3.4.2 Aluminum jacketing with a factory applied moisture barrier shall be used for the weatherproofing of hot insulation on equipment shells in horizontal or vertical position. For equipment 5 feet in diameter and smaller, the jacket shall be the same as for hot piping in paragraph 3.2.2. For equipment larger than 5 feet diameter and up through 16 feet diameter, the aluminum jacket shall be 0.024" thick with 1/4" x 1-1/4" corrugations. For equipment larger than 16 feet diameter, the aluminum jacket shall be 0.032" thick with 5/8" x 2-1/2" corrugations.

3.4.3 Expansion joints where required for weatherproofing on vertical vessels shall be located at insulation support rings. Expansion joints for weatherproofing on horizontal vessels shall be located as required.

3.4.4 The aluminum jacketing shall have a 3" overlap on both the circumferential and vertical or horizontal directions. Jacketing shall be installed such that the longitudinal lap joints are staggered at least 6". All lap joints shall be arranged and designed to shed water.

---

3.4.5 The jacketing material shall be cut to provide for a single wrap around the circumference of the equipment shell where possible. When the shell diameter is too large to allow the use of a single wrap, the jacket may be applied in two or more sections with overlap and sealing of joints as specified in paragraph 3.4.4.

3.4.6 Jacketing shall be secured in place by 3/4" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel bands on 12" centers and with a band at each circumferential joint. If necessary, rivets may be used between the bands in order to close gaps in the lap joint. Expansion springs shall be used the same as specified in paragraph 3.3.6.

3.4.7 Horizontal overlaps of jacketing material shall be provided with S-clips spaced 24" on centers. S-clips shall be 0.040" x 3/4" wide 304 stainless steel.

3.4.8 Vessel Heads

#### 3.4.8.1

Bottom heads on vertical vessels and other surfaces not covered with metal jacketing shall be weatherproofed with a metallic gray breathing type mastic coating with fiber reinforcing mesh, or breathing type asphalt emulsion coating with fiber reinforcing mesh. Weather barrier coatings shall be installed per the manufacturer's instructions. After the surface has been prepared as described in paragraph 3.3.11, the surface shall be coated with 1/8" thick metallic gray breathing type mastic and covered with one layer of fiber reinforcing mesh smoothly stretched and fastened to eliminate all bulges and loose spots. A final coat of metallic gray breathing type mastic shall be applied over the fiber reinforcing mesh to a minimum wet thickness of 1/4". The final coat shall be troweled and brushed to a smooth surface free of lumps, indentations, or excessive feathering cracks. The junction between the mastic on the head and the weatherproofing jacket shall be provided with a 0.016" soft aluminum metal shield to provide positive water shed.

#### 3.4.8.2

Top heads on vertical vessels and horizontal vessel heads shall be weatherproofed with pie shaped aluminum segments, fitting the contour of the insulation. Weatherproofing shall be 0.032" thick smooth aluminum jacketing with attached moisture barrier. The aluminum jacketing shall have a 3/4" minimum overlap with crimped edge. Crimped joints shall receive a bead of caulk. Rivets shall be used instead of metal screws to secure aluminum jacketing to weather shield angle. The head to shell lap on horizontal vessels shall be 5" minimum.

3.4.9 Caulking material shall be applied at all possible locations of moisture penetration, such as the intersections of insulation at piping; equipment manways, supports and nozzles; pipe supports and hangers; and other parts protruding through the surface of the finish. The caulking material shall be applied over all intersecting surfaces without leaving pockets or voids, to prevent water infiltration.

---

## 3.5 REMOVABLE INSULATION

3.5.1 When hot piping or equipment is specified to be insulated, the following items shall be insulated with custom fit removable reusable covers, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

3.5.1.1 Flanges and manway covers for vessels and tanks.

3.5.1.2 Heat exchanger channel heads, bonnets, and removable pipe spools.

3.5.1.3 Flanged valves.

3.5.1.4 Flanged joints in VOC services.

3.5.1.5 Mechanical equipment, pumps, and turbines (top half).

3.5.1.6 Inspection openings for piping and equipment.

3.5.1.7 Maintenance access areas for filters and strainers.

3.5.1.8 Steam traps only when manufacturer recommends insulation for the specific type of trap.

3.5.1.9 Other items which require periodic access for maintenance or monitoring.

3.5.2 The removable insulation covers shall be laminate layers consisting of the following components, per material specifications in section 6.7 of this standard.

3.5.2.1 Inner Liner - positioned against the hot surface, as follows:

- For operating surface temperatures 400°F and lower, 17 oz/sy silicone rubber coated heavy duty fiberglass fabric, encapsulating the insulation blanket.

- For operating surface temperatures 401°F through 600°F, 18 oz/sy heat treated fiberglass fabric, encapsulating the insulation blanket.

- For operating surface temperatures 601°F and higher, 304 stainless steel knitted wire mesh.

3.5.2.2 Intermediate Liner - as follows:

- For operating surface temperatures 600°F and lower, intermediate liner is not required.

- For operating surface temperatures 601°F through 750°F, 18 oz/sy heat treated fiberglass fabric, encapsulating the insulation blanket.

- For operating surface temperatures 751°F and higher, stainless steel foil, encapsulating the insulation blanket.

---

3.5.2.3 Insulation Blanket – Non-resinous needled fiberglass or needled ceramic fiber sandwiched between outer jacket and liners.

3.5.2.4 Weatherproofing - 17 oz/sy silicone rubber coated heavy duty fiberglass fabric positioned on the outer surface exposed to the atmosphere.

3.5.3 When the operating temperature of piping, valves, vessels, or equipment is higher than 500°F, removable covers shall not enclose any bolted flange areas or valve packing glands, or extend over the flange bolts. When the operating temperature is 500°F or less, insulation covers shall extend over flanges and bolts and overlap the permanent insulation by at least 4". When fabricating removable covers for flanged joint areas, the cover dimensions shall be adjusted plus or minus to account for the length of the bolts extending out from the back of the flange. The dimension adjustments shall be verified by field measurements of the bolts prior to cover fabrication.

3.5.4 The silicone coated weatherproof material should not come in contact with surfaces at temperatures above 500°F. For operating surface temperatures above 500°F, the inner liner shall come up and around the edges of the cover to prevent the silicone from contacting the hot surfaces.

3.5.5 For flanges in vertical position, a drain tube shall be inserted through the bottom of the removable cover.

3.5.6 At the edges of each section of the insulation cover, the liner shall be sewn, stapled, or secured with hog rings to the outer cover in a manner that totally encapsulates the insulation.

3.5.7 Insulation covers shall be fabricated in sections to fit around projections from the exchanger channel, such as nozzles and lifting lugs.

3.5.8 The covers shall fit properly. There shall be no gaps between the removable covers and the adjacent permanent insulation on pipe, valves, and equipment, unless otherwise specified herein. Braided stainless steel wire drawstrings are permitted to obtain tight fits.

3.5.9 The minimum insulation thickness shall be 2" for operating temperatures above 600°F, and 1" for operating temperatures 600°F and lower. The cold face temperature shall not be higher than 110°F. Insulation covers shall be fabricated in a way to minimize the number of segments.

3.5.10 When removable covers are used on high temperature items which require 4" or thicker insulation, consider the use of a 2 component layer system to make it easier to fabricate and install.

3.5.11 Quilting fasteners shall be used to hold the laminar layers of the insulation covers together. Sufficient fasteners shall be installed to prevent layers from separating.

---

3.5.12 Lacing hooks shall be used to secure insulation cover sections together. Lacing hooks shall be installed at a proper spacing to support the insulation covers.

3.5.13 Each blanket shall be identified with a metal (non-carbon steel) tag giving equipment number and description.

3.5.14 For large and heavy covers, such as used on exchangers heads, lifting handles made from the fiberglass cloth shall be fabricated and secured to the cover to aid installation. The use of ceramic fiber should be considered as an alternate for large covers due to its lower weight.

3.5.15 Removable covers shall be furnished with diagrams indicating the assembly procedure, and each cover segment shall be match marked.

## SECTION 4: COLD THERMAL INSULATION

## 4.1 APPLICATION OF INSULATION ON COLD PIPING

4.1.1 Cold piping, operating in the temperature range of -320°F to ambient, shall be insulated with polyurethane or foam glass in molded semi-circular sections and factory lags or curved segments with all end joints staggered or broken.

4.1.2 Double layer insulation shall be required for temperatures below -50°F and the lateral and longitudinal joints of the second layer staggered from those of the first layer. Adequate provisions shall be made in design and application for expansion and contraction of the material being insulated.

4.1.3 The outside layer of polyurethane pipe insulation shall have a factory attached vapor barrier jacket with a longitudinal seam overlap of 2". The lap of the outside jacket shall be given a coating of nonbreathing type mastic and then pressed into place. The inside layer of insulation shall not require a vapor barrier.

4.1.4 Each layer of insulation shall be separately banded in place with 3/4" wide glass reinforced tape and shall have one band at each edge of the 4" wide seal strip at each joint.

4.1.5 Piping, including pipe, valves, fittings and flanges shall be cleaned and made free of rust, scale, oil, grease or other foreign material, and shall be painted prior to the application of insulation. For operating temperatures from 0°F to ambient, the piping shall be painted per STP 500 System #1. For operating temperatures below 0°F, the type of paint shall be approved by the BP CHP Corrosion Engineer. Piping shall be free of frost and have a dry surface during application of insulation.

4.1.6 Each abutting surface (longitudinal and end joints) shall be given a coat of nonbreathing type mastic on the outer layer.

4.1.7 Pipe insulation shall be square cut, stopped short of studs and nuts on flanged joints to permit their removal from both sides. Clearance shall be equal to the

---

stud length plus 1". Studs and nuts shall be capable of being removed without damage to the insulation. Space shall be filled with loose material, and flanges shall be enclosed in box of same thickness and material as the line.

4.1.8 Vertical lines shall have metal support rings for insulation welded to the pipe. These support rings shall be spaced not more than 20 feet apart and shall have a width 1/2" less than the total thickness of insulation.

4.1.9 Insulation for valves, fittings and flanges shall be prefabricated of the same insulation material as the adjacent pipe insulation. Each abutting surface (longitudinal and end joints) shall be given a complete, thin coat of nonbreathing type mastic.

4.1.10 The minimum thickness of the prefabricated sections or covers must be equal to the thickness specified for the adjacent pipe insulation.

4.1.11 All voids between the prefabricated insulation cover and the fitting shall be filled with a suitable low temperature expansion joint filler.

4.1.12 Pipe hangers or supports shall normally be applied to the outside of the insulation and a suitable reinforcing plate of galvanized steel shall be provided to protect the insulation.

4.1.13 Pipe hangers, brackets and other connections attached directly to the pipe shall be insulated beyond the pipe insulation surface for a minimum distance of 12" or four times the thickness of insulation, whichever is greater. The end of the insulation must be sealed to the pipe hanger or bracket in such a manner as to prevent the entrance of moisture or vapor.

## 4.2 WEATHERPROOFING OF INSULATION ON COLD PIPING

4.2.1 After piping insulation has been installed in a satisfactory manner and upon approval by BP CHP, the insulation shall be weatherproofed.

4.2.2 Pipe insulation shall be covered with 0.016" thick 3/16" corrugated aluminum jacketing with a factory attached moisture barrier. The longitudinal and circumferential joints shall overlap a minimum of 3". The aluminum jacketing shall be secured in place with 1/2" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel bands spaced at not greater than 12" centers. Bands shall be installed at all circumferential overlaps of the aluminum jacketing. Bands shall be painted blue to indicate asbestos-free insulation underneath.

4.2.3 Jacketing on vertical lines shall be installed by starting at the bottom and working upward. Precautions shall be taken to prevent the jacketing from telescoping by employing staples, screws, rivets, or clips.

4.2.4 Jacketing shall overlap nonbreathing type mastic weatherproof coatings by a minimum of 3" at all common joints. If necessary, the nonbreathing type mastic

---

weatherproof coating shall be extended along the pipe insulation in order to meet this requirement.

4.2.5 The finishing and weatherproofing material for all prefabricated insulation covers for valves, fittings and flanges shall be one layer of open weave fiber reinforcing mesh embedded in 1/8" thick (wet) coat of nonbreathing type mastic followed by a 1/4" thick (wet) coat of nonbreathing type mastic. The fiber reinforcing mesh must be sealed to valve bonnets and similar items which protrude through the insulation. A nonbreathing type asphalt based fibrated weather barrier coating with a fiber reinforcing mesh may be used in place of the mastic and mesh.

4.2.6 Openings in the insulation required to permit the ingress or egress of meter leads, instrument connections, or similar items, shall be flashed weathertight using non breathing type mastic and fiber reinforcing mesh.

## 4.3 APPLICATION OF INSULATION ON COLD EQUIPMENT

4.3.1 This section covers the insulation of cold equipment, operating at ambient temperature or below, including vessels, machinery, and similar items. All equipment shall be cleaned and made free of rust, scale, oil, grease or other foreign material, and shall be painted prior to the application of insulation. For operating temperatures from 0°F to ambient, the equipment shall be painted per STP 500 System #1. For operating temperatures below 0°F, the type of paint shall be approved by the BP CHP Corrosion Engineer. The equipment shall be free of frost and have a dry surface during application of insulation.

4.3.2 For the temperature range of -320°F to ambient, the insulation for equipment shall be polyurethane blocks preformed to fit the curvature of small diameter equipment. Where tank or vessel is 12 feet or more in diameter, flat stock can be used. Block shall be applied with length parallel to the greatest length of surface being covered. The base course shall be applied with alternate length block so that all successive end joints are staggered. Each course shall be banded with 3/4" x 0.020" 18-8 stainless steel bands on 6" centers before the succeeding course is applied. Double layer insulation shall have all joints of the second layer staggered from those of the first layer. Adequate provisions shall be made in design and application for expansion and contraction of the equipment being insulated.

4.3.3 Insulation shall be butted together and each abutting surface (longitudinal and end joints) shall be given a coat of nonbreathing type mastic.

4.3.4 Equipment insulation shall be secured in place with type 18-8 stainless steel straps. For equipment 4 feet in diameter or less, use 1/2" x 0.015" straps. For equipment over 4 feet and up through 8 feet diameter, use 3/4" x 0.020" straps with one 2" long or 4" long 300 series stainless steel flat expansion spring. For equipment over 8 feet diameter, use 3/4" x 0.020" straps with one 2" long or 4" long 300 series stainless steel flat expansion spring for every 25 feet of band. Strapping shall be spaced on 12" maximum centers.

---

4.3.5 The base row of insulation for vertical equipment shall rest on a solid metal ring, which is seal welded to the shell and sloped down to the outside for water drainage. A solid metal ring seal welded to the shell and sloped down to the outside shall also be used as applicable at the top of the insulation, 2" below the head to shell seam weld and at any shell transition sections, to prevent water intrusion behind the insulation. All intermediate insulation support rings shall be bolted or welded to clips that are welded to the shell, and shall have a minimum clearance of 0.375" between the shell and the inside edge of the support rings to provide a clear drainage path for any water that gets behind the insulation. The outside radius of intermediate support rings shall be at least 1/2" less than the outside radius of the insulation. Vertical spacing of insulation support rings shall not exceed 5 feet. Welding of insulation supports on new equipment should be done by the equipment fabricator. No welding shall be done on installed equipment without approval of BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

4.3.6 Insulation bands and girdles shall be machine stretched and sealed.

4.3.7 Alternate methods for securing insulation to equipment, such as welded studs, will be given consideration, providing full details and justification of the proposed alternate methods are furnished to BP CHP Reliability Engineering for approval.

4.3.8 Skirts supporting insulated equipment shall be partially insulated inside and outside with the same insulation system and thickness as the equipment. The insulation shall extend downward from the junction of the skirt and the equipment a minimum distance of 18" or four times the thickness of the insulation, whichever is greater.

4.3.9 Equipment support legs, cradles, structural steel members, hangers and other connections attached directly to the equipment or machinery shall be insulated beyond the equipment or machinery insulation surface for a minimum distance of 18" or four times the thickness of the insulation, whichever is greater. The end of the insulation must be sealed to the attachment in such manner as to prevent the entrance of moisture or vapor.

4.3.10 Insulation shall be square cut, stopped short of studs and nuts on flanged connections to allow for bolt removal. The end of the insulation shall be covered with 1/16" coat (wet) of nonbreathing type asphalt based fibrated coating. Studs and nuts shall be capable of being removed without damage to the insulation. Space shall be filled with loose material and flanges shall be enclosed in box of same thickness and material as equipment.

4.3.11 Side-wall insulation on horizontal equipment and machinery shall extend past the head by an amount equal to the thickness of the insulation so that the head insulation will fit flush and inside the extended side-wall insulation.

4.3.12 Unless otherwise specified, access openings and flanged connections shall be fully insulated.

---

4.3.13 Full insulation thickness shall be applied over all support rings, stiffeners and other projections on the surface of machinery and equipment.

4.3.14 All spaces between block or lagging insulation and equipment or machinery shall be completely filled and packed with granular insulation.

4.3.15 Vessel identification plates shall be attached on an uninsulated portion of the vessel.

## 4.4 WEATHERPROOFING OF INSULATION ON COLD EQUIPMENT

4.4.1 After equipment insulation has been installed in a satisfactory manner and upon approval by BP CHP, the insulation shall be weatherproofed.

4.4.2 A vapor barrier coating shall be applied over all insulating block before it is covered with aluminum jacketing, as follows: One layer of fiber reinforcing mesh embedded in 1/8" thick (wet) coat of nonbreathing type mastic, followed by a 1/4" thick (wet) coat of nonbreathing type mastic. The fiber reinforcing mesh must be sealed to all nozzles and similar items which protrude through the insulation.

4.4.3 Equipment insulation shall be covered with aluminum jacketing with a factory attached moisture barrier. The jacketing shall be the same as specified in Section 3.4.2. The longitudinal and circumferential joints shall overlap a minimum of 3". All lap joints shall be arranged to shed water.

4.4.4 Jacketing shall be secured in place by 3/4" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel bands on 12" centers and with a band at each circumferential joint. If necessary, rivets may be used between the bands in order to close gaps in the lap joint. Expansion springs shall be used the same as specified in paragraph 3.3.6.

4.4.5 The jacketing material shall be cut to provide for a single wrap around the circumference of the equipment shell where possible. When the shell diameter is too large to allow the use of a single wrap, the jacket may be applied in two or more sections with overlap and sealing of joints as specified in paragraph 3.4.4.

4.4.6 The aluminum jacketing shall be attached to machinery or irregular shaped equipment by rivets, S-clips, and banding. Banding shall be located at laps and at 12" maximum centers. Rivets shall be used at vertical seams only, with a maximum spacing of 4" between rivets. The weatherproof cover shall fit without gap at all lap joints. If necessary, supplemental rivets shall be used to close any gap between sheets.

4.4.7 Openings in the insulation required for penetrations of steam tracer lines, meter leads, instrument connections, platform and ladder supports, or similar items, shall be flashed watertight using nonbreathing type mastic and fiber reinforcing mesh.

## SECTION 5: HOT THERMAL INSULATION - STORAGE TANKS

### 5.1 APPLICATION OF INSULATION TO HOT STORAGE TANKS

**5.1.1** Insulation thickness for hot storage tanks, operating above ambient temperature, shall be in accordance with that specified in the economic insulation thickness tables. For tank surfaces that require insulation for heat conservation and personnel protection, the insulation thickness shall meet the greatest applicable thickness requirement.

**5.1.2** The first course of insulation at the base of tanks shall be a material impervious to water and shall be a minimum of 2 feet in height. It shall rest on an angle or base ring located at grade.

**5.1.3** Panel, blanket or block insulation shall be applied with the greatest length in the vertical. The first course of insulation after the base course shall be applied with alternate length sections so that all successive end joints are staggered.

**5.1.4** The tank insulation shall be secured in place by one of the following methods.

- **5.1.4.1** Attached to 3/8" Nelson Welding Studs or equal, 18-8 stainless steel, length depending on insulation thickness, with 3/8" 18-8 stainless steel nuts, neoprene washers and grommets. Welding studs shall be located on each corner of the sheet. Additional studs shall be placed so that there will be a maximum distance of two feet between studs on the short side of the sheet and a maximum distance of four feet between studs on the long side of the sheet. Weld studs or weld procedure per paragraph 5.2.5.3 may also be needed. Contractor shall demonstrate to BP CHP's Representative that the proposed stud welding system and each person doing the welding can consistently produce weld strength equal to 80% of the stud's strength. The demonstration shall be by welding to the actual tank in the field or welding to a test plate under the following conditions:

  - **5.1.4.1.1** Test plate surface shall be prepared to duplicate the tank surface preparation for welding.

  - **5.1.4.1.2** Test welds shall be made through clearance holes in an insulation panel. The leads on the welding gun for the test welds shall be the maximum length of leads (hot lead and ground lead) required to install the most remote field stud weld.

  - **5.1.4.1.3** After attempting to produce satisfactory test welds, no changes in total length of leads or in weld control settings shall be made without repeating the test weld procedure.

---

5.1.4.2 Same as above in 5.1.4.1 except using 10 gage Nelson Single Pointed Insulation Weld Pins or equal, with self-locking washers, 18-8 stainless steel, length depending on insulation thickness, spaced on a maximum of two foot centers.

5.1.4.3 Secured in place with 3/4" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel straps, placed on 12" maximum centers. Each strap shall be installed with expansion springs, as described in Paragraph 3.3.6.

5.1.5 Insulation at flanged nozzles and similar attachments shall be square cut to allow removal of bolts and nuts from flanges without damage to insulation or weatherproofing.

5.1.6 Insulation shall be trimmed for tight fit and all exposed surfaces at cut-outs shall be protected with 1/4" thick metallic gray breathing type mastic, before installing corrugated aluminum sheets.

5.1.7 Insulation shall be butted up against the seal ring or watershed angle located at the top of the tank. Flashing shall be attached to the roof and turned down a minimum of 3" so that the final row of insulation and weatherproofing fit under the flashing.

5.1.8 Tank roofs which are specified to be insulated may be covered with any of the suitable materials used for tank shells. Panels, blankets or blocks may be applied to the roof with an adhesive or any other method approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering.

5.1.9 Manways shall be insulated with removable reusable covers. Insulation shall be applied in such a manner that the manway cover can be removed without damage to the insulation or weatherproofing.

5.1.10 Code stampings, nameplates, and similar items shall remain visible after insulation is applied. Insulation surrounding these items shall be beveled away from the nameplates or markings when necessary.

## 5.2 WEATHERPROOFING OF INSULATION ON HOT STORAGE TANKS

5.2.1 After tank insulation has been applied in a satisfactory manner and upon approval by BP CHP, the insulation shall be weatherproofed.

5.2.2 Corrugated aluminum jacketing with a factory applied moisture barrier shall be used for the weatherproofing of hot insulation on tank shells. For tanks 25 feet in diameter and smaller, the shell jacket shall be 0.024" thick with 1/4" x 1-1/4" corrugations. For tanks larger than 25 feet diameter, the shell jacket shall be 0.040" thick with 5/8" x 2-1/2" corrugations. Smooth 0.032" thick aluminum jacketing with a factory applied moisture barrier shall be used for weatherproofing of hot insulation on tank roofs.

---

5.2.3 Aluminum jacketing shall have a 3" overlap in both the vertical and horizontal directions. Jacketing shall be installed such that the longitudinal joints are staggered at least 6" or two corrugations, whichever is greater. A double row of screws is required to close the longitudinal lap.

5.2.4 All lap joints shall be arranged and designed to shed water.

5.2.5 Aluminum jacketing for the tank shell and roof shall be attached by one of the following methods:

5.2.5.1 Attached to 18-8 stainless steel welding studs which are fastened to the tank shell or roof as described in paragraph 5.1.4.1. Weatherproof covering shall fit without gaps at all lap joints. Supplemental screws shall be used to close any gap between sheets to provide a maximum of 6" centers between studs and screws.

5.2.5.2 Attached by 18-8 stainless steel No. 10 minimum size screws to preformed channels or angles which are fastened to the tank shell and roof. The preformed channels or angles shall be fastened to the tank shell or roof by positive means, such as by welding or by welding studs. It is not acceptable to use bands to attach the channels or angles to the tank, unless welding to the tank is not permitted and a specific attachment plan is approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering. Screws shall be located on each corner of the sheet and on both the long side and short side of the sheet on each ridge with a maximum distance of 6" between screws. Weatherproof covering shall fit without gaps at all lap joints. If necessary, supplemental screws shall be used to close any gap between sheets.

5.2.5.3 Secured in place on the shell only with 3/4" x 0.020" type 18-8 stainless steel bands spaced on maximum 18" centers, with a band always required on circumferential lap joints. Each band shall be installed with expansion springs, as described in paragraph 3.3.6. Horizontal overlaps of jacketing shall be provided with S-clips for support, with two clips per sheet of jacketing or a maximum of 4 foot spacing on centers. S-clips shall be 0.040" x 3/4" wide 304 stainless steel. Aluminum anchor bars 1/4" x 2" shall be used to secure the banding over the jacketing, and shall be drilled to suit and aligned vertically over Nelson Welding Studs specified below. A minimum of two anchor bars are required for each jacket panel. The anchor bars shall be secured with 3/8" Nelson Welding Studs or equal, 18-8 stainless steel, 3" long, with 3/8" 18-8 stainless steel nuts, neoprene washers and grommets. The studs shall be spaced vertically on 24" centers in columns, and the columns shall be spaced around the tank on maximum 40 foot centers.

5.2.6 All joints for roof weatherproofing shall overlap a minimum of 3".

---

5.2.7 A heavy coating of caulking material, minimum 1/16" wet thickness, shall be placed between the lap joints.

5.2.8 Soft aluminum 0.020" thick flashing shall be installed over the roof insulation to cover the edges of the insulation and extend downward over the tank seal ring or water shed angle.

5.2.9 All openings in the weatherproof covering, which are necessary for penetration of gauge hatches, vents, pipe connections, platform supports, and similar items, shall be flashed weathertight.

## SECTION 6: MATERIALS

## 6.1 GENERAL MATERIAL REQUIREMENTS AND PREFERENCES

6.1.1 Do not deviate from the materials specified in this standard without approval from BP CHP Reliability Engineering. Proposals to use alternate materials shall be submitted to BP CHP Reliability Engineering for consideration and approval. Justification for using the alternate materials shall be included in the proposal.

6.1.2 Perlite silicate insulation shall not be used for any services, due to excessive breakage experienced during shipping and handling.

6.1.3 Calcium silicate insulation shall not be used for services 350°F and below, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering, due to susceptibility to corrosion under insulation.

6.1.4 Mineral wool insulation shall not be used for services above 900°F, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering, due to material deterioration at high temperatures over time.

6.1.5 For services from ambient temperature up through 350°F, mineral wool is normally the preferred insulation material, due to lower absorbency of water resulting in better protection from corrosion under insulation.

6.1.6 For services 351°F and above, calcium silicate is normally the preferred insulation material, due to higher compressive strength and better durability.

6.1.7 For small diameter piping where the insulation will not be subject to compressive loads, fiberglass is an acceptable insulation material. This includes insulation for freeze protection of small water lines.

---

6.1.8 Where heavy density fiberglass insulation is selected for pipe, insulation material wrapped with a vapor jacket is preferred.

6.1.9 Insulation for stainless steel piping, equipment, and tanks requires special material requirements per section 6.8.

## 6.2 MATERIAL QUALITY

6.2.1 Insulation and accessory materials shall be new and shall be utilized before any expiration date. Mastics, coatings, sealants, and similar materials shall be supplied in factory sealed containers.

6.2.2 Insulation will be judged defective and returned at Seller's expense for any of the following reasons: (a) if there are cracks extending completely through the insulation; (b) if there are cracks which would cause insulation to break upon sawing or gentle flexing; (c) if the ends are broken or damaged; (d) more than 10% of the surface area of the insulation is composed of large voids; and (e) if any large void has a depth greater than 10% of the insulation thickness.

6.2.3 Preformed pipe and vessel insulation shall be fabricated in conformance to ASTM C450. Multilayer pipe insulation shall be ordered factory-nested when available for the material being used.

6.2.4 Asbestos shall not be used as a component in the manufacture of insulation and accessory materials. Insulation shall be color coded in some manner to indicate that the material contains no asbestos.

## APPENDIX A: HEAT SHIELDS FOR PERSONNEL PROTECTION

**Note:** The following approach is a modified version of that developed at the BP Carson Refinery. This is one acceptable approach to accomplish personnel protection, however alternative approaches will be considered. Proposals for use of alternate methods shall be submitted to BP CHP Reliability Engineering for review and approval. Proposals shall include adequate information for a technical evaluation, such as written descriptions, sketches/drawings, and calculations.

## A1 SCOPE

A1.1 Personnel protection heat shields in accordance with this Appendix shall be installed when required by section 2.1.1 of this standard. For each installation, it is the responsibility of the project manager/engineer to assure that the heat shield surface temperature when in service will be 140°F or lower, either through thermal calculations or by physical testing.

A1.2 This specification describes use of expanded metal or perforated metal for personnel protection at hot surfaces. Expanded metal is preferred and shall be used unless approved otherwise by Reliability Engineering. The preference for expanded metal is due to improved inspection visibility, improved drainage and drying after wet weather, and improved air flow for cooling the hot surface.

## A2 TERMS AND DEFINITIONS

A2.1 **Pexto Tool:** One of many sheet metal bending hand tools such as pipe crimpers and hand seamers manufactured by Roper Whitney of Rockford, Inc. Rockford, IL.

A2.2 **Stitching Screw:** A hex head self drilling screw for sheet metal applications.

A2.3 **Stud Gun:** A tool that uses an electric arc or capacitor discharge to melt the end of a stud or pin to base metal to form a bond. The stud or pin is plunged into the molten metal and the bond is formed.

## A3 PERSONNEL PROTECTION DESIGN AND INSTALLATION

A3.1 Areas that require personnel protection shall be covered with aluminum or stainless steel metal shields which are offset a minimum of 2.5" from the hot surface. Expanded metal shall be flattened type, and shall be 1/2" .051 style for aluminum and 1/2" #18 style for stainless steel. Perforated metal plate shall have 1/2" holes and shall be 0.063" thickness for aluminum and 20 gauge for stainless steel. The shield shall be adequately supported by corrugated aluminum according to drawing LB-000-0100-0100-002 and/or by metal pins as specified in Section A3.2.

**Note:** Stainless steel shields should be reserved for particularly corrosive atmospheres.

---

A3.2 When metal pins are used to support the shield, the pins shall be spot welded to the pipe or equipment. The pins shall be 1/4" diameter and minimum 2.5" long with one end threaded. Longer pins may be required to achieve the required 140°F shield surface temperature. The pin material shall be stainless steel for all carbon steel, low-alloy, and stainless steel piping. For high alloy piping, pin material shall be approved by BP CHP Reliability Engineering. The following describes approximate pin locations to adequately support metal sheet. Field shall install additional support pins as needed to provide a solid shield surface.

A3.2.1 In the longitudinal direction on pipe, pin spacing should be approximately 18"-24".

A3.2.2 For pipe less then or equal to 6" nominal diameter, a minimum of four pins should be installed equally spaced around the circumference.

A3.2.3 For pipe greater than 6" nominal diameter, approximately 8 pins should be installed around the circumference. For large pipe, consideration should be given to using additional pins for adequate support.

A3.2.4 For equipment other than pipe, pin spacing shall be adequate to support the metal sheet. Pin spacing should not exceed 24" in any direction.

A3.3 Pipe or equipment covered by metal shielding shall be coated per STP 500. Coating should be completed after spot welding when possible. Otherwise the existing coating shall be repaired at all spot weld locations after pin attachment and prior to shield installation. Coating repair shall also comply with STP 500.

A3.4 A 1-1/2" diameter 1/8" thick stainless steel disc shall be attached to the end of the pin using a stainless steel nut that is spot welded to the disc. A 1/8" hex head stitching screw with oversized washer shall then be used to secure the metal shield to the disc.

A3.5 When installed on pipe, a metal shield end cap shall be used to terminate the screen which typically occurs near a flange. This end cap should be formed with a Pexto sheet metal tool inserted onto the end of the screen. An insulation band shall then be used to secure the end cap to the screen using stitching screws thru the insulation band and cap.

A3.6 When metal shielding is installed on equipment where an end cap is not practical, such as a pressure vessel, the ends of the perforated metal shall be folded inward toward the equipment to prevent leaving a sharp edge exposed.

A3.7 Flanges, valves, instruments and other devices that require access for servicing or removal shall be covered with easily removable metal shield bands, or protected by expanded metal barriers with adequate stand-off to allow access.

A3.8 Hot flanges with surface temperatures greater than 500°F, which are not insulated to prevent bolt relaxation, shall have a special flange heat shield installed per drawing LB-000-0100-0100-001.

---

## A4 STUD WELDING

**A4.1** Spot welding shall be done with a Nelson stud gun or equivalent. The capacitor discharge process is required as this process minimizes heat input and therefore depth of penetration. Any other process such as electric arc, stored arc or gas arc requires BP CHP Reliability Engineering approval.

**A4.2** Minimum heat input shall be used to attach the pins to pipe and equipment to minimize depth of penetration of the heat affected zone. The method to be used should be tested by striking the pin with a hammer and reducing heat input until the pin connection fails, then increasing heat input just enough to produce a sound weld.

**A4.3** When stud welding on in-service pipe and equipment, the provisions of STP 42B and all Control of Work procedures shall be followed.

**A4.4** When stud welding on code piping and equipment, including piping and equipment subject to PWHT, the welding procedure shall be the capacitor discharge process without PWHT and shall be qualified in accordance with QW-192 of ASME Section IX (Stud Weld Procedure Qualification).

**Note:** An EWR (work process described in STP 42B) is required for all in-service welding (see S&H manual 520 - Section II), and all new and retrofit installations.
