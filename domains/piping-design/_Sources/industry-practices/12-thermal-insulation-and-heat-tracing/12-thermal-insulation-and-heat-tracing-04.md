## 1.0 SCOPE

1.1 This specification covers the design criteria for the hot service thermal insulation, acoustic insulation on piping and equipment such as vessels, heat exchangers, drums, small diameter tanks, heaters, pumps, compressors, turbines and filters, etc.

1.2 The use of insulation for instrumentation, heating, ventilating and air conditioning systems (HVAC), and internally refractory lined piping and equipment is not within the scope of this specification.

1.3 Hot service insulation system shall be considered for:

- Conservation of heat.
- Protection of personnel from heat.
- Maintenance of temperature for process stability.
- Protection of process fluids from freezing (winterization)
- Conservation of heat for heat traced systems (Reference –Specs L-10 and P-24).
- Insulation systems used as fireproofing.
- Noise attenuation

## 2.0 ABBREVIATIONS AND DEFINITIONS

2.2 Definitions

2.2.1 MINERAL FIBER – Mineral Wool

2.2.2 PIPE/PIPING – Cylindrical shapes with outside diameters of 24 inch NPS or smaller which can be insulated with preformed sectional pipe insulation materials. Subject to the size limitation of 24 inches outside diameter, this category includes piping with or without heat tracing, pipe fittings, valves, flanges, in-line pipe accessories, vessels, heat exchangers, piping, and process equipment includes attachments such as nozzles.

2.2.3 FLANGES – Flanges of PIPE or Equipment.

---

2.2.4 EQUIPMENT – Cylindrical shapes of more than 24 inches outside diameter for which preformed sectional pipe insulation materials are not suitable. This category includes items such as pressure vessels, heat exchangers, piping and process equipment including attachments such as nozzles.

2.2.5 OPERATING TEMPERATURE in this specification refers to the internal fluid or material temperature operating conditions as noted on the P&IDs, LDT and/or drawings. Where a range is noted for the operating temperature, the upper limit of the range shall be considered as the operating temperature for hot service. Temperatures reached during upset conditions and steam-out temperature shall not be considered as the operating temperature. Steam-out temperature and heat tracer temperature shall not be considered as a basis for the insulation thickness selection for traced pipe and equipment.

## 4.0 MATERIALS OF INSULATION

4.1     Materials and installation of insulation, metal jacketing, fasteners and thermal insulating cement shall be in accordance with Specification N-1 except for the chloride limits specified in paragraph 4.2 of this specification.

4.2     In cases where the piping or equipment to be insulated is constructed of austenitic stainless steel materials, the insulating materials shall be kept to a maximum leachable chloride content of 10 ppm to mitigate the potential for external stress corrosion cracking (ESCC) due to chloride leaching. This includes all wicking-type insulations, including calcium silicate, fiberglass, mineral fiber and perlite and shall also include any cements or mastics in contact with the stainless steel.

4.3     Manufacturer / Vendor shall have a QA/QC program which positively guarantees that the insulating materials for austenitic stainless steel contain a maximum leachable chloride content of 10 ppm.

4.4     Austenitic 321 stainless steel piping shall only be insulated with perlite.

## 6.2 Insulation Requirements

**6.2.1** The basic requirements for insulating equipment and piping are defined as follows:

a) Heat Conservation – Economically optimum insulation based on insulation and energy costs.

b) Process Stability – Insulation required to control process temperature for stable process operation.

c) Winterization – The protection of piping and equipment against the effect of low ambient temperature including hydrate formation, freezing and pour point suppression.

d) Fireproofing for equipment when specified as defined in Specification UE-1 N-3.

e) Personnel Protection – Insulation shall generally be applied to hot surfaces operating above 60°C (140°F) which can be contacted by operating personnel in normal performances of their duties. Such hot surfaces shall be insulated to a height 2m (6 feet – 6 inches) above normal walking or standing surfaces and within 0.8m (2 feet), measured horizontally, from stairways, ramps, or ladders.

---

The use of insulation to provide personnel protection shall be minimized and preference should be given to the use of guards, shields or railings.

f) Acoustical Insulation – When noise reduction is required to attain permissible levels (see section 6.6.9).

6.2.2 Unless specified otherwise, internally insulated equipment shall not be externally insulated for personnel protection, even though their surface temperatures exceed 60°C (140°F). Where personnel protection is required, guards, shields, or railings shall be provided.

6.2.3 Acoustic service insulation systems shall be used on external surfaces of equipment and piping as required by the limits set in Specifications A-4 and/or A-9

6.2.4 Items requiring insulation for noise control shall be insulated with fibrous glass for temperatures up to 204°C (400°F) and Mineral Fiber with phenolic binder from 204°C (400°F) to 454°C (850°F).

6.2.5 Valves and flanges shall not be insulated in hydrogen service or in the following toxic services: caustic, chlorine, ammonium polysulphide, carbon disulphide, toluene, Diethanolamine, Benfield solution, sulphuric acid or hydrogen sulphide. The contractor shall review the toxicity of the material to be handled with the Syncrude Industrial Hygiene group if further clarification is required.

6.2.6 External flange insulation shall be restricted to applications meeting all of the following conditions:

Max. Fluid Operating Temperature 290°C (550°F) (1)

Max. Design Pressure 4140 kPa (ga) (600psig) (1)

Flange Material C.S, or Low Alloy Steel (to 5% Cr)

Bolt Material Grade B7, B16

Insulation Details (2) (3) (4)

Notes:

1) Steam service flanges may be insulated for temperatures up to a maximum fluid operating temperature of 400°C (750°F) and a maximum design pressure of 4480 kPag (650 psig).

2) Insulation systems around flanged joints shall be provided with a NPS ½ drain tube or vent (tell tale). Drains and vents shall be piped to a safe location, for operator safety and to prevent direct impingement on adjacent equipment, for the following services: highly corrosive, flammable and combustible fluids with operating

temperatures above their flash points, light hydrocarbons [lighter than 68° API (0.709 kg/dm³ density at 15°C and 101.325 kPag)].

For hydrocarbon services, the flange periphery shall, in addition, be fitted with a leak bank.

3) Metal jacket weatherproofing is required.

4) The design shall permit removal of the insulation system without destruction of the insulation on the connecting piping.

6.2.7 Insulation over valves and flanges (and manholes when specified to be insulated) shall be customized assembly for easy removal and replacement. It shall extend a minimum of 50mm (2 inches) over adjacent jacketing.

6.2.8 Seal pots, vent chambers and drip pots shall be insulated only if they are heat traced.

6.2.9 Instruments and associated piping shall be insulated according to Specification J-16.

6.2.10 Inverted bucket type steam traps shall be insulated if installed outside.

6.3 Items not Insulated

Insulation systems designed for heat conservation shall have the insulation omitted from the following surfaces:

6.3.1 Piping or equipment components designed on the basis of reduced metal temperature resulting from heat loss to atmosphere.

6.3.2 Piping or equipment which are insulated internally.

6.3.3 Water side surfaces of coolers and condensers.

6.3.4 Cooling water piping, except bypass which shall be traced and insulated.

6.3.5 Steam traps (except inverted bucket type), expansion joints, rotating joints.

6.3.6 Body flanges of heat exchangers.

6.3.7 Unless specified to be fireproofed, or insulated to afford process protection against the effects of ambient temperature (viscosity change, condensation, etc), for steam service or for noise control, insulation shall also be omitted from the following:

a) Pumps turbines and compressors at operating temperatures below 120°C (250°F).

b) Flanged fittings, unions.

---

c) Lines which carry a fluid which is normally not flowing.

- Snuffing steam lines beyond the last block valve.
- Blowdown lines from pressure relief devices except those on boilers.

d) Vessels such as hot catalyst hoppers which are normally at ambient temperature, but which are heated above 65°C (150°F) for short periods during catalyst removal operations.

## 6.4 Insulation and Weatherproofing for Electrical Heat Tracing Systems

This section covers the mandatory additional requirements for the design of insulation and weatherproofing for EHT systems and is intended to supplement the requirements of the previous paragraphs of this specification.

**6.4.1** No equipment or fitting whatever is permitted to be left bare, even though this may be inconvenient for maintenance or operations. The only exception shall be due to safety concerns.

**6.4.2** Unless prohibited by paragraph 6.2.5 all flanges shall be completely insulated.

**6.4.3** Insulation on valves shall cover the valve bonnet, unless in services listed in paragraph 6.2.5.

**6.4.4** Equipment that must have the insulation removed for maintenance and/or operations purposes shall have removable enclosures, sectionalized with latch type fasteners, or alternatively, blanket type custom enclosures with Velcro fasteners. Enclosures shall be designed with gaskets or other means to prevent the ingress of water and to facilitate the repeated removal and reinstallation without deterioration.

**6.4.5** Additional insulation thickness shall be provided for enclosures on valves/flanges/pumps etc. due to the increased heat loss over the larger surface area of the enclosure. On large enclosures (e.g. on pumps) with large air voids inside the enclosure, the additional insulation shall be applied directly on the traced equipment to concentrate the heat from the electric tracing cables. Foil back fiberglass insulation with tie wire is acceptable for this purpose.

**6.4.6** All pipes having skin effect or steam tracing shall have their insulation oversized by 25 mm (1 inch). In the event that dual tube skin effect tracing is selected, the insulation shall be oversized by 50 mm (2 inches).

## 6.5 Material Selection for Insulation

**6.5.1** Mineral fiber insulation may be used in all areas of the plant. Insulation for piping shall be made from mandrel wound type.

**6.5.2** Mineral fiber shall be acceptable for operating temperatures up to 454°C (850°F) and calcium silicate up to 649°C (1200°F).

**6.5.3** Calcium silicate insulation shall be used where fireproofing is required on vessels. Double layered insulation with staggered joints and stainless steel jacket is mandatory. Thickness of insulation for fireproofing will be 4 inches (100 mm) minimum.

**6.5.4** Fiberglass type insulation shall not be used over tanks or equipment handling penetration grade asphalt, where spillage or leakage onto the insulation is probable.

**6.5.5** In other areas, mineral fiber or fiberglass may be substituted, based on economics or design requirements.

**6.5.6** Cellular glass insulation shall be used on piping at or below grade where exposure to water precludes the use of wicking-types of insulation, provided that the maximum operating temperature of the line is below 204°C (400°F). Cellular glass insulation may be used between 204°C (400°F) and 399°C (750°F) provided that the cellular glass material is protected against break-up due to thermal shock, by applying a layer of Owens Corning "Temp-Mat" or equal insulation, 13mm (1/2 inch) thick to the pipe surface followed by the cellular glass insulation. Alternatively, lines which are in constant hot service above 204°C (400°F) may be insulated with calcium silicate material.

**6.5.7** Acoustic insulation may be used as thermal insulation systems. The use of mineral fiber and fibrous glass in acoustic insulation are to be compatible with the thermal design required for the system.

**6.5.8** Refractory ceramic fiber shall not be used as an insulation material.

**6.5.9** Fireproofing materials for piping, equipment and structural steels shall be in accordance with Specification N3.

## 6.6 Insulation Thickness

**6.6.1** For non-traced applications. The normal operating (fluid) temperature shall be used to establish the required heat conservation insulation thickness for equipment. For piping, normal operating temperature on the LDT shall be used. The fluid operating temperature shall be determined from the upstream equipment to which the piping is connected. Anticipated start-up and shutdown conditions, including steamout, process upsets, regeneration, etc. need not be considered in establishing insulation thickness requirements.

---

6.6.2 For traced applications, insulation thickness will be based on the HOLD or personnel protection temperatures shown on the LDT's and NOT the normal operating temperature, unless the heat conservation insulation thickness required by the normal operating temperature is thicker.

6.6.3 ETI required for heat conservation shall be in accordance with Table 3 and 4 of this specification. Insulation thickness for winterization or heat traced piping shall be in accordance with Table 5 and 6. Where process stability requires a greater thickness of insulation than that listed in the tables then the required thickness shall be calculated by the Process Engineer.

6.6.4 When insulation function is used both for heat conservation and heat tracing or process stability and heat tracing, insulation thickness shall be the thicker of that required for heat conservation based on normal operating temperature or that required for heat tracing based on the hold temperature.

6.6.5 The type of insulation (calcium silicate or mineral fiber) shall be selected based on the normal operating temperature.

6.6.6 The thickness of insulation required for personnel protection shall be in accordance with Table 2. If the requirement of heat conservation is associated with personnel protection, the thickness of insulation will be determined by the more stringent requirement.

6.6.7 When a dual insulation system is used for combined thermal and acoustic control, the insulation for acoustic control shall be installed as the outer layer. No credit for noise reduction may be taken for any insulation material in the system other than the fibrous glass or mineral fiber unless approved by the Purchaser.

6.6.8 Insulation thickness requirements for items requiring insulation for process, personnel protection and noise control reasons shall be in accordance with the system requiring the greater thickness.

(Fibrous Glass 6-8 lb/ft³ ; Mineral Fiber 8-10 lb/ft³)

6.6.9 Acoustic insulation system thickness shall be based on the actual noise reduction required to attain the permissible levels as required in Specification A-4 and A-9. Insulation noise reduction shall be considered on an octave band basis in accordance with Table 1.

6.6.10 Insulation thicknesses for perlite shall be per the calcium silicate insulation thickness Tables 4 & 6.

## 6.7 Jacketing

### 6.7.1

Stainless steel jacketing is required for the following insulation systems and application:

a) Any insulation system on equipment constructed of low melting point alloys and located within a designated fire-hazardous area as defined in Specification UE-1 N-3.

b) Any insulation system on equipment and piping where the insulation is considered as fireproofing.

c) Where the actuators and wiring of motor operated valves are fireproofed.

d) Insulation systems applied to above ground critical wiring systems specified to be fireproofed.

e) High mechanical abuse area.

### 6.7.2

For the Upgrading Division (except as noted below in c and d), stainless steel jacketing will be used for the following conditions:

a) All hydrocarbon process piping, except as noted below in c).

b) All non-hydrocarbon process (i.e. cooling water, instrument air etc.) piping, two inches in diameter or greater. Non-hydrocarbon process piping less than two inches in diameter may be clad in aluminum.

c) All piping in hydrogen plants (Plants 9-1, 9-2, 9-4 and 27-1) may have aluminum clad on insulation.

d) All piping for Plant 21 may be clad with aluminum.

### 6.7.3

Aluminum and stainless steel jacket thickness will be in accordance with N-1, except stainless steel jacket with 0.020 inch thickness will be used where:

a) Insulation must be removed for maintenance such as piping adjacent to pumps, heat exchangers, control valves, etc.

b) Insulation is in area of high traffic, such as structures, pipe alleys, compressor houses, etc. These "high traffic" areas will be isolated cases, which will be identified during the 30% model review.

### 6.7.4

Aluminum jackets are acceptable for covering insulation at locations and service conditions where stainless steel jackets are not required. It is permissible to use a combination of aluminum and stainless steel jacketing on lines and/or equipment that 'cross' fire boundaries provided that all specification requirements for each type of system are met.

---

6.7.5 Piping at or below grade which is subject to exposure to ground moisture and is insulated with cellular glass insulation shall have an applied coating of an elastomeric material (such as butyl rubber) which shall be reinforced with open weave synthetic fiber fabric 6 x 6 to 10 mesh.

6.7.6 On equipment from which it is desirable to dissipate heat, such as steam condensate knockout drums and exchangers with cooling water on one side, guards, shields, or railings instead of insulation shall be provided for personnel protection.

6.7.7 The design of thermal insulation systems, including the selection of paints, adhesives, weather coating, and joint sealers, shall take into account that piping and equipment may be subject to elevated temperatures during steaming out, cleaning, and flushing operations. Insulation systems having a maximum normal operating hot face temperature under 121°C (250°F), shall be identified by caution signs indicating limitations of the insulation system to elevated temperatures.

## 6.8 Clearance

6.8.1 The clearance between the outside surface of any insulation system and adjacent equipment or structural members (including stairways) shall not be less than 25 mm (1 inch), except hand rails which shall not be less than 50 mm (2 inches). Clearances shall take into account provisions for any fireproofing coatings or insulation applied to such adjacent equipment or structural members. Where the specified thickness of insulation for thermal service or personnel protection is increased solely for noise control, piping layout shall be checked to ensure that the minimum specified clearance will be maintained.

6.8.2 Expansion joints for systems using rigid insulation material shall be provided taking into account the following:

a) The expansion of the piping or equipment, based on the design temperature of the item.

b) The expansion, contraction, or shrinkage of the insulation material, based on the design temperature of the item.

c) The resilience of the insulation.

d) The method of supporting the insulation.

**Revision Memo**
**N-6 rev. 10**
July, 2001

1. Reformatted write-up based from the table of contents.

2. Modified Table 3 increased Mineral Fiber's temperature service up to 454°C (850°F).

3. Mineral Fiber insulation can be used for areas subject to mechanical abuse or piping adjacent to pumps, heat exchangers, control valves that requires the removal of insulation.

4. Deleted fibrous glass material, use Mineral Fiber or calcium silicate as insulation material for heat conservation or personnel protection.

5. The insulation thickness in the current N6 rev9a spec were checked/updated in two representative cases using hand calculations based on Kern (Process Heat Transfer) and the Richardson PC heat loss program. Using the current official cost of energy of $2.95/Mbtu versus $1.83/Mbtu in the current N6 spec it was found that increasing insulation thickness based on the current material and labour costs exceeded a 5 year payout. It was decided to leave the recommended thickness for heat conservation unchanged

6. Added table 5 and 6 for heat traced insulation thicknesses.

7. The higher insulation thickness will be used for lines with dual service for insulation (i.e. heat conservation based on normal operating temperature or that required for heat tracing based on the hold temperature).

8. Oversized insulation will be used for all heat traced piping.

9. Stainless steel jackets with 0.020 inch thickness will be used where insulation must be removed or where insulation is in high traffic areas.

10. The insulation materials for austenitic stainless steel shall be limited to a maximum chloride content of 10 ppm.

11. Added Paragraph 4.4 to use perlite insulation on all Austenitic 321 stainless steel piping.

12. Modified N-3 to UE-1 N-3, project specific specification.

13. Changed 3" to 4" in para. 6.5.3 to be consistent with UE-1 N-3.

14. Added para. 6.6.10 to use calcium silicate Tables 4 & 6 for perlite insulation thicknesses.

15. Changed 2" to 1" in para. 6.8.1 to be consistent with UE-1 L-3.
