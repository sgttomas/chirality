# 1 Objective

The objective of this document is to describe the minimum process requirements for the measurement of produced fluids, oil, gas, water, and other components at the Nanushuk Processing Facility (NPF).

Many metering requirements are regulated by a regulatory agency, and compliance with the regulations is required, even if in excess of the requirements specified in this document.

# 2 General Requirements

Many metering locations are regulated by agency and shall meet all of the requirements specified by the governing body. In addition, the metering equipment shall be designed to be compliant with the requirements of the Project.

Typical considerations required by governing body and/or Project requirements for metering equipment are:

- Metering accuracy during normal conditions
- Metering accuracy at varying conditions, as applicable, including:
  - Varying flow
  - Varying temperatures and pressures
  - Varying composition
  - Over the usable life of the device
- Accuracy for both instantaneous flow rate as well as accuracy of totalized value
- Size of metering equipment
- Serviceability
- Pressure drop
- Interface with Process Control System
- Protection from unintentional modification of flow computer
- Meter provers installed for LACT meters

## 3 Metering Types

### 3.1 Custody Transfer Meters

Custody Transfer Meters establish the quantity of product transferred between two legal entities. These types of meters will be added when an importing or exporting of a product occurs. This will include:

- Sales Oil
- Imported Makeup Gas
- Imported Makeup Water

The sales oil line shall be LACT type meter with meter proving system installed.

### 3.2 Leak Detection Meters

Leak detection metering is used for the purposes of detecting leaks, typically in pipelines, by comparing the flow measurements on the export side of a pipeline to the import side of the pipeline. Leak Detection Meters to be designed to API 1130. The leak detection system for the Sales Oil shall be integrated with the LACT system.

### 3.3 Flare and Vent Gas

Regulatory Agencies require the metering of flared and vented gases, and as such, all flare and vented gas shall be metered, and for flare meters shall be non-intrusive to avoid obstructions in the relief path.

## 3.4 Well Fluid Meters

Produced well fluids from the drill sites shall be metered, but this scope is by others, and will not be part of the Nanushuk Processing Facility (NPF).

## 3.5 Fuel Gas Meters

Regulatory agencies require the metering of fuel gas consumed within the plant. These meters will be associated with fuel gas powered equipment such as engines, turbines, fired heaters, incinerators, etc. Fuel gas meters may not be required in equipment that returns unburned fuel gas back to the process, such as equipment that uses fuel gas as a stripping gas.

If fuel gas meters are not required by regulatory agency, fuel gas metering may be eliminated if not otherwise required, but is typically added to major fuel gas consumers for performance testing if not already included as part of the vendor package.

## 3.6 Injection Meters

Injection lines are to be metered, including gas injection, process water injection, graywater injection, any disposal wells, miscible injectant wells, etc. If different fluids are combined into one stream, only one meter is required for the stream. For example, if graywater and process water are injected into the same wells, only one meter is required for this combined stream. Each drill site shall be separately metered.

## 3.7 Rotating Equipment Flow Meters

Rotating equipment, especially centrifugal pumps and compressors require flow metering for surge and minimum flow control. Typically the requirements for these meters are defined by the vendor, but at a minimum, shall monitor the flowrate of the fluid for the purposes of controlling the rotating equipment to maintain it within its minimum to maximum operating range (such as to prevent compressor surge).

## 3.8 Chemical Injection

Small quantity injection chemicals shall be locally metered to quantify injection rate. It is not required for the metering technology to be electronic or interface with the process control system for these purposes.

## 3.9 Utility Fluids

Utility fluids such as diesel fuel, gasoline, methanol, glycol, lube oil, etc. shall be metered to monitor consumption, but are not required to be electronic or interface with the process control system.

## 3.10 Sand Metering

Metering of produced sand may be elected to be added if quantification of sand input into the process is desired. Sand detectors enable Operations to be aware of the amount of sand produced from the well,

which can be used for estimating erosion on the process equipment over time, and to potentially prevent failure due to an unknown high production of sand. Sand meters do not require high accuracy compared to other types of meters.

## 5 Additional Considerations

### 5.1 Flow Conditions Over the Life of the Facility

Certain processes may change significantly throughout the life of the Facility. These changes may include changes in the condition of the stream such as a change in flow, temperature, or pressure, to

significant changes such as the composition or ratio of Gas, Oil, and Water in the stream. This change over time needs to be considered when selecting the flowmeter technology for the service. This may be in the form of selecting a flowmeter that is usable over the entire expected range, or use of software to compensate. If the change in flow conditions over time is significant where flowmeter technology is not capable of covering the entire range, provisions to replace the flow device over time shall be designed.

This may include:

1. For flow changes over the long term (months or years), a device that can be easily exchanged with a brief shutdown may be acceptable. An example of this is exchanging an orifice plate from one orifice size or another to keep the readings accurate within the new flow calibration range. Alternatively a flow device can be installed with a manual bypass or a parallel meter to be installed so a shutdown is not required for exchanges.

2. For out of range flow changes over the short term (months or less than a month), or in services where breaking containment is not practical or desired, other flow technologies may be required. These alternates may include:

   a. Use of flowmeters with high turndown capacity

   b. Devices that can be exchanged with different flow elements without breaking containment.

   c. Non-intrusive flow devices may be used with multiple elements installed to include the required calibration range

   d. Multiple flow meters with different calibrated flow ranges may be added in parallel to each other. Installed with isolation valves around each meter so the desired flow range/condition can selected for the current operating condition.

## 5.2 Flow Computers

Flow computers can be used to compensate for variability in the flow conditions for certain meters. The flow computers use analyzers to compensate for changes in composition or other conditions. Advanced flow computers can reduce or eliminate the need to exchange out flow devices over varying process conditions that would normally be required with conventional flow devices.

## 5.3 Multiphase Meters

Multiphase meters are typically located at the drill sites to measure oil production, and are thus out of the scope of the NPF; however, this section describes the general requirements for Multiphase meters if any are required within the NPF limits.

The use of Multiphase Meters to measure oil production presents unique measurement challenges. In addition to flow rate changes over the life of a well or facility, the proportion of gas, oil and water may vary greatly and these are in turn affected by pressure and temperature of the stream. When considering

the uncertainty limits of such a meter for a specific application it is frequently found that over the facility lifetime, the accuracy may be adequate for most of the time, while the measurement may be completely erroneous for some small proportion. These meters are similarly impacted by very high and low water fractions and by high Gas Volume Fractions (GVFs).

When establishing the performance of a Multiphase Meter, it is necessary to establish the predicted lifetime of the facility/well and to predict the characteristic production rates for each product. This is typically done on a year-by-year basis. The accuracy requirements from Section 4 is the worst case uncertainty for production rates not exceeding the meter's stated limitations for GVF, flow rates, water fractions, etc. (the operational window). As a guideline, in addition to meeting the stated uncertainty, the meter is expected to be within its operational window for 100 percent of the first 3 years of operations at least, and during 85 percent of the oil production or more, for the life of the measurement equipment. Specific reservoir management, contractual or regulatory obligations may dictate different terms of functionality and will ultimately define minimum expectations.

Meters used to measure multiphase gas production streams fall into two categories--full wellstream meters, and wet gas meters. Wet gas meters are generally conventional gas meters with correction factors applied to compensate for errors caused by the presence of liquids. Additional calculated factors may be used to determine liquid quantities. Full wellstream meters are Multiphase Meters intended to simultaneously measure and calculate the quantity of each phase in the fluid. In some cases, the meters may not distinguish between different liquid phases (e.g., they don't provide separate water and condensate measurements). The uncertainties referred to in Table 1 refer to either type of measurement system, although it should be noted that the expected uncertainties may vary greatly depending on the measurement system used, the location of the meter (surface or subsea), water fraction, liquid fraction, and other variables.
