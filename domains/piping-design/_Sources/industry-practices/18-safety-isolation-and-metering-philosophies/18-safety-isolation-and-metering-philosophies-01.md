# 1 Introduction

Safety critical element (SCE) is identified for the purpose of assuring availability and functionality of equipment that provides the greatest contribution to risk reduction for major accident hazards. The intent of identifying SCE is to facilitate prioritization of inspection, testing, maintenance and performance standards by the operating company.

The intent of this philosophy is not to identify SCE, but rather give a guideline on:

1. What constitutes safety critical, pertinent to the work;

2. What studies, relevant to the Project, will define SCE and;

3. What purpose the resulting SCE list will serve.

## 2 Safety Critical Element Designation

SCE is classified as an equipment or system, whose failure could cause or contribute to a major accident, or whose purpose is to prevent or mitigate the effect of a major accident.

### 2.1 Purpose

SCE is identified for the purpose of assuring reliability, availability and functionality of the equipment that prevents and/or mitigates 'major accident hazards'. Major accident hazard is a term defined by the operating company's risk criteria.

SCE will have prioritized inspection, testing, and maintenance tasks, thus providing the greatest relative contribution to risk reduction for potential major accident events. SCE is an integral component of integrity management. The SCE list generated will be provided to the operating company for incorporation into asset and operations integrity management systems (Maintenance Management Systems, Management of Change, Procedures) to enable items to be flagged, prioritized, tracked and managed appropriately.

### 2.2 Definition of Safety Critical

Safety-critical, as will be considered by the Project, is defined by the following:

4. Pieces of equipment where the consequences of failure could cause or contribute substantially to a major accident event.

    a. Examples of this include a control valve failing closed or a pump failing to operate, resulting in an overpressure condition in upstream equipment. It should be noted that the control valve or the pump will only qualify as an SCE if the extent of the overpressure consequence is defined as a 'major accident' (see section 2.3.1).

5. Equipment that prevents, detects, controls and mitigates a major accident (see section 2.4 for examples).

## 2.3 Hazard Assessments

For the scope of work, SCE will be identified through the Hazard Identification (HAZID) Study and Hazard and Operability Study/Layer of Protection Analysis (HAZOP/LOPA). Other equipment may be considered SCE by the operating company, as determined through other criticality assessment methodologies implemented external to the Project scope of work.

### 2.3.1 Qualifying events for SCE consideration

For an item to be considered for SCE designation, all credible failure events will be evaluated. Based on these failure events, any that are categorized as a health and safety related ‘major accident’, a term defined by the operating company’s risk criteria, will be considered safety-critical.

## 2.4 SCE Evaluation and Categorization

For events determined to be safety-critical by the Project, per section 2.3, equipment will be evaluated and designated as a SCE based on the two categories that follow:

### 2.4.1 Isolation and Prevention

1. Equipment under this category, often referred to as Independent Protection Layer (IPL), is defined as the ultimate safety-critical device that by engineered design, provides managed control for avoiding a hazardous event leading to a hazardous release of hydrocarbons/ chemicals. The systematic loop analyses of these SCE components should be based on HAZOP/LOPA data.

   a. IPLs are generally specified in the HAZOP/LOPA study, include all equipment from sensing devices to ending devices, and are required to be independent of the initiating event or the action of any other protection layer associated with the scenario.

   b. Examples of IPLs include, but are not limited to: Pressure safety valves (PSVs), safety-instrumented systems (SIS), key control valve loops, critical alarms that require operator intervention, combination IPLs such as a limiting element (restriction orifice/spools, check valves or control valve that limits flow) and a PSV sized for full capacity of restricted flow.

### 2.4.2 Control, Detect and Mitigate

1. This equipment will provide detection, mitigation, and control functions in the event of an uncontrolled release of hydrocarbons/chemicals. The primary intent for these SCEs is to prevent and/or control a catastrophic release of hydrocarbons (which generally reduces severity of event).

   a. Equipment in this category are generally listed in the HAZOP/LOPA study as Safeguards.

b. Examples of these equipment types include, but are not limited to: Active fire and gas systems, ventilation systems with detection/mitigation/control equipment, manually initiated ESD and blowdown systems.

## 2.5 SCE Exclusions

Although they may be discussed during the HAZOP/LOPA, following is a list of equipment categories that are generally not considered SCE:

- Passive mitigation and evacuation systems (i.e., fire and blast walls, egress structures)

- Electrical Systems (i.e., junction boxes)

- Fixed hydrocarbon-containing equipment

  - These equipment types do not actively mitigate or prevent a catastrophic release by their function, and are not designed to alert personnel of an impending or occurred release

  - Their pressure-containment function is only passive in nature; therefore, as a rule, these equipment types not to be classified as SCE include vessels, tanks, piping, and flow lines. Pumps, compressors, and turbines are also included in this category unless they are part of a protective system.

- Occupational Personal Safety Equipment (PPE, SCBA, fall protection gear, eye wash)

It should be noted the above mentioned categories will have maintenance activities through their associated regulatory requirements and/or facility maintenance strategies.

## 2.6.1 Integrity Management of SCE

The SCE list will be turned over to the operating company for assignment of required inspection, testing and maintenance tasks and frequencies, as well as definition of performance standards, in line with the company's standards and criteria. Testing and inspection frequencies and methodologies are generally established through consideration of the following:

- Codes and standards such as the National Board Inspection Code, American Society for Testing and Materials, American Petroleum Institute, National Fire Protection Association, American National Standards Institute, American Society of Mechanical Engineers;
- Factors such as magnitude of risk from malfunction/failure of equipment, intensity of use, operating environment and variety of operations that the equipment is exposed to;
- Failure rate of equipment (published values such as those available in OREDA, as well as historical data once available) which in turn influences inspection and testing frequency;
- For SIFs, testing and inspection frequency is established in accordance with ISA-84.00.01.

Equipment/systems identified in the SCE list should be incorporated into asset and operations integrity management systems, including maintenance management systems, procedures and Management of Change. This facilitates identification of SCE, provides a means to track and ensure completion of required tasks at the required intervals, and provides administrative controls of SCE items to ensure changes to the SCE are not made that would defeat its intended function as a protective device.
