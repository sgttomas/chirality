Generally, tanks occupy large areas of the plot. Storage containing the same product shall be grouped together and spaced with a bund (dike) area containing any leakage. Sizing of bund (dike) areas shall be established as a priority early in the site layout. The height of the bund (dike) as measured from outside ground level shall be sufficient to afford protection for personnel when engaged in fire-fighting.

Separation from process areas and other occupied site facilities is important for minimizing the risk to people, assets and the environment. This separation prevents/mitigates potential fires and explosions from propagating to adjacent facilities and escalating in consequences.

## 4.6 Construction Method

Plant layout is influenced by the intended method of construction, whether the plant is being constructed on-site, or whether modules will be fabricated elsewhere and then assembled on-site. Modular design is popular when considering remote sites, severe weather and the availability of a skilled labor force. Project schedules can be shorter because site civil works can proceed in parallel with module fabrication, and site assembly can be done in more favorable weather conditions.

Modular construction requires a separate design approach; it's not effective to design by conventional methods and then divide it into modules.

## 4.7 Access Roads

Roads are an important part of the design of a plant, as they provide access/egress and separation during normal operation as well as emergency access.

Consideration to the following needs to be given:

- Primary roads shall have sufficient horizontal and vertical clearance to permit the unobstructed travel of major vehicles.
