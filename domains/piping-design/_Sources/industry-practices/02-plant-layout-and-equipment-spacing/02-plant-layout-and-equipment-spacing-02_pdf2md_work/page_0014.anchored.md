All occupied buildings need to be located away from process areas, upwind of flare, vent and fired equipment, and outside of blast overpressure contours (Blast Radius/Radiation Zone) to protect personnel in the event of vapor release, fire or explosion. Occupied building within the blast radius zone shall be design for this blast overpressure (i.e., blast resistance), reinforced and or equipped to achieve tolerable risk level within the distance of blast zone.

Building design and orientation should also address environmental considerations such as prevailing weather direction (wind, dust, rain, snow); as well as the ability to remove snow drifting from pad and provide adequate shelter for entrances.

## 4.9 Piperacks

Separation between piperacks and process equipment provides access for operations, maintenance and it reduces exposure from adjacent fires. Process equipment should never be located under onsite piperacks.

Separation distances from pumps are measured as the horizontal distance between the edge of a piperack and the nearest part of the pump (usually the stuffing box). There are no limitations on spacing pump drivers from piperacks.

Where practicable piping which interconnects modules, or multiple trains, it should be routed such that it will not remain in service through plant areas which are shut down for turnaround.

Above ground pipelines pipeways are designed in order to interconnect above ground piping between modules. The piperack sealift module or Pre-Assembled Units (PAU's) will also include interconnecting walkways to enable accessing modules at floor elevation.

## 4.10 Trenching

To enable clear access for day to day activities, plot maintenance and future development, trenches may be utilized to run interconnecting lines underground between the process/utility plant facilities and infrastructure facilities.

## 4.11 Secondary Spill Containment

A bund (or dike) is an embankment or wall of brick, stone, concrete or other impervious material, which surrounds the entire perimeter of a storage compound and provides a barrier to retain solids and liquids.

Bunds/dikes are designed to contain spillages and leaks of the substances being stored, prevent contact with incompatible materials, and to facilitate clean-up operations. As well as being used to prevent pollution of the receiving environment, bunds/dikes are also used for fire protection, product recovery and process isolation.
