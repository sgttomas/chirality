The benefits of a good layout and design cannot be overemphasized. They provide an irreplaceable safety factor throughout the life of the plant, often at very little increase in initial cost. Good plant layout should achieve an economic balance of the requirements of safety, environmental, construction, operations, maintenance, vehicle movement, future expansion and process relations that meet the Company's standards and regulatory code requirements. Where new facilities are planned within an existing live plant, minimum disruption to the existing plant must be a priority. The spatial arrangement of plant items is a critical feature in the design of any facility and has a significant impact on safety and both the installation and operating costs.

These following elements will start to define the site:

- Location of main entrance and egress requirements and to access to new and existing roads.
- Location of flare and process areas to be crosswind of one another and downwind of personnel areas based on prevailing wind.
- Location of pipelines for import and export facilities.
- The situation of power lines entering the site will suggest the location for the substation.
- Interfaces with associated drill sites, process and utility plants.
- Topographical data that affects cut and fill requirement; tankage and storage areas for example may be located on naturally lower ground.

Using the hazard assessment of each plant operation will help establish the layout or orientation of process units within the plant. Review the possible loss events and the consequences for each proposal and then select a layout which will minimize the overall property damage and related business interruption should an incident occur.

Access should be provided for fire-fighting and damage control. Main access roads within the site should run in a north-south, east-west direction, and perpendicular to one another, preferably around all sides of process and utility units, to afford good access for mobile equipment during operations, maintenance, and turnaround.

Truck movements through the plant should be routed away from the main personnel areas, and where possible, loading areas should be minimum distance from the main entrance.

The site should have at least one emergency access / egress in addition to the main entrance and be surrounded by an internal perimeter road. Dead end roads and personnel access ways should be avoided.

Environmental considerations should be incorporated into the layout. Effluent treatment plants should not be sited adjacent to residential or similar property. Toxic, non-toxic and surface water drainage systems should be separated and proper means of disposal built into the layout.
