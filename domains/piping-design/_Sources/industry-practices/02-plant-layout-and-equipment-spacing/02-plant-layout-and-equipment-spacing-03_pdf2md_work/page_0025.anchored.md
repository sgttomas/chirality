### 8.2.2 Hazardous Areas Classification

Hazardous Areas are formally ranked as being a designated hazardous area using the relevant regional or State of Alaska standards that are directly applicable to the plant design under consideration.

The extent of a hazardous area depends on the rate of release, ventilation conditions, and fluid properties and is defined by the relevant regional or State of Alaska standards.

The potential for large releases in operational situations (e.g. process vents) needs to be assessed separately. Dispersion analysis is used to determine the size of the hazardous zone.

Drain system vents also need to be considered with respect to the location of ignition sources.

### 8.2.3 Hazardous Fluids (Sources)

The relevant State of Alaska standards will define which fluids are considered as sources requiring area classification. These typically include fluids such as:

- Flammable gas or vapor.
- Flammable liquids which are handled at or above their flashpoint, or which could be heated to the flashpoint after release.
- Flammable liquid that could form a flammable mist.
- Fluids which satisfy the criteria above and which are present periodically within the plant for 100 hours per year or more (e.g. during start-up).
- Flammable liquids containing residual, volatile materials under confined storage.

### 8.2.4 Safe Areas

A Safe Area in this guideline is regarded as a space that is not ranked as being a designated hazardous area by the relevant Regional or State of Alaska standard directly applicable to the plant design under consideration. The following criteria generally apply:

- A safe distance away from hydrocarbon inventory and sources of ignition.
- A safe distance from potentially explosive dust.
- Located upwind of any processing, flare, vent and drain systems.
