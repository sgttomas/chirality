As far as safety is concerned, the most important factor is the distance between the site and populated areas. Providing this separation between a hazard and the public helps to mitigate the effects of a major incident.

The topography of the site is also an important feature. It is desirable to avoid terrain where hazardous fluids can flow down into populated areas. Potential contamination of water courses by liquid spills is also a consideration.

Site selection should also allow for availability of utilities and access for emergency services.

# 6 Plant Layout Design Criteria

Plant layouts shall maximize safety and exposure protection from the spread of fire, with ease of operation and maintenance consistent with economical design. Requirements for known future expansion shall be recognized and reasonable allowances made.

Space between units and equipment needs to be provided where necessary for safety, maintenance and personnel accessibility, but not wasted, as it usually adds to the capital and operating costs.

The main principles for safe facilities design and operation are:

- Minimizing the likelihood of uncontrollable releases of hydrocarbons and other hazardous materials
- Facilities and equipment are accessible for all operational and maintenance related tasks.
- Consideration for Area Classification
- Minimizing ignition sources
- Prevention of fire escalation
- Prevention of facility and equipment damage
- Providing for personnel protection and escape
- Available access for fire-fighting equipment and facilities including mobile (i.e., passive incipient protection or full fire-fighting)
- Consideration for Site Security
- Facilities that are safe and cost effective to construct, transport, install, hook-up & commission and operate.
- Balance of site manpower loading consideration.
- Critical emergency facilities are accessible for operators to perform emergency shutdown actions to prevent or mitigate potential fire explosion consequences.
