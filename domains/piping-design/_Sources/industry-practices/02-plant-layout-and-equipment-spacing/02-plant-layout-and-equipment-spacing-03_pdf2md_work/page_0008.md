This guideline outlines the acceptable standards, minimum technical requirements and recommended good practices. Often, fire protection and safety spacing requirements exceed maintenance accessibility requirements and legally allowable minimum distances. The relative location of the equipment or process unit depends on its potential release of flammable materials, its flammable liquid hold-up, and it's potential to be a source of ignition.

Recommended distances for different plants are compiled in the Appendices. These tables are supplemented by detailed notes in the following sections. If these guidelines cannot be followed, then additional loss control measures, such as fireproofing, water spray or blast hardening will be necessary.

## 2.3 Terms and Definitions

For the purposes of this guideline, the following terms and definitions are used throughout the guideline.

**Access ways**

Areas for either vehicular or pedestrian access within plot limit.

**Active Fire Protection**

A fire-fighting system that is normally dormant and is activated automatically upon detection of a fire hazard. (e.g. Fine Water Mist systems)

**Auto-Ignition Temperature (AIT)**

The auto-ignition temperature is the temperature at which a product will burn in the presence of oxygen without application of any other form of ignition. The AIT of most petroleum products is between 482°F and 932°F. Use 500°F (AIT for gasoline) as a guide. Also refer to NFPA 325.

**Administration Area**

That part of the site containing the main office buildings.

**Battery Limit**

A non-physical boundary defining, the limit of the Plant/Facility/Unit.

**Company**

Oil Search Alaska, LLC or it's designated representative.

**Facility**

Any building, structure, installation, equipment, or appurtenance that is connected to or associated with the recovery, development, production, handling, processing, treatment or disposal of process media or any associated substances or waste. This does not include wells or pipelines.
