## 8.3 Access Considerations

Plant facilities shall be provided with adequate access and egress for personnel, materials, and equipment by using access ways, roads, stairways, stiles, and walking / working surfaces including decks, floors, grade, platforms, ramps and walkways as per the following guidelines.

### 8.3.1 Escape and Evacuation

There is a clear distinction between means of evacuation and means of escape.

- Escape refers to safe means of personnel movement from hazardous areas to designated safe areas.
- Evacuation means leaving the installation and its vicinity in a systematic manner.

The escape and evacuation arrangements should allow everyone to reach a place of safety.

Emergency Response' procedures for possible incidents at plant shall be identified and accommodated in the plant layout. Make sure local authorities are aware of the types of possible incidents.

#### 8.3.1.1 Escape

The plant will have designated Muster areas where personnel can go during an emergency and be accounted for and from which they can then be instructed to evacuate the plant.

The main escape routes from process areas will be the main egress provided to minimize travel exits.

Escape routes from multi-storey process units shall be by stairs. Ladders may be accepted as means of escape routes where it is clearly not practicable to install stairs. Two independent paths of escape should be provided wherever possible. See the Appendices A for minimum escape route dimensions.

#### 8.3.1.2 Evacuation

The requirements relating to safe evacuation will be met by using a combination of means of evacuation according to current practice (e.g. vehicle transport, foot).

The number, size and location of evacuation systems need to be established based on facility manning, risk exposure analyses and emergency preparedness analysis.
