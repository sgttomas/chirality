## 7.1 Classifications

Processes and operations can be empirically classified into one of three classes according to their explosion and fire hazard – Moderate, Intermediate or High (as shown in Appendix B).

These classifications (Appendix B) do not substitute for a proper hazard identification or analysis method. They are intended only to be used in determining spacing requirements.

Many additional factors and judgments can still affect the class to which the process is assigned such as: vessel size, flammable liquid hold-up, unit size, gas vs. liquid phase, pressure, temperatures, loss history, interdependency, lead time to rebuild, and market share. Therefore, if any of the above features of a process are higher than normal, raise it to a higher class.

## 7.2 Application of this Guideline

Layout is concerned with the spatial arrangement of the modules and process equipment (and its interconnections) within the plant boundaries, the location of the plant area within the site boundaries, and the location of the site relative to the surrounding environment and residential communities. This guideline makes a clear distinction between the layout of the various plants/units on a site and the arrangement of process vessels or equipment etc. within the plant (or plot).

## 7.3 Legislative Regulations, Local Codes, and Standards

Local legislative regulations adopted by the State of Alaska must be used in conjunction with governing codes and standards.

Minimum distance of equipment from site property lines shall comply with national (e.g. federal), state, and local codes and safety regulations or deed restrictions. If there is a conflict, the greater distance shall govern.

If local codes and standards for plant layout conflict with guidance given in this guideline, the more stringent text shall govern upon approval by the Company.

## 7.4 Requirements

- Projects shall undergo a risk assessment to an appropriate level of detail.
  a) Calculations relevant to layout or concerning potential major releases of hazardous materials shall be made available for such a review.
  b) Less stringent exceptions to or variances from requirements specified in this guideline shall be supported and documented by a risk assessment.
- New sites shall undergo a Project Security Review (PSR).
