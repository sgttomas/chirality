Consideration to the following needs to be given:

- Site roads shall separate plots. Refer to OGE-CICRT-000001 (Civil Design Criteria) for minimum width for site roads.
- Equipment shall be located so that site roads are not classified as a hazardous area.
  a) Minor site roads shall not be in an area classified as Class I Division 1.
  b) Any minor site road or in-plant road classified as Class I Division 2 shall have controlled access.
- Where applicable, plant layout including roadway design shall take into account the safe removal and stockpiling of snow in an operating facility.

## 9.2.2 Utilities

Site utility services shall be grouped together in a non-hazardous area with exceptions being utility services integral to process unit operation.

Site utility services should be located so that essential utility supplies can be maintained under emergency conditions (i.e., in the event of a flammable vapor release). The separation distances should be determined on a risk-based approach such that essential utility supply functions can be maintained within an agreed reliability.

## 9.2.3 Firewater Pumps

Firewater pumps shall be sufficiently remote from processing, storage, and loading areas to minimize the risk of fire or blast damage to the pumps given protective measures installed (e.g. fire/blast resisting enclosure).

The pumping facilities shall be capable of operation during the reasonable worst-case fire scenario and during primary power supply outage.

## 9.2.4 Flares

Flares and/or burner booms shall be located to avoid ignition of normal or accidental gas releases. Flares may become potential sources of toxic vapors. The potential danger from radiant heat from a flare should be taken into account for all potential wind directions on flare locations. The flare and vent systems shall comply with API Std. 521/ ISO 53521 or equivalent. The radiant heat intensities or emissions from flares and vent systems shall not exceed the limits above the abnormal conditions (e.g. flame out of flare system and accidental ignition of vent).

The location, spacing, orientation, and height of flares shall be determined, for the particular site, by a full assessment of the factors involved. Use of dispersion calculations should be considered. The height of elevated flares is also determined by the radiant heat calculations.
