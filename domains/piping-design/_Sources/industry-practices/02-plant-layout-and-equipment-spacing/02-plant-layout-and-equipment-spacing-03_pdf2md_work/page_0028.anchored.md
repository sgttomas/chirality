A well-designed plant should have a uniform, functional appearance. Elevations of the various units should be determined early to establish a common site datum and relative elevations between units.

## 9.1 Philosophy

- The site area shall be divided into plots, and if possible, the plots should be rectangular.
- A single plot may contain more than one process unit or section.
- Irrespective of whether process units are on the same or adjacent plots, spacing requirements outlined in this guideline shall be satisfied. Spacing between process units shall take into account the following factors:
  - a) Exposure to possible fire radiation.
  - b) Exposure to possible explosion overpressure.
  - c) Maintenance requirements, including unrestricted hot work, of one unit while adjacent units are in service.
  - d) Business interruption potential and relative value of the units involved.
  - e) Prevailing wind direction.
  - f) Access by emergency response equipment.
  - g) Future expansion.
- Plant separation should consider the following:

  Plant separation distances depend on the size and congestion of the plant unit. The key issue is to limit escalation.

  - a) Maximum size of flammable gas cloud from one plant area does not significantly intrude into adjacent areas.
  - b) The magnitude of a gas explosion is not significantly increased by proximity of adjacent plant units.
  - c) Damage caused in one area does not lead to escalation in adjacent areas.
- If there is flow between individual units, the layout of the units should be, as far as economical, in a logical order of flow.

  The increased cost of wide spacing of process units must be weighed against the implied safety benefits in the event of a fire or explosion.
