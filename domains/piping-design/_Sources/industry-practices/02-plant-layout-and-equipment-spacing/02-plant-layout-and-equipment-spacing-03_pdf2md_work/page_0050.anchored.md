- NFPA 497 classifies liquids as follows:

  a) Class IA – Flammable liquids having flashpoints below 73°F and having a boiling point below 100°F.

  b) Class IB – Flammable liquids having flashpoints below 73°F and having a boiling point at or above 100°F.

  c) Class IC – Flammable liquids having flashpoints at or above 73°F and below 100°F.

  d) Class II – Combustible liquids having flashpoints at or above 100°F and below 140°F.

  e) Class IIIA – Combustible liquids having flashpoints at or above 140°F and below 200°F.

  f) Class IIIB – Combustible liquids having flashpoints at or above 200°F.

  g) Unstable Liquid – A liquid that, in the pure state or as commercially produced or transported, vigorously polymerizes, decomposes, undergoes condensation reaction, or becomes self-reactive under conditions of shock, pressure, or temperature.

  h) Stable Liquid – Any liquid not defined as unstable.

- Tanks shall be laid out to provide access for fire-fighting. There should be no more than two rows of tanks between adjacent access roads. Tanks of diameter 131 feet or greater should be arranged in a single row.

  The figure of 131 feet diameter is somewhat arbitrary, because fire-fighting is easier with a single-row tank layout, it may be considered preferable to group tanks slightly smaller than 131 feet diameter in a single row.

- Pumps associated with tankage operations shall not be located inside a bunded (diked) tank compound and should preferably be grouped together outside the bund (dike) provided this is practicable and economical.

  Normally, the spacing between pumps is determined by access requirements. However, if the pumps are on critical service, and failure would result in total shutdown of a major facility, then additional spacing or separation may be specified. An example of this critical duty is the main oil line (MOL) pumps on an oil-producing site, or the booster pumps on a major pipeline.
