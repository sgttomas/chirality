SYNCRUDE SPECIFICATION

PIPING MECHANICAL ANALYSIS

**5.9 Allowable Forces and Moments on Flanges**

Bending moments and forces on flanges of NPS 24 and larger shall be reviewed to determine if leakage due to flange separation can occur.

**5.10 Design for Effects of Friction**

5.10.1 Anchor design in piping systems shall include the effect of frictional resistance to thermal movement of the pipe based on a coefficient of friction of 0.3 for steel to steel contact, 0.1 for teflon to stainless steel, 0.1 for teflon to teflon, and 0.45 for steel on concrete contact.

5.10.2 Frictionlessly unrestrained movement of the piping system shall be assumed only when the entire system is supported by means of rods or spring hangers.

**5.11 Special Design Consideration**

5.11.1 Dynamic Loading

a) Dynamic loading may be expected when pulsating flow (such as at reciprocating compressors), high velocity flow, flashing fluid, fluctuating temperature or pressure, or mechanical vibration (including wind) conditions exist.

b) Piping subject to possible dynamic loading shall be carefully designed and checked to insure that its size, configuration, mechanical strength, supports, and restraints will prevent excessive stress, vibration or noise.

5.11.2 Limited Pressure Drop

Limited pressure drop requirements may constitute a major design consideration in large vapor lines, heater transfer lines, reboiler circuits, pump suctions, and gravity flow lines. Such piping shall be routed as directly as practical.

5.11.3 Other Severe Services

Other severe services include erosive, corrosive, high or low temperature or pressure conditions, or any fluids containing solids. Many such services require alloy or other special materials. Piping in these services shall be routed to minimize the effects of service severity and make most economic use of special materials.
