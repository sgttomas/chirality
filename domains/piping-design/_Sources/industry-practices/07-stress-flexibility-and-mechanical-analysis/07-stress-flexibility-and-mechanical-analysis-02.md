SYNCRUDE SPECIFICATION | PIPING MECHANICAL ANALYSIS

## 1.0 OBJECTIVE

This specification provides an analysis guide that will produce safe and reliable pressure piping system design in the plant.

Not all piping systems require formal mechanical analysis as described in this specification. According to ASME B31.3, no formal analysis of adequate flexibility is required in systems which are duplicates of successfully operating installations or replacements without significant change of systems with a satisfactory service record, or which can readily be judged adequate by comparison with previously analyzed systems.

## 2.0 SCOPE

This specification outlines the basic piping design requirements and consideration for thermal flexibility, sustained loads, vibration, fluid or gas flow reactions, environmental factors, and effects on equipment.

## 5.0 DESIGN

5.1 Mechanical analysis of piping systems is usually accomplished by using one of the following methods.

   a) Visual inspection based on past successful experience of similar configurations and design conditions as per ASME B31.3, Para. 319.4.1.

   b) Hand calculation using simplified methods such as guided cantilever and expansion loop charts commonly used by the industry.

   c) Computer analysis of simulated piping models.

The choice of analysis method shall be made by either qualified Engineers or under the supervision of qualified Engineers.

5.2 Computer analysis of thermal and deadweight load cases, with complete documentation, is mandatory for, but not limited to, the following piping systems unless paragraph 319.4.1 of ASME B31.3 governs.

   a) All lines with design temperatures over 400°C (750°F).

   b) All horizontal centrifugal pump piping NPS 4 or larger.

   c) All lines to and from reactors.

   d) All lines to and from centrifugal compressors with the exception of auxiliary piping.

   e) All steam lines to and from turbines.

   f) All air-cooled heat exchanger piping systems connecting to exchangers with four or more header box nozzles.

   g) All centrifuge piping NPS 3 or larger.

   h) All lines operating under severe cyclic conditions.

5.3 Thermal and deadweight analysis with adequate documentation, by computer or simplified methods may be used on the following systems.

   a) All lines NPS 16 and larger.

   b) Lines to and from reciprocating pumps and compressors.

   c) Lines NPS 6 and larger at design temperatures over 260°C (500°F) but less than 400°C (750°F).

---

d) Shell and tube exchanger piping.

e) Lines subject to excessive settlement of 75 mm (3 in.) or more.

f) Category M Fluid Service - Lines designated as "Category M" in ASME B31.3.

g) All process, regeneration and decoking lines to and from fired heaters and steam generators.

5.4 In addition to requirements for thermal and deadweight analysis, special consideration needs to be given to other loading conditions and design features of piping systems (see para 5.11) in the following categories:

a) Lines NPS 3 and larger subject to significant differential settlement of tanks, vessels, equipment or supports.

b) Lines subjected to mixed phase flow (liquid and vapor) shall be analyzed for vibration problems.

c) Lines subject to external pressure by reason of vacuum or jacketing.

d) Jacketed piping.

e) Small aeration, steam, etc. lines connected to large lines shall be checked for thermal differential and/or surge vibrations.

f) Lines to and from reciprocating pumps and compressors.

g) Lines subject at steam purging. Minimum design temperature shall be 180°C (356°F).

h) Hot lines subjected to cold temperature injection.

i) Lines requiring proprietary expansion devices.

j) Thin walled piping.

k) Hot (operating at over 70°C (200°F)) underground piping.

l) Larger underground cooling water lines and associated small diameter branch connections.

m) In-line pump piping.

n) Refractory lined piping.

o) Lateral stability of long straight runs of piping.

---

5.5 Complete documentation in Paragraph 5.2 analysis shall mean clear filing of the following.

a) A sketch of the analyzed layouts with nodes, reference orientation and approximate dimensions clearly indicated.

b) Hand calculation, if any.

c) Computer analysis result including maximum stresses, significant loads on equipment nozzles.

d) Data sheets for spring hangers and expansion joints, if any.

e) Indication on sketches of any thermal movement of more than 50 mm (2 in.) and loads of more than 9 kN (2 kips).

5.6 Adequate documentation in paragraph 5.3 shall mean any calculations, notes and comments which confirm that an analysis was performed and adequate consideration was given.

## 5.7 Design Conditions

5.7.1 The Flexability Analysis shall consider the most severe conditions imposed by:

Revised Layout:

- Normal operation e.g. upset condition, regeneration, recycle.
- Other expected conditions, e.g. startup, shutdown, steamout, decoking, emergency shutdown, testing.
- Abnormal conditions, e.g. loss of cooling medium flow, cessation of process flow while line heating facilities remain operative. The analysis shall be for the maximum temperature differential. The effect of minimum and maximum installation and solar temperatures shall be considered in determining the maximum temperature differential.

(1) The 50°C (120°F) temperature is considered the maximum metal temperature resulting from solar radiation with no flow in the line.

(2) Under certain conditions of dual service, the fluid temperature range may be both above 50°C (120°F) and below minimum winter dry bulb temperature.

5.7.2 Lines subject to steam purging shall be designed for a minimum temperature of 180°C (355°F). If it is possible that vessels may be steamed independently of process lines, this factor shall be considered.

5.7.3 Lines which cannot be disconnected during purging shall be designed with sufficient flexibility to accommodate the thermal displacement of the vessel.

5.7.4 Equipment thermal expansion (radial and axial) shall be considered in the flexibility analysis.

5.7.5 The use of cold spring for piping systems which connect to rotating equipment is prohibited.

5.7.6 Piping which connects to equipment shall be supported so as not to overload the nozzles and/or equipment.

5.7.7 Equipment nozzle load analysis shall take friction into account, but no credit may be taken when friction reduces nozzle loads.

5.7.8 Pipe support considerations shall include concurrent anchor loads due to friction and thermal expansion.

5.7.9 Wind loads shall be determined in accordance with the Alberta Building Code.

5.7.10 Design for snow loads is not required except as required by Syncrude.

5.7.11 Design for seismic loads is not required.

## 5.8.1 Vertical In-Line Pump

Small vertical in-line pumps, less than 900 kg (2000 lb) may be supported by soil bearing foundations or reinforced concrete slabs where approved by Syncrude. Piping forces shall be determined with the pump considered as a rigid, segment of the piping system.

## 5.8.2 Centrifugal Pumps

a) The maximum forces and moments which may be imposed on the pump nozzles shall be in accordance with API-610. The Contractor shall accept the allowables recommended by the pump Seller only if such allowables exceed the limits specified in API-610.

b) Piping force and moment limitations may be increased by 50 percent for reactions which occur only when a pump is not operating: e.g. the case of the idle pump (installed spare) or a condition during equipment steamout.

c) Use of a more rigid baseplate and support assembly for horizontal pumps shall be evaluated as an alternative to revised piping layout when computations indicate that the combined piping forces moment limitations would be exceeded (See API 610).

d) The effects of piping weight and friction forces due to thermal expansion shall be included in the evaluation of loads on pump nozzles.

e) For design purposes, when using a hot bypass the spare pump piping shall be assumed to be at 50 percent of design temperature of the operating pump.

## 5.8.3 General Purpose Steam Turbines

a) General purpose steam turbines shall be as defined in API 611.

b) The maximum forces and moments which may be imposed on the turbine nozzles shall be in accordance with API 611. The Contractor shall accept the allowables recommended by the turbine Seller only if such allowables exceed the limits specified in API 611.

c) Turbine nozzles loads shall also be analyzed in the cold condition with the permitted flange misalignment.

---

#### 5.8.4 Special Purpose Steam Turbines

a) Special purpose steam turbines shall be as defined in API 612.

b) The maximum allowable forces and moments which may be applied to turbine flanges by the steam piping shall be limited to those values which are acceptable to both Syncrude and the manufacturer.

c) Direct connections between the turbine exhaust and a surface condenser will be made using a packless expansion joint.

d) Turbine nozzle loads shall also be analyzed in the cold condition with the permitted flange misalignment.

#### 5.8.5 Centrifugal and Axial Compressors

a) The maximum allowable forces and moments which may be applied to compressor flanges by the piping shall be limited to the values allowed by API 617.

b) Compressors nozzle loads shall be analyzed in the cold condition with the permitted flange misalignment.

#### 5.8.6 Air-Cooled Heat Exchangers

a) The maximum allowable forces and moments which may be applied to air cooler flanges shall be limited to those values which are acceptable to both Syncrude and the manufacturer. API 661 criteria shall be a minimum requirement unless the allowable loads are provided by the Manufacturer.

b) For multi-bundle air cooled exchangers, thrust blocks between bundles shall be considered in the analysis. Clearances at the end bundles shall be considered to accommodate the maximum thermal expansion calculated.

   Effects of piping weight and friction forces due to thermal expansion shall be included in the evaluation of loads on nozzles.

c) See Specification L-3 for fixed header box location.

#### 5.8.7 Shell and Tube Exchangers

Allowable forces from thermal displacements of piping on equipment shall be limited in accordance with the appropriate codes. See Specification L-3 for fixed end location.

**5.8.8 Fired Heaters and Reformers**

a) API 560, 1986 edition, recommends allowable forces and moments which are conservative. Allowable forces and moments on heater nozzles shall be agreed between Syncrude (or the Contractor) and the Seller. The Seller shall specify the maximum expected movement of the heater tube nozzle and the effect of expansion and/or displacement of the tubes shall be used in the analysis of the piping system.

b) Any heater designed with a floating coil (all spring or counterweight mounted) shall be provided with fail-safe limit stops in all directions. Analysis of piping systems connected to floating heater coils shall include the heater coil or an approximate model of the coil as part of the systems and the effects of internal guides and restraints. Where heater coils are floating, the support of the connecting piping system shall be completely and independently balanced so that no dead load is imposed on the coil.

**5.8.9 Pressure Vessels**

Allowable loads will be calculated in accordance with criteria given by the API Recommended Practices, API Standards, WRC Bulletins or ASME sponsored codes which apply to the specific equipment or system. Economics of nozzle reinforcement versus additional piping restraint/anchor shall be considered.

**5.8.10 Storage Tanks**

In addition to tank thermal expansion and settlement, piping connected to tank nozzles shall include design for radial movement and rotation due to tank wall bulging caused by hydrostatic head and pressure. Nozzle loads shall be evaluated based on Appendix P of API 650 unless FEA is performed.

**5.8.11 Reciprocating Compressors and Pumps**

a) Thermal displacements will be analyzed; however, vibration control will govern. The piping systems shall be designed so as to minimize forces due to pressure pulsations.

b) Support and hold down arrangements for reciprocating equipment piping systems shall produce a natural frequency outside the equipment forcing frequency up to and including the fourth harmonic.

c) API 618 guidelines to be followed for piping design and pulsation study for all reciprocating compressors. Both acoustic and mechanical resonances shall be considered for pulsation study.

**5.9 Allowable Forces and Moments on Flanges**

Bending moments and forces on flanges of NPS 24 and larger shall be reviewed to determine if leakage due to flange separation can occur.

**5.10 Design for Effects of Friction**

5.10.1 Anchor design in piping systems shall include the effect of frictional resistance to thermal movement of the pipe based on a coefficient of friction of 0.3 for steel to steel contact, 0.1 for teflon to stainless steel, 0.1 for teflon to teflon, and 0.45 for steel on concrete contact.

5.10.2 Frictionlessly unrestrained movement of the piping system shall be assumed only when the entire system is supported by means of rods or spring hangers.

**5.11 Special Design Consideration**

5.11.1 Dynamic Loading

a) Dynamic loading may be expected when pulsating flow (such as at reciprocating compressors), high velocity flow, flashing fluid, fluctuating temperature or pressure, or mechanical vibration (including wind) conditions exist.

b) Piping subject to possible dynamic loading shall be carefully designed and checked to insure that its size, configuration, mechanical strength, supports, and restraints will prevent excessive stress, vibration or noise.

5.11.2 Limited Pressure Drop

Limited pressure drop requirements may constitute a major design consideration in large vapor lines, heater transfer lines, reboiler circuits, pump suctions, and gravity flow lines. Such piping shall be routed as directly as practical.

5.11.3 Other Severe Services

Other severe services include erosive, corrosive, high or low temperature or pressure conditions, or any fluids containing solids. Many such services require alloy or other special materials. Piping in these services shall be routed to minimize the effects of service severity and make most economic use of special materials.

#### 5.11.4 High Pressure Drop Control Valves

When high pressure drop conditions across control valves exist, sonic harmonics along with extreme noise levels can be expected. Piping subjected to these conditions shall be designed to ensure that its size and configuration downstream of the valve prevent excessive vibration and noise.
