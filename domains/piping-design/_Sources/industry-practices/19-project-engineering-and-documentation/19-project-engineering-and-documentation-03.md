# Scope and Application

The *Piping Manual* has been written for convenient reference by Company personnel engaged in technical work on piping systems. It is directed to technical personnel regardless of experience. **This manual should never be used as a substitute for sound engineering judgment.**

This manual contains guidelines and specifications for use by Company personnel. The material may be used as is, or modified for local organizational or geographic preferences, priorities, or experiences. This especially pertains to specifications.

The intent is to provide practical, useful information based on Company experience. Therefore, forms have been included in the front of the manual for your convenience in suggesting changes. Your input and experience are important for improving subsequent printings and keeping this manual up-to-date.

# Organization

The *Piping Manual* is part of a set of manuals produced by CRTC in cooperation with all Operating Companies in the Corporation. Several of these manuals contain information directly related to piping.

- A **Red** tab marks a place for you to keep documents developed by your organization.

# Engineering Guidelines (Volume 1)

This part of the manual contains: (1) material selection guidelines; (2) design principles and Corporation suggested practices; (3) construction techniques; (4) inspection criteria; (5) operational information. Because these subjects are related and most facilities require knowledge of a combination of these areas, a summary of each section of the manual with pertinent specifications is given below.

## Section 100 General Information

Piping is the bond that holds together any industrial facility, large or small. However, because of its undramatic presence piping is often taken for granted.

In fact, piping operability, ease of maintenance and, moreover, cost and safety are primary concerns. Piping is typically 20 to 25% of total plant cost, and 10% of all fires within the Corporation are related to piping. Good piping design always balances operation, maintenance, and cost. Proper design is as necessary for piping as it is for the equipment it connects.

The *Piping Manual* is a guide for the basic design and construction of piping systems. It focuses on design fundamentals, guidelines for practical installations, and specification and purchase of materials and services. It is applicable to large and small facilities whether used for upstream production, downstream refining, chemical manufacture, offshore production or marketing. Its guidelines encompass the experience of the Corporation's Operating Companies. The manual's broad applicability makes it useful to both engineers and operating personnel.

The *Piping Manual* is concerned only with piping systems in stations and plants. It does not provide design information for pipelines. The manual includes certain topics related to operations and maintenance, but not a comprehensive description of these functions.

## Section 200 Piping Component Selection

This section gives guidance on the selection and use of the mechanical components that are fabricated into piping systems. The dimensional and material standards for common components are described. Illustrations are provided of these components and of some special valves and fittings. Guidance is given on the selection and use of piping components in applications common to all types of installations.

## Section 300 Piping System Design

This section provides guidance in the design and installation of typical piping systems. It is intended for all users of this manual.

How to properly size piping systems is discussed, and good layout practices are illustrated. Methods of analyzing piping stresses and providing pipe stops and supports are reviewed. Design and support of small diameter piping are discussed. Considerations for design of buried piping are also given.

# Section 400 Nonmetallic and Nonmetallic-Lined Steel Piping

This section provides guidelines for the selection, design and installation of commonly used nonmetallic and nonmetallic-lined steel piping materials. The guidelines are general and do not provide all the information necessary for the complete detailed design of a piping system. Sound engineering judgement is especially important when these piping materials are used in services or situations where the Company has little or no experience. Consult Materials and Equipment Engineering at CRTC for technical guidance in areas not addressed in this chapter.

# Section 500 Flexible Hose

This section gives the reader general information about flexible hose and its use in the Company. Care and inspection of cargo hose for marine use is also discussed.

# Section 600 Construction

This section discusses activities involved in piping assembly and installation, pressure testing, cleaning, hot tapping, storage and handling, and safety. Specific consideration is given to welding, fabrication, fitup, bolting, field layout of small piping and other subjects falling under the aforementioned general categories.

# Section 700 Onshore Facilities

This section presents guidelines, tables, drawings, references, and piping practices applicable to onshore producing facilities such as wells, gaging stations, and field compressors.

A brief discussion of internal pressure design is presented for piping used upstream and in operations, such as field process plants, gathering systems, and steam plants. See Section 200 for a more thorough treatment of pressure design.

This section will assist you in:

- Selecting pipe, valves, and fittings for safe onshore producing facilities
- Standardizing on commonly used piping components
- Complying with national codes and standards

# Section 800 Offshore Facilities

This section covers regulations, guidelines, drawings and references, and piping practices that are unique to offshore producing facilities. It is not a comprehensive guide to offshore piping design or construction. It attempts to highlight some offshore practices that differ from the practices discussed in the rest of this manual.

For details on routine design procedures or pieces of equipment the user is directed to other sections of this manual and other Company manuals. The cross reference guide in Section 50 will help locate information in other manuals.

# Section 900 Marketing Facilities

This section discusses piping practices used for Marketing facilities. Marketing uses all of the basic elements of piping engineering, material selection, and fabrication used by the Corporation; therefore, this section will not repeat discussions on those specific topics that are more thoroughly presented in other sections of this manual.

# Section 1000 H2S Guidelines

This section recommends minimum standards for the design and construction of equipment containing moderate to high concentrations of hydrogen sulfide. These recommendations will satisfy the requirements of a major installation such as a gas plant or refinery. Additional supplemental specifications, standard drawings and recommended practices have been listed for these as well as other types of installations. The management of each facility must judge the extent to which they will be required to supplement the minimum standards recommended here.

# Section 1100 LPG Guidelines

This guideline supplements API Standard 2510, *Design and Construction of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) Installations*. It includes Company recommendations which exceed the API Standard. The design and construction of all new LPG storage facilities shall comply with API Standard 2510.

This guideline is intended for Company-wide use. Local management must determine which recommendations will be mandatory for their facility.

This Table of Contents lists **all** of the sections of API Standard 2510 (which this guideline supplements), whether or not they appear here.

# Section 1200 NH3 Guidelines

This section provides the Company's minimum standards for the design and construction of new anhydrous ammonia ($NH_3$) handling and storage facilities. Also included are anhydrous $NH_3$ recovery and handling facilities located within ammonia manufacturing plants and other processing units. Recommendations for upgrading existing anhydrous $NH_3$ facilities are provided as well.

Industry standard ANSI K61.1, Safety Requirements for the Storage and Handling of Anhydrous Ammonia, provides the basis for this section. Supplemental Company requirements are also covered in this section. This section does not restate all of the requirements of ANSI Standard K61.1 and has been prepared assuming the user will refer to ANSI Standard K61.1 when applying the requirements of this practice.

This section does not cover aqueous $NH_3$ facilities, refrigeration plants using anhydrous $NH_3$ as a refrigerant, anhydrous $NH_3$ transportation facilities, or anhydrous $NH_3$ manufacturing plants as exempted by ANSI Standard K61.1.

# Section 1300 Cl2 Guidelines

This section establishes procedures for the safe handling and storage of liquid chlorine in commercial containers of one-ton capacity and smaller, and for the application of associated equipment, for both new and existing facilities. It also provides good engineering practices for the design and installation of piping in chlorine service.

# Section 1400 Reducing Fugitive Emissions from Valves

the objective of this section is to provide Chevron facilities with comprehensive guidance on reducing fugitive emissions from valves and results of valve packing research.

Section 1420 provides a detailed summary of Chevron's recommendations on reducing fugitive emissions from valves. The remainder of Section 1400 provides substantial background information on these issues.

# Appendix B Hub-and-Clamp Piping Connectors

This discussion is derived from a memo by D. Klapko, Calgary Design and Construction Group, Chevron Canada Resources Limited, 1988-03-02. It is based on Canadian experience but the ideas are universally applicable.

# Appendix C Piping Vibration Guidelines

This appendix consolidates piping fatigue information, provides design guidance to avoid fatigue failures, and presents vibration amplitude criteria for the evaluation of existing vibrating piping.

# Appendix D Design Properties of Pipe

This appendix contains a reprint of Chemetron Corporation's document entitled, "Design Properties of Pipe" - Bulletin TT 330.

# Appendix E Plant Design Development and Execution

This appendix discusses general site and plot plan layout considerations from the point of view of an efficient piping installation.

The piping layout practices are mainly applicable to major processing facilities such as refineries, large gas plants, and large scale commercial chemicals production facilities. However, the information is applicable to any facility where high-value processing equipment handling flammable materials is concentrated in close quarters. Included are several checklists that will help design engineers cover the many considerations a large plant design requires.

## Specifications, Drawings, and Forms (Volume 2)

This part of the manual contains: (1) general instructions for using specifications; (2) model specifications, data sheets, and data sheet instructions that can be copied or modified to local preferences; and, (3) industry standards and references (API, ANSI, and ASME).

The Corporation Piping Specification (Standard Drawing GB-135169) has been revised and updated. The Specification contains over 30 individual piping class sheets that cover carbon steel piping for some basic low pressure hydrocarbon and utility services commonly found in many major manufacturing facilities. The complete Corporation Piping Specification includes the following:

- Classification Index
- Branch Reinforcing Tables
- Change of Direction Tables
- General Notes
- Abbreviations List
