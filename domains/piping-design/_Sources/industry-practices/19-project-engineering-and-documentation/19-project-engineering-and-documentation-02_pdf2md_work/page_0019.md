# 6 COMPETENCES

The Group HSE Management System states that "Competence stems from a combination of factors such as innate ability, experience, training and capacity to learn; it involves the ability to deal with routine as well as non-routine situations." Human Factors Engineering competence is no different. Training cannot be the sole source of competence development for all levels. The four levels of competence are defined in Table 2:

[TABLE: Table 2 Competence Definitions for HFE in New Projects]

| Competence level | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| Awareness (A): | (Can discuss) The individual is familiar with the HFE process and understands the principals, concepts and language. The individual knows when and how the HFE design process applies to projects and is familiar with the different project phases and timing. |
| Working Knowledge (WK): | (Can do) The individual has undergone the Working Knowledge training course. This individual can carry out the Human Factors Engineering tools in this DEP (Focusing and Work Systems Analysis Tools). This individual has a number of years of project experience and is familiar with the principles of Human Factors Engineering. |
| Skilled (S): | (Can Lead) The individual can apply and adapt Human Factors Engineering principles and tools to a variety of project settings. This person can think analytically and produce new solutions to existing problems, and can lead and coach a design team. |
| Mastery (M): | (Can Design) The individual can design Human Factors Engineering Tools and teach their use to individuals of other engineering disciplines. |
