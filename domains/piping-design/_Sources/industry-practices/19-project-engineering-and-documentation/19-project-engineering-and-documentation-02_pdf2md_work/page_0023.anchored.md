# APPENDIX 1 HFE FOCUSING TOOL

The Minimum Health Management Standards (MHMS) require Human Factors Engineering principles to be considered and applied during the early design stage of projects where design can have a critical impact on equipment usability and user safety or health. This process requires the identification of what could be done, and where and when to do it, in design. The focusing tool described below assists in the first step - identifying what could/should be done. The Work Systems Analysis Tool (Appendix 2) helps in the process of identifying where in the project HFE should be applied and when. Both tools combine to help Project Teams identify where project 'hotspots' and performance issues lie.

This HFE Focusing Tool should be jointly completed by the following individuals:

- Project Manager (PM).
- Process Engineer (PE).
- Operations/Maintenance representatives, owner of system.

Focusing questions - what are they used for?

The following questions should be asked by the team in a systematic fashion. The aim is to consider each performance factor in relation to the project being assessed (using the Work Systems Analysis Tool). Each performance factor challenges the team to identify risks and opportunities in the design that can be reduced/realised through targeted application of HFE efforts. The team is challenged to firstly recognise *if and where in the project design* there may be an opportunity, and then to be able to specify *what* should be done to realise the opportunity. Specifying *what HFE scope* needs to be planned into the design process to achieve the desired performance outcome is to be recorded on the Work Systems Analysis Tool. For example - specifying a particular scope of work at a particular time, specifying (existing) HFE standards/specs to be used in the design of particular items, or recognising what design processes may highlight additional opportunities to be exploited and to which HFE scope should be targeted at a later date - e.g. RAM studies, identification of safety critical items, & specific studies for which there should be an HFE component - e.g. escape evacuation and rescue, control room design and layout, etc.

The following questions relate to an aspect of performance to be considered, and should be applied in a creative fashion by the team.

1. **Efficiency in design**

   Is there an opportunity to achieve higher levels of operational efficiency on specified end user tasks through improvements in design? Examples: a more logical and shorter operator's round; easier and fewer manual valve operations; easier and more efficient pigging operations; easier change-out of removable items such as filters and consumables; pump start-up by one instead of two operators, etc.

2. **Maintainability**

   Is there a need to ensure a high level of maintainability for critical items through more focused attention to operator task demands? Examples: to assure achievement of availability targets; in exploiting shorter shutdown or change-out periods; to reduce the likelihood of maintenance errors on safety critical equipment; to reduce waiting time; to reduce adverse working postures; to provide hoisting equipment which is easier to use; saving on scaffolding cost, etc.

3. **Safety and Reliability**

   Is there an opportunity to improve safety and reliability through specific attention to human operations? Examples: reducing the likelihood and impact of identified critical human errors (e.g. reduced switching errors through a well designed overview; quicker and more effective urgent/emergency intervention; preventing unintended control actions, etc.).
