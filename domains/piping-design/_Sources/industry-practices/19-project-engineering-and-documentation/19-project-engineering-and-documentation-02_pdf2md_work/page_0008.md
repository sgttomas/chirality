- IT software/hardware, instrumentation.

An important principle in the approach to meeting the HFE MHMS is that the process of identifying, understanding and specifying the detailed HFE design requirements should be started at an appropriately early stage in the design process, so that these 'requirements' (technical design specifications and/or HFE scopes of work to be executed to derive/check design specifications) are incorporated in the Basic Design and Engineering Package (BDEP) / Project Specification (PS) documents. While further refinement activities may be required at later stages (e.g., in detailed design), this avoids the need to change design at a late stage, when to do so may not be cost effective.

Application of the principle is based on the premise that strong operational performance begins with good design. The operational performance requirements/expectations need to be actively considered in order to specify design requirements that will serve as a platform for desired performance levels.

## 3.3 HFE IN DESIGN

At a high level, the key design principles that should be considered include the need for a user-centred, integrated, proactive and participative approach to design. HFE principles have been developed for a range of work contexts and are all aimed at guiding the designer in the achievement of fit for purpose work systems.

The user-centred design approach should be initiated at the development phase. While HFE efforts are initiated at the development stage, the overall HFE input may well span the entire development and implementation phases.

Design activities shall be addressed from an HFE standpoint and shall not only cover hardware and software, but also consider other critical issues and design interfaces that arise from a project such as organisational design, work cycles, locations, job/task design, and operational and manning philosophy.

The following aspects shall be addressed:

1. Critical impact - This is where poor design can have a negative impact on task and system performance.

2. Equipment usability - This describes the ease with which people interface with machines or technology.

3. User safety and health - Human performance during work is optimised by a user centred design approach, leading to a safe, comfortable and healthy working environment that minimises the likelihood of human error and work related injuries or ill health. Safety refers to occupational health and safety and also major hazard safety.

The HFE design analysis process is detailed in Appendix 4.

## 3.4 ACHIEVEMENT CRITERIA

The Minimum Health Management Standard is achieved when:

1. All new facilities projects are reviewed for minimum HFE requirements.

2. Where the screening indicates that HFE input is necessary, the location or business applies the correct processes, tools and competencies at the correct stages of the project.

3. The HFE recommendations have been discussed with management and action parties and target dates agreed.
