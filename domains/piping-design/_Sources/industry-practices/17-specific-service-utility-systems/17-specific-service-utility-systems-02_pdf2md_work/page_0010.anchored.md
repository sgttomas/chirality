The oxygen flow in piping shall be in one direction only. If flow in two directions cannot be avoided, the Principal's agreement shall be obtained.

To assist removal of the degreasing agent after cleaning, low points in equipment and piping, except underground piping, shall have drain connections without a valve but closed by a blind flange. Underground piping shall not be provided with drains for this purpose.

Above ground oxygen systems shall be earthed.

Nitrogen systems used for automatic purging shall be equipped with a filter and two pressure indicators. They shall be designed to prevent the possibility of oxygen flowing back into the nitrogen system.

Connections for purging and venting shall be situated so that purging and venting of the system can only be done in the normal direction of the oxygen flow.

Where an oxygen line enters the plot of a process unit or a hazardous area, a block valve shall be installed. This valve, however, may only be used in cases of emergency and shall be operated by a coded a fire panel, or remotely from one or more suitable locations.

Where oxygen piping is connected to other piping or equipment that may contain flammable fluid or gas (such as a reactor), provisions shall be made for ensuring separation of the two media during shutdown and startup. Two possible means of achieving this are (1) two double-block-and-bleed arrangements in series, or (2) a pressurised nitrogen wedge in a section of the oxygen pipe.

### 4.3.3 Insulating flanges

Insulating flanges of cathodically protected lines shall only be fitted in a vertical line section in order to avoid the collection of particulate matter that might bridge the insulation gap, Figure 4. The selected design and materials shall be subject to approval by the Principal.

Insulating flanges shall be protected against internal sparking caused by elevated electrical potential differences across the insulation adn stray currents, induction or otherwise. Polarisation cells of sufficient rating shall always be installed across insulating joints or flanges.

### 4.3.4 Vent lines

Vent lines shall be made of stainless steel. They shall be sized for a maximum velocity of 50 m/s. Consequently the pipe diameter may have to be increased immediately downstream of the vent valve. Vent lines should be vertical and straight; however, if bends cannot be avoided, to avoid eddy turbulence they should not be installed within a distance of 20 pipe diameters downstream of the vent valve. To prevent ingress of rain or other foreign matter, a construction as shown in Figure 5 or similar curved vent lines shall be applied. The vent valve shall be sized so that the maximum allowable velocity in the line to be depressurised shall not be exceeded (4.2). In some cases, a control valve may be required.

### 4.3.5 Gaskets

Gaskets shall be carefully sized to prevent any protrusion beyond the free inner surface of the pipe. Various graphite-based gasket constructions with inlay, e.g., Sigraflex-E-foil, have been approved by BAM for conditions up to 200 °C and 130 bar, and reinforced PTFE type gaskets have been approved for 150 °C and 30 bar. Special care shall be taken to prevent graphite flaking off into the line, particularly of the smaller size pipes where thermal stresses are higher. In such cases, the use of graphite-based spiral wound 304 SS gaskets with a 304 SS inner ring may be preferable.

Encapsulated asbestos-type materials, e.g., Durable Black and Garlock 900, as formerly endorsed by the Dutch Safety Gas Association (CAO) shall no longer be used, but replaced by appropriate substitute materials, e.g., Klingersil C4430.
