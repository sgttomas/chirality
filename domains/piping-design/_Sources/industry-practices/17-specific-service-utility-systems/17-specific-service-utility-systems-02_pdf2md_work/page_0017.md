## 4.8 OXYGEN MEASURING STATION

**Amended per Circular 30/00**

When oxygen is supplied, e.g., by a third party, it is often delivered by pipeline. At the point of entry to the plant, a custody transfer measuring station is usually installed. The design and safety philosophy is described below.

Apart from its normal function for accounting purposes, the oxygen measuring station has two additional functions:

- separation of responsibilities of supplier and customer; and
- safeguarding the supplier's system against damage caused by an emergency situation at the customer's end and vice versa.

An oxygen measuring station will normally contain the following main elements (starting from supplier's end): main block valve A filter, flow measuring element, oxygen purity analyser, excess flow valve B with by-pass valve C, and block valve D, see Figure 7.

The filter serves to avoid ingress of dust and other foreign matter from the oxygen supply line into the consumer's distribution network and mainly to minimise the risk of fire in the equipment downstream of that filter (especially the valves). For safety reasons it is recommended to use a single filter only and to avoid the installation of spare filters. Relief valves shall not be installed in the measuring station. Carbon steel shall not be used for the parts of the measuring station from the filter onwards. The drain(s) at the lowest point(s) serving to remove remnants of degreasing agents may be avoided depending on the de-scaling / cleaning / pickling arrangements. Ideally, the high velocity blowing of nitrogen will remove any entrapped liquids at low ends. The benefit of not having a drain is that no liquid / contaminants are trapped unnecessarily. In addition, such a drain is an additional leak point. Main block valve A and excess flow valve B should be controlled so that on failure of one of the power media, the positions (status) of the valves remain unchanged. The valves shall only change position when a command for change is received. The power media for control of these two valves shall be from an uninterruptible maintained supply.

Control valve C is controlled so that it will close when one of the power media fails.

Block valve D has an extended spindle and is operated from behind a fire shield.

Normal manual closing of valve A (via remote station) shall only be performed when the oxygen supply line and measuring station have been taken out of operation. Under certain emergency conditions, valve A shall be closed. These are:

- manual emergency closure from the panel (manned supervising panel) or locally (at measuring station);
- at high flow (say 120 % of contract), sustained for longer than t₁ seconds (where t₁ is closing time of valve B, say 10 sec);

  NOTE: At high flow, excess flow valve B will start to close immediately.

- at low pressure upstream of orifice, sustained for longer than t₁ sec;

  NOTE: At low pressure, e.g., contract minimum less 2 bar, excess flow valve B will start to close immediately.

- at high differential pressure across valve A.

  NOTE: This arrangement keeps valve A closed in the event of a high differential pressure, in order to avoid high velocities through valve and filter during re-opening. This is particularly appropriate in supply systems with a large difference between the pressure at normal flow and pressure at no-flow, e.g., in a long transport line. In order to be able to open valve A, the downstream connection to the excess flow valve B shall be pressurised with nitrogen to approximately the supply line pressure, e.g., in complicated systems, e.g., long supply lines, the approval of equalising the differential line by a standing instruction for the operating personnel (pressure equalisation) may be considered. However, the Principal's agreement must be obtained in writing for this application.
