Aluminium and its alloys shall not be used within the scope of this DEP, which covers the temperature range from -20 °C to +150 °C. Aluminium alloys are extensively used for cryogenic heat exchangers in oxygen-producing plants because of their high thermal conductivity and their suitability for the fabricating of very large heat transfer surface areas. However, the ignition temperature in oxygen is low and so is their melting temperature. The heat of combustion is very high and once the aluminium is ignited, the damage will be extensive and a blow-out will occur.

## 3.2 NON-METALLIC MATERIALS

Organic materials have lower ignition temperatures than metallic materials. Once ignited, they may promote ignition in adjacent metal components. The volume of organic materials should be minimised and they should be surrounded by metal to maximise heat dissipation.

The most common used non-metallic materials in the temperature range of -20 °C to +150 °C are:

- Fluorinated or chlorofluorinated resins and elastomers. They can be reinforced with oxides, glass ceramic, metals and alloys to improve their mechanical properties; however, such reinforcement shall not adversely affect their resistance to ignition.
- Graphite, which can also be used at temperatures well above 150 °C. Graphite is not recommended for the sealing of infrequently moving parts (stuffing boxes), when it is exposed externally to humid atmospheric or corrosive conditions for prolonged periods.

The selection of all non-metallic components shall be based on proof of oxygen compatibility in the required range of temperature and pressure. Such a proof can be derived from the list produced by the Bundesanstalt für Materialforschung und -prüfung (BAM), Berlin, Germany). Non-metallic materials that are not proven by BAM are subject to the approval by the Principal.
