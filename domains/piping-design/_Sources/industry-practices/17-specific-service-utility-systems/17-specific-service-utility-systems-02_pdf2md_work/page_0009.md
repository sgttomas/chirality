## 4.3 PIPING AND FITTINGS

### 4.3.1 Materials

Materials for straight piping, and for bends with a radius of at least 5D, shall be selected in accordance with the velocity limits described in (4.2) and Figure 1. Nickel-based alloys in accordance with (3.1) are required for:

- Fittings where direct impingement of entrained particles may occur, such as short radius elbows and tees, see Figure 2; and valve internals.
- Piping downstream of control valves for a distance of at least 10 times the nominal diameter, see Figure 6.

In areas in Figure 1 where carbon steel or stainless steel construction is permitted, fittings, valve internals and downstream piping of the same material may be used if the velocity limit is reduced by half.

Pipe sizes smaller than DN 50 shall not be used. The minimum wall thickness for carbon steel and stainless steel piping shall be 6 mm; this does not apply to instrument tubing.

### 4.3.2 Design

Oxygen piping systems shall be kept as simple as possible, with the smallest possible number of valves, fittings, branches and nozzles.

Oxygen piping shall be located as far as possible from other piping. Pipes containing flammable fluid or gas or steam should not be installed within 1 m distance of oxygen piping. The free space may be utilised for lines carrying water, nitrogen or other non-flammable fluids at ambient temperature. At points where the above requirements cannot be fulfilled, the Principal shall be asked whether any additional preventive or corrective measures are required.

Oxygen pipelines shall be located on the outermost position of pipe bridges.

Installation of oxygen pipelines through tunnels or (covered or uncovered) trenches should be avoided. If unavoidable, flanged connections or valves shall not be used (except for low point drain connections) and the tunnel or trench shall not contain lines carrying flammable fluids or gases. Oxygen piping shall not support other pipes.

Underground oxygen piping may be installed outside process and other hazardous areas and shall be clearly identified above ground and kept separate from electrical cables and other piping.

The underground piping should have external corrosion protection.

Expansion bellows shall not be used.

Slip-on flanges, lap flanges, or threaded connections for valves and fittings shall not be used. Socket welds are not permitted.

In carbon steel and stainless steel piping systems, branches shall be made with equal tees, reducing tees or swept outlets. In nickel-based alloy piping systems, branches should also be made with equal tees, reducing tees or swept outlets, but branch outlets (weldolets) and branch fittings (nipples) may be used.

Where nickel-based alloy is required in accordance with Figures 1A and 1B, branches should have minimum wall thickness, e.g. Schedule 80.

Changes in diameter shall be minimised. Where changes in diameter are unavoidable, they should be accomplished in steps of one nominal size at a time, and the distance between successive diameter changes should be at least 1000 mm, see Figure 2.

Dead ends in piping shall be avoided, see Figure 3.
