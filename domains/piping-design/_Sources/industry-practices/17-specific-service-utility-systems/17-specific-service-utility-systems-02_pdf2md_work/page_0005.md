## 3. MATERIALS

### 3.1 METALLIC MATERIALS

Dry gaseous oxygen in the temperature range -20 °C to +150 °C is normally handled in carbon steel or austenitic stainless steel, subject to the velocity limitations given in (4.2). Higher velocities generally require the use of nickel-based (or copper-based) alloys. Specific metallic materials for oxygen system components shall be selected in accordance with the guidelines given in (4.2 to 4.7).

#### Carbon steels

Plain carbon steels are extensively used for dry gaseous oxygen service, in particular for transportation pipelines, but also for in plot piping with maximum operating temperatures below 80 °C. The ignition temperatures are relatively high whilst the specific heat and the heat conductivity are sufficiently high to prevent ignition, if the measures in this DEP are followed. Any condensation of water in the oxygen system must be excluded and contaminants in the oxygen must be non-corrosive. Strict cleaning procedures and measures to prevent corrosion during shut down are required. Adequate protection against external corrosion, in particular under external insulation or fire protection, is required as well. For minimum design temperatures below 0 °C, carbon steels shall have an impact toughness > 27J at -20 °C to avoid the risk of brittle fracture.

#### Stainless steels

Plain austenitic stainless steels, which nominally contain some 18%w of chromium and 8%w of nickel, generally have a higher ignition temperature than carbon steels and are more suitable for oxygen service. Oxygen cleanliness is more easily maintained. Suitable grades are S30403, S32100 and S31603. Grades with higher chromium concentrations should not be used. Likewise, duplex stainless steels that have higher chromium contents should not be used. Proper gas shielding during welding is required to prevent oxidation at the internal surfaces near the weld. Externally, protection is required against pitting corrosion and at temperatures above 50 °C against chloride stress corrosion cracking.

#### Nickel-based alloys

Nickel-based alloys with low chromium content are practically unignitable in oxygen. They have a high ignition temperature and the heat of combustion is low. Pure nickel (N02200) has a high thermal conductivity, but alloying elements decrease it. The most common alloys used for oxygen service are:

- 15 %Cr-8 %Fe nickel alloy N06600 (e.g. Inconel 600). This high temperature alloy usually has a carbon content of about 0.08 %w and a coarse grain size resulting in a sensitised microstructure and poor corrosion resistance in many environments. A particular concern is intergranular stress corrosion cracking in steam environments. In steam above 50° in external corrosive environment, the N06600 alloy shall be specified with a carbon content below 0.025 %w. This will reduce the allowable design stresses.

- 30 %Cu nickel alloy N04400, e.g., Monel 400. Good weldability with resistance to liquation cracking is to be assured through close specification of the silicon content.

The use of other nickel-based alloys that contain higher chromium, iron or molybdenum contents than those of the above alloys requires approval by the Principal, if the conditions are beyond the limits for stainless steel.

#### Copper and Silver

More noble metals like copper and silver have a lower heat of combustion than nickel, but their lower melting points result in lower ignition temperatures. Copper and silver with a low alloy content are suitable for low stress components in oxygen service. Alloys containing elements that are reactive with oxygen, like aluminium and zinc, should not be used.
