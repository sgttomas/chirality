## 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 SCOPE

This DEP specifies requirements and gives recommendations for the design of heat transfer fluid (HTF) systems operating at bulk temperature levels up to 400 °C. It includes the application of the organic (both mineral and synthetic) and silicone-based types of HTFs but excludes water/steam and molten, inorganic salts. The scope of this DEP is restricted to liquid phase operation only.

This DEP refers to HTFs as defined in (1.3.2) and explicitly excludes 'Hot Oils'. HTFs have the ability of long term operation at far higher operating temperatures than hot oils, at relatively low pressure and with low degradation and fouling.

This DEP is a revision of the DEP of the same number dated December 1998.

### 1.2 DISTRIBUTION, INTENDED USE AND REGULATORY CONSIDERATIONS

Unless otherwise authorised by Shell GSI, the distribution of this DEP is confined to Shell companies and, where necessary, to Contractors and Manufacturers/Suppliers nominated by them.

This DEP is intended for use in oil refineries, chemical plants, gas plants and, where applicable, in exploration and production facilities and supply/marketing installations.

When DEPs are applied, a Management of Change (MOC) process should be implemented; this is of particular importance when existing facilities are to be modified.

If national and/or local regulations exist in which some of the requirements may be more stringent than in this DEP the Contractor shall determine by careful scrutiny which of the requirements are the more stringent and which combination of requirements will be acceptable as regards safety, environmental, economic and legal aspects. In all cases the Contractor shall inform the Principal of any deviation from the requirements of this DEP which is considered to be necessary in order to comply with national and/or local regulations. The Principal may then negotiate with the Authorities concerned with the object of obtaining agreement to follow this DEP as closely as possible.

### 1.3 DEFINITIONS

#### 1.3.1 General definitions

The **Contractor** is the party which carries out all or part of the design, engineering, procurement, construction, commissioning or management of the project, or operation of a facility. The Principal may undertake all or part of the duties of the Contractor.

The **Manufacturer/Supplier** is the party which manufactures or supplies HTFs, equipment and services to perform the duties specified by the Contractor.

The **Principal** is the party which initiates the project and ultimately pays for its design and construction. The Principal will typically specify the technical requirements. The Principal may also appoint an agent or consultant authorised to act for and on behalf of the Principal.

The word **shall** indicates a requirement.

The word **should** indicates a recommendation.

### 1.3.2 Specific definitions

The temperature and pressure terms used in this DEP are defined in DEP 01.00.01.30-Gen. Further terms are defined below.

**Expansion vessel**
A vessel to accommodate expanded volume and surge of the HTF, and allows for venting of low boiling point components from the system.

**Heat transfer fluid (HTF)**
A fluid capable of transporting heat energy within a specified temperature range. Specific hydrocarbon-based fluids are available on the open market for use in heat transfer applications. Generally, this includes synthetic aromatic, silicone-based and a limited selection of specific HTF designated mineral oils.

**Heater**
The heat energy producer in the system. Either a heat exchanger, a furnace or a waste heat recovery unit (WHRU).

**Hot oil**
A mineral oil product/process stream used for heat transfer purposes but not commercially available as branded HTF.

**HTF system**
All heaters, piping, pumps, vessels, heat exchangers and auxiliaries that constitute the closed circuit containing the HTF.

**Maximum allowable bulk temperature (MABT)**
The maximum bulk temperature of the HTF allowed anywhere in the circuit, as specified by the Manufacturer of the HTF.

NOTE: The Principal may specify a Maximum Operating Temperature that is lower than the MABT for a specific application.

**Maximum allowable film temperature (MAFT)**
The maximum temperature to which the HTF may be subjected in the heater. This highest temperature is on the tube inner wall of the heater and is determined by the heat flux and heat transfer coefficient. The Manufacturer of the HTF recommends the MAFT.

**Maximum operating temperature (MOT)**
The maximum HTF bulk temperature at which the system is designed to operate, as measured at the outlet of the heat source.

**Minimum operating temperature**
The lowest HTF bulk temperature at which the system is designed to start up. It should always be higher than the pumpability limit.

NOTE: This temperature shall be selected so that the HTF viscosity is lower than the pumpability limit (HTF viscosity of 50–500 mm²/s at start up, depending on pump size) and so that the MAFT is not exceeded in the heater during start up.

**Return temperature**
The temperature at which the HTF returns to the heater.

**Storage tank**
A tank for storage of HTF.

**Supply temperature**
The temperature at which the HTF is made available to the battery limit of the users.

**Trim cooler**
A cooler in the heating circuit, used to trim the temperature of the HTF.

**Pump-out cooler**
A cooler in the run-down line to the storage tank to cool down hot HTF in case of evacuation of the system.

NOTE: The Trim Cooler may also be used as Pump-out Cooler.

## 2. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS FOR HEAT TRANSFER SYSTEMS

### 2.1 GENERAL

The Principal shall specify the HTF and MOT to be used.

Figures 1A and 1B show possible simplified schemes of HTF systems, one with a furnace and the other with a WHRU. They identify the main system components and basic controls.

NOTE: The final list of the control scheme depends on the type of heat recovery, operational requirements and conditions and HTF characteristics in relation to operating temperatures.

For detailed design guidance reference is made to standard drawing S 01.005.

The Contractor shall develop separate PFS for each operating case of the HTF system resulting from the different operating modes (operating cases) of a facility/plant. PFS shall be prepared following the requirements indicated in DEP 01.00.02.11-Gen and DEP 31.10.03.10-Gen.

The HTF system should be designed for automated control from start-up to minimum stable operating duty and to design duty with minimum fuel consumption in all operating cases. Liquid burners may require to be started locally.

The flash point of HTF reduces during operation due to formation of low boiling point components by degradation of the HTF. The reduced flash point will lower the NPSH-Available and may affect the area classification at HTF containing lines and equipment. For this reason welded instead of flanged connections are applied.

The Contractor shall specify the lowest acceptable flash point for design and operation.

### 2.2 HEATER

The heat source/energy producer may be a heater (furnace), a waste heat recovery unit (WHRU) linked to a gas turbine or other hot flue-gas producer, or a heat exchanger. The heater design shall ensure that the HTF will not be subjected to temperatures above the maximum allowable film and bulk temperatures.

The Principal shall specify the fuel or fuels for firing a dedicated furnace, if selected as main heater.

If parallel bundles are used for heating, the manifold shall be designed so that the ratio of lowest-to-highest flow is maximum 0.95.

To determine the design duty of the heater, a margin of 10 % on duty should be added to the system operating case with the highest thermal process demand. No additional process design margins should be added to the duty of the consumers in determining the heater design duty.

In addition, the Contractor shall determine and add an allowance for heat losses from the system.

For the heating coil in a WHRU, doubling the design pressure shall be considered so that higher schedule tubes (wall thickness) are applied to avoid a process shutdown due to leakage of the HTF coil.

### 2.3 EXPANSION VESSEL

#### **2.3.1 Purpose**

The expansion vessel allows for thermal expansion of the HTF and for venting low boiling point components generated in the HTF during normal operation and for venting inerts and vaporised water during initial HTF drying and start-up. The expansion vessel is used to minimise the consequences of upsets in the HTF system operation. The designer shall take into account the following:

- the need to accommodate the thermal expansion of the HTF heated from minimum to maximum operating temperature;

- the need to maintain the required Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH) for the HTF circulating pumps under all operational scenarios;

- the need to reduce the risk of HTF loss into the flare system. In the event of a tube rupture inside heat transfer equipment operating at process pressures above that of the HTF circuit, sudden displacement of fluid from the system into the system can result in HTF loss into the flare system. IPF classification shall be performed in accordance with DEP 32.80.10.10 Gen to indicate whether measures need to be taken;

- venting to remove residual water present in the circuit before start-up;

- the need for sufficient HTF inventory to allow filling of equipment and during re-commissioning after shutdown for maintenance. The largest volume of the individual equipment that can be maintained while the HTF system remains in operation will determine this inventory.

## 2.3.2 Location and configuration

The expansion vessel shall be connected to the system return line on the pump suction side. The vessel should be elevated so that the normal operating level of the HTF in the vessel is higher than the highest possible HTF level in the system. (This will facilitate proper venting and provide sufficient NPSH for the pump.)

If this requirement is difficult to meet, a lower elevation may be selected but additional design measures are required to prevent vapour lock: looking at the entire closed fluid circuit; to evacuate such a vapour lock if it occurs; to cater for HTF draining into the expansion vessel during a shut-down to avoid HTF overflow into the flare system; and to ensure that the HTF system pressure remains positive at the highest point to avoid boiling.

Refer to (2.7) for height considerations to ensure there is sufficient NPSH available.

A distinction is made between systems with and without an automatic HTF make-up and draw-off facility. An operator should normally initiate HTF make-up and draw-off; automatic HTF make-up and draw-off shall be incorporated in the design only if there is a clear justification.

The vessel and its piping shall be designed for the full flow of the HTF through the vessel. The "single-leg" design ("cold fluid" expansion vessel) may be chosen only with the approval of the Principal.

The expansion vessel shall be connected to the flare and be equipped with a nitrogen (purge quality) blanket to serve as a barrier between the hot fluid (usually operating at a temperature above the flash and fire point of the HTF) and the flare. The vessel's vapour space shall be prevented from contacting the atmosphere, as this would speed oxidation of the HTF and allow moisture to enter the system during shutdown periods (which might create corrosive acid compounds and a safety hazard).

For operation at high temperatures, particularly approaching or exceeding the boiling point of the HTF, a positive pressure of at least 1 bar to 2 bar above the vapour pressure of the HTF (at this temperature) shall be maintained.

The nitrogen supply shall be equipped with a split-range controller and a non-return valve, which will regulate the nitrogen supply and its vent to flare.

A dead pressure zone is required between the nitrogen supply pressure and the vent-to-flare set pressure. In this dead zone, the pressure is not controlled and is allowed to float freely while the nitrogen supply and vent-to-flare valves are both closed. This dead zone will reduce nitrogen consumption and lower the starting point of venting low boiling point components.

The non-return valve shall prevent HTF vapour and nitrogen back-flow into the nitrogen system in the event of a pressure increase in the vessel.

Low boiling degradation products shall be vented on pressure control and routed to the flare. If regular venting of vapours is unavoidable and/or the collection of HTF in the flare knockout drum undesirable e.g. for operational or HSE reasons, the vent line should be routed via a condenser to a collecting drum prior to venting the non-condensables to flare. The liquid HTF is collected for safe disposal off-site. Collected liquids can also be routed via the on-line purification unit for recovering part of the HTF (2.9.5).

The expansion vessel shall be equipped with a safety relief valve arrangement capable of protecting the system against over-pressure caused by events such as fluid degradation, contamination, malperation, overheating or tube failure in equipment. Other relief cases are to be considered during HAZOP or safety review.

The safety relief outlet line shall be routed to flare.

If the ambient temperature can fall below the freezing point of the fluid or its degradation products, or if accumulated high boiling point components can plug the lines, nitrogen lines and valves, vent-back-pressure lines and valves, and safety relief lines and valves shall be heat traced in order to prevent plugging.

If the ambient temperature can fall below the HTF's pumpability limit, heating the expansion vessel should be considered. In order to eliminate the risk of HTF contamination in the event of coil rupture, either electrical heating or external heat tracing should be applied.

The expansion vessel shall be insulated and shall be a vertical vessel.

## 2.3.3 Sizing

The size of the expansion vessel depends upon the expansion coefficient, maximum temperature and quantity of the fluid in the circuit. The preliminary sizing shall be based upon the estimated quantity of fluid and shall be adjusted when the final design stage is completed. It is the responsibility of the Contractor to review and, if necessary, revise the vessel dimensions during the detailed design phase, when the relevant equipment sizes, line sizes, plot plans and piping layouts are known.

The expansion vessel has the combined function of an expansion vessel and a knock-out vessel. It shall have sufficient capacity to cater for various operating upsets in the system. The vessel dimensions shall be calculated and the maximum level set according to DEP 31.22.05.11-Gen.

The expansion vessel shall allow for degassing of the HTF and shall as a minimum be fitted with a half open pipe inlet device.

The vessel can be divided into three sections, as follows:

1) Low-Low up to Low level

   The expansion vessel shall be located high enough to ensure adequate NPSH for the HTF circulation pump at Low-Low level. It shall be checked whether the NPSH requirements are met at all relevant working temperatures. The inventory between Low-Low and Low level should be 25 % of the vessel volume.

2) Low up to High level

   The volume between High and Low level shall be equal to the expansion volume of the total fluid inventory from minimum possible operating temperature to maximum possible operating temperature in the system. An additional 20 % shall be added to cater for various operating upsets in the systems such as vaporisation of residual water in the system and a tube burst.

   Although the design pressure on the HTF side may be higher than that on the process side, the working pressure may be the reverse, which means that process fluid could enter the HTF in the event of a tube rupture.

3  Above High level

The return line inlet device shall be fitted according to the requirements of DEP 31.22.05.11-Gen.

The remaining volume of the vessel volume shall allow for gas-liquid separation and be filled with nitrogen.

## 2.4 STORAGE TANK AND SPARE HTF INVENTORY

The HTF storage tank shall have sufficient capacity to hold the full inventory of the system, plus an additional 10 % volume to accommodate make-up of losses caused by venting and mechanical leaks. On plants with multiple parallel trains it may be justified to reduce the storage tank capacity to hold the inventory of a single train only unless it is feasible that these trains must be drained at the same time.

The minimum fluid level in the tank shall be set to ensure sufficient NPSH for the make-up pump. If the ambient temperature can fall below the HTF minimum pumpability temperature, the tank should be heated, preferably electrically, and the suction line to the pump should be heat-traced and insulated. The storage tank shall be equipped with a purge quality nitrogen blanket to serve as a barrier between the fluid and the atmosphere to limit aging (oxidation) and moisture ingress.

Drainage of hot HTF to the storage tank shall not be possible. Hot HTF shall be cooled prior to entering the storage tank by a pump-out cooler or other cooling means, such as cooling with the trim cooler or via the process.

NOTE: The HTF inventory can be cooled by alternative means such as with a column reboiler on HTF with the column operating on total reflux and thus using the overhead condenser as indirect means of cooling the HTF in the column. Alternatively, gas turbine combustion air fans can be used to cool down the furnace while HTF is circulated through the heater tubes.

A complete spare HTF inventory should be available to replace a total loss of HTF from the system due to leakage or contamination by a process fluid.

The spare inventory is preferably available as warehouse spare in drums, containers or a separate storage tank. The storage of spare HTF in a single tank, designed to also receive hot HTF from the operating system as indicated above, may be considered but before the operating HTF is drained into the storage tank it shall be confirmed by analysis of a sample that the HTF is suitable for further use so that the spare inventory is not contaminated with unsuitable HTF. If the HTF is no longer suitable due to aging or fouling or is otherwise contaminated, it shall be removed from the system. Facilities such as connections for a road tanker shall be provided to drain the cooled HTF from the system. The system shall be cleaned before the new HTF is introduced.

## 2.5 PIPING

Piping shall be carbon steel unless specified otherwise. To minimise the possibility of leakage, the connections in piping, fittings, and equipment shall be welded as far as possible.

HTF decomposition increases exponentially with temperature in accordance with thermal cracking principles. To reduce decomposition, the time the HTF is exposed to the maximum operating temperature shall be minimised. For this reason the supply header with HTF at MOT from heater to consumers shall be as short as possible.

All low/high points of piping and low/high points inside equipment isolation valves shall be equipped with hard piped drains/vents to a closed drain system for removal of residual liquid prior to disconnecting the system. Vents shall have a sight glass in the down-flow line to detect flow of HTF. Drain and vent lines connecting to the drain header shall be as short as possible.

NOTE: Hydro-test vents and drains shall also be equipped with a block valve and a blind.

Provisions shall be made for blowing out the system with hot air or (preferably) nitrogen to ensure the system is dry prior to start-up.

In systems with heat users operating at pressures above that of the HTF system, the piping design shall take account of all hazards caused by a tube rupture inside this equipment.

HTF distribution headers and piping to consumers shall be sized for 110 % of the maximum flow.

The spillover lines and control valves shall be sized for the flow of the largest consumer to allow for a sudden block-off of the heat user. Manual bypass lines shall be sized for 100 % flow.

The following shall apply except for double-pipe heat exchangers:

If the process pressure exceeds the HTF system pressure, the preferred arrangement is to ensure a free flow (no valves) from the consumer (heat exchanger) to the expansion vessel. If valves are installed, the following alternatives may be applied:

1. The HTF system shall be designed for the higher pressure (two-thirds rule according to DEP 01.00.01.30-Gen.); or,

2. Overpressure protection devices (safety relief valves or rupture disks) shall be installed at the outlets of the affected heat exchangers, with relief to flare via a liquid separator.

Piping shall be in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.11-Gen. and DEP 31.38.01.12-Gen. (piping class 31160 or 61160). Flanged connections to valves, fittings, and equipment shall be avoided where possible.

Pipe supports shall be in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.29-Gen.

Large systems may require the installation of Remote Operated Valves (ROVs) for sectionalising in the event of major leakage and to mitigate any loss of HTF during a fire.

## 2.6 HEAT EXCHANGERS (CONSUMERS)

If designed and operated properly, HTF systems can be considered to be non-fouling, so U-tube type heat exchangers may be applied if the HTF flows inside the tubes. This is cheaper than floating head type heat exchangers and significantly reduces the risk of leakage and, consequently, contamination of the HTF or process fluid.

To ensure proper design of the heat exchangers, the requisition sheet shall clearly mention the type of HTF to be used, or its trade name. For the design specification of HTF systems a fouling resistance of 0.00017 m²/kW shall be taken. This is lower than the values listed in DEP 20.21.00.31-Gen. due to the low fouling potential.

Effects of leakage of HTF into the process or vice-versa shall be reviewed and double welded tube-to-tube sheet connections shall be specified, as required.

All heat exchangers shall be equipped with hard piped drains and vents to allow the HTF to be drained into a collection system from where it can be pumped back to the storage tank (e.g. to allow maintenance of the system). To speed up the evacuation, a nitrogen purge point shall be installed to allow a hose connection from a nearby utility station.

## 2.7 PUMPS

Two 100 % capacity pumps shall be provided unless there is a clear justification for three 50 % capacity pumps. If continuous filtration is applied via a bypass across the pump, the capacity of the pumps shall include this additional flow.

In the event of low HTF pressure, the spare pump shall take over automatically. This shall be indicated in the control room by an audible and visual alarm. The stand-by pump shall be maintained in a pre-heated state in order to avoid thermal shock when starting. On

larger pumps this may require the bypass across the discharge check valve to be routed via the pump casing drain instead of across the check valve alone.

Pump type selection shall be as specified in DEP 31.29.02.11-Gen.

Pump seals shall be selected based on the hazard classification in DEP 31.29.00.33-Gen. Dual seals shall be applied in hazardous service as defined in DEP 31.29.02.11-Gen. When dual seals are required, the seal barrier fluid on dual seals shall preferably be HTF taken from the system, unless this is unacceptable for HSE reasons.

The requisition sheet for pumps and mechanical seals shall clearly mention the type of HTF to be used, or its trade name, and the minimum operating temperature and associated physical properties during start-up.

As the HTF will degrade or may absorb material from the flare, the vapour pressure of the HTF in the system will be higher than that of the pure HTF as supplied by the Manufacturer. This lowers the NPSH. To determine the NPSH available to the pump, it shall be assumed that the vapour pressure of the HTF is equal to the pressure in the expansion vessel at normal operating temperature. If necessary, the height of the drum shall be raised to ensure that there is sufficient NPSH available.

## 2.8 INSTRUMENTATION AND CONTROL

Instrumentation shall be selected and designed according to DEP 32.31.00.32-Gen. and DEP 32.36.01.17-Gen.

The system shall be designed to allow stable operation at continuous turndown of heat demand from design heat duty to zero. As heat demand from, and HTF flow to, the heat consumers decreases, HTF flow to the HTF heater shall be maintained at or above of certain minimum level to avoid high film temperatures, depending on the heater design. This is achieved by a flow controller in the HTF feed line to the heater, which opens the spillover bypass. An example is given in Figure 1A where the flow controller is overriding the PDC signal to the spillover bypass. If the flow through the spillover bypass is not sufficient, the signal can also override the return temperature controller and open the full-flow bypass.

A fired heater will turn down to its minimum firing condition and the required heater outlet HTF temperature shall be maintained by dissipating the heat in the trim cooler that is either parallel to the consumers or in the return line to the heater. In Figure 1A the furnace outlet temperature controller, together with the flow controller, can decrease the set point of the return temperature controller. The return temperature controller will increase the flow through the cooler by opening the full flow bypass. As a result the PDC controller will decrease the flow in the spillover bypass.

If a WHRU is used instead of a fired heater, there might be no turndown of heat input to HTF available. In such a case, the flow rate through the heating coil shall be controlled or maintained, and coolers shall dissipate excess absorbed heat. The position and number of coolers shall be optimised for the system design. In a system with only a trim cooler in the bypass across the consumers as shown in Figure 1B, the WHRU HTF outlet temperature is controlled via the flow through the WHRU, which, in turn, is controlled via a bypass across the pump. If the bypass across the WHRU is at minimum, the trim cooler bypass is closed, and the WHRU HTF outlet temperature exceeds the set point, the resulting signal from the temperature and flow controllers will decrease the set point of the return temperature controller. Similar to Figure 1A, this results in a lower WHRU inlet temperature.

In a WHRU with a cyclic operating heat recovery coil, a trim cooler is also installed in the supply line from the WHRU to the consumers as described in (2.9.1). This cooler will allow immediate reduction in temperature following a change in heat recovery of the cyclic operating coil. In addition to the control function as described above, the supply header temperature controller also also manipulates the flow to the trim cooler or the bypass.

If the temperature cannot be maintained by closing the feed to the trim cooler and opening the trim cooler bypass, the temperature controller will open the bypass across the pump to

reduce the flow through the WHRU, thereby decreasing the HTF circulation rate and increasing the supply temperature.

Heater firing shall be tripped if the HTF flow to the heater falls below the minimum allowed. The trip shall be provided with a timer (10 s) to override the trip signal during a short period of low flow. Such moments of low flow may take place when the spare circulation pump - triggered by the HTF low-pressure reading (signal) - takes over automatically. The minimum circulation flow trip shall be set at approximately 70 % of minimum flow as specified for the furnace.

The Contractor may propose alternative control strategies, subject to the Principal's approval.

## 2.9 MISCELLANEOUS

### 2.9.1 Trim cooler

In order to improve operation and increase the flexibility of the HTF system, a trim cooler should be installed which is capable of rejecting the minimum heater duty. Typically, an air-cooled heat exchanger should be selected.

NOTE: On systems with a furnace, the function of the trim cooler may be provided by a process cooler if trim cooling is only required during start-up and shut down of the system. Alternative means are e.g. a column reboiler in line with the process consumer or total reflux, thereby using the overhead condenser as an indirect means of cooling the HTF circulation system.

A trim cooler shall be installed on systems with a WHRU in the exhaust of a gas turbine as described in section (2.8) where, and the size cannot be as large as the heat duty of all consumers. In systems with a cycling operated heat recovery coil in the WHRU, such as regeneration gas heating, a separate trim cooler in the outlet of the WHRU is required to maintain a constant HTF supply temperature to the process consumers.

### 2.9.2 Insulation

Because of their low surface tension and low viscosity at operating temperatures, HTFs have a tendency to penetrate joints, gaskets and seals. This results in leaks that can lead to accumulation of fluid inside insulation. Insulation materials such as mineral wool or similar, when saturated with organic HTFs, can cause slow exothermic oxidation starting at temperatures above 250 °C. The large internal surface area, poor heat dissipation and the possible catalytic activity of the insulation material may cause significant temperature build-up within the insulation mass. Such slow reaction may progress undetected and may lead to unsafe situations such as sudden fires when cladding is damaged or opened for maintenance.

Non-absorbent insulation (e.g. foam glass) or no insulation at all shall be used at potential HTF 'creep' locations (instrument connections, valve stems, flanges and joints). For silicone-based fluid, closed cell styrofoam (or similar) may be used.

Insulation shall be as specified in DEP 30.46.00.31-Gen.

### 2.9.3 Strainers and filters

During commissioning, the suction line of the circulation pumps shall be fitted with a temporary wire mesh strainer to prevent mill scale, weld spatter, sand and other debris from entering the pump (see Standard Drawing S.38.002). To avoid flow obstruction during operation, the fine mesh should be removed as soon as these solids have been removed from the system.

Fitting permanent filters shall be considered for continuously filtering the HTF. Permanent filters shall be installed when the HTF Supplier predicts formation of high boiling point components. If permanent filters are installed, the bypass shall be fitted in the bypass around the pump. Both a differential pressure indicator across the filters and a flow indicator in the bypass shall be fitted to allow monitoring of fouling and control of the amount flowing through the bypass. Filters shall allow the installation of 75 μm to 100 μm

elements during commissioning and initial operation, and subsequently these shall be replaced with 10 μm to 20 μm elements, unless the HTF Manufacturer of makes more stringent recommendations.

If permanent filters are not installed, then suitably located and sized connection points with block valves shall be provided for periodic hook-up of filters e.g. after a maintenance shut-down.

## 2.9.4 Maintenance drain system

If specified by the Principal, a hard piped closed drain system for maintenance purposes shall be provided.

The purpose of a HTF drain system is to collect in a controlled manner the HTF from piping drains/vents, and equipment drain/vent points prior to equipment maintenance, so it can be returned later to the system for re-use or controlled disposal, as required. In addition, a drain system will reduce spillage of HTF, which could lead to HSE incidents.

The HTF maintenance drain system as indicated on standard drawing S 01.005 shall be separate from, and not be interconnected, to any other plant drain system (other than HTF).

The drain piping should be installed underground and be free flowing to a closed collection vessel. Because the installation of drain piping is underground, the drain system is solely for the collection and draining of cooled down HTF.

The drain header shall be routed as close as possible to the drain points to reduce the length of small bore drain piping.

Where a free flow of drained HTF is not feasible, then an above ground nitrogen purge assisted drain line may be considered. A suitably sized vent shall be made on the collecting drum to vent the nitrogen to flare.

The collection drum shall be purged to prevent ingress of air and/or moisture from the flare, and be located in a (dry) pit for secondary containment.

The collection drum shall be sized to receive the HTF volume from the largest consumer or group of consumers that can be taken out of service at the same time. The collection vessel shall be provided with a pump for returning the HTF into the main system, or for the filling of drums or other means for HTF disposal.

The collection drum should be fitted with a heating coil to allow hot HTF to be used to heat the contents of the vessel. This can be used to boil off any moisture and to boil off light components from fresh HTF, if the collection vessel is also used for make-up of fresh HTF into the system. For this reason a filling connection shall be available for connecting a drum pump discharge. This connection may be combined with a HTF sample disposal funnel connected to the drain vessel.

## 2.9.5 On-line HTF purification

All HTF will decompose during operation and will form low boiling point components and may also form high boiling point components. The degree of decomposition depends strongly on the type of HTF, the heater design, and the maximum (feed line) operating temperature (MOT) in relation to the MABT.

Especially the high boiling point components may lead to unacceptable fouling in a system.

When specified by the Principal, a HTF purification unit shall be installed.

The purifier shall be designed to remove the high boiling point components from the HTF depending on the quantity of high boiling point components predicted by the HTF Manufacturer. As a minimum, the purifier shall be sized to treat 1 % of the total system flow.

The purification unit is used to separate the high boiling point components from the HTF in the system via batch distillation. The HTF still suitable for continued operation is returned into the system, while the residual batch with the majority of high boiling point components is collected for disposal.

The heat duty for the distillation shall be provided by the HTF system and the duty added to the total heater duty.
