# 1   Objective

The Nanushuk Processing Facility (NPF) is an oil and gas processing facility where liquids are processed and / or temporarily stored. These liquid processing and storage facilities include module sumps, tanks, vessels and other pieces of equipment. During the life of the facility, most of these locations will periodically need to be drained to allow inspection or maintenance.

The objective of this document is to identify the minimum design requirements for the drainage of process and utility equipment and the management and control of unplanned releases associated with equipment and other plant components whether located within or outside of modules enclosures.

# 2   Summary

During planned activities, such as routine equipment maintenance, hydrocarbon liquids and produced water will be brought to a minimum liquid level through the process and then drained to the Closed Hydrocarbon Drain System (CHDS). Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility station will be used to push the liquid from equipment to the collection headers which will be sent to the LP Flare KO Drum which will act as a degassing drum. From the LP Flare KO Drum, hydrocarbon liquids and produced water will be pumped to the Slop Oil Tank where it can be introduced back into the process or trucked for disposal. Other fluids (glycol, lube oil, chemicals) are not part of the CHDS and would be drained and transported for disposal separately.

Spill containment is done by utilizing equipment skid drip rims and the module sealed floor/drip rim as the means of containing spills within a module. In each module there will be multiple small sumps equipped with a dip-tube with a hose connection so that a portable air-operated diaphragm pump could be connected to pump out the sump for disposal. There will be one large sump in each module for collecting significant liquid releases. The large sumps in all modules will be also equipped with a dip-tube so that a portable air-operated pump can be connected to evacuate the sump. In addition to the dip-tubes, the sumps in the Process and Produced Water modules will be equipped with a remote-start electric pump that can pump out the sump to a TBD location (possibly a pit within NPF) to avoid sending aerated liquid into the CHDS; this will be evaluated in FEED. The exact quantity of the small sumps and exact location of the sumps inside the module will be determined during detailed design.

Leaks from equipment located outdoors will be mitigated by minimizing flange connections in all services. No secondary containment will be provided for catastrophic liquid release from outdoors equipment or components other than containment required for storage tanks.

# 3    Design and Application

## 3.1    Closed HC Drain System

Drainage to the CHDS is a normal part of facility operation. The frequency of action may vary, but the tasks associated with that activity rarely change.

The CHDS collects fluids containing hydrocarbons or other production related liquids released from vessels, exchangers, compressor auxiliaries and other equipment in each of the modules without losing containment. These liquids are routed via a separate header collection header to the LP Flare KO Drum for degassing and then pumped to the Slop Oil Tank for disposal or re-introduced into the hydrocarbon processes for further processing. The slop oil tank will have a truck connection for disposal of drained liquid if sending back to the process is not desirable. The CHDS header should be made of CRA to withstand corrosion. Electric pumps are preferred for service where hydrocarbons are expected. Provision for biocide injection is required to inhibit growth of bacteria in the water leg of the system and LP Flare KO Drum.

### 3.1.1    Frequent Drainage

Where frequent drainage is required, such as compressor case drains prior to every startup, vessel bridles, in-line filters, instruments with chambers, such as level gauges and displacer level transmitters, bleed valve in double block and bleed isolation, hard piping will be used for connecting hydrocarbon fluids to the CHDS.

Permanent connections will be provided, as required, with restriction orifice to restrict flow into the CHDS. The CHDS will be protected from potential overpressure (blow-by) through the rigorous analysis and design practices validated / guided by HAZOP.

### 3.1.2    Equipment Drainage

For maintenance of equipment, the equipment will be drained as much as possible within the process system (i.e. vessels run to their lowest levels) followed by final drainage to the CHDS. Hard piped connections will be used to connect equipment drains to the CHDS. A spectacle blind (or dropout spool) will be provided close to the equipment for positive isolation of the equipment to the CHDS during normal operation.

Equipment with large volume remaining will be drained to the CHDS using nitrogen or fuel gas.

#### 3.1.2.1 Small Equipment

Draining small equipment equipped with isolation valves such as pumps, filters, etc. will be done via the use of a single drain pipe. Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility stations may be used to blow the liquids to the CHDS header where they would gravity drain to the LP Flare KO Drum.

For maintenance of Rotating and Static Equipment, the equipment casing drains are carried to the skid edge and terminated with a valve. For draining, hoses will be connected to these drains and connected to the CHDS headers located nearby. Compressor case drains will be hard piped to the CHDS. Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility stations may be used to help drain the equipment casings and hoses.

## 3.2 Open Drain System

All modules at the NPF will have open low point sumps to collect liquids that may fall to the module floor. The Open Drain System (ODS) will include two types of sumps. The first type of sumps will be used for containment of limited spills and wash-down fluids. These module drain sumps will be equipped with a dip tube with a hose connection so that a portable air-operated diaphragm pump could be connected to pump the sump for disposal. They will be also equipped with a truck connection at the bottom for the large spills. There will be one large sump in each module for collecting significant liquid releases. The large sumps in all modules will be also equipped with a dip-tube so that a vacuum truck can easily connect to evacuate the sump. In addition to the dip-tubes, the sumps in the Process and Produced Water modules can be equipped with a remote-start electric pump that can pump out the sump to a TBD location (possibly a pit within NPF) to avoid sending aerated liquid into the CHDS. The module sumps will not be designed as a secondary containment for process fluids, but for maintenance activities and small spills. Module sumps will be equipped with high liquid level indication to alert operations.

The Instrument Air Compressor and Air Dryer packages will continuously drain water condensate to the module sump. For this reason, a dedicated sump must be located near these skids. This water will need to be disposed of by pump or vacuum truck to a dedicated Sewage Disposal Treatment Plant. If vacuum truck is used, sump shall have hard piped drain connection.

## 3.3 Spills within Modules

Spills are those releases of liquids or loss of pressure containment which occur without prior scheduling (i.e. pump seal failure, flange gasket failure, etc.). While not scheduled, there can be some pre-planning to contain the spill to a designated controlled area.

Each module is designed with a sealed floor and a 4" tall rim around the module floor perimeter. This generates a containment volume to hold drips or spills which may occur at any location within the module. To avoid having spills running across the module floor, module sumps will be located throughout the module to allow easy collection of liquids to a common point for disposal. The location of the sump(s) would be as close as possible to high potential spill equipment. In addition to the 4" tall rim the module floor dams at interface with utilidor or equipment corridor for ease of moving equipment may be also considered or provide a ramp to facilitate equipment move from one module to another, especially from non-classified to classified module.

The response to a spill within the module will be handled differently depending on the size of the release:

- Small drips and spills will be cleaned using absorbent materials.
- Liquid on the module floor due to a larger leak will be displaced using squeegees toward a module drain sump where it will be vacuumed out. Each module sump will have a dedicated air pump that can be used to drain each sump within the module.
- Large liquid release will be contained within the 4" lip sealed floor. A hard piped open HC drain sump will allow pumping out the bulk of the liquid prior to the Operator entering the module to finalize the cleaning effort.

## 3.3.1 Small Drips and Spills

Small spills will be collected with absorbent materials. A small containment will be immediately placed under the leak until it can be attended to.

For Rotating Equipment, all skids extend beyond all equipment and have a drip rim or a drip containment lip to trap any liquid drips or small spills within the skid. This includes both process liquids and lubricating oil. The skid is sloped to a low point drain which has a valve in it. For small spills, manual cleanup of the skid can be done with towels and mops. Larger spills can be drained to small pans and transferred to a bucket for disposal.

## 3.3.2 Significant Leaks

Until it is discovered, significant leaks may run on the module floor to the nearest sump. Again, containment will be immediately placed under the leak until it can be attended to. Liquids on the floor will be directed using squeegee to the nearest module sump where it will be disposed of using a vacuum truck.

### 3.3.3 Catastrophic Liquid Release

Catastrophic liquid release due to failure of equipment or fine water discharge will be accumulated within the 4" tall rim of the sealed module floor. The open HC drain sump will be equipped with a permanent drain pipe so the large liquid release could be pumped out to the open pit without having an operator entering the module – detail for firewater release discussed in Fire Water System Sizing Criteria. The hard piped sump (or trench) will be located adjacent to the external wall and run the full width of the bay (run perpendicular to the wall). The intent is to provide an overflow edge as long as possible so, upon completion of the bulk liquid removal, the remaining liquid can overflow in the sump as easily as possible.

Once the bulk of liquid is removed and the Operator is authorized to enter the module, remaining puddles will be drained using squeegee to the hard pipe sump or vacuum directly with a spark free squeegee head (like a wet shop vacuum). Remaining small sumps will be drained using the module air pumps as described above. Each module will have its own dedicated air pump that can be used to drain each sump within the module.

## 3.4 Spills Outside of Modules

### 3.4.1 Low Pressure Equipment

Equipment with design pressure less than 15 psig, such as tanks, will be provided with spill containment of 110% of their inventory. If several tanks are located in a common area, single spill containment can be provided and will be sized to 110% of the largest volume within the containment. Liquids collected within the containment will be hauled away by a tank truck. A level transmitter at the edge of the spill containment will be provided to alert operations that the containment has taken on some liquid.

### 3.4.2 Higher Design Pressure Equipment

Equipment can be located outside the module enclosure without full spill containment provided that:

- All equipment has a minimum design pressure of 15 psig.
- Temporary spill containment is provided during maintenance of equipment that is suitable to contain a reasonable amount of liquid after it has gone through the draining process.
- All valves and instrument are located inside the module enclosure where possible.
- Flange connections on equipment in all services are minimized.

Minimization of leak points outside of module area will be observed during the design to prevent leaks and spills.
