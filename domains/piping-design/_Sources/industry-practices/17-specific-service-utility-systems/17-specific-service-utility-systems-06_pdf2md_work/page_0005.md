#### 3.1.2.1 Small Equipment

Draining small equipment equipped with isolation valves such as pumps, filters, etc. will be done via the use of a single drain pipe. Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility stations may be used to blow the liquids to the CHDS header where they would gravity drain to the LP Flare KO Drum.

For maintenance of Rotating and Static Equipment, the equipment casing drains are carried to the skid edge and terminated with a valve. For draining, hoses will be connected to these drains and connected to the CHDS headers located nearby. Compressor case drains will be hard piped to the CHDS. Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility stations may be used to help drain the equipment casings and hoses.

## 3.2 Open Drain System

All modules at the NPF will have open low point sumps to collect liquids that may fall to the module floor. The Open Drain System (ODS) will include two types of sumps. The first type of sumps will be used for containment of limited spills and wash-down fluids. These module drain sumps will be equipped with a dip tube with a hose connection so that a portable air-operated diaphragm pump could be connected to pump the sump for disposal. They will be also equipped with a truck connection at the bottom for the large spills. There will be one large sump in each module for collecting significant liquid releases. The large sumps in all modules will be also equipped with a dip-tube so that a vacuum truck can easily connect to evacuate the sump. In addition to the dip-tubes, the sumps in the Process and Produced Water modules can be equipped with a remote-start electric pump that can pump out the sump to a TBD location (possibly a pit within NPF) to avoid sending aerated liquid into the CHDS. The module sumps will not be designed as a secondary containment for process fluids, but for maintenance activities and small spills. Module sumps will be equipped with high liquid level indication to alert operations.

The Instrument Air Compressor and Air Dryer packages will continuously drain water condensate to the module sump. For this reason, a dedicated sump must be located near these skids. This water will need to be disposed of by pump or vacuum truck to a dedicated Sewage Disposal Treatment Plant. If vacuum truck is used, sump shall have hard piped drain connection.

## 3.3 Spills within Modules

Spills are those releases of liquids or loss of pressure containment which occur without prior scheduling (i.e. pump seal failure, flange gasket failure, etc.). While not scheduled, there can be some pre-planning to contain the spill to a designated controlled area.
