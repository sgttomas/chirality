# 3    Design and Application

## 3.1    Closed HC Drain System

Drainage to the CHDS is a normal part of facility operation. The frequency of action may vary, but the tasks associated with that activity rarely change.

The CHDS collects fluids containing hydrocarbons or other production related liquids released from vessels, exchangers, compressor auxiliaries and other equipment in each of the modules without losing containment. These liquids are routed via a separate header collection header to the LP Flare KO Drum for degassing and then pumped to the Slop Oil Tank for disposal or re-introduced into the hydrocarbon processes for further processing. The slop oil tank will have a truck connection for disposal of drained liquid if sending back to the process is not desirable. The CHDS header should be made of CRA to withstand corrosion. Electric pumps are preferred for service where hydrocarbons are expected. Provision for biocide injection is required to inhibit growth of bacteria in the water leg of the system and LP Flare KO Drum.

### 3.1.1    Frequent Drainage

Where frequent drainage is required, such as compressor case drains prior to every startup, vessel bridles, in-line filters, instruments with chambers, such as level gauges and displacer level transmitters, bleed valve in double block and bleed isolation, hard piping will be used for connecting hydrocarbon fluids to the CHDS.

Permanent connections will be provided, as required, with restriction orifice to restrict flow into the CHDS. The CHDS will be protected from potential overpressure (blow-by) through the rigorous analysis and design practices validated / guided by HAZOP.

### 3.1.2    Equipment Drainage

For maintenance of equipment, the equipment will be drained as much as possible within the process system (i.e. vessels run to their lowest levels) followed by final drainage to the CHDS. Hard piped connections will be used to connect equipment drains to the CHDS. A spectacle blind (or dropout spool) will be provided close to the equipment for positive isolation of the equipment to the CHDS during normal operation.

Equipment with large volume remaining will be drained to the CHDS using nitrogen or fuel gas.
