# 1   Objective

The Nanushuk Processing Facility (NPF) is an oil and gas processing facility where liquids are processed and / or temporarily stored. These liquid processing and storage facilities include module sumps, tanks, vessels and other pieces of equipment. During the life of the facility, most of these locations will periodically need to be drained to allow inspection or maintenance.

The objective of this document is to identify the minimum design requirements for the drainage of process and utility equipment and the management and control of unplanned releases associated with equipment and other plant components whether located within or outside of modules enclosures.

# 2   Summary

During planned activities, such as routine equipment maintenance, hydrocarbon liquids and produced water will be brought to a minimum liquid level through the process and then drained to the Closed Hydrocarbon Drain System (CHDS). Nitrogen or fuel gas from utility station will be used to push the liquid from equipment to the collection headers which will be sent to the LP Flare KO Drum which will act as a degassing drum. From the LP Flare KO Drum, hydrocarbon liquids and produced water will be pumped to the Slop Oil Tank where it can be introduced back into the process or trucked for disposal. Other fluids (glycol, lube oil, chemicals) are not part of the CHDS and would be drained and transported for disposal separately.

Spill containment is done by utilizing equipment skid drip rims and the module sealed floor/drip rim as the means of containing spills within a module. In each module there will be multiple small sumps equipped with a dip-tube with a hose connection so that a portable air-operated diaphragm pump could be connected to pump out the sump for disposal. There will be one large sump in each module for collecting significant liquid releases. The large sumps in all modules will be also equipped with a dip-tube so that a portable air-operated pump can be connected to evacuate the sump. In addition to the dip-tubes, the sumps in the Process and Produced Water modules will be equipped with a remote-start electric pump that can pump out the sump to a TBD location (possibly a pit within NPF) to avoid sending aerated liquid into the CHDS; this will be evaluated in FEED. The exact quantity of the small sumps and exact location of the sumps inside the module will be determined during detailed design.

Leaks from equipment located outdoors will be mitigated by minimizing flange connections in all services. No secondary containment will be provided for catastrophic liquid release from outdoors equipment or components other than containment required for storage tanks.
