# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 GENERAL

This DEP specifies requirements and gives recommendations for the design and engineering of instrument air supply systems to provide a reliable supply of instrument air of the required quality.

This DEP also specifies requirements and gives recommendations for pneumatic signal lines, including their connection to the instrument air supply lines/header; in so doing, it incorporates the relevant content of DEP 32.37.51.11-Gen., which is now withdrawn.

This DEP is a revision of an earlier DEP of the same number dated July 1985, see (1.5).

## 1.2 DISTRIBUTION, INTENDED USE AND REGULATORY CONSIDERATIONS

Unless otherwise authorised by Shell GSI and SIEP, the distribution of this DEP is confined to companies forming part of or managed by the Royal Dutch/Shell Group, and to Contractors and Manufacturers nominated by them.

This DEP is intended for use in oil refineries, chemical plants, gas plants, exploration and production facilities and supply/marketing installations.

When DEPs are applied, a Management of Change (MOC) process should be implemented; this is of particular importance when existing facilities are to be modified.

If national and/or local regulations exist in which some of the requirements may be more stringent than in this DEP the Contractor shall determine by careful scrutiny which of the requirements are the more stringent and which combination of requirements will be acceptable as regards safety, environmental, economic and legal aspects. In all cases the Contractor shall inform the Principal of any deviation from the requirements of this DEP which is considered to be necessary in order to comply with national and/or local regulations. The Principal may then negotiate with the Authorities concerned with the object of obtaining agreement to follow this DEP as closely as possible.

## 1.3 DEFINITIONS AND ABBREVIATIONS

## 2. GENERAL

### 2.1 ARRANGEMENT

The instrument air supply system shall provide the required quantity of:

- instrument air of the quality specified in (2.2);
- industrial air for approved cases, as specified in (2.3);
- air for safety equipment (2.4).

The system shall comprise an air supply plant as specified in Sections 3 to 7, and a main air supply piping system as specified in Section 8. Specific guidelines for applications are provided in Section 9.

### 2.2 INSTRUMENT AIR QUALITY

The instrument air shall be free from oil and other liquids, and from toxic, corrosive, flammable and obnoxious gases or vapours.

The quantity of solids shall be less than 0.1 g/m³ and the diameter of the particles shall not be more than 3 μm. In-line fine filters shall be installed to meet these requirements.

NOTE: Older plants may still operate successfully with 30 μm filters.

To prevent condensation in the supply piping and instruments, the dew point of the air at operating pressure shall be at least 10 °C below the lowest expected ambient temperature for the air system at any location. In mild and cold climates this normally results in standard dew point specification of –40 °C at atmospheric conditions, whereas in tropical climates 0 °C would suffice. Integrated IA systems with nitrogen/oxygen supply may have dew points specified at –65 °C depending on the possible combined air dryer service. Unless otherwise specified, the maximum instrument air supply temperature shall be 40 °C; the Principal may specify a higher temperature but, for proper operation of the instruments, this should not be higher than 70 °C.

Unless otherwise specified, the instrument air pressure in the air supply piping shall be 7 bar (ga) under normal operation. The Principal may specify a lower pressure but, for proper operation of the instruments, this should not be less than 4.2 bar (ga).

NOTES: 1. The requirements given above are in accordance with IEC 60654 (see also ISA 7.0.01 and ISO 8573-1).

2. Nitrogen or carbon dioxide shall not be used as back-up for IA systems.

### 2.3 INSTRUMENT AIR SUPPLY FOR INDUSTRIAL CONSUMERS

Instrument air may be supplied to the following industrial consumers provided a separate system is installed branching off the instrument air supply system at the battery limit of each processing unit:

- oil mist lubrication systems;
- flare ignition equipment.

Instrument air may only be supplied to other industrial consumers if:

- there is no possibility, under any circumstances, of backflow from piping from industrial consumers to the instrument air supply
- a non-return valve and safeguarding device are installed ensuring priority for instrument air; see Appendix 7 and Appendix 8
- the required quantity of instrument air for industrial use has been incorporated in the total consumption of instrument air, see (2.5)
- no connections are made between the industrial air system and the instrument air system at any other position.

Individual tool air systems may only be supplied by the air supply plant if:

- the tool air piping is separate from the main instrument air system piping;
- the buffer vessel is adequately sized to prevent interruption to the IA system;
- the main instrument air system is protected against low pressure cut-off.

NOTE: Not withstanding the above, large demands for tool air, e.g. during plant shutdown periods, should preferably be supplied by mobile compressors, so as to avoid the need for large compressed air plants covering wide fluctuations in demand.

For all other applications not specified above, agreement shall be obtained from the Principal (see also Section 9 for examples).

## 2.4 AIR SUPPLY FOR SAFETY EQUIPMENT

Safety equipment such as breathing air masks should be supplied from separate air bottles rather than from an IA system, so as to avoid any contamination or conflict of use, particularly in large distributed systems. If, however, IA is supplied to such equipment, separate branch-off points shall be provided on the instrument air header in the processing unit, with all the appropriate valves locked open to ensure guaranteed air supply from the buffer vessel to the breathing apparatus. Procedures shall be in place, including emergency procedures, for the operation of safety equipment supplied from IA.

The branch-off points shall be of size DN 15 and shall be located close to the required consumer.

The following shall apply:

- air compressor intake air quality shall be checked to ensure there are no re-circulated process emissions and that all contaminants are below the occupational exposure limits;
- rotary machines (air compressors, gas turbines) shall be segregated from the IA system during cleaning (e.g. because toxic fluids might be used);
- air quality shall be improved by using extra filters to remove dust and rust.
- air quality shall be in accordance with the relevant breathing air standard for compressed air.

## 2.5 INSTRUMENT AIR QUANTITY

The quantity of instrument air shall be estimated as accurately as possible taking into account the requirements for:

- pneumatically-operated instrumentation, based on the data stated by the Manufacturers of such equipment;
- pressurizing electrical instruments/equipment in order to prevent an explosive gas atmosphere inside their enclosures, by maintaining an over-pressure relative to the surrounding atmosphere and, where necessary, by continuous dilution;
- purging/cooling of essential instruments;
- industrial users as defined in (2.3).

This quantity is referred to as the 'Design Quantity of Instrument Air'.

NOTE: Control valves are the main instrument air consumers in modern plants. The consumption of this group shall be carefully calculated according to the specific make of positioner and/or actuator.

## 2.6 INSTRUMENT AIR SUPPLY SYSTEMS

Where the entire refinery or plant is shut down for major overhauls, the instrument air supply may be interrupted during these overhauls to allow inspection and maintenance of the instrument air supply piping. At these locations a single header air supply plant and air supply piping system should be applied, see Appendix 2 and Appendix 7.

Where the refinery or plant is only partly shutdown for major overhauls, the instrument air supply cannot be interrupted, so the instrument air supply plant shall be designed as a double header system, see Appendix 1. The instrument air supply piping shall be designed as a ring main header system for those parts of the supply piping where an uninterrupted supply of instrument air is required, see Appendix 8.

## 2.7 SEGREGATION

Where required for plant operation, the air supply system shall be segregated into an instrument air (IA) system and a priority instrument air (PIA) system. This segregation shall primarily be based on the importance of continued operation of a particular plant section or selected instruments.

In this context, utility supply plants such as electric power plants, boilers with related deaerators and boiler feed water systems, fuel and cooling water systems, shall normally be connected to the PIA system. Certain processing units (e.g. less critical or less profitable units) may be segregated from the IA system, depending on the probability of calamities or the financial consequences of an unexpected shutdown.

The above segregation shall be achieved by installing one or more isolating control valves in the instrument air supply piping, see Appendix 7 and Appendix 8. Each isolating control valve shall consist of a pneumatically-operated control valve with a local pneumatic pressure indicating controller, operating as a back-pressure controller, i.e. throttling the valve in the event of low air pressure in the upstream piping. The control valve shall be provided with a position switch, which will initiate an alarm on the main panel/console when the valve starts to close. Each isolating control valve shall have block valves and a by-pass valve.

In the event of low air pressure, industrial air supplies shall be isolated from the air supply system before instrument air is segregated.

NOTE    The option of installing manually operated isolating valves in the supply piping for segregating certain sections, e.g. to allow the commissioning of individual sections before plant acceptance test, should also be considered.

Consumers connected to the IA system which must stay in operation after a total air supply failure, e.g. valves for the depressurizing of equipment, shall be supplied from a 'secured instrument air' (SIA) system which is a buffer vessel connected to the distribution piping via a non-return device in accordance with DEP 32.45.10.10-Gen., see Appendix 11.

Details for functional design, such as back-up time and consumption rates, shall be supplied by instrument engineering.

## 2.8 CORROSIVE ATMOSPHERES

For corrosive atmospheres where carbon steel tubing and compression fittings and PVC-covered copper tubing might not be suitable, other materials such as type 316 stainless steel may be used if approved by the Principal. Materials selection shall be in accordance with DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen.

NOTE    Compression fitting materials shall be compatible with the material of the tubing.

## 3. AIR SUPPLY PLANT

For projects in existing facilities, the possibility of supplying the required quantity of instrument air from an existing (outside plot) system shall be considered. Where the use of an existing system is not possible, an instrument air supply plant shall be provided comprising:

- compressors, see (4);
- water separators, see (5);
- air driers, see (6);
- buffer vessel(s), see (7).

The instrument air supply shall be designed so that, in emergencies, a guaranteed source for a defined period is available for the utility supply plants and/or essential process instrumentation.

These facilities shall then comprise an automatically-starting emergency compressor with an associated water separator.

NOTES: 1  This emergency compressor shall be driven by a diesel engine, a petrol engine or an electric motor, the latter only if an independent emergency electric generator or independent supply system is available. If an electric motor is used, it shall be in accordance with DEP 33.64.20.31-Gen.

2  Consideration may also be given to obtaining air for essential consumers from an outside plot instrument air supply system (if available).

The air supply plant shall be located in a non-hazardous area.

For the layout of typical air supply plants, see Appendix 1 and Appendix 2. Details of air supply piping are given in (8).

The piping between the compressor discharge and the inlet to drier and buffer vessels shall have automatic condensate draining facilities at all low points. In outdoor installations in freezing climates, if stagnant lines can occur, this piping, the valves, and the bottom part of the buffer vessel shall be heat-traced and insulated.

On PEFS, it shall be shown that there are no pockets between compressor discharge and the inlet to the drier.

The main air supply line shall be provided with a flow measuring element, a low-pressure alarm and a historic trend recording facility for pressure on the panel/console.

The humidity shall be measured with a water content analyser having a local indication and a high-humidity alarm on the main panel/console.

Safety/relief valves shall be provided if required by regulations, and/or by the relation between maximum compressor discharge pressure and the maximum allowable working pressure of vessels and piping.

All equipment shall satisfy the limitations for noise generation as specified in DEP 31.10.00.31-Gen.

## 4. AIR COMPRESSORS

### 4.1 GENERAL

To ensure maximum reliability and availability of the instrument air supply, at least two compressors including their auxiliaries shall be installed driven by two different and independent utilities, e.g. steam and electricity, or by two different and independent electrical systems if steam is not available. Each compressor shall be arranged for normal operation and for stand-by, and shall be capable of supplying the design quantity of instrument air (2.5).

The installation of more than two (2 × 100 %) compressors, e.g. (3 × 50 %) compressors, should be considered for reasons of flexibility, if the fluctuations in air consumption are greater than the range of one compressor, or where a number of small compressors are more attractive than two compressors each with a relatively large capacity. The Principal shall decide which system will be applied.

However, the total capacity of the compressors driven by the most reliable utility shall be sufficient to supply the design quantity of instrument air (2.5).

NOTE: In addition to air compressors for normal plant operation, an independent emergency air compressor may also be required, see (3).

### 4.2 COMPRESSOR SPECIFICATIONS

The compressors shall supply oil-free air and shall be complete with intercoolers, aftercoolers and condensate draining facilities, etc.

Centrifugal and integrally-geared compressors shall be provided with non-return valves and surge protection. Gas turbine air draw-off may only be feasible in few cases and requires the approval of the Principal.

The type selection of air compressors shall be in accordance with DEP.31.29.40.10-Gen.

Electric motors shall conform to DEP 33.66.05.31-Gen. and be suitable for installation in the specified area classification for explosive (gas) atmospheres.

Other drivers may also be selected depending on the overall utility integration, e.g. back-pressure or condensing steam turbines, especially for large integrated systems. The compressors and their drivers shall be as specified by the Principal.

### 4.3 COMPRESSOR CONTROLS

Each compressor shall have facilities for manual and automatic starting in case of failure of the other compressor(s). The automatic starting system shall be arranged so that a compressor can only be stopped by manual controls or by its safeguarding system.

Automatic starting of the stand-by compressor(s) shall be as fast as possible. Initiators shall be provided on the piping downstream of the air drier to start the stand-by compressor(s). Stopping of a compressor shall be indicated by an alarm on the local panel/console.

The electric motor(s) shall have local start/stop controls and be protected against repetitive starting. Electrical controls supplied as an integral part of the compressor shall be interlocked with the start/stop controls and shall be located in a weatherproof housing on, or close to, the compressor.

### 4.4 COMPRESSOR INLET AND DISCHARGE

The inlet of the compressors shall be located in a non-hazardous area and the delivered instrument air shall be free from toxic, corrosive, flammable and obnoxious gases.

The bottom of the inlet shall be located at least 6 m above ground level and the inlet opening shall be fitted with a wire mesh cage of adequate size to prevent entry of objects which could cause blockage.

At locations where freezing is likely to occur, provisions should be taken to prevent ice formation and subsequent blocking of the inlet wire mesh cage.

The compressor inlet piping shall be provided with dust filter(s) and a facility to indicate differential pressure across the filters readable at ground level. The inlet piping and filter casing shall be of stainless steel (see DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen).

To reduce the load on the air drier, the air from the compressors shall be cooled to a temperature of not more than 10 °C above the cooling medium inlet temperature. If the aftercoolers supplied as an integral part of the compressors have only marginal capacity, aftercoolers of higher capacity shall be specified.

## 5. WATER SEPARATORS

A stainless steel water separator (see DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen) shall be installed downstream of the aftercooler of each air compressor. The water separator shall be designed so that the air at the outlet of the separator shall not be more than 5 % over-saturated under any operating conditions.

The water separator shall be provided with a level alarm and with automatic drain facilities.

## 6. AIR DRIERS

### 6.1 GENERAL

At least two identical (2 × 100 %) subsystems of air drying equipment shall normally be installed to ensure a high system availability and reliability. Each set typically comprises:

- Two dryers (one operating, one in regeneration/stand by mode)
- Inlet and outlet filters also for regeneration air
- One regeneration air blower
- One regeneration air heater
- One depressurization silencer

The automatic sequence control of the dryer subsystem, i.e. adsorption / regeneration (see 6.3) / standby control is part of DCS (see 6.4). The change-over between the two 100 % subsystems shall be manual. The two subsystems should be switched over on a two-weekly rotation basis.

The air drier shall reduce the dew point of the air, under operating pressure at a maximum oversaturation of 5 %, to at least 10° C below the lowest ambient temperature (see 2.2).

The selection methods of drying, regeneration and switching are usually specified by the Principal; if this has not been done, the Contractor shall submit a proposal for agreement by the Principal.

To avoid rapid pressurization/depressurization of the vessels, restriction orifices are installed in the pressurization/depressurization lines.

As a part of the dryer package, a dew point metering/control and a sample connection are installed in the discharge line of the outlet filters.

### 6.2 DESICCANT

The desiccant shall be either activated alumina or silica gel in bead form. If silica gel is used, a bottom layer of approximately 10 % of activated alumina shall be provided to achieve a better resistance to entrained water.

The desiccant needs to be replaced in good time due to aging. Aging is affected by chemical contamination in the air (e.g. olefins or oil), by abrasion, and/or excessive severity or cycling of regeneration. Well maintained desiccants may last up to 4-12 years.

### 6.3 REGENERATION

One of the following methods of regeneration shall be selected by the Principal:

- at elevated temperature and atmospheric pressure, see Appendix 3;
- at elevated temperature and operating pressure, see Appendix 4 and Appendix 5;
- at atmospheric pressure (heaterless drier), see Appendix 6.

Heat for regeneration, if required, shall be supplied by electric heaters or steam heaters external to the drying vessels.

The design and construction of the steam heaters shall be subject to the approval of the Principal.

For regeneration at atmospheric pressure and elevated temperature, water vapour is removed by means of air, which may either be taken from the outlet of the drier (own consumption 2 % to 3% by mass for regeneration) or be provided by a separate blower, see Appendix 3.

A 'heatless' drier, operating at ambient temperature, will have an own air consumption of 10 % to 15 % by mass for regeneration.

Where regeneration at atmospheric pressure is applied, the vessel shall be depressurized slowly to prevent blowing out and/or fragmenting of the desiccant, and to reduce exhaust noise. After the desiccant has been regenerated, the vessel shall be pressurized slowly before switch-over.

For regeneration at operating pressure, the rate of supply of the regeneration air shall be controlled by means of a local flow indicating controller, with a low-flow interlock on the heater and a pneumatically operated control valve with a mechanical limit stop.

An adequate number of temperature sensing devices shall be installed in appropriate positions, e.g. inlet, outlet and bed in order to monitor the regeneration cycle.

After the desiccant has been regenerated at elevated temperature it shall be cooled by a flow of cold air.

## 6.4 SWITCHING

In order to switch the desiccant vessels from the drying to the regeneration stage and vice versa, the drier shall be provided with valves at the vessel inlets and outlets. These valves may be plug or ball type, of either three-way or single port with pneumatic operators which are interlocked via an automatic control system.

NOTES:
1. Valve bodies shall normally be of cast steel; cast iron may be used only if agreed by the Principal.
2. Where three-way plug valves or ball valves are used the use of PTFE linings should be considered in order to reduce maintenance (lubrication), air leakage and the force required to operate the valve.
3. Four-way valves and/or mechanical interlocks shall not be applied.

Switching shall be initiated automatically on a once-per-shift (8-hourly) basis, except for the heaterless drier which shall be switched every 5 minutes.

Each drying vessel shall then have a drying capacity equal to the design quantity of instrument air (2.5) for a minimum period of 10 hours; regeneration, including the cooling stage shall not last longer than 6 hours.

Automatic switching (normally provided by Vendor as part of the package in a PLC program) shall be integrated with the automatic controls for the regeneration cycle and shall be on a fixed time schedule.

## 6.5 FILTERS

The quantity of solids shall be limited (2.2).

Prefilters shall be provided to prevent rust particles from settling on the desiccant.

Afterfilters shall always be installed to prevent desiccant particles from entering the air supply piping.

All filters shall be in duplicate, have isolating valves and DP measurements with alarm.

## 6.6 AFTERCOOLER

Because of the adsorption heat generated during the drying cycle, the outlet temperature of the drier may rise to 60° C or even higher. Consequently, some aftercooling may still be required to ensure the maximum temperature at downstream users is less than 40 °C. Integrated cryogenic IA-N2/O2 systems definitely use further aftercooling, which may even be extended with a chiller system as part of the ASU package.

## 6.7 DRIER SPECIFICATION

The specification of the drier shall contain all the data necessary to ensure the supply of a suitable unit.

The design and construction of the vessels shall be in accordance with DEP 31.22.10.32-Gen., DEP 31.22.11.32-Gen. or DEP 31.22.20.31-Gen. Other standards may used if approved by the Principal.

The design, fabrication, erection and testing of piping should be in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.11-Gen.

## 7. BUFFER VESSEL

One or more buffer vessels of adequate size shall be installed to serve:

- as buffer volume in the event of compressor failure;
- as a fluctuation damper when compressors are on load/unload control.

The buffer vessel(s) shall be sized to maintain the air supply from the moment the compressor fails to when the stand-by compressor(s) start(s) to operate. The period between these events should be taken as the time required for starting the stand-by compressor(s) manually if automatic starting is unsuccessful. This period depends on the size of the vessel, the capacity of the piping and the possibilities for maintenance and inspection. This period is typically 5 min for a simple system and 15 min for a critical system.

Unless otherwise specified, during this period the instrument air pressure shall not drop below the minimum value of 4.2 bar (ga), which is required for proper operation of the instruments.

The sizing of the buffer vessel(s) shall be based on the design quantity of instrument air (2.5), in order to allow the safeguarding devices to close, see (2.3).

The buffer vessel(s) shall have draining facilities. The wall thickness of the vessel shall have a 1 mm corrosion allowance, painted outside and be provided with an internal protective coating (refer to DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen). The internal protective coating shall be properly bonded to the surface so as not to be a potential source of contamination itself (e.g. by peeling).

The vessel(s) shall be installed downstream of the air driers with the inlet entering half way up the vessel and the main IA header outlet at the top of the vessel. This is to make use of the buffer vessel as a final separator in the event of carry-over or breakthrough from the drier/filters or in case the PDIC opens. A bypass may be installed around the vessel (from inlet to outlet) to allow the vessel to be maintained.

The design and construction of the buffer vessel shall be in accordance with DEP 31.22.10.32-Gen, unless another standard is approved by the Principal.

The design, fabrication, erection and testing of piping shall be in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.11-Gen.

## 8. PIPING AND TUBING

### 8.1 GENERAL

The piping system for instrument air supply shall be designed in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.11-Gen., taking into account:

- segregation (2.7);
- plant lay-out (6.2);
- pipe sizes (8.3).

The complete layout of the piping system including the take-off points and pipe sizes, etc., shall be shown on a drawing or on a set of drawings. These drawings shall also clearly indicate the demarcation between mechanical piping and instrument tubing.

All piping in pipe tracks and pipe bridges, all piping larger than DN 15 in plant sections (including branch-off points and valves), and the piping up to the air filter/reducer station in the control centre, shall be considered mechanical piping and shall be further detailed in piping layout drawings and isometrics.

Instrument tubing and the air filter/reducer station(s) with downstream piping shall be incorporated in the drawing(s) entitled 'Location of Plant Instruments', see DEP 32.31.00.34-Gen.

### 8.2 LAYOUT

The layout of the supply piping depends on:

- the layout of the plant and plant sections;
- the location of the air supply plant;
- the location of pipe bridges, cable trunking, etc.;
- the location of the instruments.

Piping for instrument air supply inside the process unit shall be completely separated from that for industrial air supply.

The layout drawings shall include all instrument air supply piping in pipe tracks, pipe bridges and plant sections, up to and including the branch-off points for individual instruments or groups of instruments.

Piping or tubing forming part of instrument engineering should not be shown on these drawings. For pneumatic hook-up details refer to Standard Drawing S.37.003.

The piping shall be arranged so that a continuous supply of instrument air is ensured, even under abnormal situations such as shutdown of plant sections, or when changes to piping have to be made.

A typical arrangement of a single header, main air supply concept is shown in Appendix 7. For larger plants or for an air supply feeding more than one plant, a ring main header concept should be considered, see Appendix 8.

Piping in the plant sections shall run close to the trunking for instrument signal cables to facilitate supporting of pneumatic signal lines, see Appendix 9.

### 8.3 LINE SIZES

For main headers, a calculation shall be made according to the air consumption requirements. The decrease in pressure between the outlet of the air drier and the most remote consumer shall not exceed 1 bar.

Piping in pipe tracks and pipe bridges should have a line size of DN 40.

Unless otherwise specified, the connections on air supply piping shall terminate in a ¹⁄₂ inch NPT female thread.

## 8.4 PIPING DETAILS

Piping shall be in accordance with DEP 31.38.01.12-Gen., piping class 11011 or 11021.

NOTE: The piping material selection shall take into account the air temperatures and corrosiveness of the surroundings (e.g. marine environment).

All main pipes shall be provided with drain valves at low points. Dead ends and branch connections for future extensions, etc., shall each be provided with an isolating valve and blind flange. Branch connections from piping in pipe tracks and pipe bridges shall be a minimum of DN 25, shall be taken off the top of horizontal piping and shall have isolating valves.

Branch connections from piping in process sections shall be a minimum of DN 15 with steel ball valves.

At least 15 % spare DN 15 valved connections shall be available at the end of the construction period, evenly distributed throughout the plant.

For typical details of air supply piping, see Appendix 9.

Underground air supply piping shall be protected against corrosion. A materials and corrosion engineer should be consulted to ascertain the best protection method.

## 8.5 TESTING AND CLEANING

Piping systems shall be pneumatically pressure tested in accordance with ASME B31.3, using compressed air.

All air supply piping shall be cleaned by carefully blowing through with air at normal supply pressure, taking appropriate precautions at open ends. The lower pressure limit, i.e. 4.2 bar (ga), should be sufficient for this purpose.

NOTE: When testing or cleaning the piping, water or other liquids or gases shall not be used.

## 9. APPLICATION SPECIFICS

### 9.1 CONTROL ROOM

A typical arrangement of an instrument air supply to the basement (applicable to older plant) under a control room is given in Appendix 10.

One or more module or plant dedicated air filter/reducer stations should be installed in the basement under the control room, or in the ancillary room if no basement is provided, for reducing the incoming air to the required pressure which is normally 1.5 bar (ga).

Instruments for integrated processing units, i.e. which cannot be operated separately, should be supplied from a common central filter/reducer station. Separate stations and air headers shall be provided for sections serving essential units, e.g. boilers which are expected to stay in operation during maintenance shutdowns of the processing units or during failure of the normal air supply plant.

Separate filter/reducer stations and air headers shall be installed for:

- processing units connected to the IA supply which might not stay in operation during a failure of the IA supply plant. Downstream pressure: 1.5 bar (ga).
- essential units, e.g. utility plants, connected to the Priority Instrument Air (PIA) supply which shall stay in operation during maintenance shutdowns or during failure of the normal air supply plant. Downstream pressure: 1.5 bar (ga).
- consumers requiring higher supply pressures, e.g. for direct operation of depressurizing valves. Downstream pressure: according to specification.

Each filter/reducing station shall consist of at least two filters in parallel followed by two top quality pressure reducers in parallel, one acting as stand-by for the other.

Each pressure reducer shall be fitted with a gauge indicating its downstream pressure. The capacity of each pressure reducer shall be such that at maximum capacity with an upstream pressure of 4.2 bar (ga) and only one reducer in operation, the specified downstream pressure is maintained.

A safety-relief valve shall be installed in the reduced air header between each reducer and the related downstream isolating valve to protect the consumers. The relief pressure setting shall be 0.2 bar (ga) lower than the maximum allowable working pressure of the instruments supplied by the header concerned.

NOTES:
1. Pressure reducers shall not be oversized by more than 10 % of the maximum air consumption, in order to prevent unstability during normal operation and to limit the size of the safety-relief valve.
2. The exhaust port of the relief valve shall be provided with a pipe of which the outlet is in a safe position more than 2 m above floor level in a vertical direction.

The air piping upstream of the reducers shall be provided with a 1/2 inch NPT female threaded connection for a pressure indicator. A 1/2 inch NPT female threaded connection shall also be provided for a low pressure alarm in the reduced air piping, downstream of the isolating valves. The low pressure alarm setting shall be 0.25 bar (ga) lower than the required reduced air pressure.

The main header shall be of welded stainless steel construction (refer to DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen), with stainless steel (ball-type) isolating valves installed in each branch-off to cabinets, racks or operating consoles. The size of the air piping downstream of the reducer stations shall be based on:

- maximum air consumption of all consumers;
- spare capacity of 30 % for future extensions.

Flexible hoses shall be applied to connect the isolating valve on the main air header to the air manifolds.

## 9.2 FIELD COMPONENTS

For general applications, the instrument air supply lines from the header up to the isolating valve(s) close to the air consumer(s) shall consist of 16 mm OD carbon steel tubing with carbon steel compression fittings or type 304 or 316 stainless steel tubing with stainless steel compression fittings.

Instrument air supply lines from the carbon steel valve(s) to the air consumer(s) and the pneumatic signal lines shall consist of 6 mm OD PVC-covered copper tubing and brass compression fittings or 304/316 stainless steel tubing with stainless steel compression fittings (refer DEP 39.01.10.11-Gen). All components, tubing and compression fittings shall be suitable for use at 10 bar (ga) and 38 °C.

Note: The application of tubing and compression fittings shall comply with the following:

- the compression fittings shall be of the make, type and composition as specified for the project by the Principal and of which all parts of the fitting are made by one Manufacturer
- the tubing shall be in accordance with the specification supplied by the Principal
- the fittings and tubing shall be installed by skilled personnel, strictly in accordance with the Manufacturer's instructions
- the instrument air lines shall be pressure tested after installation.

Each instrument and final control element requiring an air supply shall be provided with an individual air filter reducer.

Note: The types of air filter reducers shall be kept to an absolute minimum.

The maximum number of consumers that may be connected to the same ¹⁄₂ inch NPT take-off point shall be calculated with account being taken of the minimum allowable inlet pressure of each air filter reducer and the total length of supply tubing, on the assumption that all connected instruments are consuming air at the maximum rate.

All components such as air filter reducers, lock-up devices and solenoid valves shall be bolted to a stainless steel mounting plate which is fixed to a support with stainless steel bolts.

Copper tubing is considered to be self supporting up to lengths of 0.5 m; for longer lengths, the tubing shall be supported over the full length and fixed to the support at approximately 1 m intervals with cable ties.

## 9.3 SPECIAL USES AND INTEGRATION ISSUES

The following uses and integration issues shall be as specified in Section 2:

- General mechanical clean-out uses, e.g. scouring of filters;
- Agitation of heat exchangers;
- Drying, purging of equipment, e.g. peep holes in furnaces;
- Drying, purging of lines or equipment;
- Decoking furnaces (requires approval of the Principal due to large and periodic demands);
- Integration with an Air Separation Unit (requires approval of the Principal).
