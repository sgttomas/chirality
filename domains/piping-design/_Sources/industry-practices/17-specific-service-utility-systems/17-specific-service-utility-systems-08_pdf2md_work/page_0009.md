## 1044 Air Pollution

Air pollution requirements demand that the release of H₂S to the atmosphere be reduced to a minimum. Any source of H₂S leakage constitutes a source of air pollution as well as a personnel hazard.

H₂S has an obvious and unpleasant odor which is detectable even at 1 ppm concentration. It also causes blackening of some paints and tarnishing of some metals at even lower concentrations. Local regulations may restrict concentrations to less than 0.1 ppm.

## 1045 Material Considerations

The selection of construction materials for H₂S service requires special attention regardless of H₂S concentration. Other components in the process stream, such as NH₃, CN⁻, and brine, can significantly affect the stream's aggressiveness and should be considered. The following problems are common. Most of them are discussed in detail in the *Corrosion Prevention Manual*.

### General Corrosion

H₂S generally accelerates corrosion. However, under wet conditions, the rate of corrosion is greatly affected by the presence of other compounds such as ammonia, dissolved salts, or acidic substances. Copper alloys corrode rapidly in many H₂S services and are not recommended.

### Velocity-Induced Corrosion

High fluid velocities or turbulence often increase local corrosion. This is common in sour MEA or DEA, and in wet fluids containing both ammonia and H₂S.

### Sulfide Cracking

Ferritic steels, such as carbon steel, low alloys, Cr-Mo steels and precipitation hardenable stainless steels, can crack from exposure to aqueous solutions of H₂S if either the base metal or welds are too hard. To avoid the possibility of sulfide cracking of welds in carbon steel vessels, columns, exchangers, and piping, weld metal hardness should be limited to 200 BHN maximum. On compressors and other mechanical equipment, avoid use of alloys stronger than 90 ksi yield strength, or harder than 235 BHN (equivalent to about Rockwell C22). Welds in Cr-Mo steels should always be stress relieved. 18-8 stainless steels are immune to sulfide cracking and can be used for valve trim in those services where cracking of 12Cr trim is a recognized problem. In some sour services, low alloy steels and API 5LX pipe up to grade X65 have been used with success with a weld hardness of 235 BHN; however, this requires careful consideration of the aggressiveness of the sour environment. In general, carefully consider the possibility of sulfide cracking before using any material harder than 235 BHN.
