# 1040 Special Problems in H₂S Systems

## 1041 H₂S Hazards

H₂S is a fatally poisonous gas. The degree of danger depends upon the concentration in the air breathed and on time of exposure. It is easily detected by smell at low concentrations, but at higher concentrations H₂S instantaneously deadens the sense of smell (olfactory nerves) so that there may be no warning of danger. H₂S is colorless. It is heavier than air, and can therefore collect in low spots if there is little air movement. H₂S gas is flammable, has a low ignition temperature of about 558°F, and forms explosive mixtures in air within the range of 4.3 to 45 volume percent.

The data on toxicity of H₂S in Figure 1000-1 are taken from the references shown in parentheses. Additional information may be obtained from the publications listed in Section 1030.

| ppm H₂S in Air (by volume) | Effect |

| 1 | Detectable (API RP 49) |

Figure 1000-2 shows the effects of exposure time at various H₂S concentrations in air. In determining the potential effect of an H₂S leak, the dispersion of gas from a leak should be considered.

30 CFR 250 also contains mandatory requirements for the design of H₂S flaring on platform facilities.

The Railroad Commission of Texas Rule 36 provides specific rules for calculating the radius of exposure and the maximum H₂S volume available for escape (leak). These calculations are required for facilities falling under Rule 36 jurisdiction.
