### 1.3.2 Specific definitions

The temperature and pressure terms used in this DEP are defined in DEP 01.00.01.30-Gen. Further terms are defined below.

**Expansion vessel**
A vessel to accommodate expanded volume and surge of the HTF, and allows for venting of low boiling point components from the system.

**Heat transfer fluid (HTF)**
A fluid capable of transporting heat energy within a specified temperature range. Specific hydrocarbon-based fluids are available on the open market for use in heat transfer applications. Generally, this includes synthetic aromatic, silicone-based and a limited selection of specific HTF designated mineral oils.

**Heater**
The heat energy producer in the system. Either a heat exchanger, a furnace or a waste heat recovery unit (WHRU).

**Hot oil**
A mineral oil product/process stream used for heat transfer purposes but not commercially available as branded HTF.

**HTF system**
All heaters, piping, pumps, vessels, heat exchangers and auxiliaries that constitute the closed circuit containing the HTF.

**Maximum allowable bulk temperature (MABT)**
The maximum bulk temperature of the HTF allowed anywhere in the circuit, as specified by the Manufacturer of the HTF.

NOTE: The Principal may specify a Maximum Operating Temperature that is lower than the MABT for a specific application.

**Maximum allowable film temperature (MAFT)**
The maximum temperature to which the HTF may be subjected in the heater. This highest temperature is on the tube inner wall of the heater and is determined by the heat flux and heat transfer coefficient. The Manufacturer of the HTF recommends the MAFT.

**Maximum operating temperature (MOT)**
The maximum HTF bulk temperature at which the system is designed to operate, as measured at the outlet of the heat source.

**Minimum operating temperature**
The lowest HTF bulk temperature at which the system is designed to start up. It should always be higher than the pumpability limit.

NOTE: This temperature shall be selected so that the HTF viscosity is lower than the pumpability limit (HTF viscosity of 50–500 mm²/s at start up, depending on pump size) and so that the MAFT is not exceeded in the heater during start up.

**Return temperature**
The temperature at which the HTF returns to the heater.

**Storage tank**
A tank for storage of HTF.

**Supply temperature**
The temperature at which the HTF is made available to the battery limit of the users.

**Trim cooler**
A cooler in the heating circuit, used to trim the temperature of the HTF.

**Pump-out cooler**
A cooler in the run-down line to the storage tank to cool down hot HTF in case of evacuation of the system.

NOTE: The Trim Cooler may also be used as Pump-out Cooler.
