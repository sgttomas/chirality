## 1342 Container Location

Chlorine containers should be located at least 50 feet from flammable materials and structures, such as wooden cooling towers. Radiated or direct sources of heat should also be avoided.

Additional design considerations should include ample area lighting, fire protection, drainage, and the possibility of mechanical damage. Locate the containers so that drainage from hydrocarbon sources will not expose them to a fire risk.

Containers located close to roads should be guarded and be far enough from the road to provide safety from vehicular traffic while still retaining good access for service and emergencies. Standard Drawing GA-S99975, Standard Details for Removable and Stationary Guard Posts, provides a typical design.

Location relative to hydrocarbons, hot lines, occupied buildings, ventilation intakes, roads, personnel gates, and safety equipment is extremely important and must be evaluated with facility safety personnel.

Refer to Section 1370 for other applicable requirements in locating containers at the chlorine user's site.

## 1343 Piping

Piping downstream of Chlorine Institute standard container valves should be ANSI Class 300, Schedule 40 for Monel pipe sized NPS 1½ and smaller, and installed per ANSI/ASME B31.3 as a minimum requirement. Socket-welded construction is preferred for NPS 1½ and smaller. Do not use unions in straight chlorine service.

Seal-weld all threaded joints, excluding the container isolating valves, pigtails and connections to instruments. Nonmetallic pipe and unions, such as PVC, are acceptable in chlorinated water service.

Piping should be located aboveground and be as simple as possible. Isolating valves should be readily accessible. Minimize the number of threaded joints and flanges that cannot be seal welded. Provide adequate support and protection from mechanical damage. Steel angle, channel or equal should be used to protect nonmetallic piping.

Avoid trapping liquid chlorine. Slope piping so that it is self-draining. Liquid chlorine has a high coefficient of thermal expansion and will produce high pressure and, possibly, piping failure when trapped and heated. Good practice will limit the system to a pair of isolation valves located at each container.

Do not use hydrocarbons, nonchlorinated solvents, alcohols, cutting oil, sealants, or grease in the fabrication or cleanup of chlorine piping systems. Chlorinated solvents such as trichloroethylene may be used to clean the piping before placing the system
