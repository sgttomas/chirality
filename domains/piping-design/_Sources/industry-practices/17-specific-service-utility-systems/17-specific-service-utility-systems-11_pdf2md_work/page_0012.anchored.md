If the container integrity is uncertain, contact CHEMTREC (800-424-9300) or your local CHLOREP (chlorine emergency plan) team for assistance before trying to move the container.

Providing additional spacing away from fire exposure; and using a radiant heat barrier, a water spray, fire monitors or hose stations may be considered.

## 1360  Facility Identification and Guarding

Equipment and lines handling chlorine should have paint stenciled identification, and signs should be posted in compliance with the Company's *Safety in Designs Manual*.

Guard posts, and "caution" and "no parking" signs should be installed at the perimeter of the chlorine storage area and each location where chlorine is used.

## 1370  Inspection

### 1371 Frequency

Due to the potential hazard that can result from equipment and/or piping failure, an effective semiannual facility inspection should be conducted. A typical inspection checklist is included in Section 1372. The inspection should be primarily visual. Nondestructive examination (NDE) may be used on piping as appropriate. Local facility inspection practices should be followed.

Some of the checklist items are suitable for more frequent inspection. Checking for leaks should be a daily routine. Emergency repair kits should be restocked after each use as well as inventoried at the semiannual inspections. It is suggested that inspection and testing be done weekly for safety equipment, including respiratory units, shower/eyewash and protective clothing.

### 1372 Typical Checklist

#### Housekeeping/Design

1. Number of containers and size? Vertical or horizontal? Are there more than the normal number of containers? If so, why?

2. Are guard posts installed?

3. Are positive hold-down clamps in position? Are safety chains in place?

4. Is the area free of combustible material?

5. Are "caution" and "no parking" signs in place?

6. Is the discharge line connected to the one-ton container gas valve in the 12 o'clock position? Is liquid valve in the 6 o'clock position capped?
