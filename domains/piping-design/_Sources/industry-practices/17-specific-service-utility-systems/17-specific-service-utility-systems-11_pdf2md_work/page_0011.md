## 1345 Sizing Criteria

The dependable continuous discharge rate of chlorine gas from a single one-ton container without frosting under normal conditions (70°F) is about 15 lb/hr against a 35 psig backpressure. The rate for a single 150-lb cylinder is about 1.75 lb/hr under similar conditions.

If the gas discharge rate from a single container will not meet usage requirements, alternate arrangements may be considered, such as manifolding more than one container to a common chlorinator or installing an evaporator. Operability and safety of these alternatives require review and approval by the facility management.

If two or more containers are manifolded together, they must be at the same temperature to prevent the transfer of chlorine from a warm container to a cool container. If a container becomes completely filled with liquid and the container valve is then closed, subsequent pressure buildup as the container warms may cause it to burst.

## 1350 Vapor Control and Fire Protection

### 1351 Vapor Control

Vapor control should be done only by personnel specifically trained in handling hazardous materials and experienced in using the appropriate protective equipment.

Alert designated emergency response personnel and downwind facilities regarding any leak. A liquid chlorine leak is appreciably more hazardous than a gaseous leak. Isolate the containers from a piping leak by using the isolating valves at the containers. If the container body is leaking, orient the cylinder or one-ton container so that gas, rather than liquid, is released. This will reduce the rate of release by a factor of fifteen.

To protect internal building environments and the occupants, immediately close doors, windows and air conditioning inlets and vents, and turn off the air ventilation system.

If a chlorine leak should occur, use water to knock down and safely direct the gas away from personnel. Do not apply water directly into the leak, and do not immerse the container in water or alkaline solution. Mixing water with chlorine produces hydrochloric acid, whose corrosive action can increase the size of the initial opening.

Contain drainage water if possible and route to a neutralizing reservoir. Liquid chlorine must be kept out of the sewer system. Soda ash may be used to contain the puddle.

### 1352 Fire Protection

Under fire conditions keep the chlorine containers cool to avoid releasing chlorine through their fusible metal plugs. If possible, remove containers from the fire zone and relocate to a remote area that will not endanger facility access or public safety.
