## 1335 Accidental Releases

Federal law (CERCLA) requires that the release to the environment of hazardous substances such as chlorine be immediately reported to allow necessary emergency response to protect the public and environment. Any chlorine release of 10 pounds (1.2 gallons, 49.9 SCF) or more within a 24-hour period must be reported to the National Response Center (NRC) in Washington, D.C. (telephone 1-800-424-8802) and, if exposure occurs to persons outside of the facility, to the state and local emergency response agencies required by Federal Law SARA—Title III. Additional state and local agencies may also require notification.

## 1336 Materials Considerations

Materials selection must take into account the possibility of mechanical damage as well as service conditions. Dry chlorine at room temperature is noncorrosive to carbon steel, copper and the nickel alloys. However, the frequent changing of chlorine containers exposes the piping internals to atmospheric moisture. The corrosion products resulting from the use of carbon steel piping will foul the downstream chlorination equipment.

Chlorine can be handled in Monel piping, tubing, and valves. Container "pigtails" can be cadmium plated copper tubing and should be rated at least 500 psig minimum working pressure. Container "pigtails" should be capped or plugged when out of service to mitigate the intrusion of moisture.

Few alloys can continuously resist wet chlorine. PVC may be considered for the parts of a chlorination system that are continuously wet with, for instance, chlorinated water.

Lead gaskets are used for the container isolating valves. A good practice is to use new lead gaskets when placing a container into service.

Be sure all packing, seals, and gasketing are compatible with chlorine service. Teflon and Viton are acceptable materials.

Consult with your materials engineer for guidance if the recommendations provided in this practice are not suitable for your application.
