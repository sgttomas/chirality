Chlorine as a liquid is also hazardous to personnel. Liquid chlorine in contact with the skin will produce severe burns and blisters. In contact with the eyes it can cause blindness.

## 1332 First Aid and Personnel Protection

Plant personnel who handle chlorine containers and operate chlorination equipment should be trained in first aid, and safe operating and emergency response procedures. Procedures should be included for such activities as:

- Changing containers
- Using container emergency repair kits
- Vapor control and fire fighting

For these and similar activities use impervious neoprene or rubber clothing and a breathing-air apparatus with a vapor-proof, full-facepiece mask. Never attempt a rescue without wearing proper respiratory protection.

Safety shower/eyewash units must be installed as stipulated in the Company's *Safety in Designs Manual* and maintained in good working order.

Safety shower/eyewash units, breathing-air apparatus and protective clothing should be within 50 feet of the chlorine containers. Backup breathing-air apparatus and protective clothing should be provided at other readily accessible locations.

When appropriate, atmospheric monitoring of chlorine releases and sounding of an alarm should be considered.

## 1333 Chlorine Flammability

Chlorine is not flammable but it will support combustion. Chlorine containers are very vulnerable in a fire; cylinders and one-ton containers are fitted with fusible metal plugs that have a melting point between 158°F and 165°F. The purpose of the fusible plug is to allow the pressure to release rather than have the container rupture at high temperature. Chlorine cylinders and one-ton containers do not have conventional pressure relief valves. Therefore, care must be taken in locating and storing chlorine containers to mitigate their risk to fire exposure and release of chlorine.

## 1334 Chlorine Leaks

Only specially trained and equipped personnel should ever attempt emergency repairs on chlorine containers. Emergency repair kits approved by the Chlorine Institute (kit A for cylinders and kit B for one-ton containers) should be available and personnel should be trained in their use. Emergency repair kits may be stored in a central location.
