## 1310 Introduction

This section provides the Company's minimum standards for the design and installation of new facilities using commercial liquid chlorine containers of one-ton capacity and smaller, chlorinators and associated equipment, and piping. In the Company, chlorine is used primarily as a biocide in cooling water treatment.

This section may also be used to upgrade existing facilities.

The Chlorine Institute's *Chlorine Manual*, the National Safety Council (NSC) Data Sheet I-207, and other references listed in Section 1380 were used in preparing this section. Refer to these resources as appropriate when applying the recommendations of this engineering guideline.

This section does not cover bulk chlorine shipping containers such as tank cars, tank trucks or barges.

## 1311 Nature of Chlorine

Chlorine is classified as a nonflammable gas that liquefies under pressure. The gas has a characteristic pungent and suffocating odor and a greenish-yellow color. It is about two-and-one-half times as heavy as air. The liquid is amber in color and is about one-and-one-half times as heavy as water.

Both liquid and gaseous chlorine will react vigorously and explosively with hydrogen to form hydrogen chloride. Reaction with hydrogen sulfide forms hydrogen chloride and sulfur; reaction with ammonia or ammonium compounds can form various mixtures of chloramines, such as NCl₃, which is an explosive compound. Reaction with hydrocarbons, alcohols, and ethers can be explosive. While dry chlorine does not corrode most metals, it can become very corrosive when moist or heated.

## 1312 Summary

Chlorine is a hazardous substance. When designing facilities for chlorine service, rigorous practices are warranted. A failure may release liquid chlorine, which can quickly vaporize and produce large volumes of chlorine gas. Because it is heavier than air, chlorine gas collects at low points and in poorly ventilated areas. Chlorine can be a serious hazard to personnel at low atmospheric concentrations. Minimizing the potential for release of chlorine requires safe, isolated storage and careful handling, tiedown and hookup of the containers by knowledgeable personnel.

## 1313 Key Emergency Actions—Leaking Container

- Alert downwind personnel and facilities.
- Rotate container so only gas leaks and, if possible, move the container to a safe location.
- Use emergency repair kit—obtain from storage.
