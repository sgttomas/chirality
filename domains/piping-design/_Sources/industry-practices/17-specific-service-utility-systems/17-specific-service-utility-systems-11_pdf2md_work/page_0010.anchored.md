Chlorine piping systems should be pressure-tested when new and prior to being placed in service after repairs or alterations. Piping sections operating under vacuum (between the container valve and chlorinator) may be pressure-tested using N₂ gas and soap-tested for leaks. Safe testing practices using a compressed gas should be adhered to. A 150-pug test pressure is recommended for metallic piping systems, but may need to be lowered for nonmetallic piping materials.

Piping for pressurized chlorine feed systems should be hydrostatically tested in accordance with ANSI/ASME B31.3 requirements. Chlorine Institute Pamphlet 6, Piping Systems for Dry Chlorine, should be used for guidance in preparing a hydrostatic test procedure. It is important to thoroughly clean and dry the piping system after testing and prior to the introduction of chlorine. A dryness to a minus 40°F dewpoint is recommended.

## 1344 Vendor-Supplied Systems

All chlorination systems and their proposed site locations shall be reviewed, evaluated, and approved by facility management.

Vacuum-operated chlorinator systems with vacuum regulators attached at the containers offer the safest operation. Applications with chlorine requirements above 20 lb/hr may require a pressure-vacuum system. The pressurized portion of a system should be minimized to reduce the possibility of a leak. Manufacturers of chlorine gas feed equipment can supply their standard system design for evaluation by engineering and safety personnel. They also can provide system setup, maintenance, and periodic service.

Liquid chlorine usually damages the chlorinator components. The following methods may be used to prevent liquid chlorine from entering the chlorinator:

- Install a pressure reducing valve to flash residual liquid.
- Install an electric heater in the chlorinator (most can be factory equipped).
- Heat trace the piping between the containers and chlorinator.
- Install a liquid trap in the piping upstream of the chlorinator.

Chlorinators should be installed in weatherproof enclosures suitable for both the environment and electrical area classification. The enclosure should permit viewing the chlorinator safely through a suitable window.
