## 910 General Considerations

Marketing facilities include: light products terminals (gasoline, diesel, etc.), lube blending and packaging plants, package warehouses, airport fueling facilities, asphalt terminals, asphalt emulsions plants, asphalt refineries, and service stations. For piping used in these facilities, excluding service stations, Marketing endorses the piping design practices found in this manual.

## 911 General Facilities

Piping installations for all Marketing facilities, except service stations, conform to the ANSI/ASME B31.3 Code. Company Specification PIM-EG-2505 should be used for all major piping installations. Marketing piping must comply with OSHA regulations and no cast iron piping elements or equipment shall be used in any hydrocarbon service. Underground hydrocarbon piping is not recommended.

## 912 Service Stations

Service station piping must comply with the Uniform Fire Code and any applicable local codes. Fiberglass is the predominant material for underground piping, including hydrocarbon product piping, because it will not corrode or contaminate the product. Service stations are the only locations where underground hydrocarbon piping is permitted.

Secondary containment of underground piping is required in California and is expected to be required in all states within the next few years. Methods of secondary containment are being explored at this time. Figure 900-1 shows one method using fiberglass troughs around the underground piping.

The steel and stainless steel components of an underground system must be cathodically protected or be externally coated. The choice between cathodic protection and external coating will be determined by the method of secondary containment, because the containment structure can block the flow of cathodic current.

No metal components of an underground piping system are allowed to be in contact with the soil. Therefore, when a flexible joint must be used in a fiberglass piping system, the metal parts of the flexible connector (plastic lined braided metal hose) must be enclosed in a sealed high density polyethylene vent jacket.

Galvanized screwed fittings are only allowed on the top swing joint section of the tank vent riser piping. The rest of the underground vent piping is fiberglass.

## 913 Light Products Terminals

In light products terminals, the piping is standardized on ASTM A106 Schedule 40 carbon steel seamless pipe with ANSI Class 150 carbon steel flanges (Corporation Piping Class E), because it is not cost effective to design, inspect, and administer programs for thin wall pipe or pipe made by other than standard manufacturing processes. Terminal piping jobs are too small to justify special designs, and standard

Schedule 40 seamless pipe is more easily obtained by the local contractors doing the jobs. Piping Class E can be found in the Corporation Piping Specification in Volume 2 of the manual.

### 914 Aircraft Fueling Facilities

Aircraft fuel must be kept scrupulously clean. High efficiency filters and water separators are required. The low points in the piping must be drained. Dust caps are required on all hose connection points or sample test points. Fiberglass piping can be used at small private airports. Large airports use carbon steel piping with welded and flanged joints.

All grades of jet fuel products (Jet A, JP-4, etc.) require carbon steel or stainless steel piping components. No copper, copper alloys, galvanized iron, or cadmium plated iron is allowed in the pipe, valves, or wetted parts of instruments.

## 920 Pressure Design

### 921 General Facilities

Over 95% of Marketing's piping applications are served by the standard pressure ratings (275 psi at 100°F) of ANSI Class 150 carbon steel flanges and Schedule 40 carbon steel pipe.

### 922 Asphalt Refineries

For high temperature use in asphalt refinery piping, ANSI Class 300 flanges are used to maintain tightness through multiple startups and shutdowns.

### 923 Service Stations

The fiberglass pipe used in service station piping should have a design pressure rating of 250 psi at 150°F. Fiberglass pipe is generally Smith Fiberglass Red Thread II or Ameron Dualoy 3000. Fiberglass pipe should conform to Underwriters Laboratories Specification UL-971.

## 930 Hydraulics

### 931 Service Stations

Service station piping systems are designed so that each product dispenser delivers 7 to 9 gpm and each product tank pump delivers 70 gpm at 28 psi. The typical pump shutoff head is 33 psi. This leads to typical pipe sizes of NPS 2 for product piping from the tanks to the dispensers.

### 932 Light Products Terminals

In light products terminals, the typical gasoline and diesel product truck loading systems have flow rates of 600 gpm with discharge pressures up to 80 psi. Pressure

at the outlet of the loading arm should be 5 to 15 psi. Middle distillate (diesel) products are limited to a flow rate of 640 gpm to control the buildup of static electricity.

### 933 Other Plants

Lube plants and asphalt refineries have no set hydraulic standards. The piping systems are designed to meet the desired "nameplate" process capacity of the plant.

## 940 Layout

### 941 General Facilities

Lube plants, emulsion plants, and asphalt refineries use the same piping layout, clearance rules, and flexibility rules that are used in major refineries.

### 942 Light Products Terminals

All piping systems for light products terminals are designed to the same rules as refinery tank farms. All require good fire protection practices. See Section 200 of the *Tank Manual* for guidance. In the loading rack areas, special attention is paid to providing loading arm cross-over space and good, clear escape lanes for truck drivers in case of a flash fire. Load rack piping is placed overhead to control spills and groundwater pollution. See Figures 900-2 and 900-3

### 943 Service Stations

Service station piping is laid out in the most direct route from tank to dispenser island, to minimize pipe length and secondary containment requirements. A typical underground piping layout is shown in Figure 900-4.

## 950 Installation

### 951 Piping Specifications

Marketing endorses the Corporation Piping Specification for all of its nonretail facilities. Terminals and lube plants use pipe classes A, C, and E. Emulsion plants use classes A, C, E and R. Asphalt refineries use classes A, C, E, H, L, N and R. See the Corporation Piping Specification in Volume 2 of this manual.

### 952 Construction Specifications

**Carbon Steel Piping.** Marketing uses Company Specification PIM-EG-2505 for construction of carbon steel piping systems and has no other standard piping specification.

![Fig. 900-2 Typical Light Products Loading Rack; Side Elevation](figures/17-specific-service-utility-systems-07_p0007_fig01_fig-900-2-typical-light-products-loading.png)

![Fig. 900-3 Typical Light Products Loading Rack, End Elevation](figures/17-specific-service-utility-systems-07_p0008_fig01_fig-900-3-typical-light-products-loading.png)

**Fiberglass Piping.** Fiberglass piping is fabricated per the manufacturer's instructions. These systems use bell-and-spigot joints and epoxy adhesive.

After the adhesive joints are cured, the fiberglass piping section is given three tests for quality of workmanship.

- A 10-psig air and soap test for initial tightness.

- A shock test for joint strength. The pipe section is air-pressurized to 100 psi for one minute, then depressurized. The cycle is repeated 5 times. After completion of all cycles and final depressurizing, all joints are inspected for evidence of joint separation.

- After making the final connection to the tank and backfilling the trench, a volume-loss (Petro-tite) test is performed at 40 psig, with product as the test medium. The piping section must not lose more than 0.01 gal/hr to be acceptable.
