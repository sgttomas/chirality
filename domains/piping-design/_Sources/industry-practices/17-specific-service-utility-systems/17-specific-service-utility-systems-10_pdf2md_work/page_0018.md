**Level Indicators.** External level indicators are preferred for pressurized storage. Differential pressure (dp) cells should be considered for spheres. Both cells and external float chambers are suitable for horizontal vessels. Other types of level devices that can be isolated from the storage vessel also should be considered. Capacitance probes are an example.

When internal float-type gages are used—for example, for atmospheric storage—provide sealing valves (ball, plug, etc.) for maintenance use or emergency shutoff. Tape-view windows should be avoided, but as a minimum the viewing unit should have a self-closing shutter. Locate the float and guides so the filling stream does not impinge on the float. Consider the use of stilling wells to avoid damage to the tape.

Avoid the use of gage glasses. Where gage glasses are used, such as on the udders of spheres, normally-closed "deadman" valves should be installed on the leads. Frost-free glass extensions should be used on all gage glasses.

**Level Alarms.** Provide high level alarms for ammonia storage vessels. These alarms should be independent of the primary level gage or indicator to guard against a high level occurring as a result of the malfunction of the primary device.

**Signal and Control Leads.** The signal and control leads for those critical instruments and valves that are necessary for the control, shutdown, or isolation of ammonia equipment should be routed where they are unlikely to be exposed to or damaged by fire. Where this is not practical, these leads should be fireproofed within 50 feet of hazardous equipment such as pumps, compressors, or furnaces. See the *Insulation and Refractory Manual* for recommendations on fireproofing of critical valves, instrumentation, and shutdown systems.

**Vibration.** In vibrating service, consider the use of details similar to those shown in Appendix C.

## Atmosphere Monitoring for Ammonia

Consideration should be given to monitoring the atmosphere around ammonia facilities for low concentration ammonia leaks on the order of 20 ppm or less.

Sensors can be located near critical sources and the facility perimeter, and the instrument and alarm unit mounted in a remote control center.
