**Vibrating Service.** Refer to Appendix C for valve recommendations in vibrating service.

**Wafer Valves.** If flangeless wafer-type valves are considered, they should be the fully-lugged pattern. The full-lug style provides the necessary fire protection for the stud bolts.

**Ball Valves.** Orbit ball valves with a type "K" seat have been effective where tight shutoff is required. They offer an elastomer seal with a secondary metal-to-metal seating capability. Because they are more expensive than a standard gate valve, they are normally only used at primary shutoff points, e.g., block valves at fluid reservoirs, plot limit manifolds, and other points considered critical in an emergency.

## Branch Connections

Reinforced branch connections in ammonia service must be designed for cyclic service and use tees, weldolets and sockolets or thredolets. Table E in the Corporation Piping Specification (GB135169) meets this requirement. Miters greater than 15 degrees are not permitted.

**Vibration Design.** Design branch lines and connections to avoid resonant vibration. Root valves (NPS 1½ and smaller) should be bridge-welded in vibrating service in accordance with Standard Drawing GD-L1057. See the Company Piping Manual for vibration design criteria.

## Utility Connections

Minimize connections to utility systems and design them to positively prevent entry of ammonia into the utility system. See Figure 1200-1.

## Sample Connections

The number of ammonia sample connections should be minimized. Those that are provided should be designed to permit safe sampling. See Figure 1200-2.

Provide block valves on all piping and equipment connections used for sampling. The sample valve must be self-closing. Where operation requires that the self-closing valve be held in the open position for prolonged periods, a fusible-link type of self-closing valve and a remotely operated high-flow shutoff valve must be used.

A three-way valve permits the line to be flushed for a fresh sample and minimizes the possibility for accidental release to the atmosphere.

Locate the outlets of sampling and draw-off lines from ammonia vessels and storage tanks outside the shell drip line so that, if a leak develops, there is no possibility of ammonia impinging on the shell or supports.

## Protection From External Damage

Avoid areas with vehicular traffic when routing lines. Where it is necessary to route near roads and railroads, provide adequate protection for the pipe. Install pipelines aboveground where possible. Where aboveground lines must be routed underground for short distances, as under roads, provide highly reliable protection against frost heaving and external corrosion. Refer to API RP 1102.
