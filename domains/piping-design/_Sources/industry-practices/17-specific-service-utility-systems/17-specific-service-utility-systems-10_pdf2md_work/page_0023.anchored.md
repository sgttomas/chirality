## Fire Water System Design

A fire water system will generally be required around ammonia handling and storage facilities to provide water for diluting releases, to enable access for closing valves, and to handle fires. The size of the fire water system will depend on the amount of and size of the equipment handling NH₃. The system should be looped so that the area is normally supplied through two directions. The pumping system as well as fire monitor locations, hose reel locations, and hydrants should be based upon principles covered in the *Fire Protection Manual*. In storage areas where dilution of NH₃ vapor with water is anticipated, the size of the system may be determined by the number of nozzles that could be put into operation at one time.

## Fireproofing

In areas exclusively handling ammonia, fireproofing may practically be reduced to one to two hours and fireproofing of pipe supports may be eliminated. The prime areas of concern are within 25 feet of pumps, compressors or other equipment handling ammonia liquids or vapors under pressure where releases could be anticipated. Fireproofing of bolted connections is particularly important. Where hydrocarbon handling equipment is also present in the immediate vicinity, the requirements for this equipment must be considered.

In any case, fireproofing is not required for saddles with a minimum web height of 9 inches above a horizontal vessel support structure. This includes exchangers larger than 16 inches in diameter. Normally, fireproofing of vessels and structures above 30 feet in height is not justified.

Anchor bolts for equipment such as columns and vessels should not be fireproofed. Anchor bolts may experience severe corrosion problems if they are fireproofed.

Refer to the *Insulation and Refractory Manual* for recommendations on how much fireproofing to use for process plant supporting structures and where to use it. Acceptable types of fireproofing and installation considerations are also covered there.

## Vessel Connections

Bolts in manholes, flanges, and other bolted connections are the most vulnerable parts of vessels containing ammonia during a fire, since they are not cooled by the evaporating liquid. Bolted connections should be made only as they are required for maintenance or inspection and should be located where they would be least affected by a fire.
