5. Seal-weld all screwed piping.

6. Replace any unconfined gaskets.

7. Eliminate piping smaller than NPS ¾. Use ½-inch OD, 0.035-inch wall minimum (0.065-inch wall preferred) seamless stainless steel tubing at instruments.

8. Review branch connection reinforcement and upgrade as good engineering judgment dictates.

9. Install restriction fittings in instrument leads. (See the Company Standard Drawing GB-J1223 in the *Instrumentation and Control Manual*.)

10. Remove unnecessary cross-connections and jumpovers.

11. Simplify piping by removing any dead legs and all out-of-service or unused piping systems.

12. Eliminate redundant backup instrumentation and make the primary instruments reliable.

## 1272 Inspection

Due to the potential hazard that can result from equipment and/or piping failure, an effective scheduled inspection program is essential. Inspection methods and scheduled frequency must address the probable nature and rate of corrosion. The objective is to provide effective insurance against unexpected failures or leaks. Refer to specific equipment safety instruction sheets (SIS) or local facility inspection records for additional details on plant inspection requirements.

### SCC Growth Rate

Stress corrosion cracks usually grow slowly. Therefore, the potential hazard due to SCC can be greatly reduced by an effective inspection program.

The only truly effective inspection method for detecting ammonia SCC is wet fluorescent magnetic particle inspection. Dry magnetic particle and dye penetrant inspection often find cracks but can be expected to miss many of them.

### Typical Locations for SCC

Ammonia stress corrosion cracks typically appear at welds, on the inside of the vessel. The cracks are most often transverse to the weld seam rather than parallel to it (nozzle welds excepted). This orientation is fortunate because a through-crack in this direction is more likely to simply leak than cause a catastrophic rupture.
