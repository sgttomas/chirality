For structural strength, each gage glass chamber shall be connected to a bridle or a vessel. Do not string out or "daisy chain" gage glasses with short pipe nipple interconnections.

Socket weld or union ends ease field fit-up. Socket weld ends are preferred. Flanged ends are acceptable, but avoid fitting-to-fitting makeup such as with a flanged angle cock, unless the piping can accommodate the stresses.

**Precautions:** Several manufacturers offer cast iron glass covers in their lowest pressure rated glasses. These are not acceptable. Other manufacturers have been found to use a lower quality aluminosilicate glass. **Specify tempered borosilicate glass.**

**Gage Glass Connections.** Socket weld, flange, and union connections are acceptable. Socket weld is preferred. Stuffing box connections shall not be used.

**Gage Glass Unions.** Proprietary gage glass cocks are available with union seats designed to accommodate significant offset and/or angularity. They are acceptable **except** for direct connection to a vessel.

**Gage Glass Restriction Fittings.** In clean services, a flow limiting device shall be installed between the glass and the bridle or vessel. Either an excess flow ball-check valve or a flow restrictor (see Section 5.1.7.4) shall be used as the flow limiting device. Some facilities consider proprietary excess flow ball-check valves (as supplied integral with gage glass cock valves) less reliable than individual checks. Use of either is acceptable based on the experience and preference of the facility involved.

### 5.1.4.4 *(Addition)* Valves

Shutoff valves should be ¾-inch minimum ball, plug, or gate valves with bolted OS&Y (outside screw and yoke) bonnets with bolted gland followers. Unsecured union (screwed) bonnets and screwed packing gland followers are not permitted. Valves shall meet the appropriate piping classification.

### 5.1.4.5 *(Addition)* Vessel Connections

Where a single gage glass is used, the connection may be a flanged NPS 2, ANSI Class 300 (8-bolt minimum) flange with a full-sized valve (if the first valve off the vessel), or reduced using a reducing flange to a ¾-inch or 1-inch socket welded or bridge welded process valve. See Standard Drawing GD-L1057.

Screwed or union connections should not be used between the first valve and the vessel.
