Gate isolation block valves shall be oriented so the gate cannot fall to the closed position should its stem connection fail.

Carseals may be used in place of locks on ¾-inch valves. Carseals are permitted under Appendix M of the *ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code*, Section VIII, Division 1, and should be considered except where prohibited by jurisdictional rules.

*(Addition)* **Fire Protection Considerations, API 2510A Section 2.10.3.3.5**

Consider a safety analysis which evaluates realistic incidents that may result in a storage vessel relief valve discharge with consequent damage restricting or blocking the common header. Where this risk is found unacceptable, a second full-capacity relief valve may be installed on each vessel. This back-up relief valve should be vented to the atmosphere, and be set to a slightly higher pressure to ensure overpressure protection under all fire emergency conditions, without the danger of venting to the atmosphere during operational upsets.

#### 5.1.6.4.7 *(Addition)* **Relief Valves**

Relief valves shall be steel with flanged process connections and bolted bonnet connections. Small relief valves provided to protect against thermal expansion normally have screwed or socket weld (preferred) process connections. Relief valves should be mounted as close as possible to the equipment they protect.

#### 5.1.6.4.8 *(Addition)* **Rupture Disks**

Rupture disks shall not be used in place of relief valves on LPG equipment because they cause uncontrolled depressuring. Consider installation of rupture disks under relief valves when corrosion or leaking valves are a concern. In this case, special rupture disk holders should be employed to protect the thin disk from piping stresses. These also allow a vent and pressure gage to be installed between the disk and the relief valve to provide continuous confirmation of rupture disk integrity. Even with a rupture disk, the relief valve should be built of corrosion-resistant materials.
