In addition to these ATGs, differential pressure transmitters (also called d/p cells) and mechanical float-operated ATGs have been used in many installations. The Company's experience with these has not been very satisfactory because of unreliability or inaccuracy. At a relatively low cost, d/p cells and mechanical float-operated ATGs are still being used to provide rough (i.e., +/- 5 to 10%) level measurement.

**For cylindrical vessels—**At present, many of the LPG bullets are not equipped with any kind of ATG. Some bullets are equipped with d/p cells for rough level measurement. Other ATGs described above can be used, but may not be cost-effective for a large number of small bullets.

For process vessels, displacement-type (float) level transmitters are used. The displacer, or float, is usually in a small cylinder external to the vessel.

LPG storage vessels may also be equipped with sight-mounted gage glasses. These are discussed in Section 5.1.4.

## 5.1.4 Level Gages

### 5.1.4.1 *(Addition)* General

Other commonly used terms for level gages are "gage glass" and "sight level glass." A gage glass is intended to provide a direct visual indication of the liquid level in the vessel. One of the gage glass designs (magnetic follower gage) uses a float inside the vessel to move a magnetic indicator outside which provides a rough level or percent fill indication.

### 5.1.4.2 *(Addition)* Bridles

Mounting gage glasses on NPS 2 or larger bridles is preferred for increased mechanical strength, fewer and stronger vessel connections, and double block and bleed capability during instrument maintenance. A bridle is particularly suited for mounting more than one instrument.

### 5.1.4.3 *(Addition)* Gage Glasses

Gage glasses shall be the heavily encased type with ¾-inch minimum connections. They shall be a maximum of 60 inches in length, with confined glass-to-chamber gaskets. If longer ranges must be covered, use two or more overlapping gage glasses, preferably bridle mounted.
