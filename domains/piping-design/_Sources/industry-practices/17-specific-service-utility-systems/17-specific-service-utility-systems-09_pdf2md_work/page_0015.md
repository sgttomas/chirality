## 5.1.2 Liquid-Level Gaging Equipment

### 5.1.2.1 (General)

*(Addition)* The term "liquid-level gaging equipment" here refers to automatic tank gages (ATGs) used in spheres, spheroids, and cylindrical LPG storage vessels (i.e., "bullets" and "sausages"). The type of ATG chosen depends on the application, service, and type of storage. Commonly used ATGs are listed in Section 5.1.2.6.

### 5.1.2.2 (Second, Independent Level Gages)

*(Exception)* Although use of a second, independent ATG is called for in API Standard 2510, this is not yet a common practice. The merits of providing a second, redundant ATG versus independent level switches should be investigated for tanks in high-risk locations, as determined by a safety analysis.

### 5.1.2.3 (Independent High-Level Alarms)

*(Addition)* An ATG with alarms at predetermined levels may be installed, but should not be used in place of an independent high-level alarm. An independent high-level alarm should not be used as a daily operating tool to fill a sphere or bullet, but should be regularly tested. The time required for an operator to react to the alarm and stop filling should be considered in setting the high-level alarm height.

Similar criteria should be used on low-level alarms to prevent pulling a vacuum on the vessel and to ensure sufficient NPSH to operate the pump.

### 5.1.2.5 (High Level Cutoffs)

*(Addition)* In some high-risk locations, a high-high level alarm/cutoff switch may be needed to shut down the pump or stop filling. If installed, this high-high level switch should be independent of the first high-level alarm. The cutoff switch should be fail-safe, and certified by UL, FM or CSA, as required. A self-checking feature is highly preferred.

The design philosophy described in API RP 2350, *Overfill Protection for Petroleum Storage Tanks*, can be used for reference.

### 5.1.2.6 *(Addition)* Commonly Used Level Gages

**For spheres and spheroids**—servo-operated ATGs (e.g., Enraf-Nonius), radar level gages, magnetic switch ATGs (e.g., Gem Dalavat), inductive ATGs (e.g., Sarasota Automation), hydrostatic tank gages (or HTGs; e.g., Foxboro, Rosemount and Texas Instruments) have been used by the Company and by others with varying degrees of success.
