#### 7.3.1.5 *(Addition)* Pump Shutdown

##### Remote Manual Shutdown

Remote shutdown of all electric pumps shall be provided at a safe distance in case the pumps are involved in a fire. Collect all pump shutdowns at one or more remote points. A motor control center can serve this purpose. The remote shutdown point should be no less than 200 feet from the nearest pump and shall be identified by a clearly visible sign.

##### Coordinate Pump and Suction Source Shutdown

Pump motors must be tied in with the vessel suction valve(s) to shut down simultaneously. If the unplanned shutdown of certain suction system/pump combinations would result in an operational upset, consider installing anticipatory fire or hydrocarbon vapor detection alarms. See Section 5.1.7.3.4, Early Warning Systems, and API 2510A, Section 5.5 for additional information.

#### 7.3.1.6 *(Addition)* Unattended Pumps

##### Startup of Remote and/or Normally Unattended Pumps

Because of the potential for seal failures at pump startup, local start only (by an operator) is preferred for remote or normally unattended pumps. Local start is particularly important when the pump must start against a head greater than the vapor pressure of the pumped fluid and/or when downtime between operations gives the fluid in the suction lines a chance to vaporize and cause the pump to lose suction.

##### Surveillance of Remote or Normally Unattended Pumps

Hydrocarbon vapor detection, television surveillance, or fire detection systems (tied into the responsible control center) are recommended for remote or unattended pumps that are critical to operations and/or within 100 feet of other facilities. Vapor detectors should only be used for area monitoring by spacing them approximately 5 feet or more from pumps or other potential leak sources. Where the pumps are not required to be in continuous operation and/or are not critical to operations, low-flow shutdowns (usually in the discharge line) are recommended.
