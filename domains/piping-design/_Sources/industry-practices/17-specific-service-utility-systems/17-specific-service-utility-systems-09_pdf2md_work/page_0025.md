## 6.2.2 Piping Joints

### 6.2.2.1 (Vessel Nozzle Block Valves)

*(Addition)* For vessel connections below the liquid level, block valves shall be installed (without intervening joints) as close to the vessel as possible. (See Section 2.5.)

### 6.2.2.3 (Minimize Fittings)

*(Addition)* Minimize the number of flanges and valves; eliminate them from the design if they are not necessary for operations or maintenance.

### 6.2.2.5 (Pipe Joints Smaller Than 2 Inches)

#### 6.2.2.5.1 *(Addition)* General

Use no pipe (or fittings) less than ¾ inch in diameter (except for orifice tap root valves).

Socket welded, butt welded, bridge welded, union, threaded (subject to the constraints of Section 2.5), or flanged joints are acceptable.

Bridge welding of all short nipples is recommended, including all nipples for vent and drain valves, orifice taps and other small valves per Standard Drawing GD-L1057.

Seal welded, threaded, and socket welded joints are acceptable where (1) pipe dope or tape has not been used to make up the threaded joint and (2) all exposed pipe threads are covered.

Four-bolt flanges have limited fire resistance and should be avoided. They should not be used in higher fire risk areas such as around pumps and loading racks.

Where flange leaks could cause direct LPG impingement on vessels or equipment, consider providing metal shields that cover up to one-half of a piping flange circumference.

#### 6.2.2.5.2 (Exception) Threaded Connections

Threaded connections may only be used past the first block (root) valve for NPS ¾ to 1½ piping in gas plants, production sites, and remote locations.
