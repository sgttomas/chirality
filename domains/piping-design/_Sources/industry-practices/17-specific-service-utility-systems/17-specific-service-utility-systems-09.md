## 1110 Section 1—General

The Company's HE&LP (Health, Environmental and Loss Prevention) Group fully supports and concurs with the requirements and recommendations of API Standard 2510, *Design and Construction of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) Installations*, and API Publication 2510A, *Fire-Protection Considerations for the Design and Operation of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) Storage Facilities*. All new LPG facilities shall be designed and constructed in compliance with API 2510 and API 2510A.

This guideline covers only those areas where Company recommendations exceed or differ from those in API 2510. It is a **supplement** to API 2510 and must be used in conjunction with that document. For easy reference, the organization, numbering and paragraph headings of this guideline match those of API 2510, Sixth Edition, April 1989. Sections are noted as being *additions or exceptions* to API 2510.

### 1.1 Scope

*(Addition)* All new LPG facilities shall be designed and constructed in compliance with API 2510, *Design and Construction of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) Installations*, which this guideline supplements. This guideline covers only those safety features where Company recommendations exceed those in API 2510. It is intended for Company-wide use. However, it is the responsibility of local management to assess their facility's exposure (the potential human, economic and environmental impact) in the event of an LPG release incident and to decide how closely they will adhere to the recommendations of this guideline.

The philosophy and principles of 2510A, *Fire-Protection Considerations for the Design and Operation of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) Storage Facilities* should also be incorporated into the design, operation and maintenance of all new LPG facilities. Local management should assess the applicability and value of API 2510A in reducing risks.

*(Exception)* Although the scope of API 2510 and API 2510A covers only **LPG storage and loading facilities**, the principles and guidance contained in this guideline, API 2510, and API 2510A may be helpful in reducing risk if applied to the design and operations of **LPG processing facilities**.

#### Background *(Addition)*

In designing LPG facilities, special precautions are necessary because failure of piping or equipment can release liquid hydrocarbons which quickly vaporize to form a heavier-than-air, highly flammable cloud that does not readily disperse. For this reason, the fundamental objective is to minimize the release of LPG to the atmosphere. This is accomplished by providing high equipment reliability, minimizing the number of connections, avoiding unnecessary equipment, and protecting against inadvertent damage or failure.

To select which safeguards presented herein are appropriate for use in the design of a new facility, potential hazards must first be identified and analyzed. The massive failure of a vessel with a full inventory of LPG is of greatest concern. Although risk

of such an accident will always exist, the probability of this type of failure can be reduced to an acceptable level with a properly engineered, operated, and maintained facility.

Historically, most LPG fires have resulted from a small leak that ignited. A more serious danger is the potential for massive explosions if a small leak, over time, creates an accumulation of heavy autorefrigerated vapors in a low spot on a calm day.

The two causes of LPG leaks are equipment failure and human error. The most likely sites of equipment failure are pump seals, valve packing, and pipe joints. The most common examples of human error are overfilling of vessels and the release of LPG to the atmosphere during water drawoff or sampling.

This guideline and its associated references present a collection of requirements, recommendations, practices and procedures which, if carefully considered and judiciously applied, will greatly reduce the risk of serious fires and/or explosions in LPG facilities.

# 1120 Section 2—Design of LPG Tanks

## 2.1 Applicable Design Construction Codes

**Spheres** *(Exception)*

Field constructed tanks shall conform to Specification PVM-MS-997 or be of equivalent quality.

**Heat Exchangers (if any)** *(Exception)*

Heat exchangers shall conform to Specification EXH-MS-2583, or equivalent quality.

**Repairs and Alterations** *(Addition)*

Repairs and alterations shall be performed in compliance with API Standard 510, *Pressure Vessel Inspection Code*, or the code under which the vessel was initially constructed; they should also comply with any state or local jurisdictional requirements.

**Burying and Mounding** *(Exception)*

Direct burial or mounding of LPG vessels is not recommended. See API Standard 2510, Section 8.12.

#### 2.1.1 (Vessels)

*(Exception)* Vessels (and columns if any) shall conform to Specification PVM-MS-4750, or be of equivalent quality, except in those instances where other, more rigorous specifications apply because of heavy wall thicknesses or use of special alloys.

## 2.3 Design Vacuum

*(Addition)* API Standard 2510 requires protection against vacuum collapse. It recommends full vacuum design for LPG vessels as the first choice followed by a vacuum breaker admitting (in order of preference) inert gas, hydrocarbon gas, or air.

The Company requires that all LPG vessels be designed for an external pressure of at least 10 psi at 450°F. Full vacuum design is recommended where the added cost would be less than vacuum breaking using inert gas, fuel gas, natural gas, or air.

In determining whether a vacuum breaking system is warranted, factors to be considered include extreme low winter temperatures, rundown temperatures, rapidity of storage turnover rates, and stored product mix.

## 2.4 Materials of Construction

### 2.4.3 *(Addition)* **Brittle Fracture Design**

All vessels should be designed to resist brittle fracture at the temperature that would result from the autorefrigeration resulting from the vessel's pressure suddenly being reduced from 100% to 40% of its maximum allowable working pressure. **Take precautions to ensure that the equipment does not autorefrigerate below the minimum pressurizing temperature (MPT)** when being filled or removed from service.

Low temperature fracture-resistant materials such as ASTM A516 are now readily available at costs only somewhat higher than for A285C. These materials are increasingly used in all types of pressure vessels, as well as for increased protection from brittle fracture at the wall thicknesses and autorefrigeration temperatures commonly found in LPG storage vessels.

For more information on fracture-safe design, see the *Pressure Vessel Manual*, Section 500.

## 2.5 Vessel Connections

### 2.5.2 (Flange Connections)

**Gaskets** *(Exception)*

Use confined gaskets for all manways and closures. Raised-face flanges with spiral-wound gaskets (with a centering ring) or ring joints are acceptable.

## 2.5.4 *(Exception)* Process (and Utility) Connections

All vessel connections through to the first block valve shall be NPS 2 minimum, flanged, ANSI Class 300 minimum. A valve shall be installed on each nozzle below the liquid level and located immediately adjacent to the vessel. Raised-face (preferred) or ring joint confined gaskets shall be used. (See Section 6.2, Pipe, for piping details.)

For gas plants, production sites, and remote facilities, certain exceptions may apply:

- Class 150 flanges may be acceptable, except that four-bolt flanges (NPS 2 and smaller) shall not be used.
- ¾-inch threaded male end valves may be used if threaded directly into a threaded Class 6000 coupling that is welded to the vessel.

Consult local management on specific requirements.

## 2.5.5 *(Exception)* Individual Instrument Connections

An exception to the requirements in the preceding paragraph applies to small, isolated, single instrument installations where direct vessel connections are required, such as for a small level gage on a dropout leg or an isolated level switch. For these, NPS ¾ or 1 bosses (or Class 6000 couplings) are acceptable if growth in associated external (process or utility) lines from a fire cannot impose significant piping forces on the assembly. Pressure gages which are not required to communicate directly with the vessel should be connected to external piping. In all cases, a valve shall be installed immediately adjacent to the vessel connection. See Section 5.1.7.

## 2.5.6 *(Addition)* Bridles

To avoid multiple vessel connections, NPS 2 minimum flanged pipe bridles are recommended for connecting instrument assemblies. To ensure that a bridle can handle the weight of the instrumentation, increase its size and/or provide elevation growth-type supports. Refer to Standard Drawing GB-J1170 and Section 6.2 of this guideline.

## 2.5.7 *(Addition)* Minimum Connections

Keep the number of vessel connections to the minimum required for operations, maintenance and inspection.

## 2.5.8 *(Addition)* Connection Locations

Bolts in manways, flanges and other bolted connections are the most vulnerable parts of LPG vessels during a fire since they are not cooled by the evaporating liquid. If possible, locate bolted connections where they would be least affected by a fire. Orient flanged joints so that they present the lowest risk of a jet fire impinging on a vessel or piping should the flange leak.

### 2.5.9 *(Addition)* Manways

Provide two manways on spheres, one top and one bottom. Horizontal vessels should have two manways for ventilation and access if handling sour LPG; otherwise, only one manway is required on horizontal vessels. The manway should preferably be located on the head opposite the piping end.

## 2.6 Previously Constructed Vessels

#### 2.6.1 *(Addition)* Moving Existing Vessels

Existing vessels that are moved to a new location (or facility) for use in the same (or a new) service and newly purchased used vessels shall:

1. Meet the criteria of API 2510, Section 2.6, and
2. Be hydrotested to 1.5 times the maximum design pressure (or maximum allowable pressure if downgraded).

#### 2.6.2 *(Addition)* **Full Penetration Butt Welds**

Prior to 1950 the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section VIII did not require full penetration butt welds. Vessels constructed to this older code **must have all butt welds replaced with full penetration butt welds** if they are moved to a new location (or facility) for use in **identical or similar design conditions.**

Vessels which are moved to a new location (or facility) for use at **less severe conditions must be downgraded to the appropriate joint efficiencies prescribed by the current ASME Code** to arrive at the new or revised design conditions.

Contact the Pressure Vessel Consultant at CRTC Materials and Equipment Engineering for any necessary guidance in this area.

## 2.7 Insulation

*(Addition)* External shell surface corrosion has been experienced in insulated LPG storage vessels. Contributing factors are: 1) lack of an effective vapor barrier, 2) humid climate, and 3) rundown or storage temperatures below the dew point.

To determine the need for insulation, consider all of the factors discussed in API 2510, Section 2.2, Design Pressure and Temperature, so that insulation will not have to be added later to prevent overpressure conditions during the summer months.

The requirements for use of thermal insulation as fireproofing are discussed in API 2510, Section 8.10, Fireproofing of LPG Vessels. If insulation is required for fire protection, control of external shell corrosion should be considered in the selection and installation of insulating materials. Consult Materials and Equipment Engineering at CTRC for specific recommendations.

# 1130 Section 3—Siting Requirements and Spill Containment

### 3.1 Siting

*(Addition)* When choosing a site and laying out new LPG facilities, consider the probability and effect of a major release incident and what additional precautions may be taken. Consider whether nearby facilities are frequented by personnel and whether the new facility is critical to operations. Refer to AIChE's *Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures* and API Publication 2510A, *Fire Protection Considerations for the Design and Operation of LPG Storage Facilities*. See also the *Fire Protection Manual*.

#### 3.1.2 Minimum Distance Requirements For Aboveground LPG Tanks

**Spacing** *(Exception)*

Storage vessels, except spheres, should be arranged in groups of not more than 6 with a minimum of 10 feet between vessels and 50 feet between groups. Install spheres at least one diameter apart. CRTC's Health, Environment and Safety Group staff is available to offer guidance on spacing and layout.

### 3.2 Spill Containment

**Objectives** *(Addition)*

- Prevent the accumulation of liquid under LPG storage vessels and piping.
- Minimize the chance of flame impingement from a burning spill.
- Provide an impoundment location that minimizes the risk to critical facilities, piping and equipment if the pool ignites.
- Minimize the surface area of a spill to reduce the rate of vaporization of the material that collects and limit the size of the resultant vapor cloud.

#### 3.2.1 (Surface Drainage)

*(Addition)* General recommendations for surface drainage are covered in the *Civil and Structural Manual*, Section 500, Drainage.

##### 3.2.1.1 (Grading)

*(Addition)* The drainage system shall slope away from vessels, tanks, manifolds, and piping to a safe containment location.

#### 3.2.3 (Remote Impoundment)

*(Addition)* Remote impoundment is strongly recommended.

#### 3.2.3.2 Containment Dikes and Swales

*(Addition)* Containment dikes and swales are acceptable means of directing spills and are less costly than walls. These, as well as all drainage areas, should be designed to prevent spilled LPG from approaching closer than 5 feet to other piping, vessels or equipment.

#### 3.2.3.3 (Remote Impoundment Location)

*(Exception)* Locate remote impoundment areas downwind of LPG equipment to minimize the chance of flame contact with adjacent storage areas. A 100-foot separation is preferred.

Impoundment should be at least 200 feet from and preferably downwind from any fixed ignition or static discharge source (furnace, loading dock, etc.).

#### 3.2.3.4 (Impoundment Design Criteria)

*(Addition)* Design impoundment to minimize fire potential:

1. Minimize the liquid surface area:
   - Slope the bottom and make judicious arrangement of drainage paths.
   - Make provisions to drain rain and fire water. Use valves and piping suitable for the autorefrigeration temperatures. Locate drain valves so they may be operated manually or remotely during a spill or fire.
2. Minimize the wetted surface area:
   - Avoid using loose material such as gravel or rock on the surface of the drainage path or impounding area. (These materials will increase turbulence and vaporization of LPG.)
3. Minimize heat input:
   - Use special substrate such as insulating concrete or compacted clay where warranted. The latter, when slightly damp, can freeze, further isolating the pool from subsurface heat.
   - Avoid very wet soil or standing water, which serve to increase heat transfer before the surface freezes.
4. Minimize external fire hazards:
   - Keep all combustible materials (including vegetation) well away from LPG facilities, LPG drainage paths, and impoundment areas so that they will not become involved in any fire.

*(Exception)* LPG vessels shall **not** be supported using unreinforced concrete or masonry.

*(Addition)* Combine or tie together adjacent equipment, structures, and/or support foundations to avoid overstressing piping and connections from differential settlement. Where this is not practical, design the piping to accommodate 1.5 times the predicted differential settlement and install bench marks so settlement can be monitored. *(Exception)* The use of floating foundations is not recommended. Use spread or pile-supported foundations.

### 4.2.7 Loads on Supporting Structure

*(Addition)* In addition to the loads required by API Standard 2510, the loads and load combinations specified in the Uniform Building Code shall be incorporated in the design.

### 4.2.8 Support Design

*(Addition)* Support structures or piers shall be designed to withstand friction-generated forces from vessel thermal expansion or contraction.

#### 4.2.10 Diagonal Members

*(Exception)* Diagonal members shall be attached to the structural support system (i.e., ring girder or top of structural column). This point of attachment should be designed and reinforced to resist the loads of the brace.

#### 4.2.11 Design of Supports

#### 4.2.12 Saddles

The program will select the appropriate support saddle from Standard Drawing GA-C99694 for the vessel geometry and calculate support reactions for two loading conditions: operating weight and temperature, and hydrostatic test loads.

# 1150 Section 5—Tank Accessories, Including Pressure- and Vacuum-Relieving Devices

## 5.1 Mandatory Equipment

### 5.1.1 General

**Pressure and Temperature Ratings** *(Addition)*

The pressure and temperature ratings of all instruments shall be equal to or greater than those required for the associated pipe class.

## 5.1.2 Liquid-Level Gaging Equipment

### 5.1.2.1 (General)

*(Addition)* The term "liquid-level gaging equipment" here refers to automatic tank gages (ATGs) used in spheres, spheroids, and cylindrical LPG storage vessels (i.e., "bullets" and "sausages"). The type of ATG chosen depends on the application, service, and type of storage. Commonly used ATGs are listed in Section 5.1.2.6.

### 5.1.2.2 (Second, Independent Level Gages)

*(Exception)* Although use of a second, independent ATG is called for in API Standard 2510, this is not yet a common practice. The merits of providing a second, redundant ATG versus independent level switches should be investigated for tanks in high-risk locations, as determined by a safety analysis.

### 5.1.2.3 (Independent High-Level Alarms)

*(Addition)* An ATG with alarms at predetermined levels may be installed, but should not be used in place of an independent high-level alarm. An independent high-level alarm should not be used as a daily operating tool to fill a sphere or bullet, but should be regularly tested. The time required for an operator to react to the alarm and stop filling should be considered in setting the high-level alarm height.

Similar criteria should be used on low-level alarms to prevent pulling a vacuum on the vessel and to ensure sufficient NPSH to operate the pump.

### 5.1.2.5 (High Level Cutoffs)

*(Addition)* In some high-risk locations, a high-high level alarm/cutoff switch may be needed to shut down the pump or stop filling. If installed, this high-high level switch should be independent of the first high-level alarm. The cutoff switch should be fail-safe, and certified by UL, FM or CSA, as required. A self-checking feature is highly preferred.

The design philosophy described in API RP 2350, *Overfill Protection for Petroleum Storage Tanks*, can be used for reference.

### 5.1.2.6 *(Addition)* Commonly Used Level Gages

**For spheres and spheroids**—servo-operated ATGs (e.g., Enraf-Nonius), radar level gages, magnetic switch ATGs (e.g., Gem Dalavat), inductive ATGs (e.g., Sarasota Automation), hydrostatic tank gages (or HTGs; e.g., Foxboro, Rosemount and Texas Instruments) have been used by the Company and by others with varying degrees of success. Except for HTGs, these ATGs are top mounted and they are usually installed on a stilling well. Refer to the *Tank Manual*,

Section 700, Instrumentation/Measurement, for more information on ATGs.

In addition to these ATGs, differential pressure transmitters (also called d/p cells) and mechanical float-operated ATGs have been used in many installations. The Company's experience with these has not been very satisfactory because of unreliability or inaccuracy. At a relatively low cost, d/p cells and mechanical float-operated ATGs are still being used to provide rough (i.e., +/- 5 to 10%) level measurement.

**For cylindrical vessels—**At present, many of the LPG bullets are not equipped with any kind of ATG. Some bullets are equipped with d/p cells for rough level measurement. Other ATGs described above can be used, but may not be cost-effective for a large number of small bullets.

For process vessels, displacement-type (float) level transmitters are used. The displacer, or float, is usually in a small cylinder external to the vessel.

LPG storage vessels may also be equipped with sight-mounted gage glasses. These are discussed in Section 5.1.4.

## 5.1.4 Level Gages

### 5.1.4.1 *(Addition)* General

Other commonly used terms for level gages are "gage glass" and "sight level glass." A gage glass is intended to provide a direct visual indication of the liquid level in the vessel. One of the gage glass designs (magnetic follower gage) uses a float inside the vessel to move a magnetic indicator outside which provides a rough level or percent fill indication.

Although they are sometimes seen on small to medium size LPG storage vessels, gage glasses are more commonly used on cylindrical process vessels. Gage glasses are usually mounted on an assembly called a **bridle**. The bridle assembly and its major piping components are described below:

### 5.1.4.2 *(Addition)* Bridles

Mounting gage glasses on NPS 2 or larger bridles is preferred for increased mechanical strength, fewer and stronger vessel connections, and double block and bleed capability during instrument maintenance. A bridle is particularly suited for mounting more than one instrument.

### 5.1.4.3 *(Addition)* Gage Glasses

Gage glasses shall be the heavily encased type with ¾-inch minimum connections. They shall be a maximum of 60 inches in length, with confined glass-to-chamber gaskets. If longer ranges must be covered, use two or more overlapping gage glasses, preferably bridle mounted.

For structural strength, each gage glass chamber shall be connected to a bridle or a vessel. Do not string out or "daisy chain" gage glasses with short pipe nipple interconnections.

Socket weld or union ends ease field fit-up. Socket weld ends are preferred. Flanged ends are acceptable, but avoid fitting-to-fitting makeup such as with a flanged angle cock, unless the piping can accommodate the stresses.

**Precautions:** Several manufacturers offer cast iron glass covers in their lowest pressure rated glasses. These are not acceptable. Other manufacturers have been found to use a lower quality aluminosilicate glass. **Specify tempered borosilicate glass.**

**Gage Glass Connections.** Socket weld, flange, and union connections are acceptable. Socket weld is preferred. Stuffing box connections shall not be used.

**Gage Glass Unions.** Proprietary gage glass cocks are available with union seats designed to accommodate significant offset and/or angularity. They are acceptable **except** for direct connection to a vessel.

**Gage Glass Restriction Fittings.** In clean services, a flow limiting device shall be installed between the glass and the bridle or vessel. Either an excess flow ball-check valve or a flow restrictor (see Section 5.1.7.4) shall be used as the flow limiting device. Some facilities consider proprietary excess flow ball-check valves (as supplied integral with gage glass cock valves) less reliable than individual checks. Use of either is acceptable based on the experience and preference of the facility involved.

### 5.1.4.4 *(Addition)* Valves

Shutoff valves should be ¾-inch minimum ball, plug, or gate valves with bolted OS&Y (outside screw and yoke) bonnets with bolted gland followers. Unsecured union (screwed) bonnets and screwed packing gland followers are not permitted. Valves shall meet the appropriate piping classification.

### 5.1.4.5 *(Addition)* Vessel Connections

Where a single gage glass is used, the connection may be a flanged NPS 2, ANSI Class 300 (8-bolt minimum) flange with a full-sized valve (if the first valve off the vessel), or reduced using a reducing flange to a ¾-inch or 1-inch socket welded or bridge welded process valve. See Standard Drawing GD-L1057.

Screwed or union connections should not be used between the first valve and the vessel.

**5.1.4.6** *(Addition)* **Bridle Connections**

Any of the above valves that are suitable for connection to vessels are suitable for use with bridles. In addition, solid or union shank gage cocks may be used on bridles in either straight or offset patterns.

## 5.1.5 Pressure Gage

### 5.1.5.1 *(Addition)* Construction

Pressure gages shall be solid-front blowout-back type with shatter-proof lenses. Type 316 stainless steel Bourdon tubes and sockets with hardened stainless steel movements shall be used in all sweet, salt-free services. Special materials are required for sour, salt-contaminated, and other corrosive service conditions (e.g., H₂S service). Brass or other low melting point materials shall not be used.

### 5.1.5.2 *(Addition)* Mounting Location

The vessel's vapor space pressure shall be provided at (or visible from) grade. To avoid unnecessary small connections on vessel shells, mount pressure gages on associated piping, such as a bridle.

### 5.1.5.3 *(Addition)* High-Pressure Alarms

As a minimum, all LPG vessels should be provided with a connection leading to the vapor space for installation of a high-pressure alarm. Spheres shall have panel-mounted high-pressure alarms in an attended location.

## 5.1.6 Pressure- and Vacuum-Relieving Devices

### 5.1.6.2 Pressure Relief Valve Flow Capacities

*(Addition)* The pressure relief valves and relief piping system(s) shall be designed for both fire and overfilling, whichever places greater demand on the relief system, in accordance with API 520.

### 5.1.6.3 Pressure Relief Valve Information

*(Addition)* All relief valves shall be tested and tagged in accordance with local facility instructions.

### 5.1.6.4 Pressure Relief Valve Installation

#### 5.1.6.4.5 (Relief Valve Isolation for Maintenance)

*(Addition)* To ensure that vessels can never be blocked off from relief, the recommended method of providing for in-service maintenance is by using dual full capacity relief valves with multiple-way or interlocked isolating block valves.

Alternatively, locked open gate or ball valves may be used for isolation under (or around) dual full-capacity relief valves, but great care must be taken to ensure that these isolation valves are,

indeed, fully open so that vessels are protected at all times when their respective relief valves are intended to be in operation. When a relief block valve is closed for maintenance, an operator should continuously monitor the process until the relief block valve is locked open.

Gate isolation block valves shall be oriented so the gate cannot fall to the closed position should its stem connection fail.

Carseals may be used in place of locks on ¾-inch valves. Carseals are permitted under Appendix M of the *ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code*, Section VIII, Division 1, and should be considered except where prohibited by jurisdictional rules.

*(Addition)* **Fire Protection Considerations, API 2510A Section 2.10.3.3.5**

Consider a safety analysis which evaluates realistic incidents that may result in a storage vessel relief valve discharge with consequent damage restricting or blocking the common header. Where this risk is found unacceptable, a second full-capacity relief valve may be installed on each vessel. This back-up relief valve should be vented to the atmosphere, and be set to a slightly higher pressure to ensure overpressure protection under all fire emergency conditions, without the danger of venting to the atmosphere during operational upsets.

#### 5.1.6.4.7 *(Addition)* **Relief Valves**

Relief valves shall be steel with flanged process connections and bolted bonnet connections. Small relief valves provided to protect against thermal expansion normally have screwed or socket weld (preferred) process connections. Relief valves should be mounted as close as possible to the equipment they protect.

#### 5.1.6.4.8 *(Addition)* **Rupture Disks**

Rupture disks shall not be used in place of relief valves on LPG equipment because they cause uncontrolled depressuring. Consider installation of rupture disks under relief valves when corrosion or leaking valves are a concern. In this case, special rupture disk holders should be employed to protect the thin disk from piping stresses. These also allow a vent and pressure gage to be installed between the disk and the relief valve to provide continuous confirmation of rupture disk integrity. Even with a rupture disk, the relief valve should be built of corrosion-resistant materials.

#### 5.1.6.5 Discharge Vents

##### 5.1.6.5.2 (Atmospheric Relief)

*(Addition)* If available or if required by law, a closed relief system which recovers or flares gas should be employed. Atmospheric relief is acceptable if occasional or accidental release of LPG is permitted by applicable laws and regulations and a closed relief system is not available.

The codes permit infrequently used vents/drains (for startup or system cleanup) to not be permanently connected to a closed system. An atmospheric relief valve is recommended as an emergency backup when the primary relief valve is connected to a closed system. See Section 5.1.6.4.5 above.

##### 5.1.6.5.4 (Atmospheric Relief Vent—Moisture Protection)

*(Addition)* Do not use hinged covers on relief vents, as they can rust or freeze into position.

**Atmospheric Relief Vent—Drain Connections** *(Addition)*

A 3/8-inch diameter hole at the low point or a ¾-inch 90-degree elbow is recommended as a drain connection on the bottom of an atmospheric relief stack to prevent flame impingement on the vessel shell. If a hole larger than 3/8 inch is used, it must have an elbow oriented to direct venting gases away from the vessel or associated piping. Because of plugging problems, do not run small piping from this drain to grade.

##### 5.1.6.5.7 *(Addition)* Atmospheric Vent Stacks

These shall be sized to produce an exit vapor velocity of 100 ft/sec. Refer to API 520, Part II, for a method of calculating the resulting discharge reactive forces. Provide vent stack bracing to resist these forces, as necessary. (It is noted that API 520 suggests a higher exit velocity for dispersion; however, recent jet plume dispersion programs have confirmed 100 ft/sec.)

##### 5.1.6.5.8 *(Addition)* Relief System Service Piping

Design relief system piping to drain continuously (sloped lines), without low spots or traps, from the relief valve to a liquid collection point. Ensure that the material of the discharge piping and liquid drums can resist brittle fracture due to shock chilling associated with flashing LPG, and design to accommodate the low temperature induced movement.

##### 5.1.6.5.9 *(Addition)* Vapor Depressuring Systems

Vapor depressuring systems have only limited application in LPG storage systems. See API Standard 2510A and consult with the

HE&LP (Health, Environmental and Loss Prevention) Fire Protection staff.

## 5.1.7 Shutoff Valves

### 5.1.7.1 (Shutoff Valves)

*(Exception)* Shutoff or "root" valves permit isolation of instruments from the process at its source, or "root." A shutoff valve shall be installed immediately at any pipe or tubing connection to a process vessel or line on all instrument installations. Minimum root valve size is NPS ¾, except for orifice flange tap valves, which, up through ANSI Class 600 flange rating, require NPS ½ root valves.

### 5.1.7.3 (Safety)

#### 5.1.7.3.1 *(Addition)* **Quick-Closing Valves**

Quick-closing valves shall be installed immediately downstream of the block valves on all LPG storage vessel fill and pump suction lines, and on all other lines below normal liquid level (except instrument connections) which communicate with piping and equipment external to the specific vessel. Swing-check valves are required on fill lines, and air-operated quick-closing valves are required for suction lines.

#### 5.1.7.3.2 *(Addition)* **Remote Vessel and Pump Shut-In**

Shut-in valves shall be capable of being manually actuated from remote locations, as well as being automatically actuated. Automatic shut-in shall be from fire detection system(s) at all associated LPG pumps and loading stations within 200 feet. Alarm actuation of these systems shall be installed in attended control centers. (See also Section 7.3.1, Pumps, covering simultaneous shutdown of associated pumps.) Valves shall close upon power failure.

#### 5.1.7.3.3 *(Addition)* **Fire Detection Systems**

LPG services; fire detection systems Acceptable fire detection systems are:

1. Pneumatic systems using plastic tubing (or other types of sensors) that is run close to any potential location for a fire
2. Flame detectors
3. Heat detectors, when they can be located close to any potential location for a fire. Alarm actuation of these systems should be installed in attended control centers. (Flame and heat detectors should be relied upon only in facilities with a strong maintenance and testing program for them.)

Ultraviolet fire detection systems may be tripped by welding and radiography crews. If shutdown capability must be temporarily bypassed, maintain alarm capability.

#### 5.1.7.3.4 *(Addition)* **Early Warning Systems**

In cases where the unplanned shutdown of LPG pumping systems would result in an operational upset, it may be appropriate to install a prefire early warning system using hydrocarbon vapor detectors to warn dependent facilities of an impending shutdown.

#### 5.1.7.3.5 *(Addition)* **Distinguishable Alarms**

All alarm systems should distinguish between serious situations (such as a manual shutdown because of a spill) and emergency situations (a fire). See also Section 1170.

#### **5.1.7.4** *(Addition)* **Restriction Fittings**

Restriction fittings are recommended for installation immediately after all root valves leading to pressure, flow, level, and analyzer instruments. Their purpose is to minimize the release of LPG in case of failure of a downstream component such as thin wall instrument tubing, small piping, or a gage glass. Exceptions are level controllers, where such fittings would prevent proper instrument function, and in fouling services, where plugging is a strong likelihood. All restriction fittings should be tagged.

**Recommended Restriction Fittings**

1. Tubing connections: a 304 SS, screwed hex-head 3/16-inch drilled pipe-to-tubing "Restriction Connector" in accordance with Standard Drawing GB-J1223.

2. Piping connections (such as for level instruments):

   a. A carbon steel screwed and seal welded hex-head 3/16-inch drilled "restriction adaptor" (also in accordance with Standard Drawing GB-J1223 in the *Instrumentation and Control Manual*); or

   b. A 1/8-inch restrictive orifice plate mounted either between a pair of flanges or in a gasketless orifice union (Clayton Mark, or equal—see Section 6.5, Unions below; orifice plates are recommended for ease of cleaning); or

   c. A ball-check excess flow valve. (Usually used only with gage glasses. Recommended for clean non-fouling services only; grit and fouling may block their complete closure as well as cause them to stick closed.)

#### 5.1.8 Temperature Indicator

##### 5.1.8.1 *(Addition)* Location of Temperature Point

Liquid temperature indication shall be at (or visible from) grade.

##### 5.1.8.2 *(Addition)* Thermowell Construction

Thermowells may be flanged or threaded. Threaded thermowells are safer in a fire because they have no bolts that may overheat, but care should be exercised to ensure that threaded thermowells are not unscrewed in error while a vessel is in service (instead of unscrewing their dial thermometer or temperature element).

# 1160 Section 6—Piping Requirements

## 6.2 Pipe (Including Pressure Tubing and Excluding Instrument Piping)

**Note** *See Section 6.2.3, Minimum Specifications, does cover Instrument Piping. The title of Section 6.2 (which excludes instrument piping) is taken directly from API Standard 2510..*

### 6.2.1 Recommended Pipe

*(Addition)* Construct all piping in accordance with the latest revision of Company Specification PIM-MS-2505 for carbon steel pipe or PIM-MS-4770 for stainless steel pipe.

**Restricted Materials of Construction** *(Addition)*

Do not use materials having limited resistance to fire (unless other metal-to-metal features, as found in some soft-seated valves, make them fire resistant). Examples of common piping and equipment elements that **shall not be used** are:

- Cast iron, brass, bronze, or copper in pressure-retaining parts.
- Rubber, plastic, or Teflon as used in Victaulic couplings or packing in valves and pumps, and woven metal jacketed flexible tubing.
- Thin-walled equipment and piping such as bellows expansion joints.
- Expansion joints having limited ability to accommodate offset, such as packed or Dresser couplings.
- Gasketed pipe couplings such as Victaulic couplings.

- Low-melting alloys such as in valve handwheel bushings. (Follow API Standard 600, Section 3.)

## 6.2.2 Piping Joints

### 6.2.2.1 (Vessel Nozzle Block Valves)

*(Addition)* For vessel connections below the liquid level, block valves shall be installed (without intervening joints) as close to the vessel as possible. (See Section 2.5.)

### 6.2.2.3 (Minimize Fittings)

*(Addition)* Minimize the number of flanges and valves; eliminate them from the design if they are not necessary for operations or maintenance.

### 6.2.2.5 (Pipe Joints Smaller Than 2 Inches)

#### 6.2.2.5.1 *(Addition)* General

Use no pipe (or fittings) less than ¾ inch in diameter (except for orifice tap root valves).

Socket welded, butt welded, bridge welded, union, threaded (subject to the constraints of Section 2.5), or flanged joints are acceptable.

Bridge welding of all short nipples is recommended, including all nipples for vent and drain valves, orifice taps and other small valves per Standard Drawing GD-L1057.

Seal welded, threaded, and socket welded joints are acceptable where (1) pipe dope or tape has not been used to make up the threaded joint and (2) all exposed pipe threads are covered.

Four-bolt flanges have limited fire resistance and should be avoided. They should not be used in higher fire risk areas such as around pumps and loading racks.

Where flange leaks could cause direct LPG impingement on vessels or equipment, consider providing metal shields that cover up to one-half of a piping flange circumference.

#### 6.2.2.5.2 (Exception) Threaded Connections

Threaded connections may only be used past the first block (root) valve for NPS ¾ to 1½ piping in gas plants, production sites, and remote locations.

#### 6.2.2.5.3 *(Addition)* Small Piping Root Valves

Small piping (NPS ¾ to 1½) is recommended only for instrumentation and auxiliary services (sample connections, instrument piping, odorizer injection, etc.). These piping nuts should be

equipped with a root valve to isolate them at their junction with the process line.

**6.2.2.5.4** *(Addition)* **Utility Connections**

Minimize connections to utility systems. Utility connections should include provisions for disconnection when they are not in use, such as a turn spool or spectacle blind, which will positively prevent entry of LPG into the utility lines. Ensure that a check valve is installed in each utility connection to a process system.

Figures 1100-1 and 1100-2, Utility Connections, present suggested ways to do this. The connection in Figure 1100-1 is preferred. This type of connection uses a turnout or removable spool to isolate the process system from the utility system. The connection in Figure 1100-2 is permitted where the utility is frequently used, for example, a water flush connection to a process instrument. This type of connection uses a spectacle blind for isolating the process system from the utility system.

## 6.2.3 Minimum Specifications

*(Addition)* All piping shall be in accordance with the Company piping Classes H1 and N1, or in accordance with a class specifically designed for LPG service and approved by local management for use in LPG service.

### 6.2.3.4 *(Addition)* Instrument Piping—Specifications

Generally, all level instruments are installed using pipe. All piping associated with level instruments and all other instrumentation shall comply with the requirements in Section 1160, Piping Requirements. Specifically, all piping components shall conform to the material.

temperature and pressure rating, and branch connection reinforcement requirements of the appropriate piping classification.

## 6.2.4 Pressure Tubing

### 6.2.4.1 *(Addition)* Tubing Specifications

Type 304 SS and/or 316 SS, ½-inch minimum diameter tubing with 0.065-inch (16 gage) minimum wall is recommended. Some facilities allow ⅜-inch, 0.049-inch wall and 3/8-inch, 0.035-inch wall tubing. No copper fittings or tubing are permitted. Do not weld the tubing or the tubing connectors.

### 6.2.4.2 *(Addition)* Tubing Fittings

Tubing fittings may be from any recognized national manufacturer, but should match fittings currently used in the facility. Ensure that all fitting components in a single joint are from the same manufacturer, that the components are installed in the correct sequence and orientation, and that they are tightened in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

### 6.2.4.3 *(Addition)* Tubing Runs

Minimize the length of small instrument tubing and piping that contains LPG. Mount transmitters as close as possible to root connections and brace securely against damage. Minimize the length of vent and bleed connections from instruments to the first valve (positive shutoff) location.

## 6.2.5 *(Addition)* Vibrating Services

For vibrating services, 100% radiography and heavily reinforced branch connections are recommended in accordance with Table E of the Corporation Piping Specification. Small connections (1½ inches and under) shall be bridge welded per Standard Drawing GD-L1057. This would apply to rotating and reciprocating machinery and associated piping. Refer to Appendix C of this manual for more information.

Avoid mounting instruments on vibrating equipment or piping. Where this is unavoidable, mounting and support shall be arranged to allow longer sections of bent or coiled tubing so that no significant cyclic bending stresses can be induced (see Appendix C). Tubing is recommended because it can be bent without the use of fittings. In both pipe and tubing, vibration (bending) stresses are greatly intensified at fittings. Threaded not covered by seal welding and overtightened tubing compression fittings worsen the situation.

## 6.2.6 *(Addition)* Bridles

All piping and tubing runs must be well supported and protected against mechanical damage, and instruments must be mounted so that they are structurally stable. Bridles are preferred. Bridles shall be used where the

length of the connection from a vessel to an instrument (including valves and fittings) exceeds two feet. Refer to Standard Drawing GB-J1170, in the *Instrumentation and Control Manual*.

#### 6.2.7 *(Addition)* Line Identification

In plants handling products other than LPG, it is recommended that lines containing LPG be labeled and/or painted distinctively for easy recognition. Light, non-chalking colors such as white or aluminum are suggested because they minimize heat input from the sun. In plants with large numbers of LPG lines, painting in conformance with the general painting scheme is satisfactory.

#### 6.2.8 *(Addition)* Dead Legs

Eliminate dead legs because they can lead to corrosion, present a clean system flush, and collect water that may freeze during cold weather. Pockets of LPG within piping and equipment systems present a serious personnel hazard during maintenance operations.

### 6.3 Fittings

#### 6.3.1 Threaded Fittings and Couplings

*(Addition)* For small piping, socket weld fittings are recommended. In vibrating service, threaded fittings are acceptable only if seal or bridge welded.

### 6.4 Plugs

*(Exception)* Use only solid bar-stock steel plugs. Plugs shall be installed in all small open-end valves such as sample connections, vents and drains. (See Section 6.7.2.11 for special valves for LPG services.)

### 6.5 Unions

*(Addition)* Pipe and instrument union connections are acceptable except when used immediately against LPG vessels without an intervening valve. Unions are more difficult to make leaktight than flanges, particularly if their seats are damaged from repeated coupling. However, properly made up and tight, unions are more fire safe than small four-bolt flanges whose bolting can be loosened by high temperatures. Unions exposed to severe fire conditions may part due to expansion of the union nut, so they should not be used in higher risk areas.

When used, unions should be installed in matched pairs from the same manufacturer and should meet criteria of the Manufacturers Standardization Society's document MSS-SP-83.

### 6.6 Valves

*(Addition)* All valves shall be API Standard 600 valves (except specialty valves) with ANSI/ASME/API body-bonnet closure designs—bolted, welded, or pressure seal style with secondary mechanical retention features. Threaded bonnet or union bonnet valves should not be used except as needle valves or instrument manifold

valves, as covered by Section 6.6.5, Instrument Valves. Conventional gate and globe valves should have an outside screw and yoke (OS&Y). Valves shall be steel, fire-resistant, and supplied with confined body gaskets with socket weld, butt weld, or confined gasket flanged ends.

#### 6.6.1 Primary Shutoff Valves

##### 6.6.1.3 (Flangeless Valves)

*(Addition)* Only full lugged pattern fire-resistant valves are acceptable, because the long bolts are protected from fire exposure by passing through the lugged body of the valve.

The use of flame impingement shields on existing flangeless valves is a compromise for a deficient design.

##### 6.6.1.5 *(Addition)* Tight Shutoff

Valves that mechanically increase seat loading when closed are recommended when tight shutoff is required. Of this type, Orbit valves are considered more reliable. Also acceptable are API 6D valves (such as WKM bolted bonnet Powerseal) and API Standard 600 valves (such as the Crane/Pacific HIS). Thermal relief valves are required on Powerseal type valve body cavities in all liquid services (see Section 6.6.4).

##### 6.6.1.6 *(Addition)* Plot Limit/Remote Block Valves

Provide block valves in protected locations so that they are safely operable, manually and/or remotely, in case of a fire. These valves provide a means to prevent discharge of stock from long lines into a burning area. Plant plot limits and storage area plot limits are excellent locations for these block valves.

##### 6.6.1.7 *(Addition)* Blinding

A minimum of a double block and bleed is required for installation of blanks (between the block valves). Quick change blinds are not recommended for LPG service. The convenience of these devices does not justify the increased hazard from lighter flanges and fewer bolts.

#### 6.6.4 Thermal Relief Valves

**Liquid Phase Thermal Expansion** *(Addition)*

Provide steel liquid expansion thermal pressure relief valves for all sections of piping that can be blocked off in normal operation, and for the body of certain double-seated valves (see Section 6.6.1.3).

This is particularly important when all outlets to a section of line that is blocked off in normal operation are equipped with tight shutoff valves. Refer to the *Instrumentation and Control Manual*, Section 1200, for additional details.

**6.6.5** *(Addition)* **Instrument Valves**

Instrument root valves (the first valve off a process line, vessel, or equipment) shall comply with the associated piping classifications. (Exception: See proprietary gage cocks in Section 5.1.4, Gage Glasses, under Valves.)

Instrument valves shall be either of piping classification or stainless steel barstock needle valves.

Union bonnets are acceptable in instrument needle valves as long as a positive keeper (or tack weld) prevents loosening of the bonnet when the stem is turned. Threaded packing followers are also acceptable provided the valve stem and plug are positively retained by a shoulder within the bonnet and cannot blow out through the packing gland.

**6.6.6** *(Addition)* **Control Valves**

All control valves shall have injectable grease seal stem packing. Globe-type valves are preferred. Select wetted materials consistent with trim requirements for the piping classification.

**6.6.7** *(Addition)* **Throttling Valves**

Any valve or other device used to throttle LPG flow can reach low autorefrigeration temperatures. Such devices, as well as a portion of the downstream piping, should be constructed of materials suitable for the lowest temperature reached.

## 6.7 Location, Installation, and Flexibility of Piping, Valves, and Fittings

### 6.7.1 Mandatory Practices

#### 6.7.1.2 (Flexibility)

*(Addition)* LPG piping should be designed for special flexibility in the event of an earthquake, and for expansion and contraction due to the sun, autorefrigeration and fire. See Section 6.7.2.8 regarding prohibition of bellows-type expansion joints.

#### 6.7.1.4 (Water Draw Disposal)

*(Addition)* All water draws shall drain to a closed system designed to withstand full pressure and LPG exposure. Disposal shall be via a hydrocarbon recovery system or at a safe remote site. Drains shall have two block valves (minimum) including a self-closing (deadman) valve. Where operation requires that the self-closing valve be held in the open position for prolonged periods, a fusible-link remotely operated valve shall be used. This fusible-link valve shall be located near the open end of the drain so that exposure to a water draw fire would cause the valve to close.

#### 6.7.1.6 *(Addition)* Routing and Protecting Lines

All LPG lines shall be routed for greatest protection from external damage. When it is necessary to route near roads, railroads, or other hazardous areas, provide adequate protection for the lines. Specific guidelines are:

- Do not allow lines to rub against other lines.
- LPG lines on overhead road crossings should be shielded from accidental damage by a passing crane or A-frame boom by locating structural steel or other less hazardous large lines at the pipeway's edge. If the pipeway is multi-layered, place LPG piping on the upper level.
- Keep LPG lines well away from the edges of roadways. Provide guard posts when necessary.
- Guard or eliminate small piping "steps" that might be used by persons unaware of the hazard. Pump drain valves are a common example.

## 6.7.2 Recommended Practices

### 6.7.2.4 (Location)

*(Addition)* Install LPG lines aboveground where possible, and prevent contact with the ground. Where piping must be buried, as in oil field gathering systems and pipelines, reliable external cathodic protection is essential. Refer to API RP 1102, *Recommended Practice for Liquid Petroleum Pipelines Crossing Railroads and Highways*, for further guidance on railroad and highway crossings.

### 6.7.2.6 Railroad Tracks and Roads

**Permits** *(Addition)*

Railroad rights of way and other public roads require permits and design approval by regulatory authorities.

### 6.7.2.8 (Thermal Expansion)

*(Exception)* Bellows-type expansion joints shall not be used; they have poor fire-resistance.

### 6.7.2.11 (Vent and Drain Valves)

*(Addition)* Drains used during normal operation shall be equipped with double valves (one self-closing) and drain to a closed system for appropriate disposal, just as for water draws (see Section 6.7.1.4).

All vent and drain valves should be socket welded or screwed and bridge welded to the process line or equipment and plugged with a barstock steel plug when not in use. Both Smith and Vogt make special gate valves specifically for this service. They have one end (male)

extended threaded or socket weld, the other end female threaded. The valves are reduced port and therefore are not recommended for services where rodding-out might be required.

For gas plants, production sites or remote facilities, use of a threaded male end valve threaded directly into a threaded boss is acceptable. See Section 2.5, Vessel Connections.

# 1170 Section 7—Loading, Product Transfer, and Unloading Facilities

## 7.3 Transfer, Loading, and Unloading Equipment

### 7.3.1 Pumps

#### 7.3.1.1 *(Addition)* General

If a centrifugal pump is required, select a high quality pump with low shaft deflection. Pump selection shall follow API Standard 610 and Company Specification PMP-MS-983. Cases shall be steel. Vertical pumps are acceptable. Cast iron, malleable iron, nodular iron, brass, and bronze case materials shall not be used. Do not use copper alloys. Refer to the *Pump Manual* for further detailed requirements.

#### 7.3.1.2 *(Addition)* Seals

##### Mechanical Seals

Use a reliable mechanical seal. An external clean seal flush is strongly recommended. (See the *Pump Manual*, Section 700, Mechanical Seals.) Where indicated by environmental concerns, the pump shall be equipped with multiple seals. Tandem mechanical seals with primary seal failure alarms are being used successfully by organizations with a good training and maintenance program for their use. Pumps with single seals should be ordered with stuffing boxes large enough to accommodate a future retrofit to tandem or double seals for increased leak protection.

##### Seal Failure

Seal failure is the most likely cause of an unplanned LPG release. Narrow boiling range liquids such as LPG are inherently poor lubricants. LPG can vaporize completely across seal faces, causing seals to fail unless a high differential seal chamber pressure is maintained.

Nevertheless, LPG loading pumps with good quality single seals can perform reliably, even though they start and stop frequently. Good design and specific operational procedures can prevent (1) startup without a full liquid pack (from solar heating and inadequate venting), (2) flow shutoff at the truck valve before shutting down the pump, and (3) pump shutdown when a vessel is pumped dry.

#### 7.3.1.3 *(Addition)* **Low Flow Protection**

Listed below are three options for preventing low flow conditions, along with some associated operational problems. See the *Pump Manual* and the *Instrumentation and Control Manual* for more information.

1. Route pump seal and case vents back to the suction vessel. These vents can be left open if the pump is adequately sized.

   Operational problems:

   - These vents may be inadvertently blocked off when switching service, or left open to the wrong vessel with the potential for product cross-contamination.
   - This option may be inconvenient if venting is required frequently.

2. Route the minimum flow bypass back to the suction vessel. The operational problems of this alternative are similar to "1." above.

3. Install low-flow shutdown instrumentation. Use of low-flow shutdown switches is quite common. Generally speaking, the three major kinds of low flow switches are:

   - Mechanical (e.g., paddle type)
   - Differential pressure (uses an orifice plate as a flow measurement device)
   - Thermal (uses a thermistor to measure the rate of heat dissipation in order to detect low flow)

At present, differential-pressure type low-flow switches are commonly used on LPG storage vessels. Paddle-type switches have been found unreliable by some users. Thermal-type switches need electrical power to the switch (either 110V AC or 24V DC) but do not cause a head loss. Refer to the Flow section of the *Instrumentation and Control Manual* for more details.

Cautions when using shutdown flow switches:

- If used as an automatic shutdown device, failure of the flow switch will unnecessarily shut down the operation
- The impact and sequence of an abrupt shutdown must be considered
- Some shutdown logic (e.g., time delay) may be needed to avoid the effect of "water hammer."

#### 7.3.1.4 *(Addition)* **Throttle Bushings and Atmospheric Vents**

Close-clearance, radially floating carbon throttle bushings shall be fitted into the gland plate outboard of the seal. Pipe the vent to a safe location for disposal to minimize potential hydrocarbon releases in the

vicinity of truck loading areas. (Caution: Connection to a common relief header through a check valve has proved troublesome in past installations because of contaminated blowback through a leaking or stuck check valve.) Alternatively, plug the drain and vent holes.

#### 7.3.1.5 *(Addition)* Pump Shutdown

##### Remote Manual Shutdown

Remote shutdown of all electric pumps shall be provided at a safe distance in case the pumps are involved in a fire. Collect all pump shutdowns at one or more remote points. A motor control center can serve this purpose. The remote shutdown point should be no less than 200 feet from the nearest pump and shall be identified by a clearly visible sign.

##### Coordinate Pump and Suction Source Shutdown

Pump motors must be tied in with the vessel suction valve(s) to shut down simultaneously. If the unplanned shutdown of certain suction system/pump combinations would result in an operational upset, consider installing anticipatory fire or hydrocarbon vapor detection alarms. See Section 5.1.7.3.4, Early Warning Systems, and API 2510A, Section 5.5 for additional information.

#### 7.3.1.6 *(Addition)* Unattended Pumps

##### Startup of Remote and/or Normally Unattended Pumps

Because of the potential for seal failures at pump startup, local start only (by an operator) is preferred for remote or normally unattended pumps. Local start is particularly important when the pump must start against a head greater than the vapor pressure of the pumped fluid and/or when downtime between operations gives the fluid in the suction lines a chance to vaporize and cause the pump to lose suction.

##### Surveillance of Remote or Normally Unattended Pumps

Hydrocarbon vapor detection, television surveillance, or fire detection systems (tied into the responsible control center) are recommended for remote or unattended pumps that are critical to operations and/or within 100 feet of other facilities. Vapor detectors should only be used for area monitoring by spacing them approximately 5 feet or more from pumps or other potential leak sources. Where the pumps are not required to be in continuous operation and/or are not critical to operations, low-flow shutdowns (usually in the discharge line) are recommended.

#### 7.3.1.7 *(Addition)* Overpressure Cautions

Occasionally a pump must be installed that can produce shutoff pressures in excess of that allowed on a downstream component. In such a case, either a redundant high-pressure shutdown switch or a relief

valve discharging to a safe location (such as back to the vessel from which the pump takes suction) is required. In systems where a pump may take suction from more than one vessel, plan operational procedures to prevent the pump relief return line from being blocked in or directed toward the wrong vessel.

**7.3.1.8** *(Addition)* **Preventive/Predictive Maintenance**

Institute a regular preventive/predictive maintenance program. In particular, concentrate on elimination of seal/bearing failures. Routine vibration monitoring and analysis such as used in the Integrated Machinery Inspection program is recommended.

**7.3.1.9** *(Addition)* **Vertical Pump Installations**

Direct burial of vertical LPG pump outer pressure casings is not recommended because of the potential for undetected corrosion and leaks. Instead, install pumps in individual steel pipe sleeves with welded steel bottom closures to prevent the entrance of water. Provide long-term corrosion protection for the sleeves.

**7.3.1.10** *(Addition)* **Reciprocating Pumps**

Avoid using reciprocating pumps in LPG service because leakage is inevitable with these pumps. Leakage around piston rods or plungers is difficult to contain in a closed system. If a reciprocating pump must be used (e.g., pipeline injection), provide a steel liquid end, including steel rods. Do not use copper alloys.

**7.3.4.5***(Addition)* **Boundary Isolation Valves**

Install boundary isolation valves as an added precaution where pump block valves are more than 100 feet away from the loading point. See also Section 6.6.1.3.7, Plot Limit/Remote Block Valves.

*(Exception)*Use electrically conductive hoses that meet the requirements of API Standard 2510, are UL-21 approved, and have stainless steel connections. Refer also to API RP 2003.

## 7.5 Hose and Other Flexible Connectors for Product Transfer

**7.5.5** *(Addition)* **Direct Mounting of Excess Flow Valves on Trucks or Rail Cars**

Using a reducing adapter (and correspondingly smaller diameter hose or loading arm) on truck and rail loading/unloading connections can limit flow to the point where the excess flow valve in the vehicle would fail to close should the transfer line be ruptured or torn away. If it is necessary to use a reducing adapter, the loading hose or arm must be equipped with a backflow check valve, a properly sized excess flow valve, or a shutoff

valve capable of remote operation. The shutoff device must be at the end of the hose or arm that connects to the truck or rail car. This requirement does not apply if the truck or car is equipped with a quick-closing internal valve that can be remotely operated.

## 7.6 Blowdown or Venting of Loading and Unloading Lines

### 7.6.1 *(Addition)* Block Valves

LPG loading hoses or swivel joint loading arms may be equipped with a block valve located at the truck/car terminus of the loading hose to reduce escape of vapor or liquid when disconnecting the hose after product transfer. This valve should be compatible with the truck/car terminus, and may be brass, bronze or aluminum. It should be rated for the maximum operating pressure. Quick shutoff lever-operated ball (or other) valves may be used. This block valve may be lightweight and need not be fire-resistant. Refer to API 2510A, Section 3, for recommendations on proper operating procedures for product transfer.

### 7.6.2 *(Addition)* Pressure Relief Valves

Install pressure relief valves to protect the loading hoses/arms from thermal expansion pressure buildup.

### 7.6.3 *(Addition)* Return Vent Line

A return vent line (usually running back to the supply or suction vessel) shall be provided to prevent truck and car loading over-pressures. This line shall be equipped with a steel check valve to prevent backflow.

## 7.10 *(Addition)* Loading Area

### 7.10.1 Control of Loading Operations

A supervisory center to control traffic and limit access to the LPG loading/unloading facilities may be justified where more than two trucks might be waiting and where drivers and vehicles should be monitored.

The supervisory center shall be located outside the fire zone and overlooking the loading area, if possible. The **Emergency Response Plan** and the **Loading Operating Procedures** shall be available in the supervisory center. The loading operating procedures shall also be posted at the loading facility.

### 7.10.2 Fire & Shutdown Trouble Alarms

Install trouble alarms and annunciators in the nearest attended supervisory center that has responsibility for the area. See Section 5.1.7.3.

### 7.10.3 Communications

If radio communication equipment is not always carried, provide alternate means of communicating with appropriate control center(s) near the loading racks, pumps, and with all remote manual shutdown points.

#### 7.10.4 Protection Against Vehicular Damage

Protect loading racks against damage from vehicles:

1. All loading/unloading points shall be arranged so that trucks may enter and park head-in to be loaded/unloaded. Provide a combination of roads or, where space permits, a generously sized area for turn-around of the largest anticipated truck so that drivers may avoid backing. Provide ample space so trucks arriving may wait at a safe distance (50 feet) until it is permitted to enter the loading area.
2. Provide curbing, posts, and/or other barriers to guard against a truck damaging pressure-retaining equipment and piping.

#### 7.10.5 Drainage

The loading area should be graded and paved to direct spills away to a safe area. Locate gas-sealed drain catch basins (Standard Drawing GD/S99992) away from LPG loading positions. Loading spots shall be sufficiently level to ensure that liquid will not wet or cover the relief valve opening on any vehicle as it is parked during LPG transfer.

## 8.1 General

**Ignition Source Control** *(Addition)*

Risk is best controlled by good design, although proper maintenance and operating procedures play a major role in reducing potential ignition sources to a minimum.

- **Site selection:** See Section 1130, Siting Requirements and Spill Containment.
- **Site layout:** Route roads well away from LPG facilities. Lay out facilities to eliminate unnecessary personnel and vehicular access and to allow close control of necessary maintenance and operations activities. Plan for (and install as needed) segregated areas for equipment and materials assembly, smoking, parking areas, and smoking areas for employees, visitors, tradesmen, maintenance contractors, etc.
- **Electrical design:** Provide electrical grounding and bonding as recommended by API 2003, *Prevention of Ignitions Arising Out of Static, Lightning and Stray Currents*. Prepare and follow area classification drawings with particular attention to conduit seal fittings. Refer to the *Fire Protection Manual* for electrical area classification.

### 8.14.3 *(Addition)* **Testing and Calibration**

Facilities shall be installed so that complete testing and calibration of alarm and shutdown instrumentation and control systems can be performed while the equipment is in full operation.

The tests shall confirm that all components, including primary sensors and final actuators, function correctly without requiring the help of instrument mechanics or electricians. Disabling of any portions of the system for testing shall be alarmed so that it gives a clear warning to operating and supervisory personnel as long as the instrumentation remains disabled.

Disabling of each primary sensor or selected groups of sensors must be accomplished without disabling the remainder of the shutdown system. For most sensors, the shutdown system will be a padlocked disabling switch

adjacent to the primary sensor. Ensure that the plant can continue to operate safely during testing with a minimum increase in operator attention.
