## 6.1 GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS

Note that these considerations are only a handful of instances that may help to determine the type of repair method used to eliminate a leak. This is not a comprehensive list, as each situation can present a unique set of requirements/issues. The purpose of stating these considerations is to give the reader an idea of what unforeseen issues can arise when installing clamps or performing leak repairs. When in doubt, defer to the Reliability Engineer and Inspection Department to help give specific guidance when making repairs.

## 6.2 COMPOSITE WRAPS

a) Composite wraps may be utilized for non-leaking external repairs in all pressure classes, but shall be limited to Class 150 services for leaking defects. All repairs shall adhere to ASME PCC-2 and careful consideration shall be given to the process media and abnormal conditions such as steam out. Repairs that were leaking shall be considered temporary and shall be permanently repaired at the next turnaround. A UV topcoat should be applied to repairs that will remain in service greater than two years. The use of intumescent coatings should be considered within process areas. In many cases additional fire protection will not be necessary, as the damaged original pipe may still be adequate for a short duration fire event.

Further guidance may be found in ASME PCC-2 Articles 4.1 & 4.2, GN 36-008 and ISO 24817.

## 6.3 CLASS 1500 HYDROGEN SYSTEMS

a) Drill and taps on live process equipment is not allowed on Class 1500 hydrogen systems.

## 6.4 REPAIRS WHEN CORROSION IS PRESENT

a) When equipment is being repaired due to corrosion, regardless if there is presently a leak or not, consideration should be given to whether the corrosion is internal or external. If corrosion is external, often times the corrosion rate can be mitigated by coating the equipment before performing the repair, making the corrosion issue negligible. When the corrosion issue is internal, careful consideration should be given to the source of the corrosion and corrosion rate, then that information should be used to guide the type of repair that is made and how long that temporary repair can be kept in service. Consideration should also be given to the material compatibility with the service, should the corrosion continue far enough to cause hole through the equipment and cause exposure to the repair material. If corrosion is internal to the pipe, consideration should be given to how subsequent inspection can be performed with the repair in place to monitor the corrosion rate.
