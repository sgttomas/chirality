## SECTION 1: BACKGROUND

This standard establishes policies and procedures for the repair of a leak that has occurred on equipment, piping, pipe flanges, valve bonnets, or valve packing while it is in service in an operating unit at the BP Cherry Point Refinery. If the leak repair is a temporary repair, only to be in place until a permanent repair can be made, then the following procedure will be used. This procedure also applies to clamps (i.e. temporary repair enclosures) and wraps installed on thinning piping or equipment that is not yet leaking.

This procedure is NOT applicable if:

1. The repair restores the component and system to their design condition (e.g. a leaking valve is replaced with a new valve that meets the line specification).

2. The repair is a permanent repair that changes the design condition.

3. The repair involves killing a valve. Killing a valve shall be handled as an AMR.

   a) An AMR shall be created to document killing the valve.

   b) A second AMR shall be created to replace the valve and restore it to service.

   c) A TAR workorder shall be created to complete the work in the second AMR.

4. The repair consists only of the tightening of flange studs, valve packing gland bolts, or valve bonnet bolts, provided the tightening of bolts eliminates the leak. Before tightening flange studs or bonnet bolts, Reliability Engineering should be contacted to determine feasibility of tightening bolts, and to provide required torque values, if applicable.

## SECTION 2: PURPOSE

This Site Technical Practice (STP) is intended to ensure that temporary leak repairs are properly designed, installed, documented, tracked until permanent repairs may be completed at planned outages. The intent of this STP is to provide a means to mitigate the risk presented by unplanned outages by repairing leaks on equipment, piping, pipe flanges, valve bonnets or valve packing while online.

Items that have been repaired using this STP should be permanently repaired at the next planned outage, whenever possible. The next planned outage is often the next applicable turnaround, but this may not always be the case. It is the responsibility of the person filling out the leak repair form to work with necessary work groups to determine the proper time at which permanent repairs can be made. This date should be placed in the Leak Repair Form in the applicable field.

## SECTION 3: SCOPE AND APPLICATION

There are two classes of repairs. The permissions required to be obtained for each class of repair are defined by the service and the extent of the required repair method to eliminate the leak. These two classes are defined as Class 1 and Class 2 repairs:

---

## 3.1 CLASS 2 REPAIRS:

a) Installation of any clamp (i.e. temporary repair enclosures).

b) Any repair where there are signs of corrosion.

c) All repairs to hydrocarbon, chemical, steam (above 140#) and boiler feed water systems.

d) All other repairs to any system not specifically listed in section 3.2.b as a Class 1 repair.

## 3.2 CLASS 1 REPAIRS:

a) Shall not be used for the installation of clamps (i.e. temporary repair enclosures) and/or repairs where there are signs of corrosion.

b) Class 1 repairs may be used for Class 150 flange or lower systems in the following services only: steam (140 PSIG or less), condensate, air, utility water, cooling water, or firewater.

## 3.3 LEAK REPAIR DATABASE:

a) The Leak Repair Database exists as a module in the Addition and Modification Request (AMR) / Management of Change (MOC) Tracking System. The Leak Repair Form mentioned in this STP is the on-line form in the database.

b) The Leak Repair Form includes a Drawing File Path field. A folder for each leak repair shall be created in the **R:\CHPcomm\Leak Repairs** folder. The folder name shall be the Leak Repair number generated by the Leak Repair Database. All documents associated with the leak repair shall be stored in the folder and should be sufficient to verify the adequacy of the repair design as well as provide sufficient information to plan the permanent repair.

## SECTION 4: PROCEDURE AND RESPONSIBILITIES

### 4.1 PROCEDURE FOR REPAIR:

a) Operations (an **Operator** or the **Reliability Coordinator**) initiates the leak repair by filling out the Leak Repair Form in the Leak Repair database.

b) The **Reliability Coordinator** reviews the Leak Repair Form and informs the Fixed Equipment Engineer that there is a leak repair that requires action.

c) The **Fixed Equipment Engineer** and **Reliability Coordinator** together determine a suitable repair method and decide if it is a Class 1 or Class 2 repair. In making this determination they consult, as needed, with others, such as the **Unit Process Engineer**, **Unit Inspector**, the **Corrosion Engineer**, the **Metallurgist**, or a **Leak Repair Contractor**.

   i) For a Class 2 repair, the **Fixed Equipment Engineer** notifies the Unit Inspector and then electronically signs the Inspection Notification field on the Class 2 Repair portion of the Leak Repair Form. Upon the request of the Reliability Engineer, the Reliability Coordinator may notify Inspection and complete the sign off.

   ii) The **Inspector** directs the Inspection contractors to perform any inspections required to determine the suitability of the various repair options and forwards the results to the Fixed Equipment Engineer.

   iii) The **Fixed Equipment Engineer** reviews and approves any drawings and calculations produced by the Leak Repair Contractor.

   iv) The **Fixed Equipment Engineer** completes the engineering requirements on the Leak Repair Form, approves the repair method, and ensures that all supporting documents are filed in the Leak Repair folder described in Section 3.3.b. Supporting documents could include marked P&IDs, marked isometric drawings, clamp drawings and calculations, mill certifications, composite wrap calculations, etc.

   v) If a clamp (i.e. Temporary Repair Enclosure) is to be installed (or if a flange will wrapped and peened), the **Fixed Equipment Engineer** ensures that the requirements of Section 5 of this STP are met and then completes Appendix A of this STP and routes it to the Unit Process Superintendent for review and approval. The **Process Superintendent** then reviews and approves Appendix A.

   vi) The **Reliability Coordinator** creates Work Orders in the Maximo system as needed for the Leak Repair Contractor or BP Maintenance Shop.

d) The **Unit Control of Work Area Authority** arranges for a risk assessment appropriate for the repair method to be used and the system to be repaired.

## SECTION 5: REQUIREMENTS FOR TEMPORARY REPAIR ENCLOSURES

### 5.1 TEMPORARY REPAIR ENCLOSURE SCOPE

A temporary repair enclosure is a device such as a clamp or welded leak box that encloses a leak location. The interior of the enclosure (and the piping components it encloses) may be subject to the service fluid conditions. Wrapping and peening of flanges is also excluded in the definition of a temporary repair enclosure, as their use can result in the wetting of components (e.g. bolting) that was not designed for service fluid conditions. Requirements for clamps in this STP also apply to wrapping and peening.

### 5.2 REQUIRED EVALUATIONS AND DOCUMENTATION

a) Use of clamps requires special considerations. Some of these considerations and requirements were documented in Refining and Marketing High Value Learning (HVL 17). HVL 17 provides the basis of the requirements in Appendix A and Appendix B.

b) Before a clamp is installed the Fixed Equipment Engineer shall perform an engineering assessment using Appendix A. Approval to install the clamp must be given by the Unit Superintendent and documented on Appendix A.

c) Before rework (either reinjection or retoquing) is performed on a clamp, Appendix B must be completed by the Fixed Equipment Engineer. Before reinjecting a clamp for the fourth time, a complete re-evaluation of the clamp must be reperformed, using a new copy of Appendix A.

e) A copy of all Appendices (A and B) shall be retained in the Leak Repair Folder referenced in Section 3.3.b.

f) Articles 2.4 and 3.6 of ASME PCC-2 Repair of Pressure Equipment and Piping may be used as guidance when conducting the engineering assessments.

### 5.3 TEMPORARY REPAIR ENCLOSURES INSTALLED OVER CRACKS

If a crack is present in the damaged component, enclosures shall not be installed unless one of the following conditions has been met:

a) Conditions that lead to the crack formation and propagation have been eliminated or mitigated so that the crack will not grow during the planned life of the repair.

b) A fitness-for-service assessment determines that crack growth during the planned life is acceptable, and that the crack will not propagate across the enclosure boundary.

c) Crack circumferential has been determined and the enclosure is designed for the case of full circumferential separation of the cracked component.

---

d) Damaged components are a cracked vent or drain and the enclosure fully encapsulates the cracked vent/drain.

## 5.4 SEALANT INJECTION IN TEMPORARY REPAIR ENCLOSURES

a) The potential for damage to the enclosed component(s) due to the annulus pressure and/or uneven loading from the injected sealant shall be considered in the design and selection of enclosure type (void-filled or perimeter seal) and the injection details selected so as to minimize the likelihood of damage.

b) The possibility and consequence of sealant seeping into the damaged component shall be considered. Examples of such consequences include blocking strainers, orifice flanges and small connections; preventing the correct function of control valves; or causing a reduction in relieving capacity.

## 5.5 OTHER CONSIDERATIONS FOR TEMPORARY REPAIR ENCLOSURES

a) When equipment (including piping and piping components) that is normally not exposed to service is going to be encapsulated to eliminate a leak, it should be assumed that the service fluid can now come in contact with that equipment. Consideration should be taken to ensure that the equipment can withstand the service it is now being exposed to without failure. When equipment may not be suitable for the service, it should be upgraded or protected from the service by a suitable method. If the equipment cannot be upgraded or protected from the service, then a different repair method should be used. For example, if considering encapsulating a leaking flange or valve bonnet, the studs holding that connection together should be suitable for service. If they are not suitable, then they should be changed out with studs that can withstand the service. When it is not possible to change studs out because they are galled, or changing them out would increase exposure to personnel, then other methods of eliminating the leak should be incorporated. Such methods can include a strongback on a clamp that will hold the connection together, should the studs fail due to exposure to service. (It is important to remember that the B7 and B16 studs used in steam services can be susceptible to Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) if they come in contact with steam. Often times, the only way to mitigate the SCC concern is by installing strongbacks on clamps).

b) When clamps or strongbacks are being incorporated into the repair method, consideration should be given to the additional weight of the repair materials and if there is adequate support. In making this evaluation, it may be necessary to perform non-destructive examination to ensure there is adequate existing wall thickness. In some cases, additional support may be necessary to prevent excessive stress on the equipment the clamp is being installed on.

## SECTION 6: OTHER REPAIR CONSIDERATIONS AND REQUIREMENTS

---

## 6.1 GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS

Note that these considerations are only a handful of instances that may help to determine the type of repair method used to eliminate a leak. This is not a comprehensive list, as each situation can present a unique set of requirements/issues. The purpose of stating these considerations is to give the reader an idea of what unforeseen issues can arise when installing clamps or performing leak repairs. When in doubt, defer to the Reliability Engineer and Inspection Department to help give specific guidance when making repairs.

## 6.2 COMPOSITE WRAPS

a) Composite wraps may be utilized for non-leaking external repairs in all pressure classes, but shall be limited to Class 150 services for leaking defects. All repairs shall adhere to ASME PCC-2 and careful consideration shall be given to the process media and abnormal conditions such as steam out. Repairs that were leaking shall be considered temporary and shall be permanently repaired at the next turnaround. A UV topcoat should be applied to repairs that will remain in service greater than two years. The use of intumescent coatings should be considered within process areas. In many cases additional fire protection will not be necessary, as the damaged original pipe may still be adequate for a short duration fire event.

Further guidance may be found in ASME PCC-2 Articles 4.1 & 4.2, GN 36-008 and ISO 24817.

## 6.3 CLASS 1500 HYDROGEN SYSTEMS

a) Drill and taps on live process equipment is not allowed on Class 1500 hydrogen systems.

## 6.4 REPAIRS WHEN CORROSION IS PRESENT

a) When equipment is being repaired due to corrosion, regardless if there is presently a leak or not, consideration should be given to whether the corrosion is internal or external. If corrosion is external, often times the corrosion rate can be mitigated by coating the equipment before performing the repair, making the corrosion issue negligible. When the corrosion issue is internal, careful consideration should be given to the source of the corrosion and corrosion rate, then that information should be used to guide the type of repair that is made and how long that temporary repair can be kept in service. Consideration should also be given to the material compatibility with the service, should the corrosion continue far enough to cause hole through the equipment and cause exposure to the repair material. If corrosion is internal to the pipe, consideration should be given to how subsequent inspection can be performed with the repair in place to monitor the corrosion rate.

## 6.5 INJECTION SEALANT

---

a) When repairs involve the use of injection sealant, the company performing the temporary repair should be aware of possible swings in operating temperature that the equipment may be exposed to. Typically, there is a high temperature injection sealant and a low temperature injection sealant. For example, if a clamp is being installed on a warm-up line for a spare pump, the temperature of the line at the time of installing the clamp may be lower than when the spare pump is put into service. If the low temperature sealant is used because temperatures of the line at the time of installation were lower due to steady state conditions, then it may heat up and volatize when the spare pump is put into service. This can lead to gas being emitted from the clamp which is actually the injection sealant volatizing, not the process media leaking out. Typically, low temperature sealant is intended for services –300F and below, and high temperature sealant being intended for services –300F and above. It should be noted that these temperature breakdowns are only guidelines, and it is the responsibility of the person evaluating the leak repair method to work with the company installing the clamp to determine the actual temperature limitations. In some cases, temporary heat tracing and insulation may need to be installed on the clamp in order to keep it hot enough in steady state conditions for the high temperature sealant to stay set. When this is the case, consult with inspection to determine if any adverse affects will be caused by keeping stagnant service at hotter temperatures.

## 6.6 WELD REPAIRS

a) When the repair plan includes welding, consideration should be given to ensuring there is adequate existing wall thickness for the repair.

## SECTION 7: INSPECTION AND SELF AUDIT

a) Any leak repair clamp that has an intended removal date greater than 6 years from the date of installation shall have a Preventative Maintenance plan placed against the secondary work order created for the permanent repair (Section 4.1.c & 4.2.a.5) that has an inspection requirement to verify that the leak repair is suitable for continued use. The specific requirements and inspection intervals of the Preventative Maintenance plan should be determined by the Reliability Engineer and Unit Inspector.

b) A Compliance Assurance system task will be used to perform annual audits to ensure this policy is being followed.
