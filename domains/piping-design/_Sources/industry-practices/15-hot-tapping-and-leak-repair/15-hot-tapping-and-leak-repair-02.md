## 1.0 Purpose

**1.1** To provide the guidelines to design and perform online welding and hot taps.

**1.2** Compliance with this standard will ensure rigorous, systematic approach for proper identification, design, fabrication, QA/QC Control and safe installation as well as formal technical and management approval.

**1.3** This standard shall be used whenever it is proposed to make a welded attachment to piping, pressure vessels or tanks and to cut the pressure boundary while exposed to the process hazard.

**1.4** Definitions

The "Engineering" - means Suncor engineering group, or designated representative (e.g. EP / EPC / EPCM contractor) responsible for engineering of Suncor projects or jobs.

The "Design Engineer" - means engineer responsible for design of the hot tap and stamping of the design drawings.

"Inspector" – - means Suncor plant inspector assigned to specific plant and part of Suncor Maintenance and Engineering team or QA/QC inspector assigned to projects responsible for mechanical QA/QC activities.

The "Construction Coordinator (CC)" – means the Suncor construction coordinator or his designated representative responsible for field coordination of the hot tap design and installation.

The "Hot Tap Contractor" - means the contractor responsible for executing online welding and hot tapping.

## 3.0 General and Safety Considerations

3.1 Hot tapping is potentially hazardous and therefore should be undertaken when shown to be technically feasible and to offer a clear advantage over other alternatives.

3.2 Hot tapping is a multi-disciplinary activity, involving several departments, each carrying their own responsibilities. Coordination between departments and planning of activities is of vital importance.

3.3 Each hot tap must be evaluated individually by a Hazard and Operability review (HAZOP) in accordance with the HAZOP procedures. A copy of the report shall be included in the design package.

3.4 The operating department shall write a shutdown procedure for shutting down the subject line in case of fire or blowout. During the welding and drilling operation, the Operations department shall have a representative at site to initiate the shutdown if necessary.

3.5 The Fire department must be notified and have an opportunity to review the area and procedures. An emergency rescue plan shall be in place. If hot tap happens to be in a congested area, escape routes shall be

---

identified and marked by the appropriate personnel prior to start of hot tap activities.

3.6 A pre-job meeting shall be held at the start of each shift. Attendees shall include construction coordinator, contractor, workers executing the job, operating personnel and emergency service personnel.

3.7 A retrieved coupon shall be handed over to Inspector. Any coupon that is not retrieved from the system during the extraction of the hot tap machine shall be considered as a potentially serious problem. Any such problem shall be immediately reported to Operations to review the risks associated and to plan further action.

3.8 Before starting the hot tapping, the machine, cutter, and pilot shall be carefully inspected that all components are in satisfactory condition. The seals must be pressure tested.

## 4.0 Hot Tap Design, Restrictions, and Welding Considerations:

### 4.1 Hot Tap Design

4.1.1 The design engineer shall be responsible for hot tap design per applicable codes (i.e., B31.1, B31.3, or ASME BPV Code) and regulations. A registered professional engineer shall stamp the design calculations and drawings.

4.1.2 Hot tap design and the required thickness calculations shall take the following into account.

- 4.1.2.1 Design and operating temperature and pressures.
- 4.1.2.2 Actual wall thickness and required corrosion allowance.
- 4.1.2.3 External pressure to be applied to the existing pipe/equipment during testing of the hot tap connection weld.
- 4.1.2.4 The loads applied by the hot tap machine.
- 4.1.2.5 Where required per Suncor standards and/or in the opinion of the engineer, pipe stress analysis of the tie-in point shall be performed to ensure flexibility and integrity during and after installation of tie-in. Finite Element Analysis (FEA) shall be performed as required.
- 4.1.2.6 Carbon equivalent of the pipe material.

---

4.1.2.7 Limitations of the services and metallurgy of the run pipe.

4.1.3 For hot tap connections, external pressure calculations shall be prepared for the carrier pipe based on the hot tap hydrostatic test pressure minus the pipe operating pressure, to verify the carrier pipe strength against the external load.

4.1.4 In consultation with the hot tap contractor, nozzle length shall be determined for particular tapping machine that will be used in order to ensure a complete cut through the piping wall. For flanged nozzles, length shall be adequate to permit removal of flanged bolts.

4.1.5 The hot tap machine shall be checked for suitability of use at low ambient temperatures. When the ambient temperature is less than -45°C (-50°F), hot tapping or on-line welding is not permitted. Under all circumstances hot tapping shall be limited to the pressure and temperature rating of the machine.

4.1.6 Maximum allowable pressure during hot-tap welding:

4.1.6.1 Shall be calculated based on temperature de-rating which allows for reduction in pipe strength at predicted pipe temperature during online welding as per the applicable code. Predicted temperatures may be calculated by log mean temperature (LMT) formula given below

LMT = (T2-T1) / Ln (T2/T1)

T1 = Operating temperature of the pipe, Deg C
T2 = Temperature at base of Heat Affected Zone (HAZ), Deg C

*Note1: It should be recognized that stresses and temperature will be higher in the reduced strength zone during welding operation than in the normal operation of the pipeline. Sufficient thickness shall be ensured so that the stresses within it are below the temperature corrected design stress.*

*Note-2: Present studies indicate that a burnthrough will not occur if the inside surface temperature is below 1800° F when using low-hydrogen electrodes and the wall thickness is 0.25" or greater.*

---

4.1.6.2 A pressure reduction may be justified to prevent from rupturing during the installation of a full encirclement sleeve on the basis of protecting the welding / installation crew.

4.1.6.3 Reduced wall thickness shall be calculated based on actual thickness minus the reduction in wall thickness during welding, representing the weld pool penetration. Conservative assumption of weld pool penetration may be 0.1".

4.1.6.4 For metal thickness greater than ½", the effect of flow on both weld cooling rates and the risk of burn-through may be negligible.

4.1.7 Hot tap connections shall be one of the following:

4.1.7.1 Integrally reinforced outlet branch connections shall be per Suncor standard 0202.

4.1.7.2 Reinforcing pad (stub-in with reinforcing pad) connections shall be per Suncor standard 0202.

- When branch/run ratio exceeds 50%, in addition to area replacement considerations, analysis of ASME VIII div1 App 1-7 shall also be applied.

- When branch/run ratio exceeds 70% in addition to ASME VIII div 1 App 1-7, a finite element analysis shall be performed, following ASME VIII div 2 approach.

4.1.7.3 Other branch (such as reducing tee) connections shall be using the *Full Encirclement Sleeves* (weld-on sleeves). The weld details shall be per Fig.328.5.4D of ASME B31.3. The diameter of the branch should be at least one standard pipe size smaller than diameter of the run pipe. It should be recognized that the hole drilled into the run pipe will be smaller than the internal diameter of the branch.

4.1.7.4 Pre-formed split tees (TD Williams or equal) shall be considered only after thoroughly evaluating the other design options and requires owner's approval. Split tees shall be designed to withstand full pipeline pressure. Split tees shall

---

not be used in corrosive and steam service where the corrosion rate will be accelerated due to existing crevice.

*Note-1. Split Tees shall be considered as temporary fittings and they shall be replaced by standard fittings at the first available opportunity. ABSA approval shall be obtained for use of split tees and this item shall be properly tagged and noted as an action item for plant maintenance. After execution of hot tap, a work request shall be initiated in the Maintenance Management System to track until the split tee is replaced.*

*Note-2. All hot tap fittings (welded branch fitting or split tee) may be considered as permanent for piping systems conveying non-expansible fluids. However, design engineer shall consult the area maintenance engineer for permanent tappings. Refer to Alberta Regulation 293/94, para 1(2)(c) for definition of expansible fluids. Further, also see ASME B31.3, para 300.1.3 for code exemptions.*

4.1.7.5 A hot tap, less than 70% of run diameter, may be performed under Suncor's quality control program provided it is designed in accordance with the applicable piping code, taking into account the need for reinforcement of the branch connection, and that a detailed hot tap procedure exists.

A hot tap on a boiler and pressure vessel shall be submitted to ABSA for review and approval prior to implementation.

4.1.8 Valves, packing, gaskets, and other piping components shall be in accordance with the piping material specifications per Suncor standard 0202.

4.1.9 Full-port gate valve with the same NPS and rating as branch connection shall be specified per Suncor standards.

4.1.10 The seals and materials of the construction of the hot tapping machine shall be compatible with the contents of the piping or equipment.

4.1.11 Vessels or lines to be welded or hot tapped shall be inspected for adequate wall thickness and absence of laminations and/or other

---

imperfections. The base thickness shall provide support for new connection and hot tap machine.

4.1.12 The carbon equivalent of the material on which hot tap is proposed shall be determined. The formula to be used is:

$$CE = \%C + \%Mn / 6 + (\%Cu+\%Ni) / 15 + (\%Cr+\%Mo+\%V) / 5$$

Relationship between CE and cooling rate that produces HAZ (Heat Affected Zone) should be recognized. As a guideline, a CE of up to 0.4 may be used. However if the CE is greater than 0.4, metallurgical engineer shall be consulted to review the CE, cooling rates and welding procedure for a specific hot tap

4.1.13 All pressure hot tap fitting shall be registered with ABSA and have CRN numbers.

## 4.2 Restrictions on Hot Tap Applications

### 4.2.1 Process Fluids

**4.2.1.1** Hot tapping should not be performed on equipment/piping containing the following materials: If required, a detailed engineering evaluation and risk assessment shall be performed.

- Toxic chemicals such as concentrated hydrogen sulfide, chlorine, sodium hydroxide, ammonia and acids.

- Vapor/air or vapor/oxygen near or within their flammable explosive range.

- Oxygen or oxygen enriched atmospheres.

- Compressed air systems.

- Temperature-sensitive, chemically reactive materials which may undergo reaction or decomposition leading to dangerous increase in pressure, explosion or attack on metal.

- Caustic service and amine service where concentration and temperature require PWHT.

---

- Hazardous substances such as Acetylene, Butadiene, Chlorine, Ethylene, Ethylene oxide, Rich/Lean DEA/MEA, Hydrofluoric acid, Sulphuric acid etc.

## 4.2.2 Materials

**4.2.2.1** Hot tapping is usually not permitted where for process or metallurgical reasons PWHT is required unless determined by Suncor engineering review to be acceptable. The area metallurgical engineer shall be consulted in such cases. In such cases, a pre-approved on-line welding procedure specification is required.

**4.2.2.2** Hot tapping on pressure vessels is not recommended. If required, a detail engineering review shall be carried out to check the feasibility of hot tapping.

**4.2.2.3** Hot tapping shall not be performed on piping and storage tanks that are jacketed, cladded or lined.

**4.2.2.4** Minimum wall thickness shall be 1/4" unless approved by the responsible Suncor engineer. Lower thickness may be permitted with the approval of Suncor Engineer after detail review of design and welding specifications.

## 4.2.3 Process Conditions

**4.2.3.1** Welding on a line under no-flow conditions is not permitted. Minimum flow velocity of 1.3 ft/sec should be maintained. A high flow condition in gases is permitted subject to attainment of acceptable interpass temperatures. High liquid flow will usually cause rapid cooling which can cause excessive hardness. A suggested maximum velocity for liquid flow during welding is 4.0 ft/sec. In case of high velocities and rapid cooling, special interpass heating techniques may be needed or the welding procedures amended.

**4.2.3.2** Because of insufficient flow or unstable operating conditions, hot -tap welding on flare lines is feasible only with special precautions such as flushing of the

---

flare line with steam or an inert gas during welding operations creating a minimum velocity of 1.3 ft/sec and application of a quick-acting warning system.

4.2.3.3 Any conditions exceptional to the above shall be reviewed and approved by Suncor Engineer.

4.2.3.4 Hot Tapping on Tanks:

4.2.3.4.1 Do not pump into or out of tank during hot tapping or online welding. Avoid operation of any valve including gas-blanketing valve.

4.2.3.4.2 Do not operate heating coils during hot tapping / welding.

4.2.3.4.3 Do not hot tap if the tank liquid level is below 3 feet above the area where the welding or hot tapping is being done.

4.2.3.4.4 Hot tapping is not permitted on the deck of a floating roof tank that is in service.

4.2.3.5 Hot tapping shall be avoided on piping systems that are in vibrating service.

4.2.3.6 Downstream equipment, rotating machinery, control valves, orifices etc. must be considered to ensure that cuttings from the hot tap machine, or loss of coupon will not create dangerous or detrimental conditions.

## 4.3 Welding

4.3.1 Proper flow through the pipeline shall be maintained during welding to prevent overheating or burn-through, excessive cooling rate and the risk of cracking.

4.3.2 The hot tap contractor shall submit an on-line Welding Procedure Specification (WPS) and associated Procedure Qualification Record (PQR) for approval.

4.3.3 Storage and handling of electrodes shall be in accordance with Suncor procedures and manufacturer recommendations.

---

4.3.4 Only the welder shall be allowed to change the settings of the welding machine during online welding. All welding equipment shall be maintained in good working order and shall comply with site safety regulations.

4.3.5 Run-pipe shall be prepared by removal of coating, surface cleaning, ovality check and other defect checks. Ovality checks may done in advance to properly design encirclement sleeves

4.3.6 AWS Class EXX16 and /or EXX18 basic coated low-hydrogen electrodes shall be specified in the approved WPS and used throughout.

4.3.7 The run pipe and branch should be preheated to avoid weld cracking and high hardness. Preheat temperatures should be determined based on CE and weld thickness. Preheat temperatures of approx. 200 Deg F is usually required when CE>0.4 and thickness is over 0.75". However, preheat temperature shall be maintained as specified on WPS.

4.3.8 To minimize the risk of burn through, the first weld pass to piping should be made with 3/32" diameter welding rods to limit the heat input.

4.3.9 Welding including reinforcement pads (if required) or hot tapping shall not be permitted closer than 18 inches to a flange or threaded connection, or approx. 3 inches to a weld seam (including longitudinal seam of welded piping) unless determined by engineering review to be acceptable.

4.3.10 For longitudinal butt welds of full encirclement sleeves and split tees, the joints should be fitted with a back-up strip to prevent penetration of weld into the carrier pipe.

4.3.11 The longitudinal seams on full encirclement sleeve and split tees should be completed prior to circumferential seams. These seams shall be welded simultaneously so that contraction strain will improve the fit-up between fitting and run pipe. However, circumferential welds shall not be welded simultaneously. Use API-1104 for welding sequence guidelines.

## 5.0 Inspection and Testing

5.1 NDE personnel for inspection shall be qualified to CGSB Level II or Level I under the direct supervision of a Level II.

---

5.2 The weld area plus 3" on either side shall be fully examined by magnetic particle inspection (liquid penetrant for non-magnetic materials) and ultrasonic examination to locate any defects and/or laminations prior to welding.

5.3 Visual inspection of all attachment welds shall be performed after welding the hot tap fitting and before attaching the hot tap machine. Ensure that weld root is not excessive as shown on the hot-tap engineering drawing.

5.4 Magnetic particle inspection (liquid penetrant inspection for non-magnetic materials) shall be performed on 100% of root and cap welds.

5.5 Ultrasonic inspection shall be performed on 100% of the finished welds, 48 hours after the completion of welds. Alternatively, MPI or LPI shall be performed on the root pass before welding the 2nd pass and MPI shall also be performed on the weld, 48 hours after completion of the weld. However, area metallurgical engineer may vary the delayed inspection time depending upon the factors such as cooling rates, restraints, carbon equivalence, hydrogen contamination from moisture or surface contaminants etc.

5.6 Hardness testing shall be performed on welding and HAZ and the results shall be reviewed by inspector.

5.7 The valve to be installed at the hot tap location shall be leak tested in accordance with API 598 or as shown on the engineered drawing prior to installation in the field.

5.8 Valve to be inspected for sufficient clearance of cutter prior to tapping. Valves must have clear, full round openings at least 1/8" greater in diameter than the specified drill or cutter OD.

5.9 The branch connection shall be hydrostatically or pneumatically tested prior to cutting the pipe or equipment. Hydrostatic testing is limited to a skin temperature of 200° F (93°C). For temperatures above 200° F, pneumatic test must be performed with the use of inert gas (Nitrogen) or Helium. Inspector to verify the test. Caution to be exercised during the pneumatic testing of the branch connection. Online welding or hot tap contractor shall submit the pneumatic procedure to Suncor field engineering for review and approval.

In case of pneumatic testing, extreme caution shall be exercised to ensure that pressure is increased in very small steps and allow stabilization of pressure at elevated temperature experienced in the branch. In no case, the

---

pressure shall be permitted to exceed the test pressure required by governing standard. Suncor field engineer shall determine the need for a safety relief valve (minimum ¾" size) that is set at the test pressure to prevent overpressure.

5.10 The test pressure shall be as follows:

The maximum value of the test pressure shall be calculated in accordance with the particular design code. However, following shall be also taken into account in determining the test pressures:

Test pressure for pre-formed split tees and full encirclement sleeves shall be summation of actual operating pressure in the run-pipe and maximum allowable pressure differential with respect to buckling of the run pipe calculated as defined in ASME Section VIII, Division 1, and Section UG-28.

In any case, the test pressure shall not be greater than the designed code test pressure of the branch pipe.

*Note: Design Engineer may use Williamson Industries published guide titled "Approximate Guide for Maximum External Testing Pressure on Run Piping" to determine the maximum differential pressure and test pressures. However, in no case shall the test pressure exceed the design code test pressure of the branch pipe.*

5.11 The general testing procedure to be followed shall be:

5.11.1 Shop leak testing of the hot-tap valve, preferably with the valve not installed on the branch, for duration of at least 5 minutes.

5.11.2 Testing of the branch connection, with the valve installed and in the open position, to demonstrate leak tightness and strength. Duration shall be at least 30 minutes.

5.11.3 Leak testing of the drilling machine mounted on the valve (with valve closed), for a duration of at least 5 minutes. Leak test pressure shall be equal to the highest operating pressure in the run pipe during the hot-tap operation.

Note: Testing sequence and testing combinations may be modified depending upon the field or contractor's practice. However, any modified procedure shall be reviewed by Suncor field engineer.

5.12 The saddle or reinforcing pad shall be pneumatically tested at 15 PSIG. The Inspector shall verify this test.

### SUGGESTED HOT TAP EXECUTION FLOW CHART FOR MAJOR PROJECTS

---

In this section hot tap execution methodology is outlined by providing the hot tap execution sequence and responsibilities of the engineering and construction. A flow chart is also attached. However, this flow chart is based on the current organization set-up within MCU Project and may be modified to suit a particular organization / Silo.

## 1.0 Sequence and Responsibilities

### 1.1 Sequence of Events

1.1.1 Engineering shall identify the location of hot tap after evaluating that the hot tap is appropriate in coordination with operations. API-2201 guidelines shall be used to determine this.

1.1.2 Once the location of the hot tap installation is determined, Engineering shall initiate Appendix-I with preliminary HT package containing PFD, P&ID, and other available information on HT location, pipe size, coordinates, photos, etc. Construction Coordinator shall coordinate with Area Operations, Inspector and HT contractor to complete the "Pre-Engineering Checklist" Appendix-I and obtain the approval for location. The checklist shall be forwarded to Engineering for detailed design of the hot tap.

1.1.3 After hot tap location is approved, Construction Coordinator shall tag the hot tap location per procedure, PGP0049A, in coordination with operations.

1.1.4 Engineering shall finalize the detail engineering in accordance with applicable codes and Suncor standards and complete the "Detail Engineering Checklist" per Appendix-II. An Engineering Work Package (EWP) shall be prepared and issued to Construction Management Team (CMT). The package shall contain:

- Tie-in List
- Process Flow Diagram (PFD) – Optional
- Appendix-I with available results
- P&ID
- Design Check List (Appendix-II) with carbon equivalent and copy of welding procedure
- Design Calculations
- Tie-in location drawing , Pictures (if available)
- Line Designation Table (LDT)
- Isometrics
- Other documents as necessary

---

1.1.5 Upon finalizing the installation time and schedule, construction coordinator shall convene a meeting consisting of representatives from Operations, Maintenance & Engineering (if required), Safety and Fire Department to finalize the work plan specific for each hot tap.

1.1.6 Construction Coordinator shall obtain the required approvals on "Hot Tap Authorization Form" per Appendix-III.

1.1.7 After final approval, but before any fieldwork (except scaffolding and site preparation), Construction Coordinator shall hold a "Kick-Off" meeting for each stage of the job.

1.1.8 The hot tap is executed as per the work plan, complete with final inspection, sign-off, site clean up and filing of documentation.

Note: Refer to the suggested 'Hot Tap Execution' flow chart at Appendix-V.

1.2.2.6 After final approval but before any field work (except scaffolding & site preparation), Construction Coordinator shall hold a "Kick-off" meeting with welders & pipe fitters, hot machine operator, permit issuer and other members as deemed necessary for each stage of the job. Shall also review the Appendix-IV with the concerned persons and obtain the necessary signatures.

1.2.2.7 Shall be responsible to coordinate the execution of hot tap including obtaining the permits, supervision during

hot tap, QA/QC inspections, hydrostatic testing, closeouts and filing of final documentation.

**1.2.4.3** Shall complete the design checklist (Appendix-II) and prepare necessary drawings showing the fit-up / welding details, hydrostatic test pressures, NDE requirements, MAWP calculations, nozzle flange-pipe tolerances, stress analysis and Finite Element Analysis (if required).

### 1.2.5 Welding Contractor / Welding Operator

**1.2.5.1** Shall submit qualified on-line weld procedure specification and Procedure Qualification Record (PQR) for each specific on-line welding operation to Construction Coordinator and Design Engineer.

**1.2.5.2** Shall submit welding personnel qualification to the Suncor Inspector for approval.

1.2.5.3 Shall perform the on-line welding.

### 1.2.6 Hot Tap Contractor

1.2.6.1 Shall verify that the selected machine has the required pressure and temperature ratings for the job.

1.2.6.2 Complete the hot tap operator checklist (part of Appendix-IV).

1.2.6.3 After installation of machine, check the alignment and clearance between the cutter and valve seat rings and do a pressure test of the machine and of the assembly as specified on drawings.

## APPENDIX V

### SUGGESTED HOT TAP WORK FLOW CHART

![Appendix V — Suggested Hot Tap Work Flow Chart](figures/15-hot-tapping-and-leak-repair-02_p0029_fig01_appendix-v-suggested-hot-tap-work-flow-c.png)

The flow chart covers two organizational swim lanes — **ENGINEERING** and **OPERATIONS, MAINTENANCE & CONSTRUCTION** — and traces the full hot tap authorization and execution sequence from initial task list through close-out.

Key process steps shown in the chart (left to right, top to bottom):

**Engineering lane:**
1. Engineering Task List with preliminary package reviewed by Engineering and Operations and HT Contractor
2. Decision: Hot Tap (HT) or Cold Tap (CT)?
3. Engineering to study the feasibility of HT and initiate Appendix I in coordination with Design and Construction
4. Engineering starts detailed design
5. Prepare engineering package and EWP including completed P&ID and Process Piping
6. Decision: ABSA approval required?
7. ABSA approved?
8. Apply for ABSA approval

**Operations, Maintenance & Construction lane:**
1. Appendix I sent to Construction Coordinator (CC) to coordinate with Inspection and HT Contractor
2. Appendix I to be completed by Inspection
3. Area Operations approval on Appendix I
4. Tag HT line-in by CC in coordination with Operations
5. Send approved Appendix I to Engineering
6. CC is to ensure final plans for Emergency Shutdown, Emergency Response (ER) or FSS (risk management) etc. are in place
7. Obtain Operators approval for Appendix III
8. CC convenes meeting of Operations & Maintenance (O&M), Fire & Maintenance, Construction Head/prior review
9. CC to prepare EWP and schedule the work
10. Appendix IV to be reviewed and completed by QA/QC Inspector and HT Machine Operator
11. CC signs off Appendix IV
12. Welder to weld the connector torque under supervision of Inspector
13. Complete QA/QC of welds
14. Install valve
15. Hydrated (Hydrotest)
16. Execute the hot tapping
17. Close-out

**Final step:** Final Docs by CC
