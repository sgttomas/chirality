# SELECTION OF PIPING COMPONENTS AND MATERIALS

## 1.0 SCOPE

**1.1** This Practice covers requirements for the selection and application of piping components and materials for use in refineries. Refer to the Environmental Design REP 3–1–1 for design requirements to satisfy environmental regulations.

**1.2** Supplemental requirements including data to be furnished by the Purchaser, the Manufacturer's responsibility, documentation, marking, packaging and shipping for pipe and piping components are covered in REP 5–2–4.

**1.3** The requirements in this Practice establish the basis for the Phillips 66 Piping Standard, and shall be followed when additions, changes, or new specification standards are necessary. The Phillips 66 Piping Standards are listed in REP 5–2–3.

**1.4** Any deviation from this Practice must be approved by the procedure described in REP 1–1–3.

**1.5** An asterisk (*) indicates that a decision by the Owner's Engineer or Owner is required, or that additional information is furnished by the Purchaser.

**1.6** A revision bar indicates all changes made to this Revision.

## 3.0 DEFINITIONS

**3.1** Aggressive Environmental Service (AES) – A fluid/material service, as defined in REP 10–2–1 and REP 10–2–2.

**3.2** Contractor – Company or business that agrees to furnish materials or perform specified services at a specified price and/or rate to the Owner.

**3.3** Fluid Service – Fluid service as defined in REP 5–1–1.

**3.4** Fluid Service and Service Classification – Fluid and service classification as defined in REP 5–1–1.

**3.5** Inspector – A Phillips 66 appointed engineer or inspector.

**3.6** Manufacturer – The recipient of a direct or indirect purchase order for materials and/or equipment. In this context, a direct order is one issued to a manufacturer by a contractor or the Owner. An indirect order is one issued to a manufacturer by a vendor (recipient of a direct order) for materials, fabricated components, or subassemblies.

**3.7** Owner – Phillips 66.

**3.8** Owner's Engineer – A Phillips 66 appointed engineer.

**3.9** Pipe Nipple – A short piece of pipe with or without threaded ends.

**3.10** Piping Service Class – Piping system classification as defined in REP 5–1–1.

**3.11** Piping System – Piping circuit as defined in REP 5–1–1.

**3.12** Purchaser – The party placing a direct purchase order. The Purchaser is the Owner's designated representative.

**3.13** Service Cyclic Conditions – Thermal Load conditions producing significant fatigue effects as defined in ASME B31.3.

**3.14** Seal Welding – A weld use to seal threaded joints in small bore piping; for purpose of this document it shall cover all the threads creating a smooth transition between the tap component and the nipple or valve body.

**3.15** Small-Bore Piping (SBP) – Piping with size as defined in REP 5–1–1.

## 5.0 MATERIALS

### 5.1 General

**5.1.1** All materials used for piping components shall be per the requirement of this Practice and ASME B31.3 or ASME B31.8, as applicable.

**5.1.2** Reclaimed metallic and non-metallic materials shall only be allowed with inspection for service and approval from Owner's Engineer.

**5.1.3** (*) The use of materials at temperatures above or below those for which allowable stress values are shown in ASME B31.3 shall require prior approval from Owner's Engineer.

### 5.2 Metallic Materials

**5.2.1** Acceptable materials for all metallic pipe, fittings, forgings, and valves shall be as provided in Table 1. The selection of materials not listed in Table 1 shall require approval of Owner's Engineer before use.

**5.2.2** Piping materials and PWHT requirements for Aggressive Environmental Service shall be in accordance with the requirements of REP 10-2-2.

**5.2.3** Supplemental material requirements for carbon steel, low alloy steel, stainless steel and high alloys shall be as stipulated in REP 10-2-2. These requirements include items such as carbon equivalent, chemistry control, heat treatment, and Charpy impact requirements.

**5.2.4** Acceptable materials for tubing shall be as provided in Table 11. The selection of materials not listed in Table 11 shall require approval of Owner's Engineer before use.

**5.2.5** (*) The use of materials with greater than 70,000 psi minimum tensile strength for pressure containment shall require written approval from Owner's Engineer.

**5.2.6** Limitations on the use of copper, and copper bearing alloys (UNS Cxxxxx), shall be as given below for the following services:

1. Ammonia: Alloys with a copper content greater than 50% shall not be used in ammonia service or in aqueous solutions containing ammonia where the pH exceeds 8.5.
2. Caustic and Alkaline: Alloys with a copper content less than 85% shall not be used.
3. Acetylene: Alloys with a copper content greater than 50% shall not be used in services where acetylene in a wet environment is present.

**5.2.7** Copper, aluminum, and their alloys shall not be used for piping components in flammable service, except that aluminum may be used in ASME B16.5, Classes 150, 300, and 600 for temperatures below -50°F.

**5.2.8** If aluminum castings (for example, valve bodies) have been subjected to welding or thermal cutting, their ratings and/or allowable stresses shall require approval from Owner's Engineer. Use of aluminum castings other than ASTM B2.6 Grade 356 T6 shall require prior approval from Owner's Engineer.

**5.2.9** Lead, tin, and their alloys shall not be used in flammable fluid services. They shall not be used in other pressure piping without prior approval from Owner's Engineer.

**5.2.10** (*) Lead is not to be used in fire water or underground cooling water services. Ductile iron pipe shall be centrifugally cast and suitable for a minimum working pressure of 125 psig and a design pressure of 150 psig.

**5.2.11** Low melting point alloys, such as brass or bronze, shall not be used for piping or piping components in flammable material or combustible liquid services. Aluminum alloys shall be permitted in cryogenic service.

**5.2.12** Supplemental material requirements for piping in Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) service shall be as covered in REP 5–19–1.

**6.0 PIPING**

**6.1 General Requirements**

**6.1.1** Selection of piping materials shall be per Table 1.

**6.1.2** Pipe dimensions shall be in accordance with the following:
1. ASME B36.10 for welded and seamless wrought steel pipe.
2. (*) ASME B36.19 for stainless steel pipe; stainless steel pipe per ASME B36.10 shall be acceptable if approved by Owner's Engineer.

**6.1.3** All pipe threads shall conform to ASME B1.20.1 taper pipe threads. Butt weld preparation shall conform to ASME B16.25.

**6.1.4** Pipe in sizes NPS 3 and greater shall be furnished with both ends prepared for buttwelding in accordance with ASME B16.25, Figure 2A or Figure 3A as applicable. Flame cut weld bevels shall not be permitted.

**6.1.4.1** Pipe in sizes NPS 2 and smaller shall be furnished with square cut ends unless otherwise specified by Purchaser.

**6.1.4.2** (\*) With Owner's Engineer approval NPS 2 pipe may be furnished with both ends prepared for butwelding in accordance with ASME B16.25, Figure 2A or 3A as applicable. Flame cut weld bevels shall not be permitted.

**6.1.5** Pipe shall be furnished to the specific or random lengths as specified by the Purchaser. Couplings or joiners shall not be used for pipe sizes NPS 24 and less. Joiners for pipe NPS 26 and larger shall require Purchaser's approval. Girth welds shall be in accordance with the material standard and ASME B31.3.

**6.1.6** Unless otherwise specified by Purchaser, the length of pipe nipples shall be either 3-inch or 6-inch as specified in the pipe class per REP 5–2-3. All burrs shall be removed from pipe nipples. The use of "close" nipples (pipe nipples that are threaded along, or nearly along, the entire length of the nipple) shall be strictly prohibited in process service.

**6.1.7** All plates used in the fabrication of pipe having less than a 6-to-1 reduction from the original conventionally cast ingot or continuously cast slab shall be subject to ultrasonic examination using an angle beam technique. Examination shall be performed in accordance with ASTM A577.

**6.1.8** When pipe is cold expanded, the amount of expansion shall not exceed 1–1.2% of the outside diameter of the pipe. Materials, such as austenitic stainless steels and some nickel alloys (for example, Alloy C-276 and Alloy 800HT), which undergo a phase transformation by cold working, shall not be cold expanded.

**6.1.9** (\*) The use of non-metallic pipe or plastic lined metallic pipe shall require prior approval from Owner's Engineer. Requirements for non-metallic piping and plastic lined metallic piping shall be in accordance with REP 5–9–1 and REP 5–9–2 respectively.

## 6.2 Size Limitations

**6.2.1** The minimum size of branch connections to large lines, etc., shall be NPS 3/4 for hydrocarbon service, except that atmospheric vent and drain connections from small equipment, certain instrument connections, and steam tracing connections may be NPS 1/2. The minimum size of pipe downstream of first block valve shall be NPS 1/2. The minimum size of piping connections to pressure vessels shall be in accordance with REP 7–1–1D.

**6.2.2** The minimum bore of piping shall be 0.250-inch.

**6.2.3** Pipe sizes of NPS 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1–1/4, 2–1/2, 3–1/2, and 5 shall not be used except for the circumstances listed below. Mechanical equipment or instrument connections in these sizes shall be changed to a permissible piping size immediately adjacent to the equipment.

**6.2.3.1** Pipe sizes of NPS 1/8, 1/4, 3/8 may be used for rotating and reciprocating equipment instrumentation with like size pipe connection on both the instrument and equipment.

**6.2.3.2** Pipe sizes of NPS 1/8, 1/4, 3/8 may be used for grease fittings.

## 6.3 Corrosion Allowance and Wall Thickness

**6.3.1** The wall thickness of piping shall not be less than that required by ASME B31.3 or ASME B31.1, as applicable. The design conditions used to compute the wall thickness shall be established using REP 5–1–1.

[Phillips 66 logo]

**6.3.2** (\*) Dead ends/dead legs (piping with little or no flow) shall be avoided especially for piping conveying fluids that may form a corrosive condensate. If dead ends cannot be avoided (for example, startup lines and equipment bypasses), the piping shall receive special consideration. If the fluid is determined by Owner's Engineer to be more corrosive under these conditions, the piping shall be constructed of components made from materials that will withstand the corrosion or from components with extra corrosion allowance.

**6.3.3** Corrosion allowances and thread depth allowances shall not be less than the minimum values shown in Table 13. For threaded-end pipe, the thread depth allowance shall be added to the corrosion allowance.

**6.3.4** The minimum wall thickness of piping, excluding the mill tolerance (e.g., 12- 1/2 % for seamless pipe, 0.01-inch for ERW pipe, etc.) shall not be less than any of the following:

1. The thickness required for pressure plus corrosion allowance, and thread depth allowance, if any.
2. The thickness determined from Table 13, Column A or B, whichever results in the heavier wall thickness. For Column B, nominal thickness must be reduced by 12-1/2% for mill tolerance.
3. The thickness required by the D/t ratio criterion. See the next paragraph.

**6.3.5** (\*) The minimum wall thickness shown in Table 13, Column B for High Alloy and Non-Ferrous Materials (except Aluminum), Hydrocarbon and all other process services may be reduced to Schedule 10S for sizes NPS 3 and larger, with approval of Owner's Engineer and the site's REP Section 5 Technical Committee member. To permit the use of the Schedule 10S wall thickness, the following criteria shall be met:

1. The use of Schedule 10S is prohibited for any cyclic or vibratory service, whether mechanically, thermally, or process flow induced.
2. The use of Schedule 10S is prohibited for piping connected to and within 50 feet downstream of any pressure letdown valve. This includes relief valves.
3. Schedule 10S piping shall not be placed in a location where there is a high probability of mechanical abuse, such as at grade level near roadways. Safeguarding measures shall be considered (e.g., placing in the top of a pipe rack to avoid potential issues with road traffic, visibly marking the piping as Schedule 10S, providing some physical shielding of the piping, etc.). The adequacy of support spacing shall be re-verified when using Schedule 10S piping.
4. Fabrication considerations shall be taken into account, especially concerning fabricated branch connections. As a minimum, integrally reinforced branch fittings are mandated for use with Schedule 10S pipe, or fabricated stub-in connections with full size repads shall be used.
5. A formal engineering analysis shall be performed on the piping system and an assessment of whether mechanical, thermal, or flow induced vibration can occur must be documented.

**6.3.6** The D/t ratio (nominal diameter/wall thickness minus the corrosion allowance) shall not exceed 150. With the approval of Owner's Engineer, a ratio greater than 150 may be used if there is no possibility of forming a vacuum. When vacuum is a normal design condition, ASME B31.3 shall govern the required thickness. An analysis shall consider the following factors:

1. Draining.
2. Blocked pump suction.

3. Sudden condensation.
4. Unloading rate versus product vaporization rate.
5. The existence of a vacuum breaker system.

## 7.0 PIPING JOINTS

### 7.1 General Requirements

All piping joints shall be designed, inspected, tested, and fabricated in accordance with REP 5–1, REP 5–5–3, and ASME B31.3 or ASME B31.1, as applicable.

### 7.2 Welded Joints

**7.2.1** All welding of piping components shall be per REP 5–5–1 and REP 5–5–2.

**7.2.2** For piping larger than NPS 2, butt welded construction shall be used.

**7.2.3** For NPS 2 pipe, buttwelding shall be preferred.

**7.2.3.1** (\*) Threaded joints subject to the provisions of Section 7.3 may be used with site approval.

**7.2.4** For NPS 1-1/2 and smaller, socket weld design shall be preferred. Socket welding shall not be used where:

1. Crevice corrosion or severe erosion is expected.
2. Fatigue/cyclic service as defined in REP 5–1–1 will be experienced.
3. Required to be butt welded per site requirements.

**7.2.5** Where the use of butt weld or socket weld joints NPS 1-1/2 and smaller is not an option, threaded piping may be used per Section 7.3. Seal-welding requirements for threaded joints shall be per REP 5–5–2 and REP 5–5–3.

### 7.3 Threaded Joints

**7.3.1** Threaded piping joints shall only be allowed in piping NPS 2 and smaller (see paragraph 7.2.3.1), with exceptions provided in paragraph 7.3.1.6. Fluid service and/or service classification per REP 5–1–1 limit(s) the use of threaded joints, as follows:

**7.3.1.1** Threaded joints shall not be used:

1. In high pressure fluid service as defined in REP 5–1–1.
2. (\*) In any AES (with the exception of HF/HF Alkylation Service) unless approved by an experienced Phillips 66 Corrosion/Materials Engineer.
3. (\*) Where crevice corrosion or severe erosion is expected unless approved by an experienced Phillips 66 Corrosion/Materials Engineer.

**7.3.1.2** Threaded joints may be used in fatigue/cyclic service providing that:

1. The joint is seal welded per REP 5–5–2.
2. Base pipe is a minimum of schedule 80.
3. The nipple is a minimum of schedule 160.
4. Valves used are Class 800 or Class 1500 Extended Body (EB) neck valves.
5. Appropriate bracing is applied per REP 5–1–1.

**7.3.1.3** Threaded joints may be used in flammable/combustible fluid service (including light ends) and non-flammable hazardous fluid service providing that:

1. The joint is seal welded per REP 5–5–2.
2. Base pipe is a minimum of Schedule 80.
3. The nipple is a minimum of Schedule 160.
4. Valves used are Class 800 or Class 1500 extended body (EB) neck valves.

**7.3.1.4** Threaded joints may be used in alloy piping circuits in flammable/combustible and non-flammable – hazardous fluid services providing that:

1. Seal welding per REP 5–5–2 is used.
2. (\*) Any threaded joints in alloy materials requiring PWHT is approved by an experienced Phillips 66 Corrosion/Materials Engineer.
3. PMI requirements of REP 15–1–4 are followed.
4. Requirements for PWHT in REP 5–5–3 are followed.

**7.3.1.5** Threaded joints may be used in Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) alkylation service in Phillips 66 licensed HF units and UOP licensed HF units, with the provisions of REP 5–10–1.

**7.3.1.6** Threaded joints may be used in the following fluid services or applications with noted provisions:

1. In all category D fluid service.
2. In the following special application without seal welding:
   a) For galvanized piping, NPS 4 and smaller, in non-process service.
   b) Where access or openings need to be created downstream of the first normally close valve, such as plugs in vents, drains, and cleanouts.
   c) Where equipment and instrumentations require frequent removal for maintenance and has appropriate design components for access/removal, such as instruments, thermal relief valves, instrument taps, with approval of Owner's Engineer.
   d) In oil mist lubrication systems, per REP 6–9–3.
   e) (\*) Threads where one or both components are made from cast iron, ductile iron, or other materials on which welding is prohibited by Owner's Engineer.
   f) Union bonnets of valves.

**7.3.2** Prior to seal, full engagement shall be accomplished as required in paragraph 7.3.3.

**7.3.3** Threaded joints shall be tightened to ensure full thread engagement per the following:

1. For NPS 1 and less: Five engaged threads.
2. For NPS 1–1/2 (NPS 2 for heritage users): Six engaged threads.

**7.3.4** Threaded joints that are not to be seal welded shall be made up with a lubricant suited for the specific service. The lubricants shown in Table 1 of REP 5–5–3, or their equivalents, shall be recommended as indicated.

## 7.4 Flanged Joints

**7.4.1** Requirements for flanges, bolting and gaskets shall be as stipulated in REP 5–2–2.

**7.4.2** Materials for flanges shall be per Table 1 in this Practice. The flange material shall be the same as the piping material to which it is attached. Materials for bolting and gaskets shall be in accordance with REP 5–2–5.

**7.4.3** Steel line blanks shall be per ASME B16.48 except that the blank thickness shall be based on ASME B31.3. The excess line blank thickness above the ASME B31.3 calculated thickness required for pressure shall be listed as available corrosion allowance for the line blank on the piping isometric.

**7.4.4** Above-ground cast iron pipe shall have flanged joints, either cast integral with the pipe or threaded carbon steel flanges.

**7.4.5** Underground ductile iron pipe with bell and spigot joints may be made up with rubber rings. Rings shall be per ASTM D1869, synthetic rubber, oil-resistant type.

**7.4.6** Requirements for proprietary flange connectors shall be as stipulated in paragraph 7.6.

## 7.5 Unions

**7.5.1** The use of pipe unions shall be governed by the following:

1. Unions shall not be used above ASME/ANSI Class 150.
2. Unions shall not be used in severe cyclic or Category M service.
3. The maximum permissible size for unions is NPS 1–1/2 for non-Category D service.
4. The maximum permissible size for unions is NPS 3 for Category D service.
5. The use of pipe unions between process vessels, lines, or other equipment and the first block valve shall be prohibited.
6. Unions shall be Class 3000 per MSS SP-83.

**7.5.2** Gasketed unions may be used in NPS 3 and smaller sizes, subject to the requirements of the previous paragraph.

## 7.6 Proprietary Joints

**7.6.1** (*) All proprietary and special pipe joints shall be approved by Owner's Engineer prior to use.

**7.6.2** Proprietary joints such as Grayloc connectors of REFLANGE E-CON or G-CON connectors may be considered as alternatives to ASME B16.5 flanges for Classes 600 and higher.

## 8.0 TUBING

## 8.1 General Requirements

**8.1.1** Selection of tubing materials shall be per Table 11. All tubing shall be seamless.

**8.1.2** (*) Tube shall be furnished with square cut ends unless otherwise specified by Purchaser.

**8.1.3** (*) Tube shall be furnished to the specific or random lengths as specified by Purchaser.

## 8.2 Size Limitations

**8.2.1** Tubing shall not be used in sizes larger than 2 inch outside diameter.

*Note: For tubing sizes larger than 1 inch, the ferrules must be pre-swaged through the use of a hydraulic swaging unit.*

## 8.3 Corrosion Allowance and Wall Thickness

**8.3.1** The wall thickness of tubing shall not be less than that required by ASME B31.3 or ASME B31.1, as applicable. The design conditions used to compute the wall thickness shall be established using REP 5-1–1.

**8.3.2** No corrosion allowance shall be required for tubing. Materials shall be selected such that anticipated corrosion during the tubing's service life does not exceed 10 mils.

**8.3.3** The minimum wall thickness of tubing shall not be less than any of the following:

1. The thickness required for pressure.
2. The thickness shown in Table 15.

## 9.0 TUBING JOINTS

### 9.1 General Requirements

All tubing joints shall be designed, inspected, tested, fabricated, and assembled in accordance with REP 5–5–1, REP 5–5–3, and ASME B31.3 or ASME B31.1, as applicable.

### 9.2 Proprietary Joints

(\*) All proprietary and special tube joints shall be approved by Owner's Engineer prior to use.

### 9.3 Bite-Type Compression Fittings

**9.3.1** (\*) Joints with flared, flareless, or compression bite-type tubing fittings shall not be used in severe cyclic conditions without prior approval from Owner's Engineer.

**9.3.2** (\*) Bite-type compression fittings shall be used with tubing. Compression fittings shall be per Table 16 for refinery sites listed; otherwise, compression fittings shall be per individual refinery practice to ensure compatibility of connections throughout the refinery. Component parts of these fittings shall not be interchanged between manufacturers' models. These joints are limited to 375°F and 150 psig if made of brass, to 375°F and 1000 psig if made of carbon steel, alloy 400 (UNS: N04400) or alloy 625 (UNS: N06626), and to 1200°F and 3100 psig if made of AISI Type 304, 316 stainless steel or Alloy 20 (UNS: N08020). Derating factors per the manufacturer shall be used based on design temperature. Higher pressures and temperatures shall be permitted with submittal of ASME B31.3 Code calculations and approval of Owner's Engineer.

## 10.0 VALVES

**10.1** Requirements for valve selection and design shall be as stipulated in REP 5–3–1.

**10.2** Materials for valve bodies shall be per Table 1. Valve trim materials and other material requirements shall be as specified in REP 5–3–1.

## 11.0 FITTINGS

### 11.1 General Requirements

**11.1.1** Fittings, including bends and branch connections, shall be in accordance with REP 5–1–1, ASME B16.3 or ASME B31.1, as applicable, and the additional requirements of this Practice.

**11.1.2** Materials for all fittings shall be per Table 1. All fitting materials shall be of the same nominal composition as the pipe to which they are attached.

**11.1.3** Fittings shall be per Table 17 and the additional requirements of this Practice. Unless otherwise specified by the Purchaser, fittings shall be furnished to match the pressure rating, size and wall thickness of the attached pipe.

**11.1.4** The use of cast steel buttwelding fittings conforming to ASME B16.9 shall be prohibited.

**11.1.5** The use of cast steel fittings conforming to ASME B16.5 shall be prohibited.

**11.1.6** (\*) The use of welded construction in the fabrication of fittings conforming to ASME B16.9 shall be subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer. If welded construction is used to fabricate an ASME B16.9 fitting, Manufacturer shall ensure that all welded seams have a joint efficiency of 1.0 as defined ASME B31.3.

**11.1.7** Pipe nipples shall be of the same material as the piping to which they are attached.

## 11.2 Branch Connections

The selection and fabrication requirements for piping branch connections shall be per the requirements of REP S-5-1.

## 11.3 Bends and Mitered Bends

**11.3.1** Butt welding elbows shall be long radius, unless short-radius elbows are required and specified on drawings.

**11.3.2** To avoid excessive stiffness for piping flexibility, a maximum thickness limit of 30% above nominal shall be specified for elbows in sizes greater than NPS 12.

**11.3.3** (\*) Pipe bends shall be limited to use in lines handling solids, certain pigged lines, and other applications with special requirements. They shall not be used for general piping where weld fittings can be used unless approved by Owner's Engineer. When bends are used, an increased wall thickness may be required to satisfy the intended service. When used, pipe bends shall comply with the following:

1. Either seamless or welded pipe may be formed into bends, except the furnace buttwelded type.
2. Centerline radius of pipe bends shall be equal to at least 5 times the nominal pipe diameter.

**11.3.4** Miter bends may be used when more economical than welding elbows for the following services:

1. Water piping.
2. Drain lines.
3. (\*) Internal piping in pressure vessels when approved by Owner's Engineer.
4. (\*) Other services subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer.

**11.3.5** Fabrication requirements for pipe bends and miter bends shall be as specified in REP S-5-1.

## 11.4 Reducers, Pipe Caps, and Plugs

**11.4.1** Buttwelding and socket-welding reducers and swaged nipples shall be preferred to flanged reducers for reductions in run. Swaged nipples shall be used when a reduction in size is made at threaded equipment.

**11.4.2** Long-radius buttwelding reducing elbows made in accordance with ASME B16.9 shall be considered for line reductions at end suction pumps.

**11.4.3** In threaded pipe, reducing nipples shall be used; the use of bushings shall be prohibited. Socketwelding reducer inserts shall be used in socket-welded piping (NPS 2 and smaller) for reductions of two or more pipe sizes.

**11.4.4** Welding caps shall be preferred for closures in welded piping, except that bolted flanged covers shall be used where access is needed (for example, for cleaning, inspection, or a future tie-in). In sizes for which welding caps are not commercially available, closure shall be made by means of ellipsoidal heads.

**11.4.5** Threaded plugs shall be fabricated from solid barstock and match the material of the line class.

Exceptions to this requirement shall include any of the commonly used steam traps (such as Armstrong or Sarco-Spirax) and strainers (such as Mueller).

**12.2** The materials used in special components, such as steam traps, strainers, separators, and filters, shall conform to the manufacturer's standards and the requirements of Section 5.0.

**12.3** Metal and fabric expansion joints shall be designed to comply with the requirements of REP 5–4–1 and REP 5–4–2, respectively.
