Chevron has developed non-asbestos valve packing recommendations relative to service, temperature, and packing configuration. This information is contained in Standard Drawing GC-L99771 "Valve Packing Recommendations," located in Volume 2 of this manual, and will be covered in more detail in the following section. The Standard Drawing is intended for use with outside screw-and-yoke (OS&Y) style valves with the traditional stem stuffing box, gland, gland follower, and bolting.

**Flexible Graphite Valve Packing [3].** In March of 1987, Materials and Equipment Engineering at CRTC completed a test program to find a suitable replacement material for asbestos packing. The primary emphasis at that time was on the ability of packing to seal after fire exposure.

For normal hydrocarbon service, the packing system identified as the only suitable fire-safe replacement for asbestos packing was flexible graphite. More specifically, flexible graphite die-formed rings with braided carbon/graphite fiber end-rings, proved to be the most effective combination.

Based upon its superior ability to seal in Chevron fire tests and on available literature from manufacturers, users, and research organizations, we believe the flexible graphite system is also the most suitable for reducing fugitive emissions through valve stuffing boxes.

The fire test results are consistent with tests completed by a major petrochemical company and several valve manufacturers. Flexible graphite packings have been used for many years in Chevron's plants in critical services such as high pressure, high temperature hydrogen and steam. In power plant applications, flexible graphite packing has been found to be far superior to asbestos packing in its ability to seal.

*Advantages of Flexible Graphite Packing.* There are several positive attributes of flexible graphite stem packing:

1. It is essentially pure graphite with no binders, lubes, or blocking agents. (Binders in some braided graphite yarns serve to hold the threads together so they can be braided. Blocking agents are added to maintain a better seal by filling the voids between braids.) The oxidation of these additives is partly responsible for high temperature weight loss, which can be directly correlated with loss of sealing ability.

2. It is resistant to attack by most service environments ranging in pH 0-14, except strongly oxidizing services such as sulfuric and nitric acid.

3. It is thermally stable which prevents hardening with temperature, pressure, or time, which makes gland adjustment possible while the valve is in service.

4. It has an excellent ability to seal. Flexible graphite under the proper stem and stuffing box conditions can effectively seal with less gland pressure than asbestos, as shown in Figure 1400-14. As a direct result of using flexible graphite packing, some valve manufacturers are redesigning stuffing boxes to reduce the box depth and require fewer packing rings.
