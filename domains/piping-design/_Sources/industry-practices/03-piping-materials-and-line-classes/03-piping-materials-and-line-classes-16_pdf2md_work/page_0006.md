There are a number of methods available for estimating emissions. Details on methods and factors can be obtained from the CRTC Health, Environment and Safety Unit, Air Team.

For estimating emissions, average emission factors are the easiest to use but are the least accurate; they can greatly overestimate emissions. Correlation equations, which calculate emissions using screening data, are the most accurate method for estimating fugitive emissions, but they are more complex to use than the average factors and require a lot of data. If screening data is collected in a facility, correlation equations should be used to estimate emissions. The resulting estimate will be significantly less than the average emission factor estimate.

## 1420 Critical Factors in Reducing Fugitive Emissions from Valves

### 1421 Summary

This section focuses on what can be done to reduce fugitive emissions from valves. The three critical tasks are:

1. making sure the valve is in good condition
2. using the right packing for the valve
3. installing the packing correctly

If any one of these three factors is ignored, the other two may have no impact on emissions. These three factors, good valve conditions, proper packing, and correct installation, can be represented by the three sides of a triangle representing low valve emissions. If any one side of the triangle is missing, it is incomplete, and low emissions may not be achievable. For example, installing a good packing in a valve in poor condition may not reduce emissions and neither will improperly installing a good packing into a new valve.
