## Valve Types and How Emissions Occur

Several types of hand operated valves are used in the petrochemical industry. These include gate, globe, plug, ball, butterfly, and check valves. Emissions from valves are the result of leakage around the valve stem as well as at the valve bonnet. The most common source of leaks is at the seal between the stem and the valve packing. Few leaks are found at the valve body/bonnet bolted flange joint although this is a possible location for leaks [1]. Hand operated valves can be classified by the type of stem motion: rising stem (gate, globe), non-rising stem, and quarter-turn stem (plug, ball, butterfly). It is thought that quarter-turn valves may offer an advantage for reduced fugitive emissions because of the limited range of stem motion. The lack of an exposed stem may help prevent stem corrosion. Previously their use had been limited in hydrocarbon service because elastomer seats and seals were used which limited the allowable operating temperatures. The recent introduction of metal seated butterfly and ball valves is allowing their use in more petrochemical services. Metal seated quarter-turn valves offer other advantages, in addition to potential lower fugitive emissions, such as possible weight, size, and initial cost benefits.

Currently, no data is available indicating if one type of valve has lower emissions than another type. It is expected that all valve types will work equally well if all guidelines for achieving low emissions from valves are followed, including making sure the valve is in good condition, using the proper packing, and installing the packing correctly.

## Research on Effect of Mechanical Parameters

Valve mechanical design features such as stem and stuffing box clearances and finishes, stem taper, corrosion, and runout can all affect the fugitive emission leak rate from valves. For instance, stem corrosion and pitting can damage the valve packing each time the valve is cycled leading to increased emissions. Damage to the stem or nuts can lead to increased emissions by not allowing for proper compression of the packing. Stem runout may lead to misalignment of the stem allowing increased emissions.

To further assess the effects that the various mechanical variables have on fugitive emissions, a Petroleum Environmental Research Forum (PERF), Project No. 93-20, was established in 1994 and completed in early 1996 [2]. This project identified the following eight mechanical variables for investigation:

1. stuffing box finish
2. stem to bushing clearance
3. stem corrosion
4. gland to stuffing box clearance
5. stem finish
6. stem runout
7. stem taper
8. stem to gland clearance
