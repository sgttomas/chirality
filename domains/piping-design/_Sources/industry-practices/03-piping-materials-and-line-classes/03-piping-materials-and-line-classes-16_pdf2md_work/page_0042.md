The best performing packings listed earlier (based on the research studies) do not have to be specified for new valves if the valve manufacturer's standard packings meet the above criteria. These packing requirements were used in selecting the recommended and acceptable valve manufacturers (discussed in "Valve Manufacturer & Reconditioner Alliances" on page 1400-22).

Facilities should not be restricted to using the packing types that were determined to be best performers in the above studies for valve repacking. New packing types that have not been evaluated in studies can still be used by the facilities for VOC service valves provided that the packing manufactures follow the above recommended guidelines and provide test data to show the packing's leak performance. It is recommended that valves with any new packing types be tracked in a data management system and periodically be evaluated to determine the effectiveness of the packing in reducing fugitive emissions.

## Bellows Sealed Valves [4]

An alternative to using low emission valve packing to control valve emissions is to use bellows sealed valves. Figure 1400-21 shows a typical bellows sealed valve. Because the bellows covers the valve stem, emissions from the valve are essentially zero during normal operation. However, in the event the bellows breaks or cracks, the resulting leak initially may be large due to the back-up packing system being loose. The packing, once tightened, should give anti-emission performance equivalent to a regular packed valve. In addition to fugitive reduction considerations, some other factors that must be considered when evaluating the use of bellows sealed valves include intended service, stem sizing and orientation, and cost.

Manufacturer-cited examples of services where bellows sealed valves were tested and demonstrated good performance are steam and benzene. The application in steam service is driven by the economics of maintenance cost reduction and elimination of steam loss. Application in benzene service is to satisfy regulations for emission control [6]. There are also reports of the use of bellows sealed valves in dry gaseous chlorine service. Bellows sealed valves are an option for VOC service, and they may be the preferred solution when frequent monitoring is not possible or desired. The El Segundo Refinery has had a good experience with using bellows sealed valves in several services over a four year time period (1992-1996).
