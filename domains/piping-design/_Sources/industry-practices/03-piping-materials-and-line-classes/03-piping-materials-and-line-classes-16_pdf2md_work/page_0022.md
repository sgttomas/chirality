To prevent releases due to corrosion or fire, the material chosen for the valve is very important. For valve stems, seats, and discs, hardened 12% chromium steel is the most widely used material for general service. Based on research results presented in the previous section, it may be more cost effective to use higher quality stem metallurgy and prevent leaking valves due to stem corrosion. Guidance on material selection is available in Section 220 of the *Piping Manual*, project piping specifications, or from Materials and Equipment Engineering at CRTC.

Reconditioning of valves should meet the OEM's (Original Equipment Manufacturer's) design dimensional tolerances and the standards to which it was built. The purchase of reconditioned valves or surplus valves (other than your own) is not recommended.

## Valve Manufacturer & Reconditioner Alliances

To help reduce the total cost of ownership of valves, Chevron established a team in 1995 to identify the best valve manufacturers and reconditioners and form alliances where possible. By establishing these alliances, Chevron will be able to work more closely with the manufacturer or supplier to provide valves that meet Chevron's needs. In addition to meeting an established level of environmental design necessary for reducing fugitive emissions (valve tolerances, packing type, in-house testing), alliance companies are also chosen based on a variety of other quality issues. The initial selling price may play only a minor role in determining what the total cost of valve ownership is and should not be used as the only basis for supplier determination.

For gate, globe, and check valves, Plant Specialties Inc. has formed an alliance with Chevron for reconditioning valves. McJunkin, the valve distributor, has also formed an alliance with Chevron. These companies were chosen based on their commitment to quality, and by using products from these companies, the total cost of valve ownership should be reduced. Chevron has been working with Plant Specialties Inc. and McJunkin to further improve the alliances to meet all of Chevron's needs and to further reduce the total cost of ownership.

In addition, several valve manufacturers have been recommended as potential candidates for an alliance partnership based on their commitment to quality. A few others were also determined to be high quality manufacturers during the evaluation stage and are approved for use, although an alliance will not be pursued. These recommended and approved companies are listed in Figure 1400-13. By using products from these manufacturers, the total cost of valve ownership should be reduced.
