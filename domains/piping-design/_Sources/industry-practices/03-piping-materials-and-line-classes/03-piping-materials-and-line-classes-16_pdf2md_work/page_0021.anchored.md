Based on these studies, it is apparent that the condition of the valve does play a role in the amount of fugitive emissions. When performing maintenance on an existing valve, these critical features (stem corrosion, finish, and runout) should be qualitatively examined to assess whether or not the valve is in good condition. If the valve is not in good condition, replacing the packing or tightening the bolts most likely will not improve emissions performance. The valve may have to be reconditioned or replaced to stop the leak.

The optimum valve parameter values (shown in Figure 1400-11) resulting from the PERF study, in addition to existing standards discussed in the next section, should be used as a gauge when determining the potential performance of existing conditioned valves or in evaluating valve suppliers. We are not suggesting that you order new valves to exactly meet these specifications. Special specifications may increase the cost of the valve. We suggest that you use these standards to select valves that have similar specifications. These specifications have been taken into consideration in choosing Chevron alliance manufacturers and reconditioners (discussed in "Valve Manufacturer & Reconditioner Alliances" on page 1400-22), and obtaining valves through these alliances should ensure that the valves will be made to minimize fugitive emissions.

Finally, to ensure that the valves stay in good condition, they need to be handled with care. Avoid lifting valves by their handwheel or stem. Instead, cradle sling the body area with nonmetallic slings. Ensure that the valves purchased or reconditioned are also handled with the care necessary to ensure proper operation after installation. Improper lifting methods and the use of cheater bars can bend the valve stem which will contribute to leakage problems [4]. In addition, valve stems on open valves should be cleaned off before operating the valves to prevent damage to the packing which would cause increased emissions.

## Existing Valve Standards

Much guidance exists on valve specifications that, when followed, should help reduce the chance of poor fugitive emission performance. Standards for valves have been written that take into consideration the type of valve service and appropriate valve material, fire protection, stream temperature and pressure, as well as fugitive emission reduction. All of these factors need to be considered in ordering valves, in addition to the research results presented above. When replacing a valve it is important to follow the appropriate piping specification and ordering procedure. "Stash piles" should not be used. By using valves that have been manufactured within specific tolerances and by using the appropriate materials, incidents and fugitive emissions due to problems with the valve itself should be minimized.

In general, valves designs for petrochemical facilities meet American Petroleum Institute (API) Standard 600 and/or American Society of Mechanical Engineers/American National Standards Institute (ASME/ANSI) B16.34 for basic design and pressure and temperature Class ratings [5].
