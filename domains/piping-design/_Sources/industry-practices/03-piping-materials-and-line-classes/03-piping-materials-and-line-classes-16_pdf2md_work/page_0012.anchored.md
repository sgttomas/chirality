## 1431 Valve Condition of Hand Operated Valves

One key factor to reducing fugitive emissions from valves is ensuring that the valves being used are in good condition. The following section covers various topics related to overall valve condition, including:

- the types of valves and how emissions occur
- research on the effect of various mechanical parameters on fugitive emissions
- existing standards for valves
- the benefits of the newly established valve manufacturer and reconditioner alliance

### Summary of Valve Condition Research

The findings and recommendations relating to valve condition are summarized below:

- A PERF study found that stem finish and stem corrosion were the valve mechanical variables with the largest impact on emissions. Another study found that almost half of leaking valves had some form of stem damage.
- Examine leaking valves during repair to assess the valve condition. If the valve is obviously in poor condition, it should be reconditioned or replaced.
- Handle valves with care: avoid lifting valves by their handwheel or stem, cradle valves when transporting, recognize that cheater bars can damage a valve, and clean an exposed valve stem before operating the valve.
- To prevent incidents and emissions due to fires and corrosion, follow all existing specifications for valve selection taking into consideration material, service, temperature, pressure, and tolerances.
- Only purchase your own reconditioned valves. Do not buy other's surplus or reconditioned valves.
- Use Chevron alliance suppliers to reduce the total cost of ownership for valves. The alliance supplier for valve reconditioning is Plant Specialties Inc. For new valve purchases, McJunkin is the alliance distributor and there are several valve manufacturers that have been recommended for alliance partnerships.
- When using other suppliers, check that manufacturer's or reconditioner's valve specifications are similar to the PERF study recommendations and meet API or ANSI standards for valve design (see "Research on Effect of Mechanical Parameters" on page 1400-13).

In general, the condition of the valve is a critical factor in achieving low emission performance. Anything that can be done to ensure or improve the integrity of the valve in service should help prevent incidents and emissions from occurring.
