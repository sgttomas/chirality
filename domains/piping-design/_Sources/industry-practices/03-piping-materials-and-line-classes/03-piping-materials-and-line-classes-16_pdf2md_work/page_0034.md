The following are additional results and conclusions of the PERF 90-05 study:

Significant emission differences were observed among packings as shown in Figure 1400-16. Five packings (group D) had measured emission rates of 0.06 - 0.2 lb/yr while the group A packings had >200 lb/yr emissions, a factor of 1000 difference.

- No packing tested could achieve zero emissions, and some of the tested packings may not achieve emissions as low as 500 ppm, as required by some regulations.

- The more expensive packings will not necessarily have lower emissions.

- The manufacturer's recommended packing compression level may not always be the optimum for lowest emissions. The effect of packing compression on valve emissions varies significantly among packing types. Some packings are more "forgiving" than others during installation. Emissions from the best performing packings were, in general, less sensitive to the level of compression than other packings.

- Lower density (70 lb/ft³) die-formed graphite packings resulted in lower average emissions than higher density (95 lb/ft³) packings.

- Packing manufacturers recommended either a certain "percent physical compression of the packing" or a certain "packing gland bolt torque" to be applied to their packing. It was found that it is more reliable and easier to apply and measure "packing gland bolt torque" at installation than "percent compression".

- For the most part, the relative performance of the packings did not change over the 9 month "Aging Phase".

- Valve cycling increased emissions for most packings.

- Repeat tightening to initial compression levels (with a torque wrench) in general improved the performance of the packings.

- The presence of the Teflon spacers between the ARGO packing graphite rings reduced the emissions control effectiveness of the packing.

Because these results were obtained under a carefully controlled experimental set-up, with new identical valves under constant pressure of methane at ambient temperature, they may not be applicable to all possible conditions that can be encountered in an industrial facility.
