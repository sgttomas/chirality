Laboratory experiments were used to determine the influence of each variable on emissions performance. To conduct this test a typical range of parameters for each variable was tested as shown in Figure 1400-11.

From this testing it was concluded that stem corrosion and stem finish had a definite influence on the emission rate as shown in Figure 1400-12. It was also concluded that stuffing box finish, stem to bushing clearance, and stem taper had minimal influence on emission rate.

This study found that the following specifications give the minimum amount of emissions:

- Clearances:
  - gland to stuffing box – 10 mils
  - stem to gland (diametrical) – 30 mils
- Stem finish – 32 rms
- Stem corrosion – 0 pits
- Stem runout – 3 mils

Some of the mechanical variables were all held at a constant value since they were found to have a minimal impact on emissions:

- Stuffing box finish – 125 rms
- Stem to bushing clearance (diametrical) – 60 mils
- Stem taper – 3 mils

In addition to PERF study 93-20 [2], Exxon also performed a study to determine the condition of high leaking valves [3]. This study found that almost half of poorly performing valves had some form of stem damage. The stem damage was identified mostly as corrosion and pitting of the valve stem (42%) and visible cuts on the stem (15%). It is possible that the packing systems of the valves are damaged as the corroded valve stem moves through the packing, thereby allowing a pathway for increased emissions.
