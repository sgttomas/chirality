## 1410  Introduction

In 1995 Chevron USA Products Company and Chevron Chemical Company commissioned CRTC to assess existing and evolving technology to reduce fugitive emissions from valves. The results were published in a report released by CRTC's Health, Environmental and Safety group in November 1996. The report included sections with detailed information on Regulatory Compliance Programs and Estimating Emissions. These sections are available from the Health, Environment, and Safety Unit website, or from Technical Standards at CRTC. The balance of the initial report regarding reducing fugitive emissions from valves has been incorporated into this section of the *Piping Manual*.

## 1411  Definition of Fugitive Emissions

Fugitive emissions result from routine leaks of process fluid (gas or liquid) from joints, seals, or rotating surfaces. In the refining and chemical industries, fugitive emission leaks mostly result from valves, connectors, agitators, pressure relief valves, sampling connections, pumps, and compressors. Although the emissions from each fugitive emissions source are relatively small compared to most point sources, the large number of such sources in a facility can lead to a significant amount of volatile organic compound (VOC) and hazardous air pollutant (HAP) emissions.

## 1412  Why Reducing Fugitive Emissions is Important

There are several reasons why reducing fugitive emissions from valves is important, including:

- preventing incidents
- complying with regulatory requirements
- reducing inspection and maintenance costs

### Preventing Incidents

A key reason for reducing fugitive emissions is to prevent incidents. A study of incidents caused by valve failures over a ten year period showed that 39 fires within Chevron have been attributed to leaking valves. Leaks from valves have been responsible for 18 incidents at one facility resulting in injury, product loss, plant upsets, fire, and environmental releases in just a two year period. These incidents result in lost profit opportunity and increased liability in addition to the direct impacts on operations, personnel, and the environment. Clearly, by reducing the number and magnitude of leaks from valves, we will prevent incidents from happening.
