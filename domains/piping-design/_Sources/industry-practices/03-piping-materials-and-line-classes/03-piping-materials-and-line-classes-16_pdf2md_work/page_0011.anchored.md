## 1423 Hand Operated Valves Versus Automatic Control Valves

It is important to distinguish between the two main types of valves used in the petroleum and chemical industries: the hand operated valve and the automatic control valve.

Typically, hand operated valves are block valves that do not control flow rate; they are usually either fully open or completely closed. Typical examples are suction and discharge valves on pumps, drain and vent valves, instrument connection root valves, and valve manifolds. However, it is possible to have hand operated valves that are performing the function of controlling flow rate. Hand operated valves can be either gate, globe, or quarter turn (ball or plug) valves.

An automatic control valve, also referred to as a throttling valve, is a device, installed in a hydraulic system, that controls flow through the system by introducing and modulating pressure drop within its body. By increasing pressure drop through the system, the control valve creates a resistance to flow, thus decreasing the flow. The pressure drop is adjusted through the positioning of the valve stem.

Automatic control valves use actuators to constantly adjust the valve stem position to carefully control the flow rate. These valves are generally either globe or rotary (full ball, segmented ball, high performance butterfly, eccentric disk, etc.) valves. Segmented ball valves can be characterized to provide specific control characteristics.

The main difference between hand operated valves and automatic control valves is the frequency of operation. Hand operated valves are typically operated between once a week to once an hour, and sometimes only once a year. Automatic control valves are constantly moving as constant adjustments are made to the flow rate. In addition, the actuators of automatic valves must be able to reliably and smoothly overcome stem friction in order to match valve stem output within 0.5% to 2%. To maintain controllability while maintaining low fugitive emissions, control valve packings must produce low stem friction while maintaining a superior stem seal. Hand operated valves do not have this requirement of low friction for smooth stem movement.

Because of the differences in valve design and packing requirements, fugitive emission control for hand operated valves and automatic control valves will be addressed separately.
