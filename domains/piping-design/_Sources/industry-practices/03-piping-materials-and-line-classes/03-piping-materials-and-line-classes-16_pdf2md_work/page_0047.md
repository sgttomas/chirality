Much time, effort, and money can be saved by installing packing carefully and properly, thereby avoiding having to repair or repack the valves later.

If outside contractors are used for repacking valves, it may be necessary to certify their training or to have facility personnel oversee their performance. Having a Chevron employee overseeing the repacking effort will make sure that Chevron's interests are represented. Familiarity with the facility will also diminish work effort and the possibility of costly mistakes.

For any large valve repacking effort where new personnel, contractors, or packing type are being used, training on the correct installation procedures is recommended. It may be possible to have a representative from the packing manufacturer provide the training.

## Packing Configurations

This section discusses the following packing configurations:

- Flexible Graphite
- Braided Graphite Systems
- Teflon

**Flexible Graphite.** The recommended configuration for flexible graphite packing system is the "stacked" configuration, shown in Figure 1400-23. It consists of three die-formed flexible graphite rings, with one braided graphite end ring top and bottom. The end rings serve to prevent extrusion of the die-formed rings between the valve stem and gland and to wipe the stem and keep it free of graphite buildup. It also increases the resiliency of the flexible graphite, and thus decreases the frequency of packing adjustments. For valves with lantern rings, the recommendation is to use a stack, both above and below the lantern ring.

The required number of rings will vary depending upon the depth of the packing chamber, but should not exceed seven rings. **Ideally, only five rings should be used.** One of the findings of the EPRI report completed in May 1988, entitled "Valve Stem Packing Improvements," was that deep stuffing boxes are inefficient and can actually reduce the effectiveness of the packing [13]. EPRI found that, with more than five rings, gland pressure is not sufficiently transmitted to the lower rings, and therefore they are not compressed. Later, the packing loosens to accommodate the extra space left by the lower rings and the seal is lost or reduced.

Older valves with deep packing chambers should have a carbon or metal bushing installed in the bottom of the packing chamber so that the optimum five-ring set can be used. If that is not possible, for deeper stuffing boxes with seven or more rings, we recommend that a central lantern ring or a load braided ring be installed mid-way to compensate for the additional depth (Figure 1400-24).

**Braided Graphite Systems.** Braided graphite system installation is similar to flexible graphite packing installation, except that five rings of the spool packing should be used instead of using die-formed middle rings. A bushing should be installed to compensate for a deeper stuffing box instead of installing additional rings.
