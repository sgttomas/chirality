## 1441 Valve Condition

One key factor to reducing fugitive emissions from control valves is ensuring that the valves being used are in good condition. Stem condition and finish are especially critical. The following discussion details various areas having to do with overall valve condition, including:

- the types of valves and how emissions occur
- valve standards
- valve reconditioning
- the benefits of the newly established valve manufacturer alliance

### Valve Types and How Emissions Occur

One factor in the amount of fugitive emissions released is the valve type. Several types of valves are used in the petrochemical industry for process control. These include sliding stem (globe-style body) and rotary valves. Bellows are offered for sliding stem valves. Variations at the valve body affect where and how fugitive emissions take place. Emissions are the result of leakage at the base of the valve stem as well as at the valve bonnet. The most common source of leaks is at the seal between the stem and the packing.

Sliding stem valves operate by adjusting the distance between the plug and the seat to change the flow rate through the valve. A linear motion of the valve stem is used to change the relative position of the plug with respect to the seat. Ball and butterfly valves use a rotary motion to position the disk or ball into the path of fluid flow. Bellows sealed valves are a variation of the sliding stem valve with the stem encased in a bellows like sheath that prevents any emissions from escaping around the valve stem.

Rotary valves were initially expected to offer an advantage in fugitive emission controls over sliding stem valves. Some manufacturers still maintain that hydrocarbon emissions are lower through a "rotary seal" around a shaft than through a "sliding stem" seal between the stem and the control valve packing. Actual performance data shows that sliding stem valves with live loaded fugitive emissions packing systems perform better than rotary valve fugitive emissions type packing systems.

### Valve Standards

There are many factors to consider when selecting and specifying control valves including fire safety, ability to perform during process upsets, good control characteristics, and low fugitive emissions. The design of control valves is covered in detail in Chevron's *Instrumentation and Control Manual* [15].

Two industry standards have been recently developed to cover control valve packing performance ANSI/FCI 91-1-1994 defines performance standards for sliding stem control valve packing systems in fugitive emission applications. ANSI/ISA Standard 93 is the standard for certifying fugitive emissions performance of rotary control valve packing systems.
