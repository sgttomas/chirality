- For die-formed ring systems, use the packing gland and a spacer ring to precompress each die-formed ring 2-3% of its original height as it is installed. After the entire packing set has been installed, compress the stack 20-25% from its original height to achieve a seal.

- For braided graphite systems, install the first four rings and then tighten the packing gland until resistance is felt. Then install the fifth ring, and compress the entire set 30%. [8,10]

Alternatively, some manufacturers may specify a specific bolt torque be used in order to achieve the compression necessary for a seal. In this case, a torque wrench can be used.

## Packing and Stuffing Box Enhancements

Enhancements to the standard flexible graphite packing configuration include wedge packing and live loading.

**Wedge Systems [4].** There are a number of improvements available to the standard square flexible graphite packing configuration. Generally, these try to maximize the transfer of axial gland loading to radial packing movement, thus obtaining a "better" seal. Examples of these are Garlock's EVSP 9000 (expandable valve stem packing) and the EPRI Wedge System, manufactured by Chesterton. Garlock's EVSP will expand approximately 1/8 inch on the diameter, or 1/16 inch per side. (This is compared to about 1/32 inch per side expansion for square packing, according to Garlock.) The packing sets come as "standard" and "simplified." The die-formed rings are cut at angles in the EVSP and EPRI designs so that they slide toward the stem or stuffing box wall as the gland is tightened. EVSP uses two different ring densities - 70 and 45 lb/ft³. (See Figure 1400-25.) Garlock EVSP has performed well in emissions tests in hand operated valves.

Wedge type systems, for the most part, have been applied in actuated valves, motor operated valves and control valves. The advantage in these applications is that the packing tends to correct for wear due to frequent stem movement. Vendors claim that they also help to compensate for worn or pitted stems. Control valves will be discussed more in Section 1440, "Controlling Fugitive Emissions in Automatic Control Valves."

**Live-Loading.** The live-load system centers on Belleville spring washers, available for many years for piping flanges, heat exchanger channels, etc. Their function is to maintain a constant load on the packing, regardless of stem wear or temperature changes induced by the process. The load system stores from 20 to 30 times more energy than the conventional system. Figure 1400-26 shows a generic live-load system. Be aware that live-load systems do not increase gland loads over time, but only maintain the initial gland load.

Packing loads can change due to packing wear at the stem, consolidation of the packing, or temperature changes in the valve. A common problem occurs when a hot valve is opened. The hot stem, which has thermally expanded, passes through the packing, compressing the packing toward the packing box. As the extended stem cools, the packing may not be resilient enough to expand again toward the stem.
