## Installation Procedures

Proper attention during packing installation is essential to maintaining an effective seal. Some typical maintenance problems are: failure to remove all old packing, installing the wrong packing size, improper packing technique, improper gland loading, and not paying attention to the condition of the valve.

To remove existing packing, a water pick or water jet is recommended. It may be possible to rent the necessary equipment for use during large repacking efforts. If regular picks or hooks are used, care should be taken not to damage the stem. Be sure that all of the old packing is removed before installing new packing.

Typically, packing manufacturers will provide information on how to install packing systems. If these instructions are not provided automatically with the packing purchase, they should be requested. Some manufacturers will provide training on-site on how to properly install packing upon request.

For braided carbon or graphite installation, the selected width of the packing corresponds to the difference between stem diameter and packing chamber wall. The length of packing for each ring is found by wrapping the packing around a stem-sized mandrel and cutting the packing strip at 45° to level to produce overlap ends. The cutting tool should be a razor-blade or other very sharp tool to produce smooth cuts and to minimize damage to the packing structure. The rings should be installed with the split staggered as specified in the manufacturers' instructions.

Die-formed rings should be installed without any splits if possible, which requires that the valve be disassembled. If die-formed rings are to be installed in the field, they should be cut at 45° (to level) and very carefully twisted and slid over the stem. Care should be taken not to damage the packing structure. The rings should be installed with the splits staggered to prevent the formation of a leak path.

After the packing is installed, the valve should be stroked several times to ensure proper packing adjustment. Reset the packing to the recommended compression levels if necessary. Then the valve should be pressurized and checked for leakage. In the case of leakage, each gland bolt nut should be tightened approximately one-fourth turn or two flats. Finally, the valve needs to be rechecked for leakage.

## Gland Adjustment and Compression

In most cases, manufacturers will recommend an appropriate bolt torque or physical compression level for their packings. However, as the PERF studies revealed, these recommendations are not always the ideal compression level. If a particular packing does not work as well as expected with the recommended packing compression, one should work with the manufacturer to determine a more appropriate compression level.
