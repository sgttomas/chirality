Since the packing is no longer tight against the stem, the packing may leak. The problem is more common in valves with larger stem diameters that undergo more expansion and contraction.

Although live-loaded systems can be used with hand operated block valves, they typically are used with automatic control valves. Each operating company must judge whether live loading or wedge-type packing enhancements are justified for a particular valve. Factors such as fluid toxicity, valve accessibility, and valve availability for shutdown or isolation, must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. If a valve can be isolated and repacked without undue risk, then these enhancements may not be justified.

## 1434 Purchasing New Hand Operated Valves

When purchasing new hand operated block valves, either for a new project or to replace leaking valves, consider the need for low emission performance. Follow the valve standards outlined in "Valve Condition of Hand Operated Valves" on page 1400-12. By using the recommended valve manufacturers discussed in "Valve Manufacturer & Reconditioner Alliances" on page 1400-22, you can be sure the supplier will be high quality and environmental considerations will have been taken into account in evaluating the manufacturers.

Before purchasing, verify the type of packing that comes standard in purchased valves meets the requirements outlined in "Packing Guidelines from Valve Manufacturers" on page 1400-41. If the manufacturer can supply data that shows their standard packing works to control emissions to an acceptable level, do not specify that the valves be packed with a different packing. Doing so may add a significant cost to the valve price since the manufacturer assembles the valve with their standard packing and would have to repack the valve if you specified a different type.

The purchased valve should have an identification plate that lists the type of installed packing. This information should be entered into the LDAR data management system, so that the packing performance can be tracked.

## 1440 Controlling Fugitive Emissions in Automatic Control Valves

The main difference between control valves and block valves is their purpose. Control valves are used to vary the flow rate of a process and need to have more precise control over the stem movement. Block valves operate mostly in either the on or off position and are usually subject to less cycling than control valves. Because of this need for automatic control valves to maintain reliable and precise stem movement, automatic control valves have different design specifications and packing requirements than block valves.
