Other repair methods are lubricating a plug valve, or replacing the cap or plug on an open-ended line that had been left off by mistake.

Many deeper gate or globe valve stuffing boxes have a lantern ring and a "grease" injector. Sealant injection is an option if the valve cannot be taken out of service, but should be considered very carefully, particularly if hot work is required to install injection taps. Also, the effectiveness of sealant injection over long time periods is unknown. The lantern ring provides the mechanical cavity to ensure the flow of sealant around the stem and against the perimeter of the stuffing box. This type of stem packing has been used successfully in services that contain significant levels of hazardous material as a means of stopping minor stem leaks while the plant remains in operation. Sealants should be compatible with packing material and system service. It is important to follow the valve manufacturer's maintenance instructions, and use only the sealant injection equipment recommended. It is possible to over-pressurize a stuffing box and extrude the packing, or, under extreme circumstances, rupture the stuffing box wall and cause a valve failure.

If a valve can be put on the shutdown list, and the leak is not threatening, factors such as needing staging or a fresh air supply should be taken into consideration when deciding whether to attempt an immediate repair or to have the valve be on the "allowable leaker" list.

## 1453 Repair Success Rate

To determine how well the various repair methods work, repair data for one facility was analyzed. Adjusting the packing gland was able to achieve an 86% reduction in the number of valves leaking greater than 500 ppm. Adjusting the packing gland and injecting soft packing was the most successful method and brought 100% of packings to a leak rate below 500 ppm. The results from this analysis are shown in Figure 1400-28. This data shows that adjusting the packing gland will stop leaks in the majority of cases and should be the first repair attempt. The effectiveness of repacking leaking valves with certain packing types is not included here, but has been discussed in "Packing Performance Studies" on page 1400-32.
