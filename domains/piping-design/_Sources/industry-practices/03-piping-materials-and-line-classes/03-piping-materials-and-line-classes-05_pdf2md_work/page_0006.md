Each line class includes a list of piping components (pipe, fittings, valves, etc.) that have been selected for a given pressure, temperature and service. Use of the line class for fluid service, other than that listed in the service index, must be approved by the Company Representative.

Line class designation shall be in accordance with Section 5 of this specification.

Some piping line classes contain a set of notes that are part of that line class and are necessary to ensure the proper use of that line class.

## 3.2 Line Class Selection

The service index summarizes each line class. The assignment of a line class designation to a particular line can be made by reference to the service index for the corresponding pressure-temperature limits, type of connections, material, type of valve trim, corrosion allowances, etc.

## 3.3 Design Factor

The normal design factors to be used in B31.4 and B31.8 piping are as follows:

0.50: Compressor station piping. Gas pipelines within 660 feet of permanent living quarters.

0.60: Liquid, gas, and multi-phase piping within manned facilities; Liquid, gas, and multi-phase manifolds; Gas pipelines within 660 ft (200 m) of normally manned locations, such as camps and control rooms; Pig launchers and receivers; and associated piping.

The engineer may elect to use a lower (more conservative) design factor where conditions warrant. Higher (less conservative) design factors shall not be used without the approval of the Company representative.

## 3.4 Corrosion Allowance

The corrosion allowances specified in the individual line class are nominal. Reduction of the specified corrosion allowance must be approved by the Piping Materials Engineer.

The Piping Material Engineer may elect to use a greater corrosion allowance where conditions warrant. Lower allowances shall not be used without the approval of the Company representative. Any corrosion allowances different than shown in the Line Classes should be indicated on the Process Flow Diagrams, the P&ID, and Line Lists.

Item Description

Piping line items, including pipe, fittings, and valves, are assigned material codes. These codes are based upon a series of material code matrices that enable the code to be used for a computer-generated description of the line item.

The item descriptions, shown in the individual line classes, are abbreviated and shall not be used for purchasing.
