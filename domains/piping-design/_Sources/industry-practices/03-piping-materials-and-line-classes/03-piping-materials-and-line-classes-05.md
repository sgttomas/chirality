# 1 Introduction

## 1.1 Scope

This Specification provides guidance on the application of piping material for plant or facility piping. All designs shall meet or exceed the minimums referenced by this criteria and other noted documents. This document covers material and component requirements for process, utility and instrumentation piping material line classes. Line classes not covered in the attached service index shall be developed using this piping material specification as a minimum quality level.

# 3 Criteria

This specification covers material and component requirements for all new piping systems for Process and Utility Piping Systems built in accordance with ASME B31.3 piping code. Also covered are plants, manifolds, terminals, and interconnect piping built in accordance with ASME B31.4 and/or B31.8 piping codes using 0.5 and 0.6 design (F x SMYS) factors.

## 3.1 General

The line class designation for each piping system will be identified on the piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs) and isometric piping drawings. The Contractor Process Engineer is responsible for checking the complete line designation, especially the line classes on process and instrumentation drawings.

Each line class includes a list of piping components (pipe, fittings, valves, etc.) that have been selected for a given pressure, temperature and service. Use of the line class for fluid service, other than that listed in the service index, must be approved by the Company Representative.

Line class designation shall be in accordance with Section 5 of this specification.

Some piping line classes contain a set of notes that are part of that line class and are necessary to ensure the proper use of that line class.

## 3.2 Line Class Selection

The service index summarizes each line class. The assignment of a line class designation to a particular line can be made by reference to the service index for the corresponding pressure-temperature limits, type of connections, material, type of valve trim, corrosion allowances, etc.

## 3.3 Design Factor

The normal design factors to be used in B31.4 and B31.8 piping are as follows:

0.50: Compressor station piping. Gas pipelines within 660 feet of permanent living quarters.

0.60: Liquid, gas, and multi-phase piping within manned facilities; Liquid, gas, and multi-phase manifolds; Gas pipelines within 660 ft (200 m) of normally manned locations, such as camps and control rooms; Pig launchers and receivers; and associated piping.

The engineer may elect to use a lower (more conservative) design factor where conditions warrant. Higher (less conservative) design factors shall not be used without the approval of the Company representative.

## 3.4 Corrosion Allowance

The corrosion allowances specified in the individual line class are nominal. Reduction of the specified corrosion allowance must be approved by the Piping Materials Engineer.

The Piping Material Engineer may elect to use a greater corrosion allowance where conditions warrant. Lower allowances shall not be used without the approval of the Company representative. Any corrosion allowances different than shown in the Line Classes should be indicated on the Process Flow Diagrams, the P&ID, and Line Lists.

Item Description

Piping line items, including pipe, fittings, and valves, are assigned material codes. These codes are based upon a series of material code matrices that enable the code to be used for a computer-generated description of the line item.

The item descriptions, shown in the individual line classes, are abbreviated and shall not be used for purchasing.

For purchase of piping components, full purchase description under the applicable material code number shall be used.

Manufacturer's names are used only as a reference. Equal substitutes will only be made, subject to prior purchaser engineering approval. Piping components shall only be purchased from the latest Oil Search projects on the North Slope of Alaska Approved Manufacturer's List (AML), unless otherwise approved by the Company.

## 3.5 Pressure and Temperature

Pressure and temperature design criteria shall be in accordance with the applicable ASME piping code and the following:

### 3.5.1 Flange Full Pressure-Temperature Ratings

Generally, each line class has components designed for the full pressure-temperature ratings of the flange for the specified temperature range. Exceptions to this may result from temperature limitations from pipe wall thickness or soft seated valves. These exceptions are indicated in the service index and in the line classes. If maximum operating pressures-temperatures fall above or below the specified range, the selected piping classification is inappropriate. Move to a higher or lower flange rated piping classification listing the same service and corrosion allowance

### 3.5.2 Design Conditions and Pressures

Unless otherwise specified in the individual line class, all pressures and temperatures referred to are design conditions and pressures are positive. Piping systems subjected to negative pressures shall be investigated on an individual basis. Design temperatures and pressures are determined in accordance with requirements set forth in the process engineering "Line Summary".

## 3.6 Impact Tested Minus 20°F Materials

Some material specifications require impact testing for non-low temperature (-20°F or higher) service when the material thickness exceeds 1/2 inch. As impact-tested non-low temperature materials are not widely stocked, substituting the equivalent −50°F material will be considered.

## 3.7 Duplex Stainless Steel in Wet Sour Service

The combination of high strength and high corrosion resistance make the austenitic-ferritic duplex stainless steels very attractive for severe service conditions. The application of these alloys should be made in consultation with the metallurgist or the Company representative, as they may react differently in the presence of O2 or CO2. In ordinary applications, the pH level of the fluid will limit the allowable partial pressure of H2S as follows:

- 22Cr duplex stainless steel (30 ≤ PREN ≤ 40)

- pH 3.5 or greater: 0.1 bar H₂S (1.45 psia)
- pH 2.8 - 3.5: 0.02 bar H₂S (0.29 psia)
- NB in good agreement with latest NACE MR0175 limits for solution HT alloys

- 25Cr *and other super duplex stainless steel* (30 ≤ PREN ≤ 40)
  - pH 4.5 or greater: 0.5 bar H₂S (7.35 psia)
  - pH 3.5 - 4.5: 0.25 bar H₂S (3.6 psia)
  - pH 3.5 or lower: 0.1 bar H₂S (1.45 psia)

## 3.8 Pipes, Fittings, Flanges and Valves

This section shall be reference to "Plant Piping Design Specification". (OGE-PISPE-000005)

### 3.5.1 Thickness Transitions

Pipe and fittings of differing wall thickness may require a transition at a weld for a number of reasons: a change in material strength properties, a change in allowances, a change in line class or pressure class, or the availability of a thicker-than-specified component. The specific treatment of each of these cases is shown below:

#### Material Strengths

Some line classes allow for both high yield and low yield materials, either by size or availability. Where the high yield material is thinner than the minimum wall of the low yield material, the applicable Code will govern. B31.3 requires a transition piece of high yield material, with a thick end that meets or exceeds the minimum wall of the low yield material. This transition piece may take the form of straight pipe or a fitting. B31.4 and B31.8 are governed by Figure 434.8.6 (a)(2) and Figure I5, respectively. If the calculated wall of the low yield material is more than 1.5 times the calculated thickness of the high yield, a transition piece is required. Otherwise, the low yield material may be tapered at 14 to 30 degrees to match the high yield material.

#### Changes in Allowances

Allowances are provided for many reasons including threading, corrosion, etc. If a material has the greater allowance for threading, it may be appropriate to neglect it at the weld point and taper the connection. If the allowance is for corrosion due to an increased susceptibility in the selected material, the transition area shall not be thinner than the calculated wall with the corrosion allowance included.

#### Change in Line Class

Line class transitions at a weld should ensure that both issues of material strengths and changes in allowances are addressed per the above.

#### Increased Thickness

Where materials are supplied in a greater thickness than required, the end should be tapered on the inside and /or the outside at an angle between 14 and 30 degrees.

## 3.9 Materials Not Covered by Piping Line Class Material Specifications

Special piping items such as static mixers and in-line strainers, are not listed in the individual line classes. The Piping Material Engineer is responsible for insuring that the components are suitable for the intended service and that they meet or exceed the line class parameters. The isometric bill of material should provide sufficient information to determine suitability, such as materials of construction and design

parameters. Miscellaneous items, such as nipolets that are not specifically called-out in a line class, are permitted, unless language specifically prohibiting them is included.

## 3.10 Gasket

### 3.10.1 Asbestos Material

Asbestos shall not be permitted for any gasket material.

### 3.10.2 Non-Asbestos Gasket

Non-asbestos gaskets shall be made from compressed, flexible, and resilient fibrous sheet manufactured from selected carbon fiber material with a suitable nitrile (NBR), oil-resistant polymer elastomer binder or equivalent, bonded under pressure.

### 3.10.3 Spiral Wound Gaskets

Gaskets shall be composed of interlocked plies of preformed 304 SS and 100% graphite foil (Grafoil) filler material, retained between 304 SS inner ring and carbon steel outer (centering) ring according to ASME B16.20. Gaskets for ASME Class 150 flanges shall be supplied with low seating stress filler design having a gasket seating stress of 5,000 psi. ASME Class 300 to Class 600 flange gaskets shall be supplied with standard graphite filler having a nominal seating stress of 10,000 psi.

### 3.10.4 Full-Faced Gasket

Material for full-faced gaskets shall be Garlock 9850 or approved equal.

### 3.10.5 Ring Type Gasket

Ring type gaskets shall be used with ASME Class 900 and above flanges. Ring type gaskets will be 304 SS oval style R, RX and BX gaskets with a maximum hardness of 160 Brinell (identification stamped "S304") for all services. Octagonal gaskets and 316 SS gaskets may be substituted as needed. Soft iron gaskets, with a maximum hardness of 90 Brinell (identification stamped "D") may be used in noncorrosive service, with approval of the Company Representative. Ring type gaskets made of carbon steel are not approved.

### 3.10.6 Flange Insulation Kits

An insulating kit shall be used whenever flanges of dissimilar metals are mated, including coated flanges.

## 3.11 Bolting

Standard bolting should be ASTM A320 Gr L7 studs with two each A194 Gr 7 heavy hex nuts except where bolting will be exposed to a "sour" process stream, that is, with a total pressure of at least 65 psia and a partial pressure of H2S of at least 0.05 psia or denied direct atmospheric exposure by sealed flange protectors or sealed insulation.

Subject to availability, line classes with minimum design metal temperature of −40°F or above may substitute ASTM A193 B7 Studs with two ASTM A194 2H heavy hex nuts.

NACE "Modified" bolting, either studs or bolts or nuts, in grades B7M and L7M are required when the bolt may be in direct contact with a "sour" process stream. Bolts in direct contact include those used for vessel internals, valve assembly, and any location where the gasket- sealing surface is outside of the bolt so that fluids may be in contact with the bolt, or as otherwise determined by the designer. Bolts that are not "exposed to the atmosphere," that is, are buried or enclosed in insulation where H2S might accumulate, are also subject to this requirement. Due to ASTM listing of L7M studs only through 2½" in diameter, Vendor availability in larger diameters may be limited, resulting in unacceptable lead times or economics.

ASTM A453 Grade 660 studs with two each ASTM A453 Grade 660 heavy hex nuts, impact tested, may be used where bolting will be exposed to process fluids and L7M studs are not available.

Locations that require "modified" bolting should be specified on the drawings.
