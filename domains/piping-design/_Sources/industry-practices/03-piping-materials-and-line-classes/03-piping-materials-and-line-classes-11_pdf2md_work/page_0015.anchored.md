If ultrasonic indications above the alarm level are detected, an audible alarm shall be initiated and the suspect area shall be paint sprayed. The equipment shall also be fitted with an audible alarm to indicate loss of couplant between the probes and the pipe.

The results of each automatic ultrasonic examination shall be permanently recorded using a method that identifies the position of indications at or above the alarm level and with each record traceable to individual pipe numbers.

If any pipe end region is not scanned by the automatic technique, the appropriate area shall be inspected by compression and shear wave manual ultrasonic for longitudinal and/or transverse defects.

The reference pipe shall be of sufficient length to enable the standard reflectors to be detected during dynamic calibration of the automatic inspection equipment at its maximum inspection speed.

The ultrasonic reference standard for calibration of the automatic shear wave technique shall contain four N5 notches of the nominal wall thickness. One longitudinal and one transverse notch shall be machined on both the internal and external surfaces. Notches shall have a maximum length of 2 inches and have a maximum depth of 0.06 inch regardless of pipe thickness.

The reference standard shall also include suitably positioned reflectors that confirm the effective scan length (dead zone) of the automatic equipment and define any additional length of the pipe that is to be examined by manual ultrasonic as required by 11.2.

For pipe wall thicknesses in excess of 0.80 inch, an additional reflector located at mid-thickness may be required to assist in the detection of buried longitudinal imperfections. The type and position of the additional reflector shall be agreed upon with the Company.

In order to minimize inaccuracies in automatic ultrasonic inspection during production, the calibration pipe shall be passed through the system a minimum of ten times to demonstrate the repeatability of reflector response signals before any production run.

During pipe production, the reference pipe shall be run at the start of each shift and again at approximately mid-shift. No adjustments shall be made before any proving run.

If, during dynamic calibration, the standard defects do not produce signals of the required magnitude, the equipment shall be recalibrated and pipes accepted, since the previous successful calibration shall be reexamined.

The alarm level for automatic ultrasonic examination shall be set at a level below the lowest amplitude signal produced by any of the standard reflectors during the repeatability check procedure detailed in b. above. The alarm set point shall be agreed upon with the Company Representative.

Any imperfection causing the automatic inspection equipment to alarm shall be investigated by manual ultrasonic and/or DPI as appropriate to the defect type indicated.
