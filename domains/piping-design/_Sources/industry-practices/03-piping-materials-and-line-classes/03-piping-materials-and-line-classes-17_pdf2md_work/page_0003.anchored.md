## Table of Properties of Pipe

Table No. 1 presented on the following pages is believed to be the most nearly complete tabulation of the dimensional properties of commercially available sizes of steel pipe ever published. It includes the older weights of pipe (ST — standard weight; XS — extra strong; XX — double extra strong); the schedules given in ASA Standard B36.10 (Sch. 10 thru 160, the higher commercially standardized pipe (ST, XS, XX schedules); and the schedules given in ASA Standards B36.18 and ASTM Specification A409 (Sch. 5S, 10S, 40S); the latter being applicable to corrosion resistant materials, except that Schedule 40S is also available in carbon steel in sizes 12" and smaller.

Piping designers will find herein all the dimensional data they may need to determine:

- pipe wall thicknesses required to resist internal pressure.
- bending stresses resulting from line expansion.
- bending stresses caused by weight loadings.
- pipe column sizes required to sustain given axial loads.
- flow areas and filth powers of the diameter, useful in pressure drop calculations.
- surface area for use in evaluating heat losses and insulation and coating requirements.

**DEFINITION OF PROPERTIES** listed in Table 1

$D$ = outside diameter of pipe, inches

$d$ = inside diameter of pipe, inches

$t$ = nominal wall thickness of pipe, inches

$A_m = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d^2)$ = inside pipe surface, sq ft per ft length

$A_o = \frac{\pi D}{12}$ = outside pipe surface, sq ft per ft length

$A = \frac{\pi}{4}(D^2 - d^2)$ = cross-sectional metal area, sq in.

$A_f = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}$ = cross-sectional flow area, sq in.

$w = 3.4 A$ = weight of pipe, lb per ft of length

$w_f = 0.433 A_f$ = weight of water filling 1 ft per ft length

$r_g = \sqrt{\frac{I}{A}}$ = radius of gyration, inches

$I = \frac{\pi}{64}(D^4 - d^4)$ = moment of inertia, inches fourth

$Z = \frac{\pi}{32} \cdot \frac{D^4 - d^4}{D}$ = section modulus, inches cubic
