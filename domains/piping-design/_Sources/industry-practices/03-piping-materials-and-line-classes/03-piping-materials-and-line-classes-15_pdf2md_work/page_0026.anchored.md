Because a compression fitting actually deforms the tube to obtain a proper seal, the tube wall thickness and hardness should always be checked against the fitting manufacturer's maximum wall thickness and hardness limitations. Also, the tubing surface condition is important for proper sealing; scratches or dents can result in leaks, especially in gas service.

Pressure ratings are calculated by formula:

$$P = \frac{2tSE}{D}$$

(Eq. 200-7)

where:

- P = design pressure, psig
- t = minimum wall thickness = nominal wall thickness minus tolerance (tolerance for less than ½-in. OD is 15%) (tolerance for ½-in. and larger OD is 10%)
- S = allowable stress at temperature, psi
- E = weld efficiency (for welded ASTM A269, E = 0.8)
- D = outside diameter, in.

*The calculated values are rounded to the nearest 100 psi per ASTM A450.*

Allowable working pressures for given tubing sizes are available from tables supplied by most tubing and tubing fitting manufacturers. **However, varying pipe code interpretations are applied in these tables, and the information is inconsistent.**
