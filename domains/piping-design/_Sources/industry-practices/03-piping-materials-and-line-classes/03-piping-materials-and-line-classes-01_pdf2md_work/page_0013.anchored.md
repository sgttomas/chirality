**3.7.7** Pressure instrument gauge valve installation shall be in accordance with Shell STD. 3-1.01, Plant Piping - Design. For pressure-temperature ratings other than shown in the Piping Classes consult the manufacturer.

**3.7.8** Cast steel valves quality shall be in accordance with Shell STD. 6-2.01, Pressure Retaining Castings - Valves and Fittings - Manufacturing.

**3.7.9** All alloy valves shall be subject to Shell STD. 18-2.01, Testing and NDE Methods and Procedures for Pressure Equipment and Piping, Appendix E.

## 3.8 VALVE TRIM

**3.8.1** The valve trims to be used are indicated on the individual Piping Classes.

**3.8.2** In hydrogen service, "Free-machining" trim materials shall not be used (e.g., Type 416 SS in Carbon Steel Classes or Type 303 SS in Stainless Steel Classes).

**3.8.3** In "wet" H₂S service, only "soft" trim meeting the requirements of NACE MR0175, latest edition, is acceptable (e.g., Stellite over Carbon Steel or ASTM Type 316 SS).

**3.8.4** The user of this Standard shall verify with the valve supplier/manufacturer that the type of trim provided is suitable for the intended service.

## 3.9 CORROSION ALLOWANCE

**3.9.1** Standard minimum corrosion allowance is 1.6 mm (1/16 in.) to cover most applications in hydrocarbon service and is included in the pipe schedules in the individual Piping Classes.

**important** **NOTE** that smaller pipe sizes normally have minimum corrosion allowance for economic reasons. Larger pipe sizes normally have 3.2 mm (1/8 in.) of wall thickness available for corrosion allowance.

**3.9.2** Different corrosion allowance may be required depending on service conditions. Upon advice of the Shell Materials/Corrosion Engineer, the designer may increase or decrease the corrosion allowance.
