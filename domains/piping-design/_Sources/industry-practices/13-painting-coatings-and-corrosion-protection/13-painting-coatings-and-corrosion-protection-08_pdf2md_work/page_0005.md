### 3.2 RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN FLOW REGIMES AND CORROSION MECHANISMS

It is known that identical or similar systems (in terms of chemical compositions) might exhibit entirely different corrosion behaviours, because corrosion behaviours depend not only on the aggressiveness of the fluids, but also on how the water is transported to the internal surface. Transport of the water is the result of the fluid flow inside the pipeline. Flow patterns can create conditions that support corrosion initiation and growth. Flow patterns can also lead to conditions where there is less corrosion due to formation of a protective layer or where an oil film has a significant effect. *Table 3.2* shows the relationship between H₂S corrosion and the main flow regimes, covering the detrimental and the beneficial flow effects for a wet, sour gas pipeline.

NOTE: The importance of the relationship between flow regimes and corrosion mechanisms is reported in separate documents: see (6. [1]) and (6. [2]).

In a wet sour gas system, if the correct control measures are not applied, it is not unusual for a pipeline to suffer severe localised corrosion, up to several mm/yr, when the flow regime switches from annular mist flow (with practically no corrosion) to stratified or slug flow due to production decline.

The flow regime may affect the inhibitor type selected, e.g., for CO₂ corrosion in stratified flow, water-soluble corrosion inhibitors are used to ensure the inhibitor gets into the water phase. For flow regimes where there is a large degree of mixing between water and hydrocarbon phases, it is possible to use a hydrocarbon soluble inhibitor.
