## 4.2 WET SOUR GAS CORROSION MITIGATION RECOMMENDATIONS

### 4.2.1 Start-up

During initial well start-up, the well effluents might be more corrosive than the ones under steady state conditions. Intermittent production flows can further aggravate corrosion. Continuous injection inhibition alone might not be effective due to the much higher adsorption of inhibitors by competing solid particles.

As a rule of thumb, double batches (2 days to 3 days before and 2 days to 3 days after start-up) are required for most situations (Table 4.2). Increasing the continuous inhibitor injection rates to compensate for inhibitor adsorption by solids is also required for some types of sour gas clusters. Inhibitor adsorption by solids should be tested during inhibitor selection. Batch treatment programmes may have an impact on the downstream plant (large volumes of chemical arriving at the downstream plant); possible negative impacts should be reviewed prior to starting the batch programme.

For pipelines that have been shut in for more than 6 months, especially under a fuel gas blanket, these start-up procedures also apply. This is because one cannot rely on the existing iron sulphide scales and residual corrosion inhibitor films for start-up corrosion protection. During the prolonged shut-in, they might have undergone significant changes.

Other issues to be considered during start up include:

- status of line prior to start up (e.g. containing sour gas, fuel gas or nitrogen, at full pressure or at low pressure, cold or at operating temperature);
- sulphur solvent (if required) on line prior to start up;
- sampling of Total Suspended Solids to check for elemental sulphur;
- continuous inhibition (if required) on line prior to start-up;
- monitoring inhibitor residuals in produced water;
- handling spent acid fluids if the well was acidised before start up, see (4.2.9);
- monitoring pH and chloride content of produced water, see (4.2.9);
- checking impact of low flow and high flow;
- hydrate control;
- methanol injection requirements for start up, see (4.2.10);
- sand detection;
- ensuring no oxygen is left in the system prior to start-up.
