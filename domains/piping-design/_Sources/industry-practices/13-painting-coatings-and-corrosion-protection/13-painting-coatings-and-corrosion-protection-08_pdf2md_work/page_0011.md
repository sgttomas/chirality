NOTES:

1) This is an inhibitor selection issue. Some inhibitors partition into the water phase under stratified flow conditions. If the inhibitor partitions into the water phase, continuous inhibition works well in stratified flow and pigging is not required to distribute the inhibitor.

2) In the CO₂ corrosion regime, for most slugging environments the shear stresses are not extreme and standard inhibitors will work. For severe slugging environments, the shear stresses may be too high for standard inhibitors. However, there are some inhibitors that work in high shear situations, so this is an inhibitor selection issue. With the correct inhibitor, continuous inhibition can work in slugging conditions.

3) Only required in rare cases where TOL corrosion is an issue. Normally, TOL corrosion is dealt with by control of the condensation rate.

4) Only of use in certain situations, notably relatively low uninhibited corrosion rates and no formation water production (condensation water only).

5) Only of use for low corrosion rates and/or short design life. Even in these cases, use of a corrosion inhibitor and lower (or no) corrosion allowance should be the primary choice.

6) According to field experience, continuous inhibition is not effective in intermittent/slug flow in the H₂S corrosion regime. This differs from CO₂ corrosion, where some inhibitors are effective in this flow regime. The sulphide scales are known to be more tenacious than the carbonate scales, so this is not thought to be related primarily to scale removal. This may be due to less effective inhibitor bonding to the sulphide scales (H₂S corrosion system) than to the carbonate scales (CO₂ corrosion system). Another possible explanation is that intermittent slugging is from the wells and the slugs contain high chlorides.
