The sampling frequency shall match the expected corrosion mechanisms and rates. For example, under a specific high-chloride low flow condition, the predicted corrosion rate is 10 mm/yr. For a pipeline with a remaining wall thickness of 5 mm, if corrosion proceeds unchecked, it will fail in less than 6 months. A yearly sampling program will be useless as a failure might occur before the next sample is taken; a 1 week to 4 week sampling program would be appropriate, depending on the maximum allowable loss per corrosion event.

## 3.8 INSPECTION

To validate the performance of the mitigation program, an inspection plan shall be formulated.

Any changes in the mitigation strategy shall be linked to an increased inspection strategy, to verify that the changed strategy has not resulted in increased corrosion.

## 3.9 CORROSION CONTROL DESIGN

The corrosion mitigation strategy shall be developed with the aid of the data given in (4). This is part of the corrosion control design. For each identified corrosion risk (3.1), the corrosion control design shall include:

- mitigation strategy;
- inspection requirements;
- monitoring and sampling requirements;
- special considerations (timing / remarks / recommendations).

This shall be assessed for each of the major phases of operation:

- initial pipeline start up;
- pipeline start up after well stimulation;
- pipeline start up in all possible operating modes (e.g. line pressurised, line depressurised, methanol injected etc.);
- steady state production;
- shut-ins (of all possible durations and types; see (4.2.3 through 4.2.8);
- abandonment.

## 3.10 MITIGATION STRATEGY REVIEW

The mitigation strategy should be reviewed annually. This should include:

- Check the operating envelope of the corrosion inhibitor (provided by inhibitor supplier) against the operating conditions for each pipeline segment. Flag cases where the inhibitor is being used outside its specified criteria.
- Look for cases where the current mitigation strategy is more severe than in this DEP. Review the history of these lines. Consider a relaxation of the mitigation strategy for these lines.
- Look for cases where the current mitigation is less severe than indicated in this DEP. Review the history of these lines. If it is decided that it is acceptable to do less than is indicated in this DEP, this should be agreed with and signed off by the Principal's corrosion engineer. Otherwise, the mitigation program should be revised to comply with this DEP.
- Review known corrosion problems.
