7) In low velocity situations, any solids in the produced fluids may drop out in the pipeline. The possible effects of the solids include:

- The solids may prevent continuously injected inhibitor from reaching the pipe wall;
- The solids also absorb corrosion inhibitor, which means the inhibitor injection rate has to be increased to achieve the required concentration at the pipeline outlet;
- Flow may be sufficient to move the solids along the bottom of the line; this action can damage the protective sulphide scales;
- If elemental sulphur is present, this causes further corrosion by chemically destroying the protective sulphide scales. Operational pigging is always required if elemental sulphur is or could be present;
- Under deposit corrosion can occur.

At present, corrosion inhibitors that can provide protection in sweet systems, when solids are present in the pipeline, are being studied; this study will subsequently be extended to cover sour pipelines. Possible solids in a pipeline include construction debris, sand (from the reservoir), corrosion products and elemental sulphur.

8) Pigging is required if solids are present in the line. The frequency of pigging is assessed on a case by case basis, based on the volume of solids present, the corrosion history of the line, etc.
