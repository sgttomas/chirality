## 2. BACKGROUND

For corrosion to occur three elements have to be present: a steel surface that will corrode, a free water phase, and corrodents. It is possible to control pipeline corrosion by:

- removal of water (dehydration);
- selecting materials that do not corrode (corrosion resistant alloys and non-metallic systems);
- use of carbon steel with a corrosion inhibitor.

Selection of the corrosion control option between removal of water, use of corrosion resistant materials, or use of inhibitors to control corrosion is an economic decision, taking into account safety and local environmental factors. For many situations, Shell has determined that the use of carbon steel pipelines in conjunction with continuous injection inhibition, batching and/or pigging is often the most cost efficient corrosion control strategy for wet, sour gas pipelines. If carbon steel pipelines are used, the process described below to assure asset integrity should be followed:

- understand the corrosion mechanisms and flow regimes to determine the likely locations of corrosion, the severity of corrosion, and the likely mode of failure;
- identify all possible production scenarios including start-up, steady-state production, shut-in, turn-around, spent acid flow back, etc., as corrosion mechanisms can and will change with field operations;
- select the mitigation measures that are compatible with the expected corrosion mechanisms in all production scenarios;
- monitor the efficiency of the mitigation measures with a corresponding sampling and corrosion monitoring program;
- validate the integrity of the mitigation program by inspection.
