## 4. CORROSION MITIGATION GUIDELINES

### 4.1 SOUR GAS PIPELINE CLASSIFICATION

In a simplified combination of flow regimes and fluid corrosivity, wet, sour gas pipelines may be classified into 8 types, according to the values of the following parameters:

- flow regimes;
- chloride (Cl⁻) contents of the water;
- presence of elemental sulphur.

The flow regime profile for the pipeline is one of the outputs from HYDROCOR modelling. There may be several different flow regimes over the length of the pipeline; the mitigation strategy shall be based on the most severe regime, from the point of view of selecting line type. For example, if the line contains intermittent slug flow in some sections and annular dispersed flow in other sections, the mitigation strategy for the whole line shall be based on the intermittent slug flow regime.

If the flow regime profile for the pipeline is not known, the superficial gas velocity/critical gas velocity ratio (V_sg/V_crit) can be substituted. V_crit values are field specific and have, for example, been determined empirically for all of Shell Canada's fields (6. [6]). Application of this approach in other fields would require careful review by the Principal's corrosion engineer.

The critical chloride concentration of 10,000 ppm has been determined from field experience and laboratory testing; above a concentration of 10,000 ppm, the chloride ion can destroy the protective FeS scales. The 10,000 ppm limit has been conservatively selected.

The presence of elemental sulphur promotes corrosion in two ways: it destroys the protective FeS scale by offering a fresh steel surface for corrosion, and in addition the elemental sulphur is itself corrosive to steel. At present, any elemental sulphur is considered a problem. This may be a conservative approach when there are only low levels of elemental sulphur, but there is no data to support any alternative approach at this time. The primary risk of elemental sulphur deposition from the production fluids can be addressed by taking a bottom hole sample and analysing the fluids.

Elemental sulphur can also be caused by poor operating practices. Whenever elemental sulphur is detected, the root cause of the sulphur presence should be understood before a mitigation strategy is implemented. For example, if the sulphur deposition is caused by oxygen being injected with methanol injection, the best remedial action may be to address the oxygen being injected rather than changing the mitigation strategy.

Finally, where elemental sulphur is detected, this could also be due to poor sampling and analysis practices.

NOTE: In classifying sour gas pipelines, the boundary values for the parameters are not absolute differentiators. Pipelines with values near those boundary limits should not be classified unthinkingly, but according to field experience. The Principal's corrosion engineer shall be consulted in such cases.

The rules given in this DEP shall be used as the basis for the mitigation strategy if no other information is known. Any changes from this mitigation strategy should be understood, documented and captured in the management of change process. An example of the type of information required is:

"Pipeline xyz is classified as a type I line. Because of past corrosion resulting in a maximum of ab % wall loss, the mitigation strategy is based on a type IA line."

Where a change is required for a specific line, the mitigation strategy for the line should be based on a different type of line, rather than changing individual elements of the strategy.
