#### 4.2.5 Planned shut-in ≥ 1 month

Corrosion mechanisms are similar to those described in (4.2.4). After a pipeline has been shut in for more than 6 months, even if the shut-in procedures have been followed, the start-up procedures (4.2.1) shall be used when the pipeline is re-commissioned. During the prolonged shut-in, the existing iron sulphide scales and residual corrosion inhibitor films might have undergone significant changes; hence, they cannot provide adequate protection by themselves.

#### 4.2.6 Any planned maintenance with line intervention

If the inside of the line is exposed to the atmosphere, precautions shall be taken to minimize the conversion of iron sulphide scales to hydroxides and sulphur due to exposure to moist air. Procedural controls should be in place to minimize oxygen entry (e.g., pipeline plugs). Furthermore, if the existing pipeline steel is not resistant to HIC, upon re-start the iron hydroxide scales may be converted to greigite, which makes the steel vulnerable to HIC; see (6. [7]).

If the line is exposed during a turn-around, the corrosion mitigation measures for start-up (see Table 4.2) shall be followed when starting up the system.

#### 4.2.7 Prolonged shut-ins

For prolonged shut-ins, the line should be isolated by blind flanges or by inserting spades. Isolation by valves alone can lead to a false sense of security, because all valves leak to a greater or lesser extent.

If the shut-in lasts longer than originally planned, and the more stringent mitigation measures (pigging, de-watering, batching) could not be carried out during the turn-around, the corrosion mitigation measures for start up (see Table 4.2) shall be followed when starting up the system. In addition, it may be necessary to carry out intelligent pigging prior to restarting the line.

#### 4.2.8 Unplanned shut-ins

The actions required shall be reviewed against the expected duration of shutdown, following sections (4.2.3) through (4.2.7).
