## SECTION 2: SURFACE PREPARATION AND PRIMING

### 2.1 COMMENTS ON SURFACE PREPARATION AND PRIMING FOR THE USER

2.1.1 Ultraviolet or "black light" should be used to determine the presence of organic material prior to blasting, if the area has been in an oily environment (interior of storage tanks, for example).

2.1.2 Abrasive size shall be in the 16-40 mesh range to permit control of the surface roughness (anchor pattern) to about 1.5 - 2.0 mils depth, or as specified for the coating used.

2.1.3 Water jet cleaning is acceptable for some surface cleaning, especially on maintenance work where bare steel is not exposed by the cleaning process.

2.1.4 Mechanical cleaning is acceptable for maintenance work. 3M Company's Scotch-Brite material is an accepted means of such cleaning.

2.1.5 All fireproofed structural steel shall be galvanized or prime coated (see Section 3.7). Some proprietary fireproofing materials may have a low enough pH to attack galvanized surfaces or inorganic zinc primers and may require a barrier coating (such as an epoxy). If a primer coat (epoxy, inorganic zinc, etc.) has been previously applied, it does not need to be removed prior to application of the fireproofing.

2.1.6 Structural steel connection surfaces, if not galvanized, shall be protected with inorganic zinc primer. The film thickness should be kept to within the coating manufacturers instructions.

### 2.2 SURFACE PREPARATION

2.2.1 All surfaces to be coated shall be cleaned by an appropriate method in compliance with the instructions of the manufacturer of the specific coating to be applied.

2.2.2 Surfaces shall be dry and cleaned of all oil and dirt prior to painting.

2.2.3 Surfaces with an existing coating system of epoxy, vinyl, or inorganic zinc, in good condition and tightly adhering to the substrate shall, as a minimum requirement, be cleaned of all dirt and washed with a suitable solvent or Mirachem biodegradable cleaner as manufactured by Mirachem (Phoenix, Arizona).

### 2.3 PRIMING

2.3.1 Ferrous surfaces shall be primed before rust bloom or contamination occurs, but in any case, within eight (8) hours after completion of surface preparation.
