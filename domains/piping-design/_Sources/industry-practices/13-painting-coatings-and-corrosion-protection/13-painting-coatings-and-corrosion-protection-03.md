## SECTION 1: SCOPE

This standard establishes the minimum requirements for exterior protective coatings for underground steel piping at the BP Cherry Point Refinery (referred to as BP CHP). It includes application to both new and used pipe.

**Note:** Even galvanized pipe must be protected per this standard. Direct burial is not permitted.

Compliance with the following industry standards and specifications is required where applicable, except in case of conflict with this standard. The latest editions of the referenced documents shall apply unless otherwise noted.

## SECTION 3: GENERAL

To ensure a reasonable life of buried steel pipe, it is necessary to provide both protective coating and cathodic protection. Coatings may develop holidays over time, and cathodic protection, either impressed current or sacrificial anodes, shall be installed to protect the pipe. Consult the BP CHP corrosion engineer to review cathodic protection design. Refer to standard drawing PA-0100-94 for sacrificial anodes.

---

3.1 Selection of the most suitable coating should result from an evaluation of the following:

a) Environment of installed pipe:

- High resistance (dry) or low resistance (wet or swampy) soils, i.e., corrosivity
- Degree of soil stress
- Congested areas
- Potential bacterial attack
- Oil soaked ground or contaminated soil

b) Diameter of pipe

c) Desired useful life

d) Service temperature of pipe

e) Method of surface preparation

f) Method of application - It is possible to obtain a better coating with shop application than with over-the-ditch field application due to the controlled environment. However, the quality of the installed coating is also dependent upon the quality of the field-applied joint coating.

g) Availability of qualified inspection personnel.

3.2 Shop applied coatings should be specified whenever possible to achieve a superior protective coating. Extruded coatings are preferred, but shop applied tapes are acceptable. Field applied tape coatings are acceptable, either machine wrapped or hand wrapped. Polyethylene tape is preferred.

3.3 Suppliers and contractors shall submit their proposed procedures for surface preparation, coating and inspection, for BP CHP review and approval prior to starting work.

3.4 Whenever color choices are available from the coating manufacturer or supplier, coatings shall comply with the industry color code standard, which specifies blue for water services and yellow for gas and oil services.

3.5 For potable water service piping, in addition to the requirements of this standard, pipe materials shall be certified to NSF Standard 61. NSF certification is not required for valves and fittings.

## SECTION 4: TYPES OF COATINGS

The following descriptions will aid in determining the best type of coating for a specific job. However, there are many additional important factors to consider with each type and brand of coating. Consult with BP CHP corrosion engineer in selecting the appropriate coating.

4.1 Extruded coatings, such as Pritec, Mapec and X-Tru-Coat, are plastic coatings extruded to a thickness of approximately 40 - 80 mils over a mastic primer. They are good quality shop-applied coatings. Typical plastic backings are polyethylene or polypropylene, and the most common mastic is butyl rubber. Field joints are coated with heat shrink sleeves or tapes. Extruded coatings are preferred for most refinery applications.

4.2 Tape coatings have about 30 - 80 mil total thickness and must be applied over a primer. They have a mastic layer and a backing layer. The most common types of mastics are butyl rubber and coal tar, and the most common type of backing is polyethylene. Tapes have high dielectric strength. Tape coatings may be applied in the shop or in the field, and are the most common field applied coating. Coatings should be applied in the shop whenever possible. Selection of tapes for service temperatures above 100°F should be carefully reviewed, since many tapes are not suitable for high temperatures. Tapes can also cause problems in shielding cathodic protection currents.

4.3 Fusion bonded epoxies (FBE) are shop applied, thermally cured coatings, with typical thicknesses of 12 - 32 mils. They have excellent toughness and bonding, and excellent resistance to temperature, chemical and soil stress. Field joints on long lines are generally coated with hot applied FBE, while joints on shorter lines are generally coated with shrink sleeves or tapes. FBE coatings are rarely used in refinery systems because of higher costs.

4.4 Heat shrink sleeves are made to be field applied over weld joints. They have thick plastic backings over thick mastic layers. After wrapping the sleeve over the joint, heat is applied with a torch. This softens the mastic allowing it to flow into very rough surface on the pipe. The plastic backing is designed to shrink when heated, to enhance the adhesion to the pipe.

4.5 The Tapecoat TC 7000 epoxy system or TC Mastic (paint applications) may be used to coat flanges, valve bodies, valve boxes and other irregular fittings and surfaces where the use of tape is not practical. Surface preparation and coating application shall be in full compliance with the manufacturer's instructions.

## SECTION 5: SURFACE PREPARATION

Surfaces to be coated shall be prepared in accordance with the coating manufacturer's instructions.

5.1 Good adhesion of the coating depends predominantly on the quality of surface preparation. In a coating shop, this can be accomplished by abrasive blasting the pipe.

---

In the field, it is done "over-the-ditch" by a traveling type mechanical cleaning machine which has counter-rotating wire brushes for new pipe. For field reconditioning of pipe, the cleaning machine should have knives in addition to the brushes, and more than one pass along the pipe will be required to adequately clean it. A cut back primer may be used on rusty or scaly pipe after the first pass and the pipe allowed to stand overnight before the second pass of the cleaning machine is required.

5.2 Prior to any mechanical cleaning, an approved solvent such as Stoddard solvent should be used to remove all traces of grease, oil, mill preservatives or other contaminants. Loose mill scale, rust, dirt, moisture, old pipe coating or other foreign material must also be removed. Before cleaned surfaces are primed or coated, all weld splatter, slag, sharp edges, burrs, slivers, and knurls must be removed from welds and pipe surfaces.

5.3 Pipe fittings or other irregular areas may have to be cleaned by hand using wire brushes and chipping hammers.

## SECTION 6: PRIMER APPLICATION

Primers or liquid adhesives shall comply with the coating manufacturer's specifications. Pipe shall be primed before rust bloom or contamination occurs, but in any case within eight hours after completion of surface preparation. The primer shall be applied with a uniform coating, leaving no drips or runs, and covering the entire surface of the pipe.

Primer shall be thoroughly mixed and applied, in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. Primer shall not be thinned except as directed by the manufacturer.

## SECTION 7: COATING APPLICATION

### 7.1 EXTRUDED COATINGS

Extruded coatings shall be shop-applied in accordance with coating manufacturer's instructions, including surface preparation requirements.

### 7.2 TAPE COATINGS

7.2.1 Tape shall be spirally wrapped using a manual or power-driven wrapping machine whenever possible. The tape shall be wrapped onto a properly cleaned, dry and frost-free surface. Manufacturer's instructions shall be followed.

7.2.2 Tape shall overlap preceding spirals by the specified value ranging from 1" minimum to 50%. The end lap of each new roll shall overlap the end of the preceding roll by a minimum of six inches. This end lap shall be hand applied without tension and shall be pressed into position by several wiping strokes of the hand. Its spiral angle shall parallel the previously applied spiral and it shall be smoothly applied without twist or distortion.

---

7.2.3 Operators shall make necessary adjustments including tension adjustments to assure a continuously uniform, smooth, tight coating. Care shall be taken that no wrinkles, puckers, voids, or breaks are introduced.

7.2.4 Outer wraps are recommended to protect the tape during lowering in and backfill operations. Laminated kraft paper, felt, and some proprietary products have been used. Selected outer wrap must not interfere or shield the cathodic protection system.

7.2.5 In the coating of bends, wrinkling on the inner radius of the bend shall be minimized by increasing lap and/or wrapping tightly by hand with a narrow width tape (4" maximum).

7.2.6 Weld joints, fittings, bends, etc shall be wrapped with tape spirally applied and overlapped 50% ±15% of its width, resulting in double-wrap coverage.

## 7.3 FUSION BONDED EPOXY (FBE) COATINGS

FBE coatings shall be shop-applied to a clean, dry and oil-free pipe surface in accordance with coating manufacturer's instructions, including surface preparation requirements. The minimum coating thickness shall be 12 mils.

## 7.4 JOINT COATING AND REPAIRS

7.4.1 For piping with extruded coating, joints and minor defects shall be coated or repaired with heat shrink sleeves or tape wrap, unless otherwise approved.

7.4.2 For piping with tape coating, joints and minor defects shall be coated or repaired with tape wrap unless otherwise approved. A primer coat shall be applied in accordance with the coating manufacturer's instructions after the joint has been prepared or after any damaged coating has been trimmed back to sound coating.

7.4.3 For piping with FBE coating, joints and minor defects shall be coated or repaired per the coating manufacturer's instructions, or with a heat shrink sleeve, unless otherwise approved.

7.4.4 Repairs shall be made to all flaws and holidays in the coating, which are visually detected or identified by the holiday detector. Typical flaws include voids, pinholes, gouges, and areas of the coating damaged by installation or handling of the pipe. When non-bonded outer wrap is to be removed to make a repair, the outer wrap shall first be secured on both sides of the repair by one inch pressure-sensitive tape. Coating shall also be trimmed back to sound coating at damaged areas before applying more coating.

---

7.4.5 Heat shrink sleeves may be used for joint coating and minor repairs as specified above, and shall be applied in accordance with manufacturer's recommendations.

7.4.6 Major defects, such as severe damage, partial coating, unbonded or poorly bonded coating, or inadequate film thickness, shall be completely reprocessed starting with surface preparation.

7.4.7 Materials used to repair coatings shall be compatible with existing pipe coating.

7.4.8 Repairs and joint coatings shall be inspected per Section 8.

## SECTION 8: INSPECTION

8.1 Before coatings are applied, all piping surfaces shall be inspected to confirm that the coating manufacturer's requirements for cleaning and surface preparation have been met.

8.2 All finished coatings and coating repairs shall be subjected to visual inspection, and 100% holiday detector inspection. Occasional cutting of coating samples from the pipe may be necessary to check adhesion and bond. All areas failing inspection shall be reworked.

8.3 The holiday detector shall be used to check for defects immediately prior to lowering of the pipe into the ditch where possible. When testing is done in the ditch, the pipe shall be supported on sandbags or padded supports, which shall be removed prior to backfilling.

8.4 The holiday detector shall be operated in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions and at a voltage level appropriate to the electrical characteristics of the coating system. Holiday detection shall be in conformance with NACE SP0490 for FBE coatings, and NACE RP0274 for all other coatings with a dry-film thickness of 20 mils or greater. The detector shall be checked over a known holiday to make sure it is working properly.

## SECTION 9: EXCAVATION AND BACKFILL

The finished ditch and backfill material shall be free of loose rock, scrap, sticks, or other material which may injure the coating when the pipe is lowered.

The backfill material shall be compacted to not less than 90% of original density.

## SECTION 10: FIELD STORAGE

10.1 When coated pipe is to be stacked, it shall be placed on a sufficient number of padded skids, to prevent damage to coating. All stacking and storing of pipe shall be to the satisfaction of BP CHP.

---

10.2  The maximum number of tiers in a stack should be governed by the pipe size, type of coating, and weight; however, in no event shall the number of tiers exceed the following:

[TABLE: Maximum Number of Tiers by Pipe Size]

| **PIPE SIZE** | **MAX NUMBER OF TIERS** |
| :---: | :---: |
| 4" | 7 |
| 6" | 6 |
| 8"-10" | 5 |
| 12"-16" | 4 |
| 18"-26" | 3 |
| 28"-36" | 2 |

10.3  All booms, hooks, forks, supports and skids used in handling or storing coated pipe shall be padded and handled in such a manner as to prevent damage to the coating.

10.4  Coated pipe shall not be stored for more than six months without providing ultraviolet ray protection.
