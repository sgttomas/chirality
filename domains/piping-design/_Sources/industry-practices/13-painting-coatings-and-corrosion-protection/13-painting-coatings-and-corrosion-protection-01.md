## SECTION 1: GENERAL

### 1.1 SCOPE

This Engineering Standard, together with other contract documents, shall constitute the minimum requirements of BP Cherry Point Refinery (referred to as BP CHP) for the preparation of surfaces for painting and coating, the application of paints and coatings, and the materials to be used. No changes or modifications will be permitted unless written approval is obtained from BP CHP.

### 1.2 BIDS AND PROPOSALS

1.2.1 The contractor's proposal must be in accordance with this Standard, and with additional requirements and standards provided with the bid package. Local environmental requirements also apply for both abrasive blasting and painting.

1.2.2 The contractor's basic proposal may be supplemented with an alternate proposal providing the following conditions are met.

- a) Significant price or completion date advantages are indicated, or a superior installation is offered at a justifiable higher price

- b) All deviations from this Standard are stated in detail

### 1.3 ACCEPTABLE MATERIALS

1.3.1 The contractor shall use products as specified in Section 5.4.

1.3.2 All paint (primer, intermediate, and finish coats) for each particular item painted must be furnished by the same manufacturer unless otherwise specified in this standard or approved by BP CHP.

1.3.3 Substitution of other suppliers or other paints is not permitted without the written approval of BP CHP.

1.3.4 Colors of paints used shall be in accordance with Section 5.1 unless otherwise specified by BP CHP.

### 1.4 MATERIAL HANDLING, STORAGE AND MIXING

1.4.1 For each coating, chemical, and other material subject to federal OSHA HCR requirements, which will be delivered to BP CHP, the vendor shall provide prior to delivery, a Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) FORM OSHA-20 or equivalent. Also, all equipment and parts containing or coated with chemicals or other materials, shall be prominently tagged at openings to indicate the nature of the contents, precautions for shipping, storage, and handling. Copies of the MSDS shall be affixed in a protective envelope to the outside of the shipment.

---

1.4.2 Paint and coating materials furnished by the contractor shall be delivered to the job site in the manufacturer's original sealed container.

1.4.3 A storage area will be assigned to the contractor. Contractor shall adequately protect all stored materials from theft or vandalism, and damage due to contamination, rain, high heat, and/or freezing temperatures. Replacement of damaged, stolen or vandalized material and equipment will be borne by the contractor.

1.4.4 Materials shall be prepared for use in accordance with manufacturer's instructions.

1.4.5 No thinners shall be used unless specified by the manufacturer. When such thinners are added, they must be those specifically named by the manufacturer. No extraneous materials are to be added.

## 1.5 ITEMS NOT TO BE PAINTED

1.5.1 The following surfaces, products or items are not to be painted:

a) Surfaces used for identification purposes such as nameplates, serial number plates, underwriter's approval plates, valve identification tags, and BP CHP pennants and signs.

b) Non-metallic products such as seal fabric on floating roof tanks.

c) Gauge glasses and boards, gauge faces, light fixtures, insulators, glass, ceramics, etc.

1.5.2 The following items generally will not require painting when located outdoors. The finish paint schedule will indicate whether finish painting for appearance is required for indoor locations.

a) Copper and finished brass products such as pipe, tubing, valves and fittings.

b) New galvanized products such as structural steel, grating, stair tread, etc.

c) Aluminum products such as siding and sheathing, roofing, doors, windows, ventilators, conduit and pipe.

d) Cast iron products such as manholes, grating and their frames.

e) Products such as thermostats, receptacles, instruments, stuffing boxes and glands, and moving parts of machines (shaft, coupling, piston rod, etc.).

f) 300 series stainless steel uninsulated surfaces operating at any temperature, or insulated surfaces operating above 400°F.

g) Insulated carbon steel and alloy steel surfaces operating above 350°F.

---

1.5.3 Concrete floor surfaces and machinery bases shall generally be left unpainted. Where particular qualities such as non-skid surface coatings, high visibility, marking for safety or traffic lanes, etc. are required, special instructions will be issued by BP CHP.

## 1.6 QUALITY ASSURANCE

1.6.1 The following sub-section also includes procedures, approved by BP CHP, for vendor painted items.

1.6.2 Prior to painting, all surfaces shall be inspected as follows to assure that the specified surface conditions have been achieved.

   a) Confirm that the manufacturer's requirements for cleaning and surface preparation are met for the specific paint system to be applied.

   b) Verify surface profile.

   c) Verify that cleaned surfaces have been kept free of contamination.

1.6.3 Immediately prior to painting, all surfaces shall again be inspected for proper cleanliness and profile, and to assure that moisture, other contaminants, and surface imperfections are not present.

1.6.4 Storage, mixing, thinning, and application of all primers, intermediates, and finishes shall be verified to be in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

1.6.5 Surfaces not to be coated shall be verified to be masked off or otherwise protected prior to painting of adjacent surfaces.

1.6.6 The ambient temperature, humidity and substrate surface temperature shall be documented just prior to applying the coating.

1.6.7 The dry film thickness (DFT) of the primer, intermediate and finish coats shall be recorded.

1.6.8 Any repair work shall be documented and reinspected as outlined above.

1.6.9 Inspection instruments shall be appropriate to complete the inspection task. Test instruments shall be operated by trained and qualified personnel, and maintained and calibrated on a regular schedule as prescribed by the manufacturer. All test equipment maintenance and calibration shall be documented.

1.6.10 Inspection and testing shall be in accordance with the latest edition of the following standards:

---

ASTM D4285 Standard Test Method for Indicating Oil or Water in Compressed Air
ASTM D4417 Standard Test Methods for Field Measurement of Surface Profile of Blast Cleaned Steel
ASTM D5162 Standard Practice for Discontinuity (Holiday) Testing of Nonconductive Protective Coating on Metallic Substrates.
ASTM E337    Standard Test Method for Measuring Humidity with a Psychrometer
SSPC-PA2     Measurement of Dry Coating Thickness with Magnetic Gages

## 1.7 CONTRACTOR REQUIREMENTS

### 1.7.1

The contractor shall not commence work until permission to do so is received from BP CHP.

### 1.7.2

All equipment and materials used shall be subject to approval by BP CHP.

### 1.7.3

The contractor shall comply with all pertinent health, safety and environmental procedures and regulations of BP CHP.

### 1.7.4

If requested, the contractor shall make available their health, safety and environmental programs and procedures to BP CHP. As a minimum these programs and procedures shall address:

a) Procedures for handling spent abrasives and other debris generated during the work.

b) Storage of coatings and thinners while on-site.

c) Programs on respirators, hazard communication, and hearing conservation.

d) Ventilation in confined areas, if applicable.

e) Blasting safety.

f) Protection of workmen during blasting, mixing, and coating work.

g) Scaffolding safety.

h) Fire safety.

i) The use of life saving equipment for persons working above water, if applicable.

### 1.7.5

Contractor's work shall be performed by skilled mechanics in a professional manner. The contractor shall have a quality control program addressing the following:

a) The quality control of equipment used in the application of coating material.

---

- b) The method of selection and pre-qualification of painters hired.

- c) The on-going training program of employees.

- d) The means of identification and correction of defective work.

1.7.6 Contractor shall protect and be responsible and liable for the clean-up of adjacent equipment, pavement, platforms, roadways, and other property not designated to be coated from overspray, droppings, splatterings, damage due to abrasive blasting, etc. (also see Sect. 1.5).

1.7.7 Contractor shall furnish all necessary equipment required such as ladders, scaffolds, brushes, spray equipment, drop cloths, warning signs, abrasive blast equipment, heating equipment, air compressors, exhaust fans, power cables, etc. Electrical equipment shall be suitable for the electrical area classification of the work area unless otherwise approved by BP CHP.

1.7.8 Contractor shall remove all excess materials, equipment, and debris remaining after completion of the work. The premises shall be left in a clean and orderly condition.

1.7.9 Exterior paint shall not be applied at temperatures below the manufacturer's recommendation for the products being applied, and in no case under the following conditions:

- a) If the surface temperature is below 40°F, unless written approval is obtained from BP CHP.

- b) In wet weather.

- c) When the humidity is above 85%.

- d) In any conditions where the surface is not thoroughly dry.

- e) When the temperature is expected to drop below 32°F before the paint is dry.

1.7.10 Interior paint may be applied in cold weather, providing the temperature is maintained above a minimum of 40°F, and the surface is dry.

1.7.11 In general, the manufacturer's instructions for minimum drying time for each coat of paint shall govern. However, there shall be a minimum of 24 hours between coats unless quick drying paints are used, or wet-on-wet systems are approved in writing from the coating manufacturer.

1.8.1 All surface preparation, materials, and application of materials shall be subject to inspection and approval by BP CHP or designated representatives.

1.8.2 The film thickness and film continuity shall be subject to inspection by BP CHP or designated representatives with the use of suitable measuring devices.

1.8.3 Materials found to have been damaged or improperly applied shall be removed and reapplied at the Contractor's expense unless the damage was caused by others.

1.8.4 Inspection and approval of materials and/or workmanship by BP CHP shall not be construed as relieving the contractor's responsibilities.

## SECTION 2: SURFACE PREPARATION AND PRIMING

### 2.1 COMMENTS ON SURFACE PREPARATION AND PRIMING FOR THE USER

2.1.1 Ultraviolet or "black light" should be used to determine the presence of organic material prior to blasting, if the area has been in an oily environment (interior of storage tanks, for example).

2.1.2 Abrasive size shall be in the 16-40 mesh range to permit control of the surface roughness (anchor pattern) to about 1.5 - 2.0 mils depth, or as specified for the coating used.

2.1.3 Water jet cleaning is acceptable for some surface cleaning, especially on maintenance work where bare steel is not exposed by the cleaning process.

2.1.4 Mechanical cleaning is acceptable for maintenance work. 3M Company's Scotch-Brite material is an accepted means of such cleaning.

2.1.5 All fireproofed structural steel shall be galvanized or prime coated (see Section 3.7). Some proprietary fireproofing materials may have a low enough pH to attack galvanized surfaces or inorganic zinc primers and may require a barrier coating (such as an epoxy). If a primer coat (epoxy, inorganic zinc, etc.) has been previously applied, it does not need to be removed prior to application of the fireproofing.

2.1.6 Structural steel connection surfaces, if not galvanized, shall be protected with inorganic zinc primer. The film thickness should be kept to within the coating manufacturers instructions.

### 2.2 SURFACE PREPARATION

2.2.1 All surfaces to be coated shall be cleaned by an appropriate method in compliance with the instructions of the manufacturer of the specific coating to be applied.

2.2.2 Surfaces shall be dry and cleaned of all oil and dirt prior to painting.

2.2.3 Surfaces with an existing coating system of epoxy, vinyl, or inorganic zinc, in good condition and tightly adhering to the substrate shall, as a minimum requirement, be cleaned of all dirt and washed with a suitable solvent or Mirachem biodegradable cleaner as manufactured by Mirachem (Phoenix, Arizona).

### 2.3 PRIMING

2.3.1 Ferrous surfaces shall be primed before rust bloom or contamination occurs, but in any case, within eight (8) hours after completion of surface preparation.

---

2.3.2 Any steel surface not primed shall be reblasted if any rust develops before painting, regardless of length of time after abrasive blasting.

## SECTION 3 – PAINTING

**Refer to Table 1 in Section 5 for a detailed selection guide for the paint systems.**

### 3.1 BUILDINGS

3.1.1 Exterior brick and concrete walls do not require painting.

3.1.2 All exterior surfaces of pedestrian doors, sash, flashing, gutters, downspouts, and large swinging or rolling service doors shall be coated as specified in System #13.

3.1.3 The interior surfaces of all exterior doors shall receive the same treatment and color as the exterior surface unless otherwise specified.

3.1.4 All interior new ceilings, walls and woodwork to be painted shall be given one (1) coat of odorless undercoat and one (1) finish coat of odorless enamel with gloss, semi-gloss, or flat finish.

3.1.4.1 Block wall surfaces that are especially rough shall have one (1) filler coat applied prior to receiving finish coats.

3.1.4.2 Roof trusses, columns and exposed structural steel within buildings shall be primed in accordance with System #5 and finish painted to match the color treatment of the rest of the building interior.

3.1.4.3 Interior doors shall be finished in accordance with local guidelines. Window sash treatment shall match the wall above the Wainscot.

3.1.5 Special paints shall be indicated for areas requiring additional light reflection.

3.1.6 All control room instrument panels shall be coated as specified in System #13, or the manufacturer's standard finish.

### 3.2 STACKS, DUCTS AND SIMILAR PLATE WORK

3.2.1 Steel stacks, ducts and similar plate work shall be coated as specified in System #3 or #4 depending on the service temperature. Painting of these items may not be required when fabricated from COR-TEN steel, if approved by BP CHP.

### 3.3 PIPING

3.3.1 Insulated carbon steel and low alloy steel piping, normally operating from 0°F through 350°F but also including intermittent operation up to 500°F, shall be coated as specified in System #1. Insulated carbon steel and low alloy steel

---

piping with continuous operating temperatures above 350°F shall not be coated. Insulated carbon steel and low alloy steel piping normally operating from 0°F through 350°F, but with intermittent operation greater than 500°F, shall be coated as specified in System #9.

3.3.2 Uninsulated carbon steel piping with operating temperatures from 0°F through 200°F, shall be coated, as specified in System #3.

3.3.3 Uninsulated carbon steel piping with operating temperatures from 201°F through 750°F shall be coated as specified in System #4. Piping operating at 201°F through 450°F shall have a top coat applied as specified in System #12. Piping operating at 451°F through 750°F shall have a top coat as specified in System #11.

3.3.4 Insulated 300 series stainless steel piping operating from 0°F through 400°F shall be coated as specified in System #6, unless the piping has been stress relieved, in which case no coating is required.

3.3.5 Consult with the BP CHP corrosion engineer for any coating requirements for insulated high alloy piping.

3.3.6 Piping welds shall not be coated until after hydrotest. Shop or field fabricated piping welds may be coated only with an inorganic zinc primer, if the pipe coating will be done prior to hydrotesting.

## 3.4 PRESSURE VESSELS

3.4.1 Uninsulated carbon steel pressure vessels, including towers, heat exchangers, drums, accumulators, cooler boxes and similar equipment operating at temperatures from 0°F through 200°F shall be coated as specified in System #3.

3.4.2 Uninsulated carbon steel vessels as listed in paragraph 3.4.1 operating at temperatures from 201°F through 750°F shall be coated as specified in System #4. Vessels operating at 201°F through 450°F shall have a top coat applied as specified in System #12. Vessels operating at 451°F through 750°F shall have a top coat as specified in System #11.

3.4.3 Vessel surfaces to receive temperature indicating paint shall be prepared and coated as specified in System #7.

3.4.4 Insulated carbon steel and low alloy steel pressure vessels, including towers, heat exchangers, drums, accumulators, cooler boxes and similar equipment normally operating at temperatures from 0°F through 350°F, but also including intermittent operation up to 500°F, shall be coated as specified in System #1. Insulated vessels with continuous operating temperatures above 350°F, shall not be coated, except for any parts of the vessel which are not covered by insulation, such as supports or skirts, which shall be coated per paragraphs 3.4.1 or 3.4.2 according to the operating temperature. Insulated steel pressure vessels normally

---

operating from 0°F through 350°F, but with intermittent operation operation greater than 500°F, shall be coated as specified in System #9.

3.4.5 Vessel skirts which will be fireproofed shall be coated as specified in System #1 for operating temperatures from 0°F through 500°F, or System #9 for operating temperatures greater than 500°F.

3.4.6 Insulated stainless steel pressure vessels operating from 0°F through 400°F shall be coated as specified in System #6, unless the vessel has been stress relieved, in which case no coating is required.

## 3.5 ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT

3.5.1 New electrical equipment, motors and transformer stations, shall be coated per the manufacturer's standard. Transformer fins shall be prepared and flow coated per the manufacturer's standard.

3.5.2 For maintenance of electrical equipment, use System #2 or System #13.

3.5.3 Newly galvanized electrical conduit shall not be coated.

## 3.6 MECHANICAL EQUIPMENT

3.6.1 New uninsulated pumps, compressors, and other rotating equipment shall be coated per the manufacturer's standard.

3.6.2 Uninsulated carbon steel pumps, compressors, and other rotating equipment operating at temperatures from 0°F through 200°F and which require maintenance painting shall be coated as specified in System #13. Use System #4 with a top coat specified in System #12 for equipment operating from 201°F through 450°F.

3.6.3 Insulated carbon steel and low alloy steel rotating equipment operating at temperatures from 0°F through 350°F shall be coated as specified in System #1. Insulated rotating equipment with continuous operating temperatures over 350°F shall not be coated.

## 3.7 STRUCTURAL STEEL

3.7.1 Structural steel for buildings and outdoor structures which is not galvanized, and will not be fireproofed or encased in concrete, shall be coated as specified in System #3.

3.7.2 The joint areas of structures bolted together with high tensile bolts shall be primed prior to assembly with 2 to 3.5 mils DFT of inorganic zinc as specified in System #4.

3.7.3 Structural steel that will be fireproofed shall be galvanized (preferred) or coated as specified in System #1. The surface preparation and coating for lightweight or

---

proprietary fireproofing shall comply with the fireproofing manufacturer's recommendations.

3.7.4 All new galvanized steel which will be painted shall be conditioned before painting with a wash primer as specified in System #10. Wash primers are not required on properly aged galvanized surfaces, if prepared by "brush" blasting. Galvanized surfaces which have been damaged by welding or welding torch shall have the galvanizing repaired per BP CHP Standard 501 before painting.

## 3.8 STORAGE TANKS

3.8.1 Ungalvanized walkways, pipe supports, ladders, platforms, stair treads, handrail posts and toe plates shall be coated with the same coating as used on the tank.

3.8.2 New steel tanks shall be coated in accordance with System #3 or #14.

3.8.3 Exterior repaint of tanks with less than 50% failure of the total area shall be prepared and coated in accordance with System #2.

3.8.4 Existing tanks with a failure of 50% or greater of the total area shall be treated as a complete failure and the entire area shall be prepared and recoated in accordance with System #3.

3.8.5 When required, the interior of tanks shall be coated or lined in accordance with Systems #16 through #20. Selection of the coating system shall be as specified in section 5.2.

## 3.9 MISCELLANEOUS EQUIPMENT

3.9.1 Machine tools shall be finish coated with one (1) coat of industrial enamel with such trim painting as may be required for high visibility and safety precautions.

3.9.2 Fire hydrants and indicator valves on the fire water system shall receive one (1) finish coat of System #13 in red color as indicated in Section 5.1.1.5.

3.9.3 All finish coating or marking of safety items and physical hazards shall conform to WISHA.

## SECTION 4: SPECIAL COATINGS

## 4.1 TEMPERATURE INDICATING PAINTS

4.1.1 All surfaces designated to receive temperature indicating paints as the final coating shall be prepared and coated as specific in System #7.

## 4.2 NON-SKID COATINGS

---

4.2.1 All concrete, steel, galvanized or other non-ferrous surfaces (painted or unpainted) designated to receive a non-skid coating shall be prepared and coated as specified in System #8.

## 4.3 INTERNAL PROTECTION OF IMMERSION SURFACES

4.3.2 Interior steel surfaces of tanks and vessels specified to be coated shall be prepared to SSPC SP.5 White Metal Blast Cleaning and shall have a minimum profile of 1.5 mils. The prepared steel shall be coated within the same day as the surface preparation.

4.3.3 The selection of the immersion coating material shall be done in concurrence with the BP CHP corrosion engineer, and be based on the operating temperature and fluid service.

4.3.4 The finished coating shall be thoroughly tested with a Holiday tester suitable for the coating used and set at the proper voltage.
