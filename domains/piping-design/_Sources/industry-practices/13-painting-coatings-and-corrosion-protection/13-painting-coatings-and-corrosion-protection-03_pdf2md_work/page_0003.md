3.1 Selection of the most suitable coating should result from an evaluation of the following:

a) Environment of installed pipe:

- High resistance (dry) or low resistance (wet or swampy) soils, i.e., corrosivity
- Degree of soil stress
- Congested areas
- Potential bacterial attack
- Oil soaked ground or contaminated soil

b) Diameter of pipe

c) Desired useful life

d) Service temperature of pipe

e) Method of surface preparation

f) Method of application - It is possible to obtain a better coating with shop application than with over-the-ditch field application due to the controlled environment. However, the quality of the installed coating is also dependent upon the quality of the field-applied joint coating.

g) Availability of qualified inspection personnel.

3.2 Shop applied coatings should be specified whenever possible to achieve a superior protective coating. Extruded coatings are preferred, but shop applied tapes are acceptable. Field applied tape coatings are acceptable, either machine wrapped or hand wrapped. Polyethylene tape is preferred.

3.3 Suppliers and contractors shall submit their proposed procedures for surface preparation, coating and inspection, for BP CHP review and approval prior to starting work.

3.4 Whenever color choices are available from the coating manufacturer or supplier, coatings shall comply with the industry color code standard, which specifies blue for water services and yellow for gas and oil services.

3.5 For potable water service piping, in addition to the requirements of this standard, pipe materials shall be certified to NSF Standard 61. NSF certification is not required for valves and fittings.
