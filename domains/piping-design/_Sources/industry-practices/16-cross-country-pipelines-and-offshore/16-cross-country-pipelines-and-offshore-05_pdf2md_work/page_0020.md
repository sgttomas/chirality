Instrument air lines should also be stainless steel tubing. Do not use Teflon tape or pipe thread compound on the threads; it may clog instruments. Instrument air lines should have periodic traps to remove condensation. Do not use copper tubing in natural gas service, including gas-operated control valves. Do not use carbon steel tubing; corrosion can clog instruments. The minimum practical tubing size is ¼ inch.

Compressor interstage and discharge piping may be hot, to 250°F. All piping over 140°F must be insulated if it is a personnel hazard.

Piping around machinery may be congested. A 3-inch clearance is required around valve handles, and room should be left to disconnect the machine and remove it. Valves and instruments requiring frequent attention should be placed where it is convenient for the operator to read and adjust them, generally close to grade.

Piping and emergency block valves around machinery should always be conspicuous. Piping should be marked with flow arrows, color coding (per the *Safety in Designs Manual*), and labeled to indicate the contents.

## 738 Sour Service

Those responsible for design and construction **must** be aware of the poisonous and corrosive hazards of H₂S (hydrogen sulfide) and design accordingly. Hazards and design information are provided in Material Safety Data Sheet No. 301, Hydrogen Sulfide. Consult Section 1000 for more information on designing for H₂S service.

### Safety Aspects of H₂S

H₂S is a colorless gas commonly found in crude oil and natural gas products from oil wells. It is also found in water produced from geothermal wells. H₂S is highly toxic. Over an extended exposure period concentrations of 100 to 150 ppm can be lethal.

High concentrations of H₂S can be emitted from gage settings, sulfur removal plants, casing collection systems, and gas plants. Concentrations in the 7000 ppm range are not unusual. H₂S is heavier than air and tends to collect in low spots such as valve boxes, well cellars, sumps, and tank cleanout cellars.

In lines containing H₂S, connections should be minimized. If possible, all piping should be butt welded. If this is not possible, piping NPS 1½ and smaller should be either socket welded or seal welded. Socket weld valves are preferred. Piping NPS 2 and larger should be butt welded. Weld end valves with top entry bonnets should be considered as an alternative to flanged connections.

High pressure sour piping is more susceptible to sulfide stress cracking than lower pressure piping. At 5000 psi, NACE MR-75-01 limits H₂S concentration to 10 PPM, while at 500 PSI it is 100 PPM. Above the NACE limits, special material procedures should be followed to prevent sulfide stress cracking.
