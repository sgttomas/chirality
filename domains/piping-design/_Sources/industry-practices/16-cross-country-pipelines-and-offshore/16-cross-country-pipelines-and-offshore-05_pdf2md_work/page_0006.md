- Surplus or used material may be used if the purchaser is certain that it meets all engineering requirements. If there is any question, it should not be used.

## Fabrication

The preferred fabrication method is butt welding, using flanges or self-energizing hub-and-clamp (Grayloc) connectors. Screwed pipe is normally used in small sizes (NPS 1½ or less) where welding cost is high.

**Welded Fabrication.** Welded fabrication is covered in Section 620, Welding and Stress Relief. See also the *Welding Manual*.

**Socket Welded Fabrication.** Socket welded joints are preferred over seal welded, threaded joints. Do not "bottom out" socket weld connections. Instead, use square-cut pipe and space the pipe 1/16 inch back from the bottom of the socket. This prevents the weld from cracking as it contracts during cooling.

**Screwed Fabrication.** Screwed fittings other than galvanized should be ASTM A105 carbon steel, 3000 psi class. Valves should be carbon steel and pipe should be Schedule 80, Grade B. Fittings and pipe for higher pressures and temperature are identified on specific piping class sheets.

Seal welding of screwed joints is required in vibrating, pulsating, and toxic services. Joints should be made up dry, because thread compound, Teflon tape, and lubricant adversely affect weld quality. Also, vapors from burning Teflon tape are hazardous. To be effective in reducing stress concentrations, seal welds should cover all the threads, including the last scratch mark of the threading die.

Galvanized pipe and fittings can be used to reduce corrosion in potable water service and in all air service except engine-starting air. Use steel pipe and fittings for engine-starting air. Do not seal weld galvanized pipe; poisonous zinc vapors may be generated.

## Testing

New hydrocarbon and other hazardous piping systems should be pressure tested with fresh water (hydrotested) to detect leaks and to allow their repair before the system is put into service. See Section 600 for detailed information.

# 720 Pressure Design

## 721 General Requirements

### Types of Pipe and Manufacture

Producing facilities generally require seamless or electric resistance weld (ERW) pipe. For most applications seamless pipe is more desirable because its composition and hardness is homogeneous and it does not have a longitudinal (or spiral) joint. Seamless pipe is produced by hot extrusion (hot piercing) and sized with rolls and plugs.
