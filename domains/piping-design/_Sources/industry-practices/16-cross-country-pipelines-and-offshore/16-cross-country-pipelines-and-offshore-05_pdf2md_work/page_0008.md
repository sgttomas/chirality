**Wall Thickness.** Std, XS, XXS, Sch 40, and Sch 80 describe the pipe schedule or nominal pipe wall thickness. The actual thickness can vary 12½% from the nominal thickness. Tubing sizes and wall thickness are defined differently. See Section 200.

**Material.** Pipe used in producing facilities is usually seamless or electric resistance welded (ERW). See Section 741 for more on ERW pipe applications. Seamless or ERW pipe is usually manufactured under one of the following specifications:

- API SPEC 5L Grade B. This is the most common pipe grade used in producing facilities. It is a general purpose pipe with good weldability. It can be seamless or ERW.

- ASTM SPEC A53 Grade B. This is equivalent to API SPEC 5L Grade B and is approved for Code piping installations. It has good weldability and can be seamless or ERW.

- ASTM SPEC A106 Grade B. Although this pipe meets the same mechanical requirements as the previous two, its good quality control of chemical properties makes it superior at both high temperatures (above 450°F) and low temperatures (below 40°F). It has good weldability and is always seamless.

- API SPEC 5LX Grades X42 to X65. This high strength pipe is generally not used for producing piping. It is normally economical only in larger sizes and at higher pressures. Special welding procedures may be required. X grade pipe may not be suitable where stress corrosion cracking could be a problem.

Pipe not marked with specification numbers should be limited to noncritical services or tested to determine if it meets acceptable specifications.

## 722 High Temperature Service

As temperature increases, the yield strength of steel decreases. Pipe with a yield strength of 35,000 psi at ambient temperature has negligible strength at 1500°F. Most steels should not be used above 800°F. ANSI/ASME B31.3 takes this into account by derating the allowable stress (SE).

Few rubbers, elastomers, and fluoroelastomers can survive elevated (200°F to 400°F) temperatures. Consult Materials and Equipment Engineering at CRTC and manufacturer's data for more information. See also Section 200 for information on valve seals and packing.

## 723 Low Temperature Service

Low metal temperatures may result from ambient conditions, stock temperature or autorefrigeration by expanding gas. Metal subjected to low temperatures requires special treatments to avoid embrittlement failures. For seamless pipe, the proper treatment depends on the metal temperature, as follows:

- Below 40°F: Pipe is normalized if it is thicker than 3/8 inch. If thicker than ¾ inch it is also impact tested.
