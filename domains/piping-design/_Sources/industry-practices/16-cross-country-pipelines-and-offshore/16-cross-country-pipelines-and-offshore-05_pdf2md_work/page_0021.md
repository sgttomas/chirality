Other guidelines for layout and materials selection are as follows:

- Screwed unions are unsuitable for H₂S service because they cannot be seal welded at the union ring.
- Cast iron valves may not be used; thermal expansion or fire exposure may cause the valve to rupture.
- All gaskets must be confined.
- In general, H₂S piping and equipment should be located so that prevailing winds sweep gas leaks off the plant location, not across it.
- H₂S piping layouts should minimize congestion, especially around safety showers. Piping should be arranged so that workers can find escape routes, breathing apparatus and safety showers quickly (possibly while blinded). Piping should allow clear visibility into the plant or well area so that an over-come person can be easily seen.
- Valves must have bolted, welded, or pressure seal bonnets (with a secondary mechanical retention feature). Block-and-bleed valves with pressure seal bonnets may fail if excess pressure develops in the body, either from thermal expansion when closed or from the process. Such valves should be equipped with a pressure relieving device on the body. Union bonnets are unacceptable because they can back out of the body if packing sticks to the shaft. Screwed bonnets are acceptable only if welded closed.
- Piping runs must not have low spots or liquid traps where water can collect. H₂S and water form sulfurous acid, which accelerates corrosion.
- Pipelines containing H₂S should be clearly labeled with signs or decals.

## Corrosion

H₂S can cause rapid, catastrophic failure of ferrous metals through stress corrosion cracking. This occurs in high tensile strength steel, and its effect can be minimized by purchasing pipe, valves, and fittings with hardness not exceeding Rockwell C22. Galvanic-cell corrosion also occurs if sulfurous acid is present.

The National Association of Corrosion Engineers (NACE) guidelines for piping in H₂S service are given in Specification MR-01-75. Valves should generally be ordered to this specification for H₂S concentrations above 125 ppm. This will ensure that hardness limitations are not exceeded and provide valve materials certification. Consult the *Corrosion Prevention Manual* for more detailed information.

## 739 Emergency Shutdown (ESD) Systems

Emergency shutdown (ESD) valves are an important part of any process safety system. They must operate quickly to isolate and/or depressure the process system upon the initiation of a shutdown. The term ESD valve generally includes the valve, an actuator, and switching accessories used to set the operating characteristics of the actuator.
