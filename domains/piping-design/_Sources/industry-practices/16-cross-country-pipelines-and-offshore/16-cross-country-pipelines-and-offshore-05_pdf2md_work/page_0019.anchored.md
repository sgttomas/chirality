## Layout

Piping should be designed to minimize turbulence. This can be done by avoiding sharp changes in direction close to machine connections (using long radius elbows or bends), and using full port/high C_v valves and oversize suction lines.

Machinery should have block valves, suction and discharge piping vents and drains, and a discharge-to-suction bypass (to reduce backpressure at startup).

Compressor piping should slope up at 8% from the scrubber to a suction bottle to allow any liquids to drain back to the scrubber. Reducers should be bottom flat to avoid condensation traps.

Pump piping should generally slope up from suction to vessel or tank. Reducers in suction piping should be top flat to avoid air pockets. Suction lift piping should slope up continuously from the liquid source to the pump, and the pump casing should have a vent line so air can be purged. Suction lift piping should have a foot valve and strainer.

When machines are operated in parallel, uneven flow splitting may occur. Identical or mirror image piping arrangements will help avoid the problem.

Suction piping should be as short as possible. Pump suction piping is generally one or two pipe sizes larger than the pump connection. Often, a vortex breaker and a startup strainer are installed (see Section 200).

Compressors and positive displacement pumps require a pressure relief or safety valve, often included as part of the discharge piping. The relief valve must be sized to pass full capacity at 110% of the design pressure. Consult the *Instrumentation and Control Manual* for sizing practices.

Relief piping should be routed back to a suction vessel or tank, not to the suction piping, or overheating may occur. A bypass is usually needed so that the machine can start up under minimum load, and is also used to recirculate fluid for flow control.

Pump discharge piping is often larger than the pump connections. Compressor discharge piping may include a short length of small diameter piping between the discharge flange and the pulsation bottle. This is called a choke tube and is used to reduce vibration and pressure variations. Choke tubes are often integral with the pulsation bottle. They can be easily sized with PC computer program GLTW7100. Information on this program and other sizing methods (SWRI for example) can be found in the *Compressor Manual*.

Block and check valves should be the same size as the pump connection, not the suction line. This reduces cost because smaller valves can be used. Compressor block valves, however, may be larger because they may be installed some distance away, outboard of the pulsation bottles, choke tubes, or knockout vessels.

Machinery lubrication lines should be of stainless steel tubing and installed in rigidly mounted trays to reduce vibration damage. Connections should be ferrule compression joints such as Swagelok.
