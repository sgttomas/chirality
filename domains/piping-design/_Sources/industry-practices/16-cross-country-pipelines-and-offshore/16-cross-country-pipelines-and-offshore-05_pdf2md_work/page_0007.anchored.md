Electric Resistance Weld (ERW) pipe is formed by forcing together the edges of a long, thin ribbon of plate called skelp. The longitudinal butt joint is made after heating the joint area to fusion temperature with electrical resistance heating.

Both types have a 12½% mill tolerance for thickness.

See Section 312 of the *Pipeline Manual* for more information on pipe manufacturing processes, and Section 320 of this manual for guidance in material selection.

## Governing Codes and Standards

ANSI/ASME Code B31.3, *Chemical Plant and Petroleum Refinery Piping*, covers materials, design, fabrication, and installation for petroleum refinery pressure and vacuum piping systems. It is used for most facilities including wellhead piping.

The following related standards are outside the scope of this manual, but are sometimes encountered in producing facilities:

- ANSI/ASME B31.4, *Liquid Transportation Systems for Hydrocarbons, Liquid Petroleum Gas, Anhydrous Ammonia, and Alcohols*. See the *Pipeline Manual*.
- ANSI/ASME B31.8, *Gas Transmission and Distribution Piping Systems*. See the *Pipeline Manual*.
- Department of Transportation (D.O.T.) Federal Safety Standards for Transportation of Hazardous Materials, Code of Federal Regulations, Title 49, Transportation, Parts 192 and 195 (49 CFR 192 and 195).
- California Administrative Code, Title 8. Two sections of Title 8 pertain to piping: the General Industrial Safety Orders and the Petroleum Safety Codes. The former addresses work access requirements, insulating hot pipe for personnel protection, piping identification, pipe supports, etc.; the latter covers requirements for gas testing, blinding, drainage, etc.

## Pipe Specification

Piping used in surface installations is normally specified as follows:

**Nominal Pipe Size (NPS).** For pipe through NPS 12 the outside diameter (OD) is slightly larger than the nominal diameter. Above NPS 12 the OD equals the nominal diameter. In all sizes, the OD is constant and the inside diameter varies with wall thickness.

The Company normally uses pipe in sizes NPS ¾, 1½, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, etc. The Company never uses NPS 2½ or NPS 5. Tubing is generally used for sizes smaller than NPS ¾.
