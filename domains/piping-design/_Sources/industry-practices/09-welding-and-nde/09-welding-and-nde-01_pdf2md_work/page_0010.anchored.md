4.1.4 Procedures using any consumables with a "G" classification shall be restricted to the brand and type of electrode used for the PQR. The nominal chemical composition of the specified brand and type of consumable should be identified on the WPS.

## 4.2 WELDING TECHNIQUE AND RESTRICTIONS

4.2.1 Welding without filler metal (autogenous welding) shall not be used unless approved by BP CHP.

4.2.2 Slugging of weld joints with supplementary filler metal is not permitted, unless approved by BP CHP for specific applications.

4.2.3 Approval is required from BP CHP before buttering beveled edges of joints to achieve proper root opening. If approved, the following additional requirements shall apply.

- 4.2.3.1 When buttering the edges of joints, use low hydrogen electrodes or welding processes with electrodes of the same nominal composition as the base material.

- 4.2.3.2 When buttering, all requirements for preheat, interpass temperature, and PWHT shall be observed

4.2.4 Removable start-up and run-off tabs may be used for longitudinal welds. Tab materials shall be of the same nominal composition as the base metal.

4.2.5 Temporary attachments, such as dogs, shall be removed by flame cutting, arc gouging or grinding. Hammering shall not be permitted. The former attachment areas shall be ground smooth and magnetic particle (MT) or liquid penetrant (PT) examined. Defects such as gouges, cracks, or undercut shall be repaired.

4.2.6 Starts and stops in root pass welds shall be blended before subsequent passes are welded.

4.2.7 Peening of welds is prohibited.

4.2.8 Tack welds that are incorporated into the final weld shall be subject to the same requirements as the applicable welding procedure.

4.2.9 Back-purging is required for GTAW and GMAW processes for welding low alloy materials, unless the joint is ground or backgouged to sound metal. Back-purging or root shielding, when required, shall be maintained through the completion of at least two weld layers.

4.2.10 Block welding is prohibited. Block welding refers to the technique of either completely or partially filling separated lengths of a weld seam before intervening lengths are deposited.
