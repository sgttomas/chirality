During outgassing, the hydrogen at the interface in the Cr-Mo will decrease somewhat, but the SS hydrogen level stays at nearly 40 to 50 ppm, providing essentially no benefit.

Also, the diffusivity of hydrogen in a 300-series SS is much slower than in ferritic steels, potentially two orders of magnitude depending on the data set. The slower diffusivity explains why clad steels have a lower effective hydrogen partial pressure than bare steels in high pressure hydrogen services, and it also makes outgassing mostly ineffective for reactor cooldowns. It is hardly possible to outgas the stainless steel in a reasonable amount of time (100 hours could be required instead of 1 hour). The hydrogen in the cladding can simply replenish any hydrogen baked out of the base metal just below the cladding, which is the area of concern. Therefore, an effective bake-out is not practical, and a practical bake-out is ineffective.
