12.1.2 For process services where nickel based alloys are not compatible, the stainless steel portion of the weld must be welded first. Nickel-chromium-iron alloy shall be used to weld into stainless steel. Stainless steel may not be used to weld into deposited nickel-chromium-iron alloy weld metal.

12.1.3 The completed welds shall be PT examined prior to and after any required PWHT.

## SECTION 13: DISSIMILAR METAL WELDING

### 13.1 RESTRICTIONS FOR WET H₂S

13.1.1 Dissimilar metal welds (DMW) as defined in 13.1.2, that are full-penetration pressure-containing welds in piping and pressure vessels, including butt welds and groove welds, with or without PWHT, are not allowed where the weld fusion line is directly exposed to wet H₂S service, since hydrogen can be charged into the metal and result in hydrogen stress cracking. All DMW in wet H₂S service must be protected from the process per 13.1.4(d).

**Note:** Similar restrictions apply to DMW in HF service, but since BP CHP Refinery does not currently have any HF service, it is not addressed here. See BP ETPs GIS 42-103 and GIS 46-010 for HF service DMW restrictions.

13.1.2 For the above applications, DMW is defined as welds using austenitic stainless steel filler metal or nickel alloy filler metal to join any of the following material combinations:

a) Ferritic or martensitic steel (carbon, low alloy, or stainless steel) welded to a higher alloy austenitic stainless steel.

b) Ferritic or martensitic steel welded to duplex stainless steel.

c) Ferritic or martensitic steel welded to a nickel alloy (such as Incoloy, Inconel, Hastelloy, or Monel alloys).

d) Ferritic steel welded to ferritic steel.

13.1.3 For definition of wet H₂S services, use the same definitions specified in the hydrogen bake-out section 8.1.2.a.

13.1.4 The DMW restrictions above do not apply to the following situations:

a) Socket welds in wet H₂S services.

b) Clad pipe with DMW that is not exposed to the process fluids.

c) Internal tube to tubesheet welds.
