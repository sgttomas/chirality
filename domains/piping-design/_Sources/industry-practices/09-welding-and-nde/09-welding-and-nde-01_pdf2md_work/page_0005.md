c) Gas metal arc using solid wire (GMAW)

d) Gas tungsten arc, manual or automatic (GTAW)

e) Flux-cored arc (FCAW)

f) Electroslag welding (ESW) for alloy overlay

3.1.2 Welding processes not listed above require specific approval by BP CHP. The seller should not assume that alternative processes are acceptable during bid preparation.

3.1.3 Welding procedures shall show the electrical parameters (amperage, voltage, travel speed, heat input, etc) when applicable within reasonable ranges for each electrode/wire diameter to be used.

3.1.4 All weld repairs to base materials, including repairs of wrought, cast and forged products used in fabrication shall be done using a step-by-step weld repair procedure, and shall contain the following minimum information:

a) Nondestructive examination (NDE) method used to discover the defect and its boundaries

b) Proposed method for removing the defect and type of NDE used to ensure complete removal

c) Approved WPS/PQR proposed for making the repair weld and NDE method to be used to inspect the weld

d) Complete description of filler metals added, any cold or hot forming, PWHT requirements, and any other essential elements of the weld repair

## 3.2 WELDING PROCESS LIMITATIONS

3.2.1 SMAW of carbon and low alloy steels shall be done using low hydrogen electrodes, except for the following specific alternative. Root passes of butt welds in piping may be made with EXX10 type electrodes.

3.2.2 SAW

3.2.2.1 The SAW process shall be limited to the machine welding process. The WPS shall clearly indicate that the procedure is machine controlled. SAW semi-automatic (hand-held) welding is not allowed. SAW shall only be used on joints in the flat 1G and horizontal 2G positions. The 2G position welding is typically used for circumferential shell welds on storage tanks.

3.2.2.2 SAW shall be limited to ⅜" maximum thickness per layer of deposit for material of 1¼" thickness or greater, and ½" maximum thickness per layer of deposit for material less than 1¼" thick.
