## SECTION 1: GENERAL

### 1.1 SCOPE

This Site Technical Practice (STP) specifies the requirements and procedures for the control of welding, non-destructive examination (NDE), and pressure testing for work performed on site by BP and contractors for routine fabrication, maintenance repairs, AMRs, and projects of all sizes directed by the BP CHP Refinery Reliability department and Projects department. This STP does not apply to on site welding performed for major stand-alone projects with dedicated project staff including a dedicated BP CHP inspector. Major project welding is governed by the BP CHP Quality Assurance Manual, but the project may choose to follow the applicable procedures and practices of this STP. This STP does not apply to welding performed off site. Use of EWRs per this STP also applies to non-welded pipe for specifying pressure testing and inspection required by applicable piping codes for pipe joined by threaded joints or other types of non-welded joints, except gravity sewers.

**Note:** Welding, testing, and inspection requirements for purchased shop fabricated equipment are defined by STP 42 and the STPs for the specific type of equipment, and shall be included in the standard inspection clauses attached to the procurement documents.

### 1.2 PURPOSE

The purpose of this practice is to define the process that shall be used to specify and communicate the welding, inspection and/or pressure testing requirements and documentation for work performed on site, and the responsibilities of the engineers, QC personnel, inspectors, and/or their designees.

### 1.3 CODES AND STANDARDS

All work shall comply with the latest editions of the applicable industry codes and standards, as adopted by the jurisdictional authorities. Any conflicts between this STP and the applicable codes and standards shall be brought to the attention of BP CHP for resolution.

### 1.4 REFERENCES TO BP

All references to BP or BP CHP within this document shall mean BP Cherry Point Refinery, unless otherwise specified.

## SECTION 2: WELDING DOCUMENTATION

### 2.1 ENGINEERING WELD REQUIREMENTS (EWR) FORMS & PROCEDURES

#### 2.1.1 General

There are two types of EWR forms, basic type and spreadsheet type, which are normally used for specifying the welding, NDE, and pressure testing requirements for on-site work. Either type of EWR form is acceptable for any sized project upon agreement between the responsible engineer and inspector. Copies of the basic EWR form and two different examples of the spreadsheet type EWR forms are in Appendix A. One of the two spreadsheet example forms is for general pipe welding and the other is for piping tie-ins only. Spreadsheet type forms may also be used for any other appropriate welding applications, such as field erected tank construction. The data fields on the basic EWR form in Appendix A establishes the minimum required data fields that shall be included on all EWR forms, and shall be filled in when applicable. The two spreadsheet example forms in Appendix A do not contain the minimum required data fields as specified above and shall not be used without adding the necessary fields. Other data fields may be included on the EWR forms in addition to the basic requirements as needed by the specific users, and data fields may be in a different arrangement. Instructions for EWR preparation, including EWR numbering, generally apply to both types of EWR forms.

#### 2.1.2 Basic EWR Form

Basic EWR forms are normally used for individual welding or testing jobs for maintenance repairs, AMR modifications, and small to medium sized projects.

#### 2.1.3 Spreadsheet EWR Form

Spreadsheet EWR forms are normally used for projects with multiple welding and testing tasks, where it is more efficient to combine the multiple tasks in a spreadsheet table, rather than preparing and handling many individual basic EWR forms. A single EWR number is assigned to the spreadsheet EWR, and a copy of the spreadsheet is kept in the EWR file for the applicable department along with the basic EWRs.

#### 2.1.4 Engineering Approval of EWR

EWRs shall be approved by an authorized engineer, who normally is a member of or is affiliated with the department which is directing the related work. The authorized engineer is ultimately responsible for the EWR, but the engineer may designate an alternate to prepare the EWR form and assist with the workflow process shown in Figure 1 following this section. When an alternate prepares the EWR form, the authorized engineer shall review the input of the preparer and provide the EWR approval signature, before welding or testing begins.

---

The authorized engineer must be listed in the BP or Contractor table of Authorized Engineers For BP CHP EWR Approval. These tables are located in the Reliability Engineering SharePoint site, accessible from the Cherry Point homepage. These tables are maintained and updated as directed by the Reliability Engineering Superintendent.

## 2.1.5 EWR Preparation Instructions

To prepare the EWR form for a specific job, follow the workflow diagram in Figure 1 above, along with the written instructions below which apply to specific data fields on the basic EWR form. For spreadsheet EWR forms, follow a similar process adjusted to fit the specific form.

a) **EWR (number):** (completed by preparer) reserve and use the next available number from the EWR file index for the Reliability department or the Projects department as applicable. EWR files & index tables should exist or be created as needed for each calendar year and each refinery unit where welding is performed. The EWR number is reserved when the file index table is filled out with the EWR number, an AMR number or basic job description, and the preparer's name or initials. See sections 2.1.6 & 2.1.7 for more information on EWR number assignments.

b) **REV:** (completed by preparer) revision number for the specific EWR, starting with zero (0) on the first issue. Any subsequent changes to the EWR shall be re-issued and distributed with an appropriate increase in the revision number and with changes identified and initialed by the authorized engineer, and a new copy of the EWR placed in the appropriate department file to

---

replace the previous version. When the welding and testing requirements are revised, the inspector shall also initial the changes.

c) **Prepared by:** (completed by preparer) the name of the EWR preparer, either responsible engineer or designated alternate.

d) **Engineering Approved by:** the responsible authorized engineer provides signature to approve technical requirements (see section 2.1.4 for authorized engineer criteria)

e) **Date/Unit/Material/Thickness/Attachment/Company & Contact:** (completed by preparer) date of preparation, refinery unit number, material type to be welded, material thickness or range of thicknesses at weld locations, list of any attachments to the EWR or include in scope of work if more space needed, name of company performing the welding and their contact person if known

f) **Item:** (completed by preparer) equipment number, line number with size & pipe class, or other identifying name or description.

g) **Welding jurisdiction:** (completed by preparer) mark an "X" next to the applicable industry code that has jurisdiction over the welding to be performed; Structural, B31.3, ASME Section VIII, ASME Section I, or fill in name of other type (select only one).

h) **W.O./AMR:** (completed by preparer) authorization numbers or charge numbers, when applicable.

i) **Scope of Work:** (completed by preparer) brief description of work scope along with sketches when needed, and any special instructions such as traveler requirements, unusual testing or NDE, or required coordination with other work groups. Include drawing numbers if there are too many to fit in the Attachment space.

j) **Welding and NDE requirements:** (completed by preparer)

- Welding Procedure No.: list WPS numbers to be used, specific to the company doing the welding.

- Mill Test Reports Req'd: specify "Yes", when required by STPs or codes.

- PWHT: specify temperature range and duration, when required by STPs or codes.

- Hardness: specify maximum allowed Brinell weld hardness, when required by STPs or codes.

---

- NDE Requirements: specify UT, PT, MT, RT, and PMI when required by STPs or codes; and any additional NDE at discretion of engineer or inspector.

k) **Pressure Test Requirements:** (completed by preparer) specify test type, pressure, medium, and minimum metal temperature during test, as required by STPs or codes. For pneumatic tests, also fill in the 3 blanks in the 5 step procedure.

l) **Inspection Review:** BP CHP inspector reviews & signs EWR prior to the start of work; preparer places a copy of the EWR with inspection review signature & attachments in the appropriate department EWR file. The inspector may need to review the Weld Procedure Specification (WPS) and Procedure Qualification Record (PQR) before signing the EWR, if these documents have not previously been reviewed.

m) **Final Inspection Acceptance:** after completion of welding, NDE, and pressure testing, the BP or contractor QC personnel shall submit a copy of the EWR along with any supporting NDE reports to the BP CHP inspector or designated alternate for final acceptance signature.

## 2.1.6 EWR Files & Index Tables

Two separate sets of EWR files including index tables for each refinery unit are established and maintained to hold the completed EWR forms. The EWR numbering systems for these two file sets are slightly different, as defined in the next paragraph. One file set is maintained by the Reliability department and the second file set is maintained by the Projects department. Currently these files are contained in hard copy binders, however electronic files may be used by consensus within the separate departments. EWR files are designated by calendar year and process unit numbers. Document Control is available to assist BP and contract engineers in reserving EWR numbers for the Projects department only. The following information is required to reserve a number through Document Control: requester name & company, contact phone number, refinery unit number, and AMR number/BP project number.

## 2.1.7 EWR Numbers

EWR numbers are separated into two numbering systems (Reliability & Projects) with numbers sequential by unit, year, and department as shown below:

- Reliability EWR: unit-year-sequence number M, for example 10-13-01M ("M" originally indicated **M**aintenance department which is now called Reliability)
- Projects EWR: unit-year-sequence number T, for example 10-13-01T ("T" originally indicated **T**echnical department which is now called Projects)

---

2.1.8 Attachments to the EWR

Working copies of the EWR distributed to the applicable work group shall have all applicable sketches and drawings attached. Copies of all sketches, non-BP drawings, and marked-up BP drawings used to define scope of work shall be copied and filed with the EWR. Referenced BP drawings which have no manual mark-ups do not have to be copied and filed with the EWR, if they are currently available in Documentum.

**NOTE:** In most cases, when the EWR is issued for work, referenced BP drawings which are new will not yet be filed in Documentum.

## 2.2 Q/C TRAVELER

For code welding on pressure vessels, boilers, and piping under the jurisdiction of ASME Sec. VIII, ASME Sec. I, and ASME B31.1, a Q/C Traveler is required as specified in the Quality Assurance Manual of the company doing the welding. A copy of the BP CHP Refinery Quality Assurance Manual can be obtained from the Inspection department, or on-line at R:\CHPcomm\INSPECT\Manuals\Quality Assurance Manual. BP personnel will complete the Traveler form only when the work will be done by the BP General Shop. When a BP Traveler is written, an EWR is not required. A reference copy of the BP Traveler form is in Appendix B. When the code work will be done by a contractor, the contractor will provide and complete a Traveler form per their Quality Assurance Manual, and BP shall prepare an EWR to describe the job requirements and to specify that the contractor shall provide a Traveler.

## 2.3 BLANKET WELD PROCEDURE

### 2.3.1 Purpose

The blanket weld procedure is only for use on specific types of routine maintenance repair and replacement work performed by the BP CHP General Shop or the routine maintenance contractor Matrix Service Company. The blanket weld procedure is not for use on new installation work or modification work. The purpose of the blanket weld procedure is to reduce the number of EWRs written for routine repair or replacement of B31.3 carbon steel piping in specific pipe classes, structural steel, and non-pressure attachments. Details are provided in the Blanket Weld Procedure Guidelines table in Appendix C.

### 2.3.2 Scope

a) Except as specified in paragraph b) below, the blanket weld procedure covers only the repair or in-kind replacement of structural parts, or pipe parts in specific STP 205 pipe classes listed in Appendix C. An inspection ticket is required to authorize any welding performed under the blanket weld procedure.

b) The blanket weld procedure may be used to replace NPS 1.5 and smaller unions at existing steam trap stations in pipe classes CAH, DAH, and FAH with flanges as specified in the applicable pipe class.

---

c) Piping in caustic, amine or catacarb service is not covered by the blanket weld procedure. EWRs are required for all piping repairs or replacements in these services.

d) Repairs and replacements permitted under the blanket weld procedure apply only to welding performed by BP or Matrix using their qualified weld procedures as specified in the attached table.

e) The blanket weld procedure does NOT apply to any other pressure containing equipment.

## 2.3.3 Procedure

a) Materials used for repairs or replacements shall follow the BP Piping Material Specification, STP 205. For NPS 2 and larger piping in some line classes, the wall thickness is specified as "calculate". When repairs or replacements are made for these pipe classes, contact the BP Reliability Engineer for wall thickness or pipe schedule to be used.

b) Contact BP Inspection for the needed repair or replacement. After Inspection determines that use of the Blanket Weld Procedure Guideline is appropriate, the requester will furnish applicable information and identifying line numbers.

c) For turnaround work, Inspection will write electronic Inspection Turnaround Tickets. For non-turnaround work, Inspection will create Inspection Work Order Tickets or change the purpose of existing Work Orders into Inspection Work Order Tickets. In the text of the Tickets, the Inspector will indicate the use of the Blanket Weld Procedure Guideline.

d) At the time of the final inspection (pressure test, visual exam, etc.) BP or Matrix QC personnel will provide a paper copy of the Inspection Ticket for the Inspection representative to review. After the test or examination is witnessed and accepted, Inspection or their representative will sign the copy of the Inspection Ticket. The signed Inspection Ticket will be returned to BP Inspection and the ticket will be closed.

**Note:** Without a copy of the ticket available as above, tests will not be accepted by Inspection.

e) All NDE must be completed prior to hydrotesting the repair or replacement. BP or Matrix QC personnel will contact the NDE contractor directly to schedule the required NDE.

f) Any deviation from these procedures, the Blanket Weld Procedure Guidelines, or the applicable general notes must have approval from Reliability Engineer and Inspection.

---

g) If replacement requires more than ten butt welds or more than fifty feet of pipe, Reliability Engineering is to be notified by Inspection to investigate the cause of the failure.

h) Service tests will be witnessed by the Operations or Maintenance Foreman, Matrix QC, BP Inspection, BP QC, or an Inspection designee.

## 2.4 WELDING PERMITTED WITHOUT EWR OR TICKET

Welding does not require an EWR or Inspection Ticket for repair or replacement of NPS 1.5 and smaller carbon steel piping systems downstream from the first block valve off the main header in 140 psig and lower pressure steam or condensate services. However, the welding and testing requirements applicable for these pipe sizes and services in the Blanket Weld Procedure Guideline table in Appendix C shall be followed. The welds must be visually inspected and the service test witnessed by Operations or Maintenance Foreman, Matrix QC, BP Inspection, BP QC, or Inspection designee.

## 2.5 WELDING OR HOT TAPS ON IN-SERVICE EQUIPMENT & PIPING

For policies and forms for welding on in service equipment and piping, including hot tapping, go to Cherry Point Safety & Health Policy 520.
