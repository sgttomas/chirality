## B1.0 INTRODUCTION

### B1.1 Scope

B1.1.1 Appendix B of this welding specification addresses welding and postweld heat treatment requirements for pipelines, above ground facility piping, and wellsite piping within the jurisdiction of CSA Z662. Appendix B includes sour service requirements under Section B7, and supplements the general welding and postweld heat treatment requirements provided in the main body of this specification. This appendix is intended for pipe and components up to and including:

- Grade 359 (X52); and
- 19.05 mm (0.750") WT.

### B1.2 Reference Documents

B1.2.1 All welding and postweld heat treatment shall satisfy the minimum requirements of CSA Standard Z662, latest edition. CSA Z662-94 clause numbers corresponding to the topics discussed in this appendix are shown for the convenience of readers in parentheses.

## B2.0 GENERAL AND ADMINISTRATIVE REQUIREMENTS

### B2.1 Witnessing Of Weld Procedure and Welder Qualification Tests

B2.2.1 All welding procedure and welder qualifications shall be witnessed and accepted by the OWNER. The Pipeline Contractor shall inform the OWNER at least 48 hours in advance of the welding of test coupons, nondestructive testing of coupons and destructive testing of coupons to permit required witnessing.

## B3.0 WELDING QUALIFICATIONS

**(Z662-94 CL. 7.2.1.4 - 7.2.1.6)**

### B3.1 Welding Procedure Qualification

B3.1.1 Both nondestructive and destructive testing is required for each welding procedure qualification joint. Nondestructive examination shall be by visual and radiographic methods, with acceptance criteria defined in Owner's Specification 000-PES-5009 NDE Examination. Destructive testing requirements shall be as defined by CSA Z662 and the following paragraphs.

B3.1.2 When specified by the engineering design, weld and HAZ Charpy impact tests shall be conducted at the minimum design temperature, in a manner which covers the required range of welding variables.

B3.1.3 When ASMe weld qualifications are used as permitted by CSA Z662, the standards of acceptance for each discontinuity shall be the more stringent of CSA Z662 or ASME B31.3. OWNER interpretation of these requirements may be found in Owner's Specification 000-PES-5009 NDE Examination. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.1.4)

## B3.2 Welder and Welding Operator Performance Qualification

**B3.2.1** Each welder used by the Pipeline Contractor shall pass performance qualification tests according to CSA Z662. Qualification tests may be made on the job site or at a site suitable to both Pipeline Contractor and OWNER.

**B3.2.2** For performance qualification tests, the Pipeline Contractor shall:

- prepare coupons for welding from line pipe supplied by the OWNER
- furnish all necessary welding equipment and welding consumables
- allow test coupons to cool in still air to ambient temperature before testing
- satisfy the test requirements defined by CSA Z662 and any supplementary NDE requirements stated in Owner's Specification 000-PED-5009 NDE Examination
- record the results of all welder and welding operator performance qualification tests on forms similar to those presented in CSA Z662
- prepare and maintain a list of welders and their qualifications, which shall be updated if there are changes of welding personnel or at least once per week, and submitted to the OWNER.

**B3.2.3** If the OWNER and Pipeline Contractor agree that a test failure was caused by conditions outside the control of the welder, a retest may be permitted.

## B4.0 TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

**B4.1** All welding shall be completed using the manual SMAW process, unless otherwise approved by OWNER.

**B4.2** Mainline production welds shall be completed using a vertical down progression, cellulose-coated electrodes, and DC current with reverse polarity (electrode positive).

**B4.3** All welding consumables shall be supplied by the Pipeline Contractor, and subject to OWNER approval.

**B4.4** The following requirements govern welding electrode selection.

- Electrodes classified with a minimum tensile strength of 550 MPa (80 ksi) or higher shall not be used.
- E6010 electrodes shall be used for root beads.
- E6010 or E7010-G electrodes shall be used for mainline downhill welding of the hot pass, fill and cap. Selected classification shall be consistent with tensile strength requirements.
- Carbon steel low hydrogen electrodes (i.e., E7018-1) shall be used for fill and cap of above ground facility piping.

B5.0 PRODUCTION WELDING

B5.1 Before alignment of each joint, pipe and components shall be visually inspected for dirt, debris, and damage. All dirt and debris shall be removed. Mainline pipe shall be cleaned with a leather or a canvas belt disc of the proper diameter, or by another method acceptable to the OWNER. No dirt or debris will be permitted in the pipeline system. Any damage observed during visual inspection shall be corrected to the satisfaction of the OWNER, before fitting and welding.

B5.2 If the pipe has a longitudinal or spiral seam, the seam of each successive mainline joint shall be offset from the previous joint by at least 20°, such that the seams remain on the top quadrant of the pipe after lowering into the ditch. Longitudinal seams in field bends shall be located on the neutral axis of the bend, so that the longitudinal seam is in the top quadrant after the bend is installed.

B5.3 The line must be adequately and securely supported by proper skids or cribbing to prevent movement or sagging during welding.

B5.4 The pipe shall be supported in a manner which provides a minimum clearance of 400mm (16") between the joint to be welded and the ground.

B5.5 Cutting or welding, other than that required for the making of normal circumferential joints, will not be allowed without prior consent of the OWNER.

B5.6 Electrode holders shall be insulated.

B5.7 All mainline and tie-in welds shall be aligned using internal or external line up clamps.

- When external line-up clamps are used for the mainline, the clamp shall not be removed until at least 50% of the root bead has been completed and evenly spaced about the circumference of the pipe.
- For pipe 219.1 mm (8.625") OD and larger, after the clamp has been removed, the side boom tractor shall not be released for the next pipe until the root bead has been completed.
- When line-up clamps are used for fabrication welding, they may be removed whenever the joint is securely tacked for welding.

B5.8 During mainline construction over rough terrain, the OWNER may require that application of a hot pass equal to 25% of the pipe circumference, on the bottom of the joint, before release of the line-up clamp, and before release of the side boom tractor for the next joint.

B5.9 Field bevels may be prepared by machining, grinding, flame cutting, arc cutting, or any combination thereof. Equipment used for cutting shall be held rigid in a beveling machine. Free hand flame or arc cutting of bevels is not permitted. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.7.6)

- Regardless of the method of preparation, resulting bevels shall be at 90° to the pipe or fitting axis, and the profile of the weld preparation must conform to that specified by the pipe or fitting specification or the qualified welding procedure.
- Where flame or arc cut equipment is used for end preparation, the finished pipe end shall be made smooth and free of all surface irregularities, using power grinders and files, or field milling machines. Surface preparation shall be sufficient to remove all oxidize material from the entire bevel and root face surfaces.
- Where machining equipment is used for end preparation, the finished pipe end shall be free of all burrs, fins, metal hairs, cutting oils, lubricants, and any other contaminants.
- Damaged bevels may be repaired by light grinding or filing. The profile of the pipe end must conform to the requirements of the pipe or fitting specification or the qualified welding procedure after repair. Bevels which can not be repaired by grinding or filing must be re-cut and prepared according to methods specified above. Weld repair of the bevel area is not permitted.

B5.10 At ambient temperatures below 0°C (32°F):

- welding ends shall be heated to at least 93°C (200°F), so that the preheat temperature at the start of welding is at least 65°C (150°F); and
- a dry OWNER approved insulating cover at least 150 mm (6") wide shall be positioned over the center of the weld, securely fastened, and left for a minimum period of two hours, or until the weld has cooled to ambient temperature. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.7.7.2)

B5.12 Work shall be organized so that: (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.7.10, 7.2.7.11, and 7.2.7.12)

- the time interval between root bead finish and hot pass start does not exceed 5 minutes
- the completed root pass does not cool below the preheat or minimum interpass temperature specified on the welding procedure, before starting the hot pass;
- the time interval between hot pass finish and fill pass start does not exceed 30 minutes;
- fill and cap pass welders are not to be more than 600 meters behind the hot pass welders at any time during the workday;
- the maximum number of uncapped mainline welds at the end of the workday does not exceed the number of cap pass welders;
- all passes except the cap pass are completed by the end of each working day; and
- all welds left incomplete at the end of a work day are completed on the following work day.

When welding recommences on an incomplete weld, the weld must be preheated to the temperature specified by the welding procedure.

B5.13 Night caps of material and construction acceptable to the OWNER shall be used to cover:

- open ends of welded pipe sections at the end of each day's work;
- the free end of a pipe section being welded, to prevent movement of air through the pipe; and
- open ends of pipe sections to be tied-in later, as may occur at crossings.

Nightcaps shall not be tack welded to the pipe and shall not be removed until work is ready to resume.

B5.14 For tie-in welding:

- alignment shall be done with an OWNER representative present, to the OWNER's satisfaction; and
- after coated sections are lowered in, the pipe shall be cut in a manner which provides a proper bevel, correct alignment, uniform spacing, and the required amount of slack in the finished line.

External line-up clamps are permitted.

B5.15 The minimum length of pipe which shall be used in the mainline shall be 2 meters. Joints of 2 meters or longer shall be carried forward and welded into the line.

B5.16 Welded sections of pipe shall not be moved for any reason, until all welds have cooled to ambient temperature.

B5.17 Any fire damage to insulation or coating due to preheating, etc., shall be repaired.

**B6.0    WORKMANSHIP, QUALITY CONTROL, NONDESTRUCTIVE EXAMINATION, AND REPAIRS**

B6.1    Requirements governing workmanship, quality control, and nondestructive examination are found in this specification, Owner's Specification 000-PES-5009 NDE Examination and CSA Z662.

B6.2    Arc strikes and arc burns shall be:

- cut out as a cylinder for mainline pipe;
- treated according to CSA Z662 for non-sour above ground pipe and piping components;
- treated according to CSA Z662 if present on the outside surface of sour above ground pipe and piping components; and
- cut out as a cylinder if present on the inside surface of sour pipe and piping components, except that heat treatment of the affected area may be considered as a remedy, at the discretion of the OWNER. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.8)

B6.3    When arc strikes or arc burns are removed by grinding or filing, verification of removal is required in accordance with CSA Z662, and the OWNER may also require verification of minimum wall thickness by ultrasonic methods, and magnetic particle examination of the affected area for evidence of cracks. Cracks are unacceptable regardless of size.

B6.4    Welders shall identify their work by marking their assigned symbol beside each weld on the top quarter of the pipe, with a waterproof crayon or paint stick. Punch marks or die stamps are not permitted. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.7.12)

B6.5    The Pipeline Contractor shall cooperate fully with personnel of the NDE Contractor. Welds, including repair and replacement welds, shall be left uncoated, uncovered and accessible for NDE until verbal confirmation of final acceptability has been received from the OWNER.

B6.6    The OWNER may require cutouts to be tested during the course of construction. If a weld fails the test it will not be considered one of the cutouts. The cost of cutting out and replacing up to the maximum number of cutouts specified in the table below shall be borne by the Pipeline Contractor. The Pipeline Contractor shall supply the testing equipment and prepare the coupons.

B6.7 Repairs shall be completed in accordance with CSA Z662 and the following requirements. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.10):

- Repair of cracks is not permitted. All welds containing cracks shall be cut out and re-welded. (Z662-94 Cl. 7.2.10.5)
- Cap pass defects may be repaired by grinding out the defective area and recapping if necessary, using preheat in accordance with an OWNER accepted repair welding procedure and CSA Z662. The minimum length of weld recap shall be 50 mm.
- Fill and root pass defects may be repaired by excavation of the defect using power grinders, preparation of a suitable groove, preheating in accordance with an OWNER accepted repair welding procedure and CSA Z662, and re-welding. The minimum length of groove and resultant weld repair shall be 50 mm.
- Repairs completed by welding shall be wrapped with insulation and slow cooled.
- No additional repair by welding is permitted for an area previously repaired by welding. Rejected repair welds shall be cut out and replaced.
- When a defective weld has been removed from the pipeline, a single replacement weld shall be used to join the two sections of pipe whenever practical. If it is not practical to pull the pipe ends together for the single replacement weld, then a pup section of nominal length equal to five times the pipe diameter or 2 meters whichever is greater, shall be inserted into the line and welded into place using the same welding procedure as used for the original production welds in the section.
- The Pipeline Contractor shall ensure that repair and replacement welds are examined by the NDE Contractor.

B6.8 The daily repair rate shall be calculated as the number of welds requiring repair divided by the number of joints welded for the day. If the daily repair rate exceeds 10% for sweet service, or 20% for sour service, the Pipeline Contractor shall:

- stop welding;
- take necessary actions to reduce the repair rate; and
- prepare a 20 joint test section to assess the effect of these actions.

After the 20 joint test section has been completed, the Pipeline Contractor shall stop welding, and the test section shall be 100% radiographed. If the test section is acceptable to the OWNER, the Pipeline Contractor may resume welding. If the test section is not acceptable to the OWNER, the Pipeline Contractor shall repeat steps (a) through (c) above, stop welding, and wait until the latest test section has been radiographed and assessed by the OWNER.

## B7.0 SOUR SERVICE REQUIREMENTS

**(Z662-94 CL. 7.1.2, 7.2.5.4.5, AND 7.2.8.2.2)**

B7.1 Each sour service weld procedure qualification record shall include a cross-sectional hardness survey completed using the Rockwell or Vickers test method.

B7.2 For sour service, both nondestructive and destructive testing is required for each performance qualification.

B7.3 Treatment of arc burns is covered under Clause B6.3.
