## SECTION 1: GENERAL

### 1.1 SCOPE

This standard states the requirements for shop and field welded fabrication and inspection for the BP Cherry Point Refinery (referred to as BP CHP). When this standard specifies that a review and/or approval is required, unless otherwise stated the review and/or approval shall be by the BP CHP Inspector or their designated alternate.

## SECTION 2: GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

### 2.1 CODE COMPLIANCE

2.1.1 Welding pressure-containing equipment, including appurtenance welds, shall conform to the latest edition of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel (B&PV) Code, Section IX, Welding and Brazing Qualifications.

2.1.2 Welding storage tanks, including appurtenance welds, shall conform to American Petroleum Institute (API) standard 620 Design and Construction of Large, Welded, Low-Pressure Storage Tanks, Standard 650 Welded Steel Tanks for Oil Storage, or Standard 653 Tank Inspection, Repair, Alteration, and Reconstruction, as applicable.

2.1.3 Structural welding shall conform to the requirements of the latest edition of ANSI/AWS D1.1, Structural Welding Code - Steel.

2.1.4 When there is a conflict between this standard and the applicable equipment design and fabrication code or other BP CHP standards, the more stringent requirement shall govern.

### 2.2 REQUIREMENTS FOR SUBMITTING WPSs AND PQRs

2.2.1 Prior to the start of welding, Weld Procedure Specifications (WPS) and Procedure Qualification Records (PQR) shall be submitted to BP CHP for review and approval, for all welding covered by this standard by both fabricators and their subcontractors. Welds not made in accordance with the approved WPSs are subject to rejection. Changes to the welding procedures must also be approved by BP CHP prior to their implementation.

2.2.2 The maximum interpass temperature shall be specified in the WPS and PQR for austenitic stainless steels, non-ferrous alloys, and for carbon and low-alloy steels when impact testing is required.

2.2.3 PQRs for the following cases shall include hardness testing:

a) When the material being welded is hardenable, such as Cr-Mo steels including but not limited to 1.25Cr, 2.25Cr, 5Cr, 9Cr, and 12Cr, which typically require postweld heat treatment (PWHT).

---

b) When procedure qualification hardness tests are otherwise required by BP CHP engineering standards or job specifications.

2.2.4 When hardness testing for procedure qualification is required, testing shall be in accordance with NACE MR0103 Appendix C or similar industry standard, and shall include hardness testing of the weld metal, heat affected zone, and adjacent base metal. Hardness surveys shall be performed using the Vickers Hardness test method with a 10 kgf load per ASTM E384, or BP CHP approved alternative method. Hardness testing per ASTM E92, which has been superseded by ASTM E384, is acceptable for PQRs completed prior to the July 2010 withdrawal date of ASTM E92. Maximum hardness for PQR testing of Cr-Mo steels shall be 248 HV 10 or equivalent. Maximum hardness for PQR testing of other materials shall be as stated in the engineering standards or job specifications. Hardness test results shall be submitted with the PQRs.

2.2.5 Hardness testing for PQRs shall be done after PWHT when applicable. Specimens used for PQR hardness testing shall be given the same PWHT as the production welds will receive.

## 2.3 WELDER QUALIFICATION REQUIREMENTS

Welder Performance Qualification (WPQ) records shall be made available upon request for review by the BP CHP assigned inspector or their designated alternate.

## 2.4 PIPING SOCKET WELDS

2.4.1 A minimum of two weld passes are required for socket welds in piping.

2.4.2 For restrictions on use of socket welds for small trim piping at reciprocating compressors, see BP CHP STP 200 section 4.1.5.

## 2.5 NEUTRALIZATION FOR AKALINE SERVICES

2.5.1 Metal that has been exposed to alkaline services, such as caustic, catacarb, or amine, shall be neutralized prior to welding. The metal shall be thoroughly rinsed with water and then neutralized with ample amounts of undiluted vinegar. Allow the solution to react for approximately 5 minutes and then rinse again with water. Verify neutralization using litmus paper and repeat if necessary. After the metal is neutralized, dry the area and proceed with welding.

## SECTION 3: WELDING PROCESSES (GENERAL)

## 3.1 PERMITTED PROCESSES

3.1.1 The following welding processes are permitted:

a) Manual shielded metal arc with covered electrode (SMAW)

b) Machine submerged arc (SAW)

---

c) Gas metal arc using solid wire (GMAW)

d) Gas tungsten arc, manual or automatic (GTAW)

e) Flux-cored arc (FCAW)

f) Electroslag welding (ESW) for alloy overlay

3.1.2 Welding processes not listed above require specific approval by BP CHP. The seller should not assume that alternative processes are acceptable during bid preparation.

3.1.3 Welding procedures shall show the electrical parameters (amperage, voltage, travel speed, heat input, etc) when applicable within reasonable ranges for each electrode/wire diameter to be used.

3.1.4 All weld repairs to base materials, including repairs of wrought, cast and forged products used in fabrication shall be done using a step-by-step weld repair procedure, and shall contain the following minimum information:

a) Nondestructive examination (NDE) method used to discover the defect and its boundaries

b) Proposed method for removing the defect and type of NDE used to ensure complete removal

c) Approved WPS/PQR proposed for making the repair weld and NDE method to be used to inspect the weld

d) Complete description of filler metals added, any cold or hot forming, PWHT requirements, and any other essential elements of the weld repair

## 3.2 WELDING PROCESS LIMITATIONS

3.2.1 SMAW of carbon and low alloy steels shall be done using low hydrogen electrodes, except for the following specific alternative. Root passes of butt welds in piping may be made with EXX10 type electrodes.

3.2.2 SAW

3.2.2.1 The SAW process shall be limited to the machine welding process. The WPS shall clearly indicate that the procedure is machine controlled. SAW semi-automatic (hand-held) welding is not allowed. SAW shall only be used on joints in the flat 1G and horizontal 2G positions. The 2G position welding is typically used for circumferential shell welds on storage tanks.

3.2.2.2 SAW shall be limited to ⅜" maximum thickness per layer of deposit for material of 1¼" thickness or greater, and ½" maximum thickness per layer of deposit for material less than 1¼" thick.

---

3.2.2.3   SAW fluxes shall be limited as follows:

a) Submerged arc fluxes shall be neutral. "Active" fluxes or fluxes that contribute alloying elements to the weld shall not be used.

b) The submerged arc flux/filler combinations classified as FX (A) (P) Z - XXXX are prohibited.

c) The use of Lincoln-780 flux is prohibited.

d) All recycled fluxes shall be reconditioned prior to re-use. Recrushed slag is not allowed.

e) Fluxes recommended by the flux manufacturers for single pass welds shall not be used on multipass welds.

f) SAW fluxes designated for non-PWHT applications shall not be used for PWHT applications.

3.2.3   GMAW

3.2.3.1   For GMAW with solid wire, the type of metal transfer (globular, spray, pulsed, short circuiting) shall be stated on the WPS under electrical characteristics.

3.2.3.2   GMAW using solid wire and a globular, spray, or pulsed transfer mode is acceptable only for the following cases:

a) Root passes on all P-number group materials

b) Fill-out and completion of welds on all P-number group materials, regardless of wall thickness, except where prohibited by the job specification or other BP CHP engineering standards

c) Weld overlay only if a cross-section showing satisfactory dilution control is submitted for BP CHP review and acceptance, or if the results are indicated on the PQR for BP CHP review and acceptance.

3.2.3.3   GMAW with solid wire in a short circuiting mode is acceptable only for the following cases:

a) Root passes of single sided welds in piping and pressure vessels on all P-number group materials.

b) Fill-out and completion of butt welds in piping not exceeding ⅜-inch nominal thickness. Fill-out and completion may not be made in the downhill direction. Requirements for additional inspection are given in Section 7 of this standard.

---

c) Nonstructural and non-pressure containing attachments not exceeding ¼-inch nominal thickness, such as insulation clips and nameplates.

d) Tack welds, temporary attachments, double welded joints or other applications where the weld metal is subsequently removed.

3.2.3.4 Short circuiting GMAW shall not be used for integrally reinforced branch connections (such as pipets and weldolets), socket welds, and other attachments.

3.2.3.5 Field welding with GMAW is not acceptable unless approved by BP CHP.

3.2.3.6 For the pulsed GMAW process, the welding shall be performed with the same make and model of welding equipment using the same program settings as those used in the procedure qualification. These variables shall be considered essential and any change will require procedure requalification.

3.2.3.7 GMAW shall not be used for welding NPS 4 and smaller piping unless approved by BP CHP.

3.2.4 GTAW

3.2.4.1 GTAW with an inert gas back purge shall be utilized for the first two weld passes, root pass and hot pass, of alloy and stainless steel pipe fabrication, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP.

3.2.4.2 GTAW with an inert gas back purge shall be utilized for non-ferrous alloys for groove joints accessible from only one side.

3.2.4.3 Manual GTAW welding shall include the addition of filler metal, and except for carbon steel welding shall also include use of high frequency starting equipment or equal.

3.2.5 FCAW

3.2.5.1 For FCAW, the type of metal transfer (globular, spray, pulsed) shall be stated on the WPS, under electrical characteristics.

3.2.5.2 FCAW with an external shielding gas in a globular, spray, or pulsed transfer mode is acceptable for the following cases:

a) Completion of pressure-retaining butt welds on P-1, P-3, P-4, P-5A, P-5B, and P-8 materials. Permissible electrodes are ASME SFA-5.20 EXXT-1 and EXXT-5 for carbon steels, ASME SFA-5.29 EXXT1-X and EXXT5-X for P-3, P-4, P-5A and P-5B steels, and ASME SFA-5.22 (with chemistry matching base

---

metal) for P-8 stainless steels. The electrodes for the low alloy steels shall have the same alloy content as the metal being joined.

b) The first pass of butt welds of P-1, P-3, P-4, and P-5A (those with less than or equal to 2.25% Cr) materials when welded from both sides, provided the first pass is completely removed by back-gouging prior to starting welding from the second side.

3.2.5.3 FCAW with self-shielding wire in a globular or spray transfer mode is acceptable for the following cases:

a) Fillet welds of P-1 materials only for non-pressure-retaining parts that are limited to ¾" maximum leg length and are not subject to impact requirements.

b) Structural steel welds.

3.2.5.4 FCAW in a short circuiting transfer mode is prohibited.

3.2.5.5 FCAW is not acceptable for root passes when welding from one side only.

3.2.5.6 FCAW welding consumables shall be limited to the AWS classification used in the procedure qualification.

3.2.5.7 The diffusible hydrogen limit for FCAW electrodes (as manufactured) shall be capable of meeting the following:

3.2.5.8 FCAW shall not be used for welding NPS 4 and smaller piping unless approved by BP CHP.

3.2.6 ESW

3.2.6.1 The Electroslag Welding Process (ESW) may be used only for vessel weld overlay, and each application requires prior approval by BP CHP.

3.2.6.2 The manufacturer's flux trade name and flux/wire combination used in fabrication shall be listed on both the WPS and PQR, and shall be identical to the flux/wire combination used in the qualification of the PQR.

---

3.2.6.3    For each batch of electroslag flux, a weld test shall be conducted in accordance with the approved weld overlay procedure to ensure that the resultant overlay deposit complies with the specified microstructure and chemical analysis.

3.2.6.4    ESW overlay strip widths of 3.5" (90mm) and smaller are acceptable. Strip widths exceeding 3.5" (90mm) may be proposed by the fabricator for BP CHP consideration and approval. The fabricator will be expected to verify their capability to apply the wider overlay strips.

3.2.6.5    The weld overlay shall be applied so that the weld beads run circumferentially around the vessel. The surface contour shall be relatively smooth. Waviness is permissible, but without notches or undercuts which may act as stress raisers.

3.2.6.6    The dilution area shall be a minimum of 10%. Dilution area is defined as the area of weld penetration into the base metal divided by the total weld area, as measured in a cross-section of the weld. The dilution area shall be included on the PQR.

3.2.6.7    A magnetic steering device is required when ESW overlay strip widths are 2.375" (60mm) or greater.

3.2.6.8    For ESW overlay, the electrode voltage on the WPS and PQR is expected to be in the range of 24 to 28 volts to avoid arc instability or excess spatter. Voltage limits outside of this recommended range shall be explained by the fabricator.

3.2.6.9    Weld bead overlap of adjacent weld bead deposits shall be 3/16" minimum and 3/8" maximum.

## SECTION 4: FILLER METAL, WELDING TECHNIQUE, AND JOINT PREPARATION

### 4.1 FILLER METAL

4.1.1    Filler metals and fluxes shall conform to ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section II Materials, Part C Specifications for Welding Rods, Electrodes and Filler Metals. Carbon steel and low alloy steel electrodes per ASME SFA-5.1 and SFA-5.5, with AWS Classifications EXX 12, EXX 13, EXX 14, EXX 20, EXX 24, EXX 27, and EXX 28 are not acceptable for welding pressure parts. Use of these fillers for attachment of non-pressure parts to pressure parts requires approval by BP CHP.

4.1.2    Filler metals and fluxes shall be used only for applications, positions, and electrical characteristics as recommended by the manufacturer.

4.1.3    When welding a non-pressure-retaining part to a pressure-retaining part where the two parts are ferritic steels of different alloy content, the deposited weld metal shall match the nominal composition of the pressure-retaining part.

---

4.1.4 Procedures using any consumables with a "G" classification shall be restricted to the brand and type of electrode used for the PQR. The nominal chemical composition of the specified brand and type of consumable should be identified on the WPS.

## 4.2 WELDING TECHNIQUE AND RESTRICTIONS

4.2.1 Welding without filler metal (autogenous welding) shall not be used unless approved by BP CHP.

4.2.2 Slugging of weld joints with supplementary filler metal is not permitted, unless approved by BP CHP for specific applications.

4.2.3 Approval is required from BP CHP before buttering beveled edges of joints to achieve proper root opening. If approved, the following additional requirements shall apply.

- 4.2.3.1 When buttering the edges of joints, use low hydrogen electrodes or welding processes with electrodes of the same nominal composition as the base material.

- 4.2.3.2 When buttering, all requirements for preheat, interpass temperature, and PWHT shall be observed

4.2.4 Removable start-up and run-off tabs may be used for longitudinal welds. Tab materials shall be of the same nominal composition as the base metal.

4.2.5 Temporary attachments, such as dogs, shall be removed by flame cutting, arc gouging or grinding. Hammering shall not be permitted. The former attachment areas shall be ground smooth and magnetic particle (MT) or liquid penetrant (PT) examined. Defects such as gouges, cracks, or undercut shall be repaired.

4.2.6 Starts and stops in root pass welds shall be blended before subsequent passes are welded.

4.2.7 Peening of welds is prohibited.

4.2.8 Tack welds that are incorporated into the final weld shall be subject to the same requirements as the applicable welding procedure.

4.2.9 Back-purging is required for GTAW and GMAW processes for welding low alloy materials, unless the joint is ground or backgouged to sound metal. Back-purging or root shielding, when required, shall be maintained through the completion of at least two weld layers.

4.2.10 Block welding is prohibited. Block welding refers to the technique of either completely or partially filling separated lengths of a weld seam before intervening lengths are deposited.

---

4.2.11 Vertical welding in the downhill direction is acceptable only if approved by BP CHP.

4.2.12 Welding on wet surfaces is not allowed.

## 4.3 JOINT PREPARATION

4.3.1 Weld joints shall be prepared by machining, grinding, or thermal cutting. If thermal cutting is performed, then the joint surfaces shall be ground to sound metal prior to welding. Preheat requirements for thermal cutting shall be the same as those required on the applicable WPS.

4.3.2 If poorly fitted joints with excessive gap or offset occur, the seller shall submit for BP CHP approval a proposed procedure to bring the joint back to design requirements.

4.3.3 Permanent weld backing shall not be used unless approved by BP CHP.

4.3.4 Temporary or removable backing shall not be used without BP CHP approval. If approved, temporary backing shall meet the following requirements:

a) Temporary backing material shall conform to the nominal chemistry of the weld filler metal as defined in Section 4.1 of this standard.

b) Temporary backing shall be completely removed and the underlying weld area dressed and examined for cracks and defects using visual and dye penetrant or magnetic particle examination as applicable. Unacceptable indications shall be removed and repaired.

4.3.5 Consumable inserts are acceptable when approved by BP CHP. If approved, consumable inserts shall meet the following requirements:

a) The consumable insert shall be completely melted and fused into the weld bead.

b) The consumable insert material shall conform to the nominal chemistry of the weld filler metal as defined in Section 4.1 of this standard.

## SECTION 5: GENERAL PREHEAT

## 5.1 REQUIREMENTS

5.1.1 Preheat shall be required when the ambient temperature is less than 50°F. When required, the minimum preheat shall be 100°F.

5.1.2 The preheat temperature shall not be less than the preheat temperature shown on the applicable WPS, unless otherwise approved by BP CHP.

5.1.3 See paragraph 4.3.1 for preheat requirements for thermal cutting.

---

5.1.4 When PWHT is required, the preheat temperature shall be maintained on the part being welded until intermediate heat treatment or final PWHT is completed, unless otherwise specified or approved by BP CHP. For P-5B group materials, do not go directly from preheat to PWHT, but follow the hydrogen bake-out requirements in Section 9. For other low alloy materials, see Section 9 for preferred alternatives.

## SECTION 6: GENERAL PWHT

### 6.1 PROCEDURAL GUIDELINES

6.1.1 The applicable BP CHP engineering standard or the job specification may require PWHT for equipment due to service even when not mandated by the applicable design and fabrication code. For such welds, a WPS qualified with PWHT is required.

6.1.2 Holding temperature range (maximum and minimum) and holding time at temperature shall be stated on the WPS.

6.1.3 PWHT procedures shall describe cleaning requirements, heating and cooling rates, type of heating, thermocouple locations and necessary equipment, and shall be submitted to BP CHP for approval.

6.1.4 For local PWHT of material thicker than 2", thermocouples shall be attached to both sides of the joint in three locations around the circumference, to verify that the minimum required temperature is attained.

6.1.5 PWHT shall be performed after all welding and forming operations are completed, including non-pressure attachment welds. Equipment and piping shall not be subjected to welding, hammering, pressing, or forming after PWHT.

### 6.2 METHODS

6.2.1 Acceptable methods for postweld heat treatment are:

- a) Furnace heating
- b) Resistance heating
- c) Induction heating
- d) Radiant heating
- e) Internal firing with thermocouples attached to the non-fired side of the affected walls.

6.2.2 Methods not acceptable for postweld heat treatment are:

- a) Direct impingement furnace burners or torch flames

---

b) Exothermic methods

## SECTION 7: GENERAL INSPECTION AND TESTING

### 7.1 CODES

7.1.1 The minimum nondestructive examination (NDE) requirements shall be in accordance with the applicable design and fabrication codes. Additional NDE may be required by BP CHP as specified in this standard, the purchasing specifications, and all other applicable BP CHP Standards.

7.1.2 Unless specified otherwise, NDE methods shall be in accordance with the following applicable appendices and sections of Section VIII, Division 1, Pressure Vessels, of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC) and Addenda. Magnetic Particle Examination (MT) shall be per Appendix 6. Liquid Penetrant Examination (PT) shall be per Appendix 8. Ultrasonic Examination (UT) of welds shall be per Appendix 12. Radiographic Examination (RT) shall be per UW-51 and UW-52. UT of materials shall be per ASME BPVC Section V Article 23.

### 7.2 CERTIFICATION

NDE personnel shall be certified in accordance with ASNT SNT-TC-1A or personnel qualification procedures that have been approved by BP CHP. Certifications of NDE personnel shall be available for review by the BP CHP inspector or representative.

### 7.3 RECORDS

7.3.1 Records of nondestructive examinations, testing, and inspection shall be provided to BP CHP.

7.4.4 Production hardness testing is required for all vessel and piping pressure boundary welds in P-3, P-4, P-5A, P-5B, P-6, and P-7 materials unless otherwise specified by BP CHP.

a) Tests shall be performed on at least 10% of furnace heat treated welds and 100% of locally heat treated welds.

b) When dissimilar metals are joined by welding, the hardness limits specified for the base and welding materials in B31.3 shall be met for each material.

7.4.7 When production hardness testing is required by this document or any other applicable specification, code, or standard, the testing shall be performed as follows:

a) Hardness tests shall be performed with Brinell hardness equipment in accordance with ASTM E10, or comparison hardness testers in accordance with ASTM A833. (Hardness test methods for weld procedure qualification shall be per paragraph 2.2.4.)

---

b) All hardness tests shall be performed at ambient temperature.

c) For welds that require PWHT, hardness tests shall be performed after completion of the PWHT.

d) Pressure containing welds on vessels and piping shall have a minimum of one hardness test performed for each welding process employed and for each welder or welding operator.

e) Pressure vessel hardness tests shall be performed on both the process and external sides of the weld where possible.

f) Pressure vessel hardness tests shall be performed on each 10 feet of weld in each seam (minimum of one test per seam), at all weld seam intersections, at each nozzle-to-shell weld, and at each nozzle-to-flange weld.

g) For each pressure vessel test location, one hardness determination shall be made at the center of the weld.

h) Piping hardness tests shall be performed on the outside of pressure-retaining welds.

i) For each piping spool piece, a minimum of one weld shall be hardness tested for each welder and welding procedure used.

j) For each piping test location, three hardness determinations shall be made; one at the center of the weld, and one on each side of the weld in the heat affected zones as close as practicable to the edge of the weld.

7.4.8 If any single weld hardness test result exceeds the specified maximum value, notify BP CHP immediately for consideration of retest.

7.4.9 Production welds that are rejected for hardness shall be removed and re-welded, or have PWHT per the Code, to achieve specified hardness test results. ## APPENDIX A: COMMENTARY ON BAKE-OUT FOR PRIOR SERVICE 2.25Cr EQUIPMENT

The rationale behind the guidelines in Section 9.2 is described below:

**The primary reason** to bake-out 2.25Cr before welding is to prevent hydrogen embrittlement (HE) cracking. When welding directly onto the 2.25Cr, or onto overlay that is not thick enough to prevent forming an HAZ in the 2.25Cr, a substantial risk of cracking can occur in the HAZ (and in the weld metal if 2.25Cr) if the pre-heat is lost and if the metal has not been baked out. The combination of: (1) sufficient hydrogen remaining in the metal; (2) a hard HAZ and hard weld metal; (3) residual stress from welding; and (4) temperature in the HE cracking range (below about 200°F)– provides all the ingredients for HE cracking of the HAZ and 2.25Cr weld metal. Weld metal cracking is probably a higher risk than HAZ, but both are concerns. Removing one of these ingredients (the hydrogen) will likely prevent the cracking.

**An additional concern** with high hydrogen content is the porosity in welds due to outgassing into the weld pool. Welders sometimes talk about the weld pool "fizzing" where the base metal has a high hydrogen content. This is normally more of a problem with chemically charged hydrogen (wet H₂S environments), as the concentrations are so much higher in those cases than in high temperature hydrogen charging. However, since it is required to bake-out the 2.25Cr for the reasons stated in the previous paragraph, this concern is also eliminated.

**A final, more theoretical than practical, consideration** is that even if a HAZ is not formed in the 2.25Cr (i.e., if welding onto sufficiently thick SS overlay), an area could exist of high welding residual stress in the 2.25Cr just below the overlay, and could conceivably create the combination of stress, hydrogen, and temperature that could support HE cracking. But given that 2.25Cr in the tempered condition is not highly susceptible to cracking, and that there should not be any pre-existing crack in the area of concern for conditions causing more growth (i.e., any HE embrittlement cracking would require both crack initiation and growth, not just crack growth), the risk of HE cracking is low.

This principle applies whether dealing with an older generation, temper embrittled 2.25Cr, or a newer generation, non-embrittled version. To be ultra-conservative, a 600°F preheat could be maintained, but this is often more difficult to achieve the desired low interpass temperature for the SS overlay. Consideration should be given to the UT inspection of the overlay in the area to be welded, establishing there is no cracking at the interface or just below the overlay in the base metal.

When the situation involves welding on stainless steel cladding, bake-out is not required because the hydrogen is not removed from the cladding. This further argues against baking based on the theoretical concern noted in the preceding paragraphs. The concentration of hydrogen in the cladding will be much higher than in the base metal, perhaps an order of magnitude higher.

For example, a typical reactor will have about 3 ppm hydrogen in the 2.25Cr at the interface. The SS cladding will have 40 to 50 ppm. The effective removal of the

---

hydrogen from the cladding is not possible by bake-out because the hydrogen solubility is so high and its diffusivity is so low. During outgassing, the hydrogen at the interface in the Cr-Mo will decrease somewhat, but the SS hydrogen level stays at nearly 40 to 50 ppm, providing essentially no benefit.

Also, the diffusivity of hydrogen in a 300-series SS is much slower than in ferritic steels, potentially two orders of magnitude depending on the data set. The slower diffusivity explains why clad steels have a lower effective hydrogen partial pressure than bare steels in high pressure hydrogen services, and it also makes outgassing mostly ineffective for reactor cooldowns. It is hardly possible to outgas the stainless steel in a reasonable amount of time (100 hours could be required instead of 1 hour). The hydrogen in the cladding can simply replenish any hydrogen baked out of the base metal just below the cladding, which is the area of concern. Therefore, an effective bake-out is not practical, and a practical bake-out is ineffective.
