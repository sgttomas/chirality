## 1.0 PURPOSE

This procedure is developed and issued for the purpose of ensuring uniform application of Closure Welds.

### 1.1 Scope

This procedure defines requirements for the use of closure welds as an alternative to hydrostatic leak testing<sup>1</sup> to support modularization and field piping installation. Closure welds shall be in accordance with ASME B31.3 Para. 345.2.3(c) and in compliance with the Safety Codes Act and supporting regulations in the province of Alberta pertaining to pressure piping as defined in "The Act". The implementation and application of this procedure requires review by the Alberta Boilers Safety Association (ABSA) during the piping registration phase. It is intended for use by Voyageur constructors for new construction only, and its use is not permitted on tie-ins to previously commissioned piping systems. It describes the responsibilities of individuals and organizations, supplementary technical requirements pertaining to the application of closure welds and restrictions thereof, and basic procedures and record keeping practices necessary to demonstrate compliance with In-Process Examination and other requirements of ASME B31.3 and the engineering design.

The use of this procedure is restricted as described within. It shall be noted that every effort to avoid closure welds by completing the required code testing is preferred.

### 1.2 Definitions

ABSA – Alberta Boilers Safety Association

ASME – American Society of Mechanical Engineers

CGSB – Canadian General Standards Board

"The Act" – Alberta Safety Codes Act

Closure Weld – Per ASME B31.3 a closure weld is the final weld connecting piping systems, sub-assemblies or components that have been successfully leak tested in accordance with the requirements of ASME B31.3 345.2.3 (c). For the purpose of project field construction on a Suncor site, a "Suncor Closure Weld" will meet the requirements outlined in this document and meet the eligibility requirements as stated within.

Suncor Closure Weld – is defined as a field weld which has been approved through use of this procedure to receive in-process examination and NDE in lieu of hydrostatic testing of the entire system. This is intended for supporting modularization on Suncor Voyageur sites and does not imply the absence of hydrostatic testing on either side of the weld that will become the closure weld. Piping assemblies to be joined in the field shall, where possible, be hydrostatically tested prior to arriving at location and the subsequent interconnecting location will be the Suncor Closure Weld.

A Suncor Closure weld indicated as "SCW" within this procedure shall be restricted to:

a. Systems designed to ASME B31.3

b. P1 to P1/ S1 to S1 / P1- S1 (carbon steel) material

c. Piping systems that are not subject to Post Weld Heat Treatment (PWHT)

d. Systems that are in normal service or in Category 'D' as defined in ASME B31.3

**NOTE:** Category 'D' fluid service systems as defined in ASME B31.3 are considered for this procedure as they can be service tested per code. This shall be approved and documented by the Responsible Engineer. However, for quality and simplicity reasons, Category 'D' service lines are included in this procedure and an appropriate level of in-process examination will be elected.

e. The joining of piping systems installed onto module assemblies that have received hydrostatic testing prior to installation to meet the intent of the design and the code. This is defined as piping sections that exist as part of two modular assemblies that need to be connected, and the connection of expansion loop between piping modules.

**NOTE:** This procedure shall not be applicable to any of the following circumstances:

- Where branch piping from a modular assembly leaves the confines of the modular envelope to join with piping to or from another installation outside of the module envelope. In this case the design shall consider means to do localized or system hydro static tests by applying flanges or other means of isolation.

- Where flanges are installed, localized hydro static testing is required.

Module Envelope – The area considered as part of the module assembly, including piping that enters and leaves a module to join another module. See exclusion Note above.

## 1.3 Responsibilities

This procedure assigns responsibilities to organizations and persons defined in Section 5.0, for work required during different phases of piping system design and construction.

The Engineering Contractor, in consultation with the Voyageur EOR, shall determine if closure welds are a permissible alternative to hydrostatic leak testing for piping systems under consideration, and shall provide the necessary safety and technical justifications for use of closure welds if requested.

When closure welds are approved by the Voyageur EOR, the Engineering Contractor shall indicate same on documents submitted to ABSA for design registration, and provide ABSA with a copy of this procedure. These actions are necessary because Alberta Regulation 49/2006 requires hydrostatic leak testing of piping systems, so alternatives to hydrostatic testing must be accepted by ABSA.

When it is agreed and/or specified to the use of a closure weld as an alternative to hydrostatic leak testing, the Construction Contractor shall be responsible for all work activities including completion of the welds, In-Process Examinations, Non-destructive Examinations, and shall provide a Procedure or Work Instruction that has been developed according to the requirements of this procedure.

Where the requirements of this procedure are considered insufficient for the work or conflicting with practices of the Construction Contractor, the Construction Contractor shall prepare supplementary forms or procedures and submit these to the Voyageur EOR for acceptance. All supplementary requirements shall satisfy ASME B31.3 and ABSA requirements.

## 5.0 IMPLEMENTATION

### 5.1 Closure Weld Technical Requirements

All welding and related issues shall comply with the requirements of Suncor Standard 0903 as applicable, including but not limited to: general welding requirements, welding procedures, welder performance qualification, welding process and filler metal limitations, fabrication & fit-up, welding, preheat and interpass, and inspection & workmanship

Pipe ends for closure welds shall be cut square, at 90 degrees to the pipe or component central axis with bevel angles as per the WPS and indicated on the Traveler. Maximum deviation from square at any location shall be 0.0591 in (1.5 mm), measured as the gap created when a carpenter's square or equivalent is placed across the face of the cut end.

End preparations for groove welds shall be prepared according to ASME B31.3, and suitable for full penetration. If alternative end preparations are required for welding processes or procedures proposed by the Construction Contractor, these ends shall be approved by the Owner and prepared by the Construction Contractor.

Low hydrogen electrodes shall be used and maintained in accordance with recommendations of the electrode manufacturer.

d. Verify the physical location of the closure weld joints and legibly mark the Closure Weld Number on the closure weld joint, using an indelible marker that is non-damaging to the piping material. Address any safety issues for the location and confirm adequacy of welder access to the joint.

e. Obtain a Welding Procedure that is qualified for the applicable piping materials, registered by ABSA, approved by the Owner (when required), and suitable for the joint.

f. Initiate a Closure Weld Traveler Form (see Appendix A for example), to record the progress of closure weld completion. Indicate any witness points, hold points, or supplemental requirements.

During execution of welding and examination work on a closure weld, the In-Process Examiner shall be present to verify that:

a. Piping material specifications, grades, and dimensions are as specified by the design.

b. Joint alignment methods do not introduce unacceptable stresses.

c. Access is adequate for welding, in process examination and testing work.

d. Joint dimensions, fit-up, cleanliness, and surface quality conforms to requirements of the Welding Procedure Specification and the engineering design. This requires visual examination and actual measurements as necessary to satisfy the In-Process Examiner.

e. Welding Procedure has been reviewed and understood by welders.

f. Welder qualifications are suitable for the joint and welding procedure (record names, PQ card information, and symbols).

g. Welding equipment and any other necessary equipment is in good condition, calibrated per the requirements of the contractors QC manual and suited to the work.

A weld repair is considered such after radiography or ultrasonic testing of the completed weld.

When weld repair of a closure weld is required, the Contractor shall notify Voyageur QA and log the welder as having a repair including the type and physical location in a closure weld joint log.

Constant monitoring of welder proficiency is the responsibility of the In Process Examiner and should the welder log more than three repairs in five subsequent welds; the IPE shall report the findings to the Construction Contractor and Voyageur QA. Voyageur QA has the right to remove any Welder that is producing questionable quality in closure welds.

When a weld repair is detected, the NDE Technician shall notify the IPE immediately and provide the report documenting the repair location. The IPE shall mark the repair area under the supervision of the NDE Technician.

a. Location of defects shall be confirmed by the Welding / Fabrication Supervisor prior to excavation.

b. The IPE shall confirm visually during the excavation to ensure removal of defects, application of pre-heat and repair as required by this procedure completing in-process examination during the repair phase and recording it on an additional Traveler.

c. MT of the excavation to ensure removal of defects can be utilized at the discretion of the IPE.

d. Upon completion of the repair and once the IPE confirms visual examination is acceptable, the same NDE, either radiography or ultrasonic examination shall be completed on the repair area to confirm acceptance. Repair of repairs will not be permitted. For example, if a repair is completed in a specific location and a second repair at that location is required, the entire weld will be subject to cut out at the discretion of Voyageur QA and the Responsible Engineer.
