## 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1. This standard, and its appendices, establishes the welding, heat treatment and inspection requirements for shop and field fabrication, erection, alteration and repair of pressure vessels, piping systems, boilers, heaters, heat transfer equipment, storage tanks, and pumps addressed in the codes and standards listed in below section 3. This standard shall apply to all Suncor facilities. This standard excludes pipelines which are covered by CSA Z662, ASME B31.11, B31.4 and B31.8, and excludes structural steel welding which is covered in Canada by CSA W59 and W47.1 and in the United States by AWS D1.1.

## 4. DEFINITIONS AND ACRONYMS

### 4.1. Definitions

The following definitions apply to this standard:

- The **"Owner"** is defined as Suncor Energy Inc.
- The **"Buyer"** is defined as Suncor Energy Inc., or its designated agent.
- "Sulphur Environments" are defined as hydrocarbon streams containing sulphur compounds (polysulphides, hydrogen sulphide, mercaptans, aliphatic sulphides and disulphides) at temperatures greater than 450°F (232°C).
- "Hydrogen Service" is defined as streams containing greater than 75 psia design partial pressure of hydrogen at design temperatures greater than 400°F (204°C).
- "Sour Service" (aka wet H2S service) is defined as those services meeting the requirements of either NACE MR0103-2005, paragraph 1.3.5.1, or NACE MR0175/ISO 15156, Part 1, paragraph 3.19, as applicable.
- "Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) Services" are defined as either sour service amines (DEA, MDEA DGA), sodium hydroxide (caustic) and catacaite (potassium carbonate solution).
- "Low Alloys" are defined as standard grades of chrome-molys, up to 9 Cr (C-½ Mo, 1Cr-½ Mo, 1 ¼ Cr-½ Mo, 2 ¼ Cr-1Mo, 5Cr-1Mo, 9Cr-1Mo). Vanadium-modified grades are not included.

## 5. STANDARD

### 5.1. General Welding Requirements

5.1.1. During welding, all welds shall be protected from weather conditions that may affect weld quality.

5.1.2. All surfaces to be welded shall be dry, and free of mill scale, oil, grease, dirt, paint and similar "contaminants". Welding of Austenitic stainless steels or Nickel alloys to components which have been galvanised, or painted with "zinc-rich" paints is prohibited even if these coatings have been removed.

5.1.3. Flame cutting shall not be permitted on alloys of 9 chromium or higher. When flame or plasma cutting is used on carbon steel with a thickness greater than 1" (25 mm) or on any thickness of 1 chromium to 12 chromium alloys, the weld bevel preparation shall be ground back to bright metal and shall be followed by Magnetic Particle Testing (MT).

5.1.4. Unless otherwise stated in this standard, backing strips (either temporary or permanent) shall not be used for new fabrication. For field maintenance, backing strips are allowed with prior approval from Owner's Engineer. The risks associated with the use of backing strips shall be evaluated and shall include as a minimum:

- Crevice corrosion
- Stress corrosion cracking
- Root cracking
- Pigging applications
- Foreign object damage (e.g backing strips coming loose)
- Weld root quality

5.1.5. All pressure retaining joints, including socket welds, shall have a minimum of two passes; stop-starts on shall be staggered.

5.1.6. Peening shall not be used. The use of pneumatic tools for the purposes of slag removal is not considered peening.

5.1.7. "Vertical" welding shall be in the "vertical-up" direction only, with the exception of Gas Metal Arc Welding – Modified Wave Short Circuit (GMAW-MWSC) which can be used for root passes in the "vertical-down" direction. For field maintenance weld overlays, welding in the vertical down direction shall be subject to prior approval from Owner's Engineer. The risks associated with the use of welding in the vertical down direction shall be evaluated and shall include as a minimum:

- Lack of fusion
- Lack of penetration
- Slag inclusions

## 5.2. Welding Procedures

5.2.1. Applicable bidders' welding procedures and procedure qualifications records shall be submitted with each bid, for bid evaluation. After award, applicable vendor welding procedures and procedure qualifications records shall be submitted for review and approval by Owner's Engineer or designate. Welding shall not start until the welding procedures have been approved.

5.2.2. All welding shall be performed in accordance with a Welding Procedure Specification (WPS) that has been qualified in accordance with ASME Section IX and the requirements of the Code or Specification under which the weldment is to be fabricated. All Canadian welding procedure specifications shall have Canadian provincial regulatory authority approval prior to commencement of welding. An exception to this is the welding of rotating elements in pumps and rotating equipment where compliance with AWS D14.6 is required.

5.2.3. WPS's shall describe all of the essential, nonessential, and when required, supplementary essential variables for each welding process used. It shall also address additional requirements not covered under ASME Sec. IX, when specified.

5.2.4. All PQR's submitted for Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) Services shall have cross sectional hardness testing performed. 5.2.7. For new fabrication, welded tube-to-tubesheet joints are to be exposed to Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) Services and are not to be PWHT'd; a mock-up of the joint shall be produced in accordance with the following:

5.2.7.1. The mock-up shall supplement the welding qualification requirements of ASME Section IX.

a. It shall contain 2 rows of 5 tube-to-tubesheet welds, and shall be sectioned through all 10 welds.

b. Each weld shall be uniquely identified on the face of the tubesheet.

c. The sections shall be polished and suitably etched to reveal weld bead placement.

5.2.7.2. Close simulation of the production welds is required for the mock-up and shall include the following:

a. The maximum production thicknesses of both tubes and tubesheets in the mock-up.

b. The same production materials (standard and grade) in the mock-up.

c. The maximum carbon equivalents allowed in production shall be the carbon equivalents of the materials used in the mock-up.

d. The same production weld preparation and joint configuration in the mock-up.

e. The same access restrictions to the weld joint as will be seen during production.

f. The minimum preheat allowed in production.

g. The minimum welding heat input allowed in production.

h. The same bead placement.

i. The maximum degree of cold expansion intended for production in the mock-up.

5.2.7.3. Testing of the mock-up shall include: Vickers microhardness traverses for all ten welds, across both base metals, both heat affected zones (HAZ's), and the weld metal.

## 5.4. Welding Process and Filler Metals Limitations

5.4.1. The following processes and filler metals are allowed, subject to the listed limitations:

5.4.1.1. Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW)

a. The roots of single-sided, full penetration butt welds shall be welded with Exx10 or Exx11 electrodes.

b. E7024 electrodes shall limited to welding tank roof and tank floor plates. Tank floors shall be welded with minimum two passes. Low hydrogen electrodes shall be used for annular rings, shell courses, and tank bottom to shell welds. For annular rings in tanks, single-sided full penetration welds shall be made with a permanent backing strip.

c. E6012, E6013, E7014, and E7020 electrodes shall not be used.

d. Electrode diameters 3/16" and over shall be restricted to the flat position only (1G).

5.4.1.2. Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW)

a. Back purge gas:

i. Shall be applied for two layers or 3/8" (9.5 mm) minimum weld deposit on pipe and tube butt-welds for:

- 2¼ Cr and higher chrome-molys, stainless steels, Nickel based alloys,
- lube oil lines, hydraulic lines, instrument lines, steam inlet lines to steam turbines and fired heater tubes.

b. Autogenous welding (without filler metal) shall not be permitted.

c. Purge gases shall not be substituted with welding techniques or flux-coated wire.

5.4.1.3. General Requirements for All Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW)

a. Applicable materials: carbon steel, low alloy, austenitic stainless steel (300 series).

- 2¼ Cr and higher chrome-molys, stainless steels

5.4.1.4. Requirements for Gas Metal Arc Welding – Modified Wave Short Circuit (GMAW– MWSC) and Gas Metal Arc Welding – Pulsed (GMAW-p)

a. GMAW-MWSC shall be limited to root pass welding only.

f. GMAW-MWSC may be used to weld low alloys and stainless steels without backing gas subject to the following requirements:

## 5.4.1.5. Flux Cored Arc Welding (FCAW)

### a. General Requirements - FCAW

i. Applicable materials: carbon steel, low alloy, austenitic stainless steel (300 series). FCAW is not permitted for the joining of ferritic grades of stainless steels nor high alloys.

ii. Only inverter power sources are to be used.

iii. Restricted to shop welding.

iv. Not permitted on carbon steel for MDMT of less than -20°F (-29°C) unless production impact tests are completed on each batch of consumables used in production. Only consumables designated as low temperature shall be considered.

v. Not permitted on low alloy equipment with MDMT of less than -20°F.

vi. Not permitted on equipment or piping in hydrogen service.

vii. Shall not be used on roots of single sided welds.

viii. Only the externally gas shielded FCAW process (aka FCAW-G) shall be used.

ix. FCAW shall not be used on piping systems having flanges Class 2500 and above.

x. Shall not be used on pipe or branch connections with diameters less than 4" unless welded in the 1G rolled welding position, with minimum 2 passes.

xi. For 1¼ Cr - ½ Mo materials, fillet welds shall either be PWHT'd or a production mockup of the joint utilizing the same production materials shall be made with acceptable hardness tests completed in compliance with Figure 2 below. The acceptable hardness limit shall be 225 HBW (240 Vickers).

xii. Only mainstream established consumable manufacturers shall be used. Acceptable manufacturers are: Lincoln, Hobart, Metrode, ESAB, Teledyne McKay, Bohler Thyssen, Air Liquide, Soudometal, Arcos, & Kobelco. For those manufacturers not listed, consumable datasheets shall be submitted for review and approval by Owner or designate.

xiii. Nickel additions for carbon steel consumables shall be <1%.

xiv. Consumables for carbon steel and low alloy steels shall have minimum H4 in accordance with ASME SFA5.20 or SFA5.29, as applicable, and AWS A4.3.

xv. Bismuth additions shall be less than 0.002% for austenitic stainless steel consumables for all applications.

xvi. A weld pad for chemical analysis of undiluted weld metal for each batch of low alloy and austenitic stainless steel consumables shall be performed by the fabricator, in accordance with the applicable ASME standard.

xvii. For low alloys and stainless steels, welders shall pass a side bend test prior to production welding which shall be on material of the same P # and using the same WPS as to be used in production.

xviii. Vanadium modified grades of Chromium-Molybdenum steels shall not be permitted.

## 5.6. Preheat and Interpass Temperatures

5.6.1. Preheating, when required, shall be uniform around the joint, and the temperature shall be maintained for the duration of the welding operation.

5.6.2. Preheating may be achieved by the use of gas rings, electrical induction or resistance methods, or suitable gas torches. AWS Standard D10.10 "Recommended Practices for Local Heating of Welds in Piping and Tubing" shall be consulted.

5.6.3. Propane gas or an equivalent "clean" heating gas shall be used. The torch tip shall be appropriate for the work, e.g. a "rosebud", not a cutting tip. Heating shall be carried out to ensure sufficient time and energy uniformly soaks the entire weld area.

5.6.4. Preheat temperatures shall be confirmed through the use of heat sensitive crayons, contact pyrometers, Infra-red scanners, or other equally suitable means. Electronic instruments shall have documented proof of calibration no older than twelve months time.

5.6.5. For equipment, preheat temperatures for carbon steel and low alloys shall be a minimum of 100°F (38°C) for thicknesses ¾" (19 mm) to 1⁵⁄₁₆" (30 mm), and a minimum of 150°F (66°C) for thicknesses 1¼" (32 mm) and greater. For piping, the recommended preheat temperatures for carbon steel and low alloys found in ASME B31.3, Table 330.1.1 shall apply. Higher preheat temperatures shall be applied when designated in the WPS. Preheat (where applicable) shall apply to all welds including tack welding and welding of temporary attachments. Preheat requirements also apply to thermal gouging and cutting operations. Preheat shall be maintained for a minimum of 3 inch (76 mm) either side of the joint. The minimum preheat temperature shall be maintained until the weld is complete. If the minimum preheat maintenance is interrupted before the weld is complete, cooling shall be done under an insulation blanket and the partially completed weld shall be subject to MT or LT before resumption of welding.

5.6.7. Field preheating of P4, P5 and P6 alloys on diameters 6" NPS and larger, in all wall thicknesses, shall be carried out with resistance heating coils.

## C3.0 WELDING (including weld overlay, clad restoration and repairs)

C3.1 All production welds, including tack welds and tube-to-tubesheet welds, shall be made with addition of filler metal. Autogenous welding (that is, welding without the addition of filler metal) is not allowed.

C3.2 Plasma arc welding (PAW) and Flux cored arc welding (FCAW) shall not be used.

C3.3 The following consumables shall be used unless otherwise authorized by the Purchaser.

- SMAW: SFA 5.4 E2209.
- GTAW/GMAW: SFA 5.9 ER2209.
- SAW: SFA 5.9 ER2209. Welding shall be with a flux designed for Duplex stainless steel.

C3.4 Heat input shall be in the range of 12.5 to 62.5 kJ/in (0.5 to 2.5 KJ/mm).

C3.5 Maximum interpass temperature shall be 350°F (177°C).

C3.6 When back gouging and welding on the reverse side are not possible, the root pass shall be made with GTAW root pass and back purged until at least 1/4" (6 mm) of weld metal thickness has been deposited. Back purge gas shall be a welding grade of one of the following:

- 90% nitrogen/10%/hydrogen mixture,
- pure nitrogen, or
- argon/nitrogen mixture with not less than 5% nitrogen.

The oxygen content of the back-purged volume shall be less than 2500 ppm.

## C5.0 NDE

**C5.1** The ferrite content of all accessible production welds including weld overlay shall be checked using a Fisher Ferritescope. A minimum of 3 tests shall be made on each weld. The average value of the readings on each weld shall be 35 to 65 vol%. Testing shall be done after welding is complete and before any grinding.

**C5.2** All accessible production welds shall be PT examined. Procedures and acceptance criteria shall be in accordance with ASME Section VIII Div.1, Appendix 8.

**C5.3** Longitudinal welds of tubes and pipe shall receive 100% RT after bending.

**C5.4** Bend surface of tubes and pipe shall receive 100% PT.
