## 1.0 PURPOSE & SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to define the requirements for establishing the design pressure for process piping and vessels.

### 1.2 Scope

This standard identifies the means to determine the maximum upset operating pressure and the design pressure for systems containing expansible fluids. Specifically this standard addresses the requirements for systems protected through mechanical relief (e.g. PSVs) and systems protected by mechanical design. Protection by mechanical relief shall be required for most circumstances. For systems protected by mechanical design, the maximum upset operating pressure shall be rationalized clearly identified in the design documents. Protection by mechanical design shall be utilized only where both of the conditions a) the system is defined as acceptable by this standard and b) mechanical relief is not feasible due to reliability of operation, are simultaneously present.

1.2.1 This standard applies to the following business units that are a part of Suncor Energy Inc:

- Oil Sands
- Refining and Marketing (including Pipelines)
- Natural Gas
- In Situ
- Major Projects

Use of this standard for Business Services, International and Offshore and other Business Units involved in development of technical standards is recommended.

1.2.2 This standard does not cover determination of system design basis and determination of Maximum Upset Operating Pressures for non-expansible fluids. This standard does not cover selection of pressure vessel design basis although the maximum operating pressure as defined by this Standard shall be utilized in the pressure vessel design. The requirements for testing and registering test results of mechanical relief devices is not included in this standard. The design of safeguarding of the system including design of the Safety integrity System (SIS), design of emergency isolation valves, fire protection, etc is not described by this Standard.

### 1.3 Precautions

The decision to proceed with the selection of "protection by mechanical design" should be made only when all other mechanical relief options have been discarded.

If redundant mechanical relief protection systems are provided, isolation valves between the relief and the protected system are acceptable, however one of the redundant relief isolation valves must remain in the open position with a the position locked open with a car seal, (CSO). Closing of any Car Seal Open valves shall be only executed through an approved Management of Change that addresses how the system's mechanical relief protection is maintained (usually through opening and car sealing open another redundant relief valve).

## 4.0 DEFINITIONS AND ACRONYMS

Expansible Fluids – Vapour or Gaseous fluid or a liquid under pressure and temperature that which the liquid changes to a gas when the pressure is reduced to atmospheric pressure or the temperature is raised to ambient temperature while maintaining the specified pressure.

Pressure Vessel – A vessel used for containing, storing, distributing, processing or otherwise handling an expansible fluid with a volume exceeding 42 litres.

Boiler- A vessel in which steam or other vapour may be generated from a liquid under pressure through the direct application of heat. A vessel need not contain a space for vapour to be generated to be considered a boiler.

Reactor – a vessel where chemical compositional changes occur with or without phase change

Mechanical Relief Protection – system overpressure is protected by one or more calibrated device(s) designed to pass the fluid causing overpressure to a safe disposal location at a rate sufficient to maintain pressure containment integrity of the system under review.

Maximum Operating Pressure – the maximum pressure the system can generate when operated according to it's design basis. Since a system may have pressure generators and pressure sinks, the maximum operating pressure may be different for different sections of the system

Maximum Upset Operating Pressure – the greatest pressure generated in a system component resulting from a single jeopardy event.

Single Jeopardy - an aberrant condition or event that adversely affects the system to instigate a system change

## 5.0 STANDARD

Determination of Maximum Operating Upset pressure shall be based on the Maximum Operating Pressure. Design pressure shall meet or exceed Maximum Upset Operating Pressure. Design of mechanical components (vessels, equipment, and piping) shall be composed of components that meet or exceed the design pressure. Maximum Upset Operating Pressures shall be considered as short term event (maximum 10 hours per event, maximum 100 hours per year) and the increased allowable stress for piping as described in B31.3 may be utilized in the design for process piping unless noted otherwise in this Standard.

## 5.1 Determination of Maximum Operating Pressure

### 5.1.1 System Configuration Analysis

The system as designed shall be examined on an equipment by equipment basis for the maximum operating pressure through a detailed evaluation of flow rate, temperature range and upstream and downstream conditions. Flow rates for the fluids shall be determined from the system mass and energy balance. Consideration of two or three phase flow under the applicable system pressure and temperature range shall be included in the flow rate determination. Component maximum operating temperature to establish maximum operating pressure shall be based on normal flow conditions. Temperature increases from blocked-in flow conditions need not be considered in analysis of maximum operating pressure.

Maximum operating pressure shall be determined for each component of the system. Maximum operating pressure shall be based on the normal intent of the design where that is defined as the primary system design basis. Primary system design basis shall consider the range in feed conditions (flow, temperature and composition) and the variations in reactor catalyst reactivity, product selection variation in fractionating and distillation columns, and variation in gravity of fluid systems. Fluid pressure shall be determined at the operating process temperature creating the greatest pressure. If the maximum operating pressure does not occur coincident with the highest operating temperature, the pressure at maximum operating temperature shall be documented for the purposes of equipment and piping design in addition to the maximum operating pressure. If the system includes dynamic equipment for pressure generation (eg pumps, compressors, etc), the specific equipment selected for the design shall be utilized to determine operating pressure. ( e.g. the manufacturers pump curve shall be utilized to determine pressure generation in lieu of a theoretical pump curve). Inlet conditions that are most adverse to the dynamic machine's discharge pressure and maximum operating inlet pressure shall be utilized in determination of the maximum operating pressure. Dynamic equipment that reduces system pressure (e.g. turbines etc) shall utilize minimum RPM (or minimum flow passing) for determining upstream pressure. Maximum operating pressure as a minimum must be based on selected equipment but future process changes that would result in a higher maximum operating pressure such as increasing pump impeller size or pump RPM may be included if directed by the responsible Owner's Engineer.

Maximum operating pressure shall consider both normal line-up and blocked-in system configurations. Blocked-in configurations shall only consider dead head conditions and shall not incorporate upset

---

conditions that result from the blocked-in condition as those effects will be determined through the rational analysis used in section 5.2 for determining upset conditions. Conditions not to be considered for determination of maximum operating pressure for blocked-in conditions include but not limited to: thermal expansion from continuous operation of a pump against a closed discharge valve, continued firing of a boiler under fully vapourized conditions, reverse flow from leaky check valve, inadvertent loss of heat to distillation or fractionating columns acting in series, fluid crossover through leaky exchangers, suction vessel pressure at relieving condition, and transients. Conditions to be considered as a minimum for blocked-in maximum/minimum operating pressure include: fluid contraction from process cool-down, harp/blower/compressor deadhead pressure and fluid head from hydrostatic or hydro-pneumatic fluid weight from high-high liquid level plus fluid vapour pressure at maximum operating temperature, or external pressure source (e.g. well pressure with minimum fluid cap)

5.1.2 HAZOP and SIL Analysis

Prior to system-analysis, actions derived from HAZOP and SILs shall be incorporated into the design documentation. System analysis shall utilize the HAZOP review for evaluating probable operating events to establish governing Maximum Operating Pressure.

5.1.3 Documentation and review

The determination of maximum operating pressure for each system component shall be documented in design calculations prepared for the project records. Copies of those design calculations shall be uploaded to the 'Process Engineering' Discipline file folder in Livelink under the project folder. Separate system design calculations documenting each system component is not required as long as the calculations are combined with, or part of, an associated design document that is uploaded to the process discipline file folder in LiveLink.

The Suncor process project lead shall be responsible for review of the calculation basis and complete an audit to verify the maximum operation pressure conditions have been appropriately defined.

Maximum operating pressures shall be captured on all affected equipment datasheets and entered into the piping line list under the normal operating pressure. Piping line list shall record under the appropriate 'Normal' heading column, both the maximum operating pressure and the maximum operating temperature determined in the analysis whether coincident or not.

## 5.2 Determination of Maximum Upset Operating Pressure

5.2.1 General

Maximum upset pressure shall be determined based on the relieving capacity of the system. For systems provided with mechanical relief devices, the pressure shall be established at the pressure required to establish full relieving flow capacity through the mechanical relief device. For relief protection provided by rupture disks (RDs), the pressure shall be the greater of the rupture failure pressure or the pressure required to vent the full relieving capacity through the rupture disk opening.

For systems not provided with mechanical relief pressure, system relieving shall be considered as zero flow and pressure shall be determined based on increases (or decreases) of fluid volume at the upset condition.

5.2.2 Upset Conditions

5.2.2.1 External Conditions

External conditions that may result in system overpressure that shall be estimated and included

---

in the determination of the Maximum Upset Operating Pressure. Single jeopardy events need only to be considered for external conditions. External considerations shall include as a minimum; fire, loss of power (electrical and instrument air), loss of external cooling media, abnormal increase in external air temperature (e.g. loss of air refrigeration for cryogen facilities) and pressure transients from feed and discharge pipelines. Other external upset conditions to be considered are described in section 4 of Standard ANSI/API 521.

5.2.2.2 Internal Conditions

Internal conditions that may result in system overpressure shall be determined and included in the determination of the Maximum Upset Operating Pressure. Although single jeopardy events need only be considered, analysis of the event to demonstrate that the component is independent (and therefore single jeopardy) from the initiating event must be determined. Cascading events that result from a failure of a component shall be considered as double jeopardy to that component (e.g. a vessel failure results in a pool fire of a flammable fluid that generates a pool fire subjecting that vessel to increased pressure). Cascading events that result from a failure of a component shall be considered as single jeopardy to adjacent components isolated from the failed component (e.g. a vessel failure results in a pool fire and adjacent vessels are subject to the heat of the pool fire. The pool fire is single jeopardy for the adjacent independent vessels). Single valve operation and failure to operate of a single check valve shall not be considered as double jeopardy for either single events or cascading events. Provision of two check valves in series shall be considered adequate to provide single jeopardy back flow prevention with the exception of slurry conditions (exceeding 5% by weight solids) and adhesive or high viscosity conditions (fluid viscosity exceeds 50 cp at operating temperature).

a) Blocked-in Upset Conditions

Maximum pump/blower/compressor discharge upset pressure shall be determined as the combined pressure of the maximum inlet fluid elevation at maximum fluid density, the inlet pressure equal to the upstream relief pressure, and the deadhead capacity of the pump. If upstream to pump suction thermal relief is provided (and no reverse flow check valves or equivalent is provided), the upset pressure equal to sum of maximum deadhead pressure plus maximum operating suction pressure may be utilized to determine pump discharge upset conditions. If the pump deadhead with upstream relief protection is not provided, or the pump is mechanically isolated from the relief protection, the pressure resulting from the thermal expansion of the fluid or fluid phase change shall be determined and additive to the pump deadhead differential pressure. Thermal equilibrium established up to pump seizure resulting from a continuous block-in with external air or surrounding media normal average ambient temperature shall be utilized in determining thermal equilibrium. Pump seizure shall be determined by considering the expansion of the impeller resulting in closure of the annular space between the impeller and the suction ring. A thermal maximum recirculation flow of 2% of pump best efficiency point flow at design pump speed shall be used to determine fluid thermal equilibrium and the enthalpy value of 200% of manufacturer's design tolerance shall be utilized to determine maximum theoretical energy input. Check valves located upstream of pump/blower/compressor and not preventing reverse flow to an upstream relief device shall be considered as active and equivalent to the no upstream relief condition. Surge pressure shall be considered for compressors and blowers.

Boiler drum discharge valves that are utilized in shall ensure relieving capacity is equal or greater than all pressure sources supplying the boiler.

Tower discharge block-ins shall consider uninterrupted feed to the tower plus fluid level at high-high levels. The governing condition of non-fouling reboiler heat input or loss of condensation in overhead circuit shall be considered as single jeopardy. Failure of the SIS system in combination with the events above shall be considered as double jeopardy.

b) Composition Upset Conditions

Process equipment that change the chemical composition of the process fluids shall be evaluated for operation conditions that would impact the relief pressure experienced by the vessel or piping. Changes in the chemical composition to be considered shall include loss, mal-operation or failure of a component in the process system, loss or reduction in system circulation, loss of heat or cooling, change in reactivity of catalyst and change in feed composition. Upset fluid density of mixtures resulting from a loss of one or more mixture components shall be evaluated to establish maximum hydrostatic pressures. For slurry applications, an increase in solid particulate density by 10% shall be utilized in determining maximum fluid upset density. Slurry fluids with a solid's content higher than 80% by weight need not be considered.

c) Increased/Decreased flow

Conditions that would result in increased pressure from increased or decreased flow shall be evaluated as an upset condition. Vessels receiving discharge from pumps/blowers/compressors shall consider flow rates of 105% of pump/blower/compressor rated flow for fixed speed drives and 115% for variable speed/frequency drives. Components depending on the positive removal of non-mechanical equipment shall consider loss of the downstream equipment. Equipment such as centrifugal pumps may be considered to provide pass-through flow at a rate as determined by the hydraulic resistance of the non-rotating pump but positive displacement equipment such as reciprocating compressors shall be considered as equivalent to blocked-in flow.

d) Overfilling of Storage vessels

Fluid elevation shall be determined to a height to match upset inflow condition balanced with relief capacity. Fluid pressure shall be determined at maximum upset composition density.

e) Other Conditions

Standard ANSI/API 521 section 4 shall be considered for all other upset conditions.

## 5.2.3 Determination of Relieving Capacity Requirements

For systems protected through mechanical relief devices (PSVs, PRVs, REs etc) upset conditions shall be evaluated to determine maximum relieving capacity. If the system has segments that may be blocked-in into separate discrete areas, the relieving capacity of that blocked-in segment shall be utilized to determine relieving capacity. Systems incorporating more than one mechanical relief device may consider the combined capacity of two or more devices if and only if the two or more devices may **not** be mechanically isolated from one another. Valves between relief devices that are Car Seal Opened shall be considered as adequate protection for the purposes of relief capacity protection.

### 5.2.3.1 Upset Conditions

---

Upset conditions in general shall be utilized for deriving relief valve capacity, however if a greater flow is demanded through normal operating conditions, the relief valve shall be sized accordingly. Maximum upset operating pressure shall be derived from the upset conditions at the pressure relieving flow capacity provided. The selection of relieving capacity and corresponding upset pressure necessary to achieve relieving capacity shall be based on a suitable risk, reliability, and economic analysis. The relieving rate shall be determined in accordance with recognized engineering methods based on the system configuration. The methods as defined in API 521 shall be used unless special circumstances require a more conservative approach.

### 5.2.3.2 Relief Rate Pressure Padding

For determination of mechanical relief capacity the following shall be considered:

a) The pressure difference between Maximum Operating Pressure and the pressure required to achieve full relieving rate shall not be less

   i) 10% of maximum operating pressure for Operating pressures below 3500 kPag

   ii) 175 kPa

   iii) 350 kPa for Operating pressures exceeding 3500 kPag

b) The pressure difference between Maximum Operating Pressure to achieve full relieving rate specific to equipment type shall not be less the following unless it exceeds the MAWP of the equipment. If the MAWP does not exceed the minimum pressure padding as defined in 5.2.3.2 a), the equipment MAWP shall be re-specified or system maximum operating pressure lowered (and system redesigned or design basis for system reduced accordingly) to meet required pressure padding.

   i) 15% of maximum operating pressure for reciprocating compressors

   ii) 20% of maximum operating pressure for centrifugal pumps and compressors

   iii) 10% of maximum operating pressure for vessels determined at top of vessel

### 5.2.3.3 Relief Design and Specification

Selection and specification of relief equipment shall be completed based on estimated relief capacity and relief rate pressure padding. The supplier of the relief device shall determine actual required relieving pressure at maximum relieving rate. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be defined equal to the actual required relieving pressure. Relief sizing shall be based on proven practise. The requirements of API 520 shall be met or exceeded for relief valve sizing.

## 5.2.4 Determination of Maximum Upset Operating Pressure for systems with no mechanical relieving capacity provided

### 5.2.4.1 Upset Conditions

The upset conditions that would generate a volume increase in the system shall be analysed on the basis of no containment release. Compressible media on containment basis shall consider the containment pressure sufficient to compress the expansible fluid to counteract any system

---

volume increase caused by the upset conditions. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be defined as the containment pressure sufficient to counteract the system volume increase.

## 5.3 Determination of Design Pressure

### 5.3.1 Vessels

Vessels containing expandable fluids shall be provided with mechanical relief. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be utilized for vessel design.

### 5.3.2 Boilers and Fired Heaters

Boilers and fired heaters shall be provided with mechanical relief. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be utilized for boiler/fired heater and component design. Piping connected to the boiler/fired heater and boiler/fired heater components shall be protected by the boiler/fired heater relief equipment.

### 5.3.3 Reactors

Reactors shall be provided with mechanical relief. Where multiple beds exist within a reactor, multiple mechanical relief devices shall be considered if bed collapse from differential pressure may occur. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be utilized for reactor design. Piping connected to the reactor shall be protected by the reactor relief equipment.

### 5.3.4 Centrifugal Pumps, Centrifugal Compressors, and Blowers

Centrifugal Pumps, compressors, and blowers shall be protected by upstream mechanical relief or by protection by design. Pump/compressor/blower MAWP shall exceed Maximum Upset Operating Pressure. Piping connected upstream of the pump/compressor/blower shall be protected by an upstream vessel relief protection or by system design. Piping downstream of the pump/compressor/blower may be protected either by design or downstream mechanical relief. Piping between pump/compressor/blower discharge and downstream isolation valve shall be designed to pump/compressor/blower discharge Maximum Upset Operating Pressure and pressure shall be considered as continuous duty for determination of allowable stresses.

### 5.3.5 Positive Displacement Pumps and Reciprocating Compressors

Positive displacement pumps and reciprocating compressors shall be protected by mechanical relief. If relief is routed back to suction of pump or compressor, heat addition from recirculation load shall be considered in determination of Maximum Upset Operating Pressure. Piping connected upstream of the pump/compressor shall be protected by an upstream vessel relief protection or by system design. Piping downstream of the pump/compressor shall be protected by the downstream mechanical relief. Piping between pump/compressor discharge and downstream isolation valve shall be designed to pump/compressor discharge Maximum Upset Operating Pressure and pressure shall be considered as continuous duty for determination of allowable stresses.

### 5.3.6 Exchangers

Exchanger shells on shell and tube exchangers shall be provided with mechanical relief. Exchanger tubes, external pipe for pipe in pipe, plate exchangers and spiral exchangers shall be provided by mechanical relief or by mechanical design. Air Cooled exchanger tubes shall be protected by mechanical design for blocked-in conditions and by upstream and/or downstream vessel mechanical relief for normal operating conditions. Mechanical design for vacuum shall be provided where exchanger can be blocked in on inlet and outlet. Maximum Upset Operating Pressure shall be utilized for exchanger and exchanger component design. ## 5.4 Selection of Piping Line Class and LDT Completion

Piping components shall be selected from one Suncor Standard line class between line breaks as designated on the P&IDs. The line class selected shall have design pressure and design temperature exceeding the Maximum Upset Operating Pressure for the piping under consideration. Design pressure on the LDT may be designated as any value equal or exceeding the Maximum Upset Operating. The LDT design pressure shall be equal or less than the line class MDWP. Line class components are designed to meet or exceed the pressure rating as defined in the line class. Wall thicknesses for piping and fittings designated as "Calc" shall be determined by the engineer to satisfy the appropriate piping design code (e.g. B31.1) utilizing the design pressure as designated on the LDT and the line class corrosion allowance. Design temperature shall be the coincident temperature utilized to derive the design pressure.

Testing pressure shall be established according to Standard 0205. In general, hydrotest pressure to be defined on the LTD shall be defined as the maximum pressure that the limiting mechanical component can withstand. The "Code Design test pressure" as designated on the LDT shall be defined as the Design Pressure times the applicable code multiplier. For elevated temperature conditions, Code Design test pressure shall increased to account for differences between allowable stresses at testing temperature and Maximum Upset Operating Temperature.
