## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This Practice provides requirements for aboveground Reinforced Thermosetting Resin (RTR) piping systems.

### 1.2 Scope

This Practice describes the requirements for the design, materials, fabrication, installation, examination, and testing of aboveground RTR piping systems. This Practice applies to both process and utility RTR piping in accordance with *ASME B31.3*.

*Comment:* RTR piping is also known by other terms such as Glass Reinforced Plastic (GRP), Fiberglass Reinforced Plastic (FRP), and Reinforced Thermosetting Resin Piping (RTRP).

This practice does not apply to RTR piping for underground systems, systems with mechanical joints, or fire water piping.

## 3. Definitions

*owner*: The party who owns the facility wherein the piping system will be used.

*purchaser*: The party who awards the contract to the supplier. The purchaser may be the owner or the owner's authorized agent.

*supplier*: The party responsible for providing and installing the piping system.

## 4. Requirements

### 4.1 Design

4.1.1 In case of conflict between this Practice and the construction drawings furnished by the purchaser, the construction drawings shall take precedence.

4.1.2 All process and utility piping shall be in accordance with *ASME B31.3*.

4.1.3 Chemical resistance shall be established by either acceptable field experience in similar services or laboratory testing in accordance with *ASTM C 581* and *ASTM D 543*.

4.1.4 Wall thicknesses for contact molded piping shall not be less than shown in *NBS PS-15* for the service conditions.

---

4.1.5 Wall thicknesses for filament wound piping shall be in accordance with *ASME B 31.3*.

## 4.2 Materials

4.2.1 All material substitutions shall be approved by the owner before purchase and fabrication.

4.2.2 RTR pipe and fittings shall be joined by cemented joints or by butt and wrap process in accordance with the piping material specification furnished by the purchaser.

4.2.3 Pipe, fittings, flanges and adhesives from different manufacturers shall not be used interchangeably.

4.2.4 All fittings shall be produced to provide the same corrosion resistance, pressure retaining capability and physical integrity as the pipe.

## 4.3 Fabrication

### 4.3.1 General

4.3.1.1 All operations performed on pipe or fittings in the course of the fabrication and installation shall be in accordance with *ASME B 31.3*, and shall be performed in accordance with qualified written procedures.

4.3.1.2 The adhesive cemented joints shall have straight-tapered joining surfaces or taper-taper joining surfaces.

4.3.1.3 Preparation of fittings sockets shall be in accordance with the manufacturer's recommendations.

4.3.1.4 Preparation of pipe ends shall be performed using special tools recommended by the pipe manufacturer.

4.3.1.5 All hand lay up joints shall be in accordance with manufacturer's recommendations and *ASME B 31.3*.

4.3.1.6 All cut edges shall be coated with resin so that no glass fibers are exposed and all voids are filled. Frayed or delaminated edges shall not be permitted.

4.3.1.7 The determination of dimensions of pipe, fittings, and joints shall be in accordance with *ASTM D 3567*.

4.3.1.8 All dimensional tolerance shall be in accordance with the following standards as applicable:

a. Filament-Wound Fiberglass Pipe - ASTM D 2996

b. Centrifugally Cast Fiberglass Pipe - ASTM D 2997

c. Machine Made Fiberglass Flanges - ASTM D 4024

d. Contact-Molded Fiberglass Flanges - ASTM D 5421

---

e. "Fiberglass" (Glass-Fiber-Reinforced Thermosetting-Resin) Pressure Pipe Fittings - ASTM D 5685
f. Contact-Molded Fiberglass Corrosion Resistant Pipe and Fittings - ASTM D 6041

4.3.1.9 Fabricated parts requiring trial shop assembly shall be match-marked to facilitate erection in the field.

## 4.3.2 Application of Adhesive

4.3.2.1 Piping joints shall be made in accordance with the manufacturer's adhesive application procedures.

4.3.2.2 Records of trained personnel shall be maintained, and shall certify that training was received not more than 6 months before commencing work.

4.3.2.3 To prevent internal buildup of adhesive and restriction to the pipe flow, the use of excessive adhesive shall be avoided. However, if after joint assembly adhesive is not extruded from the free end of the socket, insufficient adhesive was applied prior to make up.

4.3.2.4 Manufacturer's recommended repair procedure shall be followed to correct problems with joint assembly.

4.3.2.5 Following assembly or reassembly, extruded adhesive shall be wiped away to form a smooth fillet which covers the free end of the socket.

4.3.2.6 After applying adhesive to the joints, the assembly shall only be moved after the adhesive has cured in accordance with the manufacturer's recommended application instructions.

## 4.3.3 Butt and Wrapped Joints

4.3.3.1 Butted joints shall be made by overwrapping the prepared butted ends of pipe or fitting with multiple layers of resin-saturated fiberglass mat and roving.

4.3.3.2 Joints for contact-molded pipe and fittings shall be in accordance with *ASTM D 6041*, Paragraph 6.2.

4.3.3.3 Custom-made supply kits with the necessary materials and detailed instructions shall be obtained from the manufacturer of filament-wound or centrifugally cast pipe and fittings.

## 4.3.4 Precautions with Joints

### 4.3.4.1 Adhesive Joints

Based on extensive experience from failures, the following precautions shall be strictly observed:

a. Contamination of joining surfaces shall not be permitted
b. Excessive use of adhesive shall not be permitted

---

c. Necessary cure time shall be provided

d. Pot life of adhesive shall not be exceeded

e. Shelf life of adhesive shall not be exceeded

#### 4.3.4.2 Butt and Wrapped Joints

Based on extensive experience from failures, the following precautions shall be strictly observed:

a. Improper fit-up of joints shall not be permitted

b. Excessive mismatch in wall thicknesses shall not be permitted

c. Inadequate guiding or contamination of joining surface shall not be permitted

d. Pot life of resin mix shall not be exceeded

#### 4.3.4.3 Curing of Joints

1. Proper curing of joints shall be performed in accordance with manufacturer's recommendation.

2. Many manufacturers recommend heat application to cure the bonded joints. Heat curing shall be performed strictly in accordance with Manufacturer's recommended heat cure requirements.

3. Thermostatically controlled heat blankets shall be used to assure proper application of heat to cure the bonded joints. Hot air guns, infrared lamps, or alternate forms of heat application shall not be permitted.

### 4.3.5 Branch Connections

4.3.5.1 Branch connections to the main shall be made with reducing saddle fittings, tees or tees with reducing bushings.

4.3.5.2 Reducing saddle fittings shall be properly bonded to the main pipe.

## 4.4 Installation

### 4.4.1 General

4.4.1.1 Installation shall be in accordance with the pipe manufacturer's recommendations and this Practice.

4.4.1.2 All necessary precautions shall be taken to ensure a safe working environment in accordance with applicable codes and standards.

4.4.1.3 The support spacing for horizontal RTR piping shall not exceed the pipe manufacturer's recommendations for the type and size of pipe, wall thickness, operating temperature, and liquid specific gravity.

---

4.4.1.4 If pipe fittings are included in the piping runs and/or if the lines are insulated, the weight of the fittings and/or insulation shall be considered in deciding support spans.

## 4.4.2 Pipe Handling

4.4.2.1 If lifting with slings, only wide fabric choker slings shall be used to lift, move, or lower pipe and fittings. Use of wire rope or chain shall not be permitted.

4.4.2.2 Slings shall be of sufficient capacity for the load, and shall be inspected before use.

4.4.2.3 Worn or defective handling equipment shall not be permitted.

## 4.4.3 Pipe Supports

4.4.3.1 The type of pipe supports shall be strictly in accordance with the pipe manufacturer's recommendations.

4.4.3.2 For the design of pipe supports for RTR piping, the following precautions shall be observed:

- a. Point loads shall be avoided by providing adequate base length.
- b. 180 degrees of circumferential support shall be provided.
- c. Elastomeric liners shall be provided to ensure intimate contact between irregular pipe surface and support.
- d. Protection against abrasion shall be provided.
- e. Excessive bending shall not be permitted.
- f. Excessive loading in vertical runs shall not be permitted.

4.4.3.3 Vertical pipe runs may be supported by bonding collars contacting 360 degrees of pipe surface.

4.4.3.4 Pipe guides shall be included in vertical runs at calculated intervals to prevent shaking or buckling. Guides should be rigidly attached to the supporting structures and should allow the pipe to move freely in the axial direction.

4.4.3.5 The design of pipe supports shall consider, but not be limited to, the following stresses induced by the piping system:

- a. Pipe span deflections
- b. Combined maximum longitudinal/mid-span flexural stress
- c. Shear stress
- d. Weight effect of valves and other attachments
- e. Thermal and pressure expansion and contraction of the system

---

4.4.3.6 External and internal attachments shall be designed to resist local stresses from induced bending moments, forces generated by thermal gradients between dissimilar materials, pressure and thermal elongations, and other sources without exceeding allowable stresses.

4.4.3.7 All attachments shall be constructed to minimize stress concentrations and preserve the flexibility of the system.

4.4.3.8 Valves and auxiliary equipment should be supported separately to reduce bending moments on the piping.

4.4.3.9 Significant magnitude thrust forces can develop in internally-pressured piping systems. The design, including support design, shall consider thrust forces and restraining strategies.

#### 4.4.4 Flange Installation (Process and Utility Piping)

4.4.4.1 Flange connections shall be installed in accordance with the pipe manufacturer's recommended procedure.

4.4.4.2 Flange faces shall be centered and aligned to each other before assembling and tightening bolts.

4.4.4.3 Flange bolts shall not be used to draw flanges into alignment.

4.4.4.4 Bolt threads shall be lubricated.

4.4.4.5 Flat washers shall be fitted under the flange bolts and nuts.

4.4.4.6 Bolts shall be evenly tightened in accordance with the tightening pattern and torque step recommendations of the flange manufacturer.

4.4.4.7 The torque values for the bolts shall be in accordance with the flange manufacturer's recommendations.

4.4.4.8 A minimum of 1 hour after initial assembly and full tightening, flange connections shall be re-tightened in accordance with the tightening pattern and torque step recommendations of the flange manufacturer.

4.4.4.9 A third and final tightening of the flange connections should be performed about 4 hours after the second.

## 4.5 Visual Examination

*Comment:* Because pipe and fittings may be subjected to rough treatment during transportation, storage, and installation, it is imperative they be fully inspected for possible damage, cracking or manufacturing defects. If the external surface of the pipe has been gouged or exhibits a depression, it is likely that the impact has also caused damage to the liner. Impact damage on translucent pipe can usually be detected by a localized color difference in the pipe.

---

4.5.1 The minimum acceptance criteria for visual examination shall be in accordance with the following:

a. Inner surfaces of contact molded pipe, fittings, flanges and couplings shall be in accordance with ASTM C 582.

b. Inner surfaces of machine-made pipe, fittings, flanges and couplings shall be in accordance with ASTM D 2563, Level II. A defect shall not penetrate beyond the inner surface thickness.

c. All other layers of all products shall be in accordance with ASTM D 2563, Level II.

4.5.2 All joints shall be examined and shall evidence complete filling of the annular volume with the cement and shall show no evidence of any unbonded areas, bubbles, void, delamination, or protrusion of adhesive greater than 25% of pipe wall thickness into the bore of pipe; cracks, softness, or tackiness.

## 4.6 Testing

### 4.6.1 Resin Cure Testing

4.6.1.1 If polyester resins are used, the following test shall be performed to indicate proper resin curing of both interior and exterior surfaces:

a. Approximately ¼ oz. (7.40 ml) of acetone or methylethyl ketone (MEK) shall be poured on the pipe surface

b. After a few minutes, but before the solvent has evaporated, the tested surface shall be wiped with a clean rag or paper tissue

c. Any sign of stickiness shall indicate inadequate curing, and pipe shall not be acceptable

4.6.1.2 After curing, the surface of piping, both interior and exterior, shall be cleaned and free of grease, mold release agents, wax, excess resin, dirt and other foreign material.

### 4.6.2 Pressure Testing

4.6.2.1 All joints (i.e., flanged and cemented) shall be inspected for leakage during the pressure test.

4.6.2.2 The cemented joints must be completely cured before pressure testing.

4.6.2.3 The RTR piping systems shall be hydrostatically tested in accordance with the following:

a. The pressure during the test at any point in the system shall not be less than 1.5 times the design pressure.

---

b. The test pressure shall not exceed 1.5 times the maximum rating of the lowest rated piping component

4.6.2.4 The temperature of the test medium and the pipe test section should be the same, and if possible, should be less than 100°F (38°C). Before applying test pressure, ample time shall be allowed for the test medium and the pipe test section to equalize.

4.6.2.5 For temperatures above 100°F (38°C), test pressure shall be reduced.

4.6.2.6 Hydrostatic testing of the piping systems with vessels included in the system shall be permitted in accordance with *ASME B 31.3*, Paragraph A345.4.3, if one or more of the following cases are applicable:

    a. It is not considered practicable to isolate the piping from the vessel.

    b. The test pressure of the piping exceeds the vessel test pressure.

    c. The piping and vessels are tested together at the vessel test pressure.

    d. The vessel test pressure is not less than 77% of the piping test pressure.

    e. The owner approves concurrent testing of piping and vessel system.

4.6.2.7 If hydrostatic pressure testing is not considered practical, the piping system may be tested using a pneumatic test, hydrostatic-pneumatic leak test, or initial service leak test provided the testing is performed in accordance with *ASME B 31.3*, Paragraph A345.

4.6.2.8 A preliminary pneumatic leak test using air at 25 psig may be performed before the hydrostatic testing to locate major leaks.

4.6.2.9 Test pressure may be limited by valves, or other lower pressure rated components. Such components may not withstand the required test pressure. These components should be either removed or isolated from the test section to avoid possible damage or failure of these devices.

4.6.2.10 For any test pressure from 1 to 1-1/2 times the system operating design pressure, the total test time including initial pressurization, initial expansion, and time at test pressure shall not exceed 8 hours.

4.6.2.11 If the test is not completed due to leakage, equipment failure, etc., the test section shall be depressurized and allowed to "relax" for a minimum of 8 hours before the test section is brought up to test pressure again.

---

4.6.2.12 The preferred testing medium shall be clean water.

4.6.2.13 The test section should be completely filled with liquid. Trapped air shall be purged. While the test section is filling, venting at high points may be necessary to purge air pockets.

4.6.2.14 Once the system is filled and all air purged, the outlet may be slowly closed and the system slowly brought up to the desired pressure.

4.6.2.15 To prevent water hammer or over-pressurization, quick-closing valves or booster pumps without suitable controls shall not be used.

4.6.2.16 Over-torquing of flanges to stop leaks is not permitted. Leaking flanges shall be remade with new gaskets and retested. If leakage still occurs, flanges must be replaced.
