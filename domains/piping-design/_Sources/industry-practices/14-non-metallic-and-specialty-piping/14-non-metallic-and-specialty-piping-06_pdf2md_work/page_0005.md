## End Fittings (Couplings) for Hose in Bulk Hydrocarbon Service

There are 3 types of end fittings that are suitable for rubber hose in bulk hydrocarbon service: built-in, externally swaged or crimped, and internally expanded. All 3 types are nonreusable.

Built-in fittings are an integral part of the hose construction; the raw hose components are assembled over the end fittings, adhesive bond is made between the tube material and the shank of the end fitting, and binding wire is used to pull hose reinforcement material down between ribs welded to the shank. The entire assembly is then vulcanized. Built-in fittings are the most reliable type of end fitting, but the most difficult type of fitting to deal with when manufacturing a hose. All submarine and floating hose have built-in end fittings (except hose in MTBE service).

Externally swaged (or crimped) end fittings (also known as pressed-on fittings) have a serrated shank that is positioned inside the hose and a serrated or grooved cylindrical ferrule that is placed on the outside of the hose. The ferrule is reduced in diameter while assembled on the hose with the shank in place by forcing it axially through a die or by crimping with a split die that moves radially. This type fitting is shorter than a built-in fitting, so the hose assembly is more flexible at the ends. End fitting selection and installation criteria are critical factors that must be considered to assure that end fittings do not leak or blow off the hose in service.

Internally expanded fittings are similar in construction details to the externally swaged type, but the shank is expanded rather than the ferrule being swaged/crimped. Internally expanded fittings are less forgiving of selection/assembly errors than externally swaged fittings and are considered less reliable. As for externally swaged fittings, end fitting selection and installation criteria are critical. Internally expanded fittings should normally be limited to tank truck bottom loading hose and aircraft refueling hose.

## Couplings for Steam Hose

Two types are available: the (older) interlocking clamp-type and the (newer) Tri-Lockt-type. The clamp-type coupling requires the steam hose user to check clamp bolt tightness before each use and occasionally the clamp must be changed to the next smaller size if the clamp halves have been tightened enough that they touch other. This procedure is time-consuming and if not done, a safety hazard exists. In addition, the clamp-type coupling's large size and protruding bolts is more cumbersome to use, difficult to use in tight spots, and snags easily on equipment.
