**BUTT-AND-STRAP JOINTS:** Layers of resin-impregnated fiberglass are wrapped around the joint and allowed to cure (usually 2 to 4 hours at 70°F). The resin is selected to be compatible with the pipe. This is a high cost, labor intensive joint for FRP pipe but is very reliable.

**FLAT-FACED FLANGES:** Flat-faced flanges are generally available for PVC, CPVC, PP, PVDF, ABS and FRP piping materials and are the most common and preferred method for joining these piping materials to dissimilar piping. The flanges can be joined to the pipe by solvent cementing, threading, heat fusion or adhesive bonding, depending on pipe material. Flanges are likely to limit the pressure capability of the system, so check with the manufacturer/supplier. Care must be taken not to overtorque the flange bolts and crack or damage the flange. Flange installation tips (per Harrington Plastics) are given at right.

1. Make sure that all the bolt holes of the matching flanges match up. It is not necessary to twist the flange and pipe to achieve this.
2. Inspect all bolts.
3. Make sure that the faces of the mating flanges are not separated by excessive distance prior to bolting down the flanges.
4. The bolts on the plastic flanges should be tightened by pulling down the nuts diametrically opposite each other using a torque wrench. Complete tightening should be accomplished in stages and the final torque values in the following table should be followed for the various sizes of flanges. Uniform stress across the flange will eliminate leaky gaskets.
