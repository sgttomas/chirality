## 3.0 Design

### 3.1 General

3.1.1 HDPE does not react with most fluids being pumped. The Engineering Characteristics brochure should be reviewed to check HDPE pipe resistance to various chemicals, the operating temperature and the concentration of the chemical itself.

3.1.2 HDPE pipe should not be used in areas where significant concentrations of hydrocarbon are present in soils.

3.1.3 In considering the design Limits for a HDPE system, the interdependence of the operating pressure, temperature, safety factor and the expected life should be taken into account Special consideration should be given to surge pressures and vacuum in the system.

3.1.4 Pipe wall thickness (DR) selection should be based on earth loads, live traffic loads, soil compaction and trench design.

3.1.5 The piping system can be designed as conventional deep bury system or shallow bury insulated system.

### 3.2 *Deep Bury Installation*

3.2.1 Pipes carrying water and other fluid that have similar freezing temperature to water should be buried at a minimum depth of 10'-8" to the top of the pipe. See Drawing A1.0 in Appendix A for details.

### 3.3 *Shallow Bury Insulated Installation*

3.3.1 Where deep bury installation is not possible, practical or economical, the piping system can be installed in insulated trenches at shallow depths. The minimum depth to the top of the pipe should be 6'-0". A layer of 5" styrofoam insulation should be placed at a minimum depth of 5'-0". In special circumstances, pipe bury depth can be revised to less than 6'-0", however, the insulation thickness will have to be increased. Insulation manufacturer's instruction shall be followed to calculate the revised thickness of the insulation. See Drawing A1.1 in Appendix A for details.

3.3.2 The width of the insulation for the pipe buried at a minimum of 6'-0" shall be 12'-0". The insulation can be designed as horizontal layer or as "inverted U". The sum of the width and vertical legs of the "inverted U" insulation shall be minimum 12'-0". See Drawings A1.2 and A1.3 in Appendix A for details.

3.3.3 Styrofoam insulation is manufactured in varying compressive strengths; insulation strength should be designed for a particular application which depends on a total load calculation.

## 5.0 Installation

### 5.1 *TRENCHING AND BEDDING*

5.1.1 The minimum and maximum trench width below the crown of the pipe shall be not less than the nominal diameter of the pipe plus 24". The trench shall be excavated so that pipe can be laid to the alignment, grade and depth required. The subgrade shall provide a uniform and continuous support for the pipe and fittings on solid undisturbed ground.

5.1.2 Where the subgrade of the trench is unstable or where it contains ashes, cinders, refuse or organic material, such material shall be excavated and the subgrade shall be made by backfilling with stabilizing gravel compacted in 3" layers. Where the gravel subgrade can not be made to properly support the pipe, spatial foundations should be designed.

5.1.3 The pipe is to be installed in sand bedding 3" below the pipe bottom and extending at least 12" above the pipe to the full width of the trench. The bedding shall be placed in uniform layers not exceeding 6" compacted to at least 95% Standard Proctor Density. The bed shall be shaped true to grade and to provide continuous, uniform bearing surface for barrel of the pipe. Do not use blocks when bedding the pipe.

Remove all rocks and sharp protrusions from rock from the bedding material area to provide a minimum of 6" of clear bedding material on each side of the pipe. See Drawings A1.0 and A1.1 In Appendix A for details.

5.1.4 Where fittings are placed in a trench where native material is fill material, as compared to installations in bedrock, the fittings shall be bedded in crushed rock bedding for a distance of three pipe diameters in each direction from the fitting.

### 5.2 *PIPE*

5.2.1 The pipe shall be installed as per manufacturer's instructions to the alignment and grade required. Minimum depth as per drawings shall be maintained with no sags or humps and with proper bury at hydrants, monitors, valves and tie-in points. Where minor deflections in line or grade are required, the deflections shall not exceed the maximum recommended by the pipe manufacturer. Ensure barrel of the pipe is in contact with shaped bed throughout its full length. Do not lay pipe on frozen bedding.

5.2.2 Do not install any pipe sections where trench conditions are unsuitable. If the pipe must be installed below the ground water table, the pipe must be sufficiently anchored to prevent floating, such as through use of swamp weights or poured-in-place anchor blocks.

5.2.3 Keep the pipe free of dirt and water and other foreign materials. Whenever work is stopped, install a removable watertight bulkhead at open end of the last pipe laid.

5.2.4 Cut pipes in an approved manner as recommended by the pipe manufacturer, without damaging of pipe and to leave smooth end at right angles to axis of the pipe.

---

5.2.5 Follow recommendations of the pipe manufacturer regarding HDPE flanged connections. The flanged connection must be tightened initially and then allowed to creep to relieve the cold working stresses for a minimum of 8 hours, and then re-tightened.

*5.3 VALVES*

5.3.1 Place valve level to ensure the vertical stem and above grade post indicator can be placed directly above the valve in a vertical orientation.

5.3.2 All buried components, including the valve body, stem, box and below grade post indicator assembly or valve box are to be wrapped in a continuous water barrier wrapping. See Drawings A1.4 and A1.5 in Appendix A for details.

5.3.3 An annular space around the stem box is to consist only of packed gravel. The packed gravel annulus must extend a minimum of 4" radially around the valve stem box. Place a Sono-Tube to contain the gravel during backfilling.

*5.4 THRUST BLOCKING*

5.4.1 Concrete reaction blocks and supports are to be in accordance with CAN/CSA A23.1. Reinforcement shall be placed in accordance with CAN/CSA-A23.1. Place thrust blocking under valves, hydrant bowls and monitors. Do not place load upon new concrete until it is set.

5.4.2 Concrete reaction blocks shall be placed between the undisturbed ground and the fitting or valve. A layer of 12 mil polyethylene sheet is to be placed between the valve body or hydrant bowl and the concrete to prevent surface corrosion.

5.4.3 The placement of the concrete block must allow access to all flange bolts on both ends of valves.

*5.5 INSULATION*

5.5.1 Protect insulation from exposure to ultraviolet light, physical damage and contact with incompatible chemicals.

5.5.2 Place a minimum of 2" thickness of granular sand material as bedding for the insulation.

5.5.3 Install insulation with staggered joints to maintain continuity of thermal protection. Cut and trim insulation neatly to fit spaces. Butt joints tightly, offset vertical joints. Use only insulation free from chipped or broken edges. Use largest possible dimensions to reduce the number of joints. Place insulation in staggered fashion with long axis parallel to the crown of the embankment.

5.5.4 Take precautions to prevent movement of the insulation during placement of the granular cover. Immediately following placement of insulation, carefully place 10" thickness of granular material over the insulation, taking special precautions not to damage the boards. This minimum coverage of the granular material is required before the surface can be subjected to any vehicular traffic.

5.5.5 Place insulation between pipes to be crossed for shallow-bury installation. See Drawing A1.6 in Appendix A for details.

*5.6 BACKFILL*

5.6.1 Backfill shall consist of replacing the excavated material in even layers not exceeding 12" in depth and compacting each layer by mechanical means to a minimum 97% Standard Proctor Density in trenches under roads, and 95% in

---

other areas. No boulders, rock, ice, snow, organic material or debris shall be permitted in the trench. Mechanical compaction equipment should not be used until there is sufficient cover to prevent damage to the pipe and insulation.

5.6.2 In areas where 95% Standard Proctor Density compaction is required, but where such compaction could not be achieved with typical construction methods and equipment, use "Fillcrete". Follow "Fillcrete" manufacturer's recommendation for placement. Leave forms in place undisturbed for a sufficient time to ensure the matrix has cured and that settlement or subsidence of "Fillcrete" cannot occur.

## 6.0 Testing

### 6.1 *Testing*

6.1.1 Testing of HDPE pipe is to be in accordance with CSA/CAN3 B-137.1.

6.1.2 Refer to Suncor OSG Standard 0205 for details. The hydrostatic testing procedure for HDPE piping is considerably different than that for steel pipe. Where composite sections of HDPE and steel pipe are tested at one time, the procedure for HDPE pipe shall be used.

6.1.3 Test preparation, test fluid and pressure testing must be done in accordance with ANSI B31.3.

6.1.4 When the line has been filled, all the air must be expelled by high point vents, pigging or over-flushing. Time should be allowed for the water to reach a constant temperature.

6.1.5 The test pressure shall be at 1.5 times maximum design pressure of the system, but shall not exceed the pressure rating of the pipe or piping components.

6.1.6 No test shall be applied until at least 7 days after the concrete thrust reaction block has been cast, and after plugged ends have been securely braced.

6.1.7 If the test fails for any section of the water main, locate and repair the leaks. After such repairs, retesting of the repaired sections shall be conducted. Note that the testing procedure for HDPE pipe requires a "relaxing" duration between retests.

6.1.8 Test equipment shall be from an approved laboratory or manufacturer and shall be furnished with a certificate showing degree of accuracy.

6.1.9 All joints and flanged connections are to be left exposed until hydrostatic and visual inspections are completed.

### 6.2 *Corrosion Protection Testing*

6.2.1 All buried steel, cast or cast components are to be cathodically protected by an electrically bonded and properly sized anode.

6.2.2 Cathodic protection shall be installed for each hydrant assembly, monitor, and valve.

6.2.3 Cathodic protection equipment is to be installed as per manufacturer's specifications. See Drawing A1.7 in Appendix A for details.

6.2.4 Magnesium anodes are to be properly sized for the amount of buried steel.

# 1.0 General

## 1.1 *Summary*

### 1.1.1 Scope of Specification

a. This specification prescribes the criteria for fusion bonding high density polyethylene piping and fittings used for pressure applications. Fusion bonding using this specification will satisfy pressure piping used for ASME B31.3 pressure piping applications and firewater piping meeting National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) requirements.

b. No deviation shall be made from this specification without written authorization from Owner's Engineer.

c. This specification may be used for shop as well as field fabrication.

### 1.1.2 Terminology

a. Shop Fabricate: Fabrication performed at a Supplier's shop, normally remote from the jobsite.

b. Field Fabricate: Fabrication performed at the jobsite, either in a field shop or at the installation site area.

c. HDPE: High density polyethylene.

d. Butt-Fusion Bonding: The use of a hot plate to heat the ends of HDPE pipe to a temperature which is conducive to allowing the ends to be fused together upon the application of pressure.

e. Sidewall-Fusion Bonding: The use of a contoured heating plate to heat the ends of a saddle face simultaneously with the surface of the pipe to a temperature which is conducive to allowing the saddle to be fused to the pipe upon application of pressure.

## 4.0 Scope-But-Fusion Bonding Procedure Qualification Testing

4.1 This specification identifies the procedures used for destructive testing a thick walled Hi h Density Polyethylene pipe joint for the purpose of qualifying the fusion bonding procedure. The basis for type of tests is based on ASTM D2657, par. 9.3. The four types of tests identified are:

- 4.1.1 Tensiles
- 4.1.2 Bends
- 4.1.3 Torques
- 4.1.4 Impacts

4.2 These tests should be considered qualitative in determining the difference between a good bond and a poor bond. The quality depends on the type of failure which occurs. What may appear as a failure may not indicate a poor bond. It is important that for thick sections, all notches are removed from the coupons. This may be achieved by removing the flash from the fusion. For qualification interpretation, Suncor shall be notified if a coupon fails a test.

*4.3 TEST COUPON SIZE AND LOCATION*

4.3.1 Coupons shall be cut from each 1200 section of the bond. Four coupons shall be extracted from each 1200 section. The coupons shall be ½" thick through the thickness of the pipe wall perpendicular to the bond. Refer to Figure 1.

![Figure 1 Typical Pipe Cross Section](figures/14-non-metallic-and-specialty-piping-01_p0023_fig01_figure-1-typical-pipe-cross-section.png)

4.4 *TENSILES*

4.4.1 Three tensile tests shall be performed. The specimens shall be machined to a size similar to ASME Section IX weld coupon requirements.

4.4.2 The specimen shall fail in a ductile manner showing a distinct amount of elongation. A specimen which fails quickly and one which indicates a shear non bonded face indicates a poor bond.

4.5 *BENDS*

4.5.1 Three 1800 inside root bends shall be performed. This shall be done by placing the specimen in a vice with the bond bead located approximately 1" above the top

---

of the vice jaws. A pipe should be used to make the bend with the edge of the pipe being located 1" from the edge of the bead.

4.5.2 If the coupon starts to buckle due to the thinness, a thicker 1" specimen may be cut and bent using a 1800 mandrel similar to that used for bending metallic weld coupons.

4.5.3 A failure which snaps in a brittle manner prior to achieving 1800 may indicate a poor bond area.

*4.0 TORQUES*

4.6.1 Three torques shall be performed. This shall be done by placing the coupon In a vice with the bond bead located approximately 1" above the top of the vice jaws. A pipe wrench shall be used to make twist the coupon a minimum of 3600, with the edge of the pipe wrench being located 1" from the edge of the bead.

4.6.2 A failure which snaps in 1800 of the twist may indicate a poor bond. Failures which occur beyond 3600 could be considered acceptable.

*4.7 IMPACTS*

4.7.1 Three impacts shall be performed. This shall be done by placing the coupon in a vice with the bond bead located approximately 1" above the top of the vice jaws. A heavy hammer or pipe can be used to "impact" the specimen.

4.7.2 A failure which occurs quickly and in a brittle manner may indicate a poor bond area. When the coupon bends and shows elongation at the bond from the force of the impacts, this can be considered acceptable.

*4.8 RE-TESTING OF PROCEDURE QUALIFICATION COUPONS*

4.8.1 If a coupon failure is indicated, upon approval from Succor's QA, two re-tests of the same coupon may be performed. If one of these coupons were to fail, the qualification needs to be redone.

## 6.0 Scope-But-Fusion Bonding Procedure Specification

### 6.1 PROCEDURE

6.1.1 The following procedure is to be used as an EXAMPLE ONLY and was developed for butt fusion bonding extra high molecular weight HMW) high density polyethylene (HDPE) piping with resins with a melt index (1902.16) of 0.09 manufactured by Union Carbide. A production fusion bonding procedure must be developed and qualified for the actual production work that will be performed.

6.1.2 Prior to performing any fusion bonding, the equipment shall be inspected by the bonder, for operability per Attachment B, "Fusion Bonding - Work Instructions - Equipment Pre-Inspection".

6.1.3 The bonder shall visually inspect the fusion bonding process and record all inspections in accordance with Attachment F, "Butt Fusion Bonding - Inspection Checklist and Record". The Fabricator's inspector shall approve all records of butt fusion bonding.

### 6.2 *EXAMPLE PROCEDURE*

The step-by-step procedure is as follows:

6.2.1 Record the ambient and pipe wall temperatures. If the ambient temperature is below 400°F, the fusion area must be hoarded and heated prior to any further work.

6.2.2 Clean the heater plate. Do not use metal tools which can scratch the coating. Set the thermostat at the temperature indicated on the Bonding Procedure Chart (normally 450°F). Plug in the heater plate and insert in protection box.

6.2.3 Identify pipe size and thickness. Select the correct heating cycle and fusion cycle pressure from the Fusion Bonding Procedure Charts. Calculate the machine pressures required using Figure 1. Record all information of the Fusion Bonding Report.

6.2.4 Set up all pipe roller supports and appropriate clamps as required.

6.2.5 Clean the inside and outside surface at the ends of the pipes to be fused using a clean dry cloth. Insert the pipe into the fusion machine so the ends extend approximately 1" past the alignment clamps. Adjust for alignment Bring the pipes together. Record the drag pressure which is required to push the pipes together as indicated on the hydraulic pressure gauge. This value must be added onto the

---

overall heating and fusion cycle pressures. As well, check for gross misalignment. Alignment may be improved by rotating one of the pipes 90° to 180°. Remember, the maximum misalignment permitted at any location is 10% of the wall thickness.

6.2.6 Position the facing tool in between pipe ends. Cut the pipe until the stops on each side of the facer are in contact with the clamp bushings. Separate the pipes and check for squareness of the cuts. DO NOT TOUCH THE PIPE SURFACE AT ANY TIME AFTER THIS STEP. Oil from the hands will contaminate the pipe and cause a poor bond.

6.2.7 Bring the two pipes together and check for misalignment. If misalignment is greater than 10% of the wall thickness at any single location, it must be corrected. Minor misalignment may be corrected by tightening the inside clamps at the high side. If the inside clamps have been adjusted or 15 minutes has elapsed since the pipe has been trimmed to when the heater plate is inserted, it will be necessary to re-trim the pipe.

6.2.8 Verify that the heater plate is at the desired fusion temperature by examining the thermometer. It is necessary to check the heater plate temperature accuracy four times daily using a contact pyrometer. The temperature shall be checked at 450 intervals around both sides of the heater plate circumference. The allowable variance is ± 10°F.

6.2.9 Separate the pipe ends and insert the heater plate, making sure that the plate does not come into contact with the pipe. Bring the two ends together against the heater. Apply, by hand or automatic actuation, the calculated heating pressure plus the drag pressure to the pipe ends. This pressure shall be applied until the appearance of a bead starts to form around the entire circumference on the outside of the pipe. Once this bead has initially formed, the pressure shall be removed, and the heating time shall start. The heating time is identified on the Fusion Bonding Procedure Chart 1. The "melt" should form an even bead around the entire circumference. Uneven melt beads are an indication that one side of the pipe is hotter than the other. This may occur during a cold sunny day, when the sun heats up the top of the cold pipe. It may be necessary to insulate the top of the pipe, to prevent this form of uneven heating. Wind and rain may also cause uneven pipe temperatures. The fusion bonding area should be protected from weather.

6.2.10 Once the heating time has elapsed, the pipes shall be separated away from the heater plate. The heater plate shall be quickly removed and the pipes brought back together with the fusion pressure, plus drag applied. The pressure shall be applied for the time indicated on the Fusion Bonding Procedure Chart 1. Upon squeezing the pipe ends together, the force applied should cause the bead to roll back onto the pipe producing an even bead around the circumference.

Note: If the bead does not "roll over" back onto the pipe in an even manner, it is an indication of a faulty fusion, and the fusion bond shall be cut out and redone. When removing the heater plate, if it comes in contact with the pipe where the melt is disturbed, the fusion shall be stopped and redone. If it takes longer than 12 seconds to remove the plate and squeeze the pipes, the fusion shall be stopped and redone.

---

6.2.11 Remove the fusion joint from the machine after the required fusion time indicated and Step 10 and when the fingernail not causes an indent into the bead. Do not apply any stress to the joint for a minimum of another 60 minutes.

## 7.0 Scope-But-Fusion Bonder Qualification Testing - HDPE Piping

This specification Identifies the procedures used for qualifying a butt fusion bonder in general accordance with ASME B31.3, par. A328.2.5.

### 7.1 Qualification Test Coupons

**7.1.1** To each bonder shall qualify a fusion based on a 4" to 12" coupon. The test shall be performed on the maximum SDR rating intended to be used during production. Passing the qualification test permits the bonder to perform butt fusion on pipes up to 12" in diameter. The coupons shall be sufficiently long enough to fit into the fusion machine.

**7.1.2** To qualify for butt fusion bonding pipes larger than 12", each bonder must pass a qualification test on the maximum size and SDR rating intended to be fused during production.

---

7.1.3 Coupons shall be cut from each 1200 section of the bond. Four coupons shall be extracted from each 1200 section. The coupons shall be 12" thick through the thickness of the pipe wall perpendicular to the bond. Refer to Figure 1, Paragraph 4.3.

## 7.2 Visual Examination

7.2.1 The coupon shall be visually examined for soundness. The melted bead shall be uniform around the entire circumference and show complete roll over on both the internal and external side of the joint. There shall be no misalignment.

7.2.2 Melt bead width will depend on the pipe size and thickness and shall not be used as a rejection or acceptance method.

## 7.3 Destructive Tests

7.3.1 Six coupons shall be extracted from the test coupon as indicated in Figure 1. Three 1800 inside face bends and three 1800 outside face bends shall be performed. There shall be no indication of disbondment or separation during the bend test.

7.3.2 Where the thickness of the pipe prevents performing a complete 1800 bend without buckling, the coupon may be cut in half Care shall be exercised to prevent implanting notches into the coupons while performing the cutting operation (remove any suspicious notches).

7.3.3 If a failure is indicated, upon approval from Suncor, two additional coupons can be removed and tested next two the failure. Failure of any of these two coupons constitutes a failed qualification.

## 7.4 Hydro Testing

7.4.1 If requested by Suncor, the full size coupon shall be hydrotested at four times the design pressure of the pipe in accordance with ASME B31.3, par. A328.52.

## 7.5 Bonder's Re-Qualification Testing

7.5.1 Bonder's who have their fusion bonds fail the above testing, shall not be re-tested without Suncor's QA approval.

## 7.6 Records

7.6.1 The fabricator shall maintain records of all bonders who have passed the above qualification testing. The record shall include the BPS qualified, size of and SDR rating of coupon qualified and date.
