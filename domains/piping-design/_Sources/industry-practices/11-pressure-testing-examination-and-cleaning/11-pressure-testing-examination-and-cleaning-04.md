# 2 Pressure Testing

## 2.1 General

1. This section specifies multiple types of testing for pipe systems. When reference is made to a "proof" test it is referring to that required by the applicable code (i.e., hydro or pneumatic test) to demonstrate that the pipe system can withstand the intended design pressure times some factor (1.1 thru 1.5 times design pressure). When the reference is made to a "leak" test, the intent of that requirement is merely to prove that the assembly does not leak at a pressure up to its rated maximums. In all cases involving fabricated piping, a proof test must be conducted. The leak test may only be used to verify the integrity of a flanged or threaded joint which may not have been included in the proof test.

2. For all pressurized proof testing (pneumatic and hydrostatic), the test pressure shall be recorded on a two-pen pressure/temperature chart recorder or Company-approved equivalent. The pressure recorder shall be calibrated with a dead weight tester at least six months prior to the test and calibration certification information shall be retained as part of the project files. The recorder range shall be such that the test pressure is above 75 percent of the chart's range for accuracy and readability. The use of alternative types of test pressure recorders (i.e., digital) is permitted only with Company's Engineer approval.

3. The hydrotest pressure shall also be monitored with a bourdon tube pressure gauge that has been calibrated with a dead weight tester within the last 30 days.

4. Calibration records for gauges and chart recording equipment (or equivalent) used for testing shall be provided to the Company and shall become part of the permanent project documentation.

## 2.2 Reinforcing Pads

1. Each reinforcing pad (or segment) for branch connections shall be provided with a 1/4 in. diameter NPT threaded hole for testing and venting.

   a. Test and vent holes shall not be plugged, except each opening shall be filled with heavy grease or sealant after testing for corrosion protection.

   b. Each reinforcing pad (or segment) for structural attachment shall be provided with one untapped 1/4 in. diameter hole for venting,

   c. The weld at the perimeter of branch reinforcing pads and structural attachment pads, such as wear pads, shall be a continuous full fillet weld. Each branch reinforcing pad (or segment thereof) shall be tested with air at a pressure of not less than at gauge 15 psi for a minimum of 10 minutes.

2. A bubble test solution (such as Snoop® or Company-approved equivalent) shall be applied to inspect all welds.

---

a. When the inside surface of branch weld reinforcing pads cannot be visually inspected, pressure tightness (as evidenced by no loss of pressure) shall be determined by a pressure gauge.

## 2.3 Pressure Test Procedures

1. Piping shall not be pressure tested at metal temperatures below 35° F or below the piping design minimum temperature (i.e., minimum design metal temperature [MDMT]).

2. Fabricator may submit request to use test mediums other than water, and testing plan for Owner's approval in reasonable time before testing is scheduled.

3. Test/wash fluid quality shall be as follows:

    a. For carbon steel pipe systems, potable water with a maximum chloride content of 250 parts per million (ppm) is preferred.

4. When austenitic stainless steel piping/equipment designed to operate above 1500 psi (105 bar) will be exposed to hydrostatic test water, the water shall have less than 50 ppm (50 mg/kg) chloride ions and a pH between 6 and 9.

5. When stainless steel not covered by Item (1), above, or when aluminum materials will be exposed to hydrostatic test or wash water, only water having less than 250 ppm (250 mg/kg) chloride ions and a pH between 6 and 9 shall be used. Potable water will meet this requirement.

6. All equipment and piping fabricated with stainless steel exposed to hydrostatic test or wash water shall be drained immediately after hydrostatic testing or water washing and dried by blowing with air [less than 140°F (60°C)]. When air-drying stainless steel process stream analyzers, the air used shall be clean and filtered.

7. The flange gaskets used for hydro or pneumatic testing shall be of the same type and grade as that to be used in the final piping installation.

8. Test procedures and test schedules of lines or systems shall be reviewed by the Company's Engineer prior to the start of the test. Procedures shall include the following:

    a. Source and disposition of test fluid.

    b. Provisions to prevent the creation of significant negative pressure (vacuum) during draining of the test fluid, etc.

    c. Specific lines to be included within a system test. A spreadsheet should be presented that includes line #'s, isometric sheet #'s, P&ID's, test medium, duration, test hold time, pressure and temperature. Most of this information, including test pressures are indicated on Engineers line summary which can be adapted for purpose.

---

d. Provisions for temporary supports (where piping may not be designed for test fluid), vent, drain, fill connections, and gauge locations.

e. A section documenting, marking and tracking hydrotested pipe spools that will be shipped loose with modules.

9. All leak tests shall be witnessed and accepted by the Inspector. The test results shall be recorded, and a copy shall be provided to the Company.

## 2.4 Proof Test Pressures

Hydrostatic test pressure shall be determined per the applicable code but shall be at least 100 psig (890 kPa), except as follows:

1. For ASME B16.5 material groups 1.1 through 2.5, the test pressure for Class 150 piping systems up through size NPS 24 shall be equal to 1.5 times the working pressure of Class 150 flanges at 100°F.

2. Test pressure for Class 300 piping systems for ASME B16.5 material groups 1.1 through 2.5 and up through size NPS 24 shall be set equal to the lower of:

    a. 1.5 times the rated working pressure of Class 300 flanges at 100°F.

    b. 1.5 times the maximum pressure rating of the limiting system component at 100°F. Where a system component limits the maximum working pressure of the piping system, consideration shall be given to isolating the limiting component from the test circuit so as to allow for an increase in the test pressure.

    c. 1.5 times the piping system design pressure.

3. The hydrotest pressure shall be maintained for the following duration:

    d. Aboveground piping, including piping spools, should be held for a minimum of one (1) hour or longer as required to thoroughly inspect all joints, after the system pressure has been stabilized. Since the minimum hold time per ASME B31.3 is 10 minutes, a Company representative may allow hold times of less than one hour if circumstances call for it and every weld is inspected under pressure.

    e. The above hydrostatic test pressure durations may be increased as a function of line criticality, fluid contents (toxic, H2S, etc.), pressure rating (Class 1500, 2500, etc.) or line location (i.e., proximity of the line to environmentally sensitive areas or the public).

4. Non-metallic piping shall be hydrotested at pressures not to exceed the manufacturer's recommendations.

---

## 2.5 Shop Pressure Testing

1. Hydrostatic (i.e., proof) testing of individual sections of shop fabricated piping (spool sections) is not required if the piping system is to be hydro-tested together as an installed assembly. All hydrostatic testing shall be per ASME B31.3 and the field testing requirements in other sections of this document as applicable.

2. Piping that is to be internally coated shall be hydrostatically tested prior to installation of the coating, unless otherwise approved by the Company's Engineer. Piping spools that are to be galvanized may be tested after galvanizing.

## 2.6 Field Pressure Testing

1. Field pressure proof testing shall be performed in accordance with ASME B31.3, except that use of pneumatic testing shall be subject to approval.

2. It is preferred that pressure testing be performed during daylight hours to facilitate visual examination for leaks.

## 2.7 Preparation for Field Pressure Testing

1. The following piping components and equipment shall not be subjected to piping system pressure testing:

   a. Rotating machinery, such as pumps, turbines, and compressors

   b. Pressure relieving devices, such as rupture discs and pressure relief valves

   c. Expansion joints

   d. Any equipment that does not have a specified test pressure at least as great as the piping test pressure.

   e. Vessels that do not satisfy impact requirements at the piping test temperature

   f. Equipment lined with uncured phosphate or chemically bonded castable materials, unless approved by the Company.

   g. Locally mounted instruments and indicating pressure gauges, where the test pressure would exceed their scale range

   h. Other equipment as designated by the Company's Engineer.

2. Piping containing soft seated valves shall be flushed and drained before the valves are installed.

3. Control valves shall be excluded from pressure testing and line flushing to protect the valves from dirt, scale, and foreign objects. The valves may be replaced with spool pieces or the ends of the pipe blanked off, as appropriate.

---

4. All restrictions that would interfere with filling, venting, draining, or flushing shall not be installed until after completion of the pressure test and line flushing operations. This includes orifice plates, flow nozzles, venturi tubes, variable area meters, positive displacement (PD) meters, turbine magnetic meters, strainers, and any other similar in-line equipment.

5. Ensure valves subject to hydrotest are in the open position and the piping is filled with water in the direction of flow if a check valve is installed.

6. Engineered supports (Spring cans / hangers) shall have there shipping stops left in during testing.

7. Instrument takeoff piping and sampling system piping, up to the first block valve (i.e., "root valve"), shall be leak tested with the piping or equipment to which it is connected.

8. Instrument lead lines, between the first block valve and the instruments to which they are connected, shall be leak tested to the test pressure of the associated piping or equipment. Testing may be performed separately or at the same time as the piping or equipment is tested, but the instruments shall be disconnected.

9. Joints, including all welds and bonds, shall normally be left unprimed and unpainted for examination during leak testing. This requirement can be waived upon OSA request and documented in the test plan. This requirement applies to the following:

   a. Joints located in environmentally sensitive areas, when specified by the Company's Engineer.

   b. Joints located in buried piping systems.

   c. Joints shall be left uninsulated during leak testing.

## 2.8 Hydrostatic Testing

1. A strainer shall be installed in the fill line to minimize the possibility of foreign matter being introduced into the system during the hydrostatic testing and line flushing operations.

2. The following shall not be tested with water:

   a. Instrument air supply lines

   b. Air lines to air motor operated valves

   c. Hydraulic power piping and tubing

   d. Lube, seal, and control oil piping

   e. Liquid natural gas (LNG) piping

   f. Nitrogen systems.

---

3. Lines in hydrofluoric (HF) acid service shall not be tested with water, unless approved by the Company's Engineer. If HF acid piping is tested with water, the piping system shall be flushed and dried to ensure all water is removed prior to pipe commissioning.

## 2.9 Pneumatic Testing

1. Pneumatic proof testing of instrument air piping and tubing shall be performed with dry instrument air following test pressure indicated on the line summary.

2. Pneumatic proof testing of other piping is acceptable only if the Company's Engineer has approved the test procedure and requires an approved technical MOC. If deemed acceptable, a detailed pneumatic leak testing procedure shall be assembled and approved by the Company's Engineer. The procedure shall include, at a minimum, the following: stored energy calculations, brittle fracture info of equipment to be tested, assumptions made, radius of exposure (i.e., personnel exclusion zones), test duration, and leak identification methods. The procedure must also include an initial "low" test pressure hold and inspect step as well as a "high" pressure hold and inspect step along with ramp up rate information.

# 3 Leak Testing and Purging

This section defines the method in which hydrocarbon service systems are to be leak tested (Service, Hydraulic, Bubble, and nitrogen [N2]/helium [He]) and purged after mechanical completion but prior to the introduction of hydrocarbons to prove the integrity of the system and provide safe startup conditions. The joint reliability (i.e., leak free state) of a pipe system is proven by a combination of 1) holding at a leak test pressure for a specified period of time, and 2) visual inspection of all joints not previously leak or proof tested or that were disassembled and then reassembled after proof testing.

## 3.1 General

1. Procedures used for leak testing and inert gas purging of hydrocarbon service systems shall be in accordance with the following requirements, unless superseded by more stringent local regulations.

2. The installation contractor shall be responsible for performing the following:

   a. Leak testing to prove the integrity of the mechanically completed system at the original construction site as a commissioning activity, and

   b. Leak testing at the systems' final destination following installation as a commissioning activity to reconfirm system integrity which may have been compromised during transit and installation due to equipment and piping movements.

3. Systems shall not be accepted until the required leak testing documentation has been completed and verified by the Company.

4. Leak testing shall be performed primarily to prove system integrity and secondarily to purge hydrocarbon service systems prior to the introduction of hydrocarbons.

5. ASME SEC V, Article 10 can be used as a guideline for leak testing, especially for helium mass spectrometer instrument calibration, but should be recognized as a component testing technique rather than a system strategy. In particular, it specifies a 10 percent helium mixture rather than a 1 percent helium mixture as used in large volume testing.

6. The Scope of leak testing includes testing of all hydrocarbon service joints and other potential external leak paths to atmosphere such as valve stem seals. This item also includes Company identified critical valve seats using ultrasonic leak detecting equipment for internal valve hold integrity.

7. Prior to pressurizing a system with nitrogen, a gross leak test with air shall be performed to the lesser of 100 psig or 10 percent of the equipment's maximum design pressure rating.

## 3.2 Safety Guidelines

Contractor shall take steps to ensure that safety is not compromised in any way during the preparation or performance of leak testing and/or inert gas purging.

1. The procedure shall include the Job Safety Analysis, which addresses:

   a. Permitting and energy isolation (lockout/tagout).

   b. Overpressure protection provisions (i.e., pressure safety valve [PSV], burst plate, etc.), and

   c. Exclusion zone distances and personnel safety provisions during joint inspection.

2. Contractor shall adhere to, at a minimum, the maintenance and construction requirements for working with nitrogen (N2) as defined by OSHA, 1910. In addition, all leak test supervisors, operators and technicians shall be competent for their respective roles.

3. When systems containing multiple pressure ratings are being tested, the maximum leak test pressure shall be based on the system with the lowest pressure rating.

## 3.4 Leak Test Medium and Inspection Method

The leak test medium to be utilized shall be approved by Company's Engineer. Flanged connections selected for leak checking shall be wrapped with tape to facilitate leak detection via a small opening in the tape. Leaks shall be clearly marked for repair. All witnessed joins shall be documented in the facility's permanent records. This includes a signature of a witnessed Joint Certificate by the Contractor and Company representative.

## 3.5 Service Testing

As used in this document, "service testing" is defined as leak testing using the process fluid as a medium.

1. For new, noncommissioned process systems, service testing is permitted only in Category D (as defined in ASME B31.3) fluid services such as water and instrument air piping.

2. For existing systems or post maintenance commissioning of gas systems, service testing is permitted only for non-lethal process systems in locations that are adequately ventilated or that can tolerate a hydrocarbon gas leak.

---

3. For existing systems or post maintenance commissioning of liquid hydrocarbon systems, service testing is permitted only for non-lethal process fluids where there are adequate provisions to isolate and safely drain the contents of the system in the event of a leak.

4. The acceptance criterion for service testing shall be as follows:

   a. For liquid systems: a visual inspection of the broken joints during re-commissioning to ensure zero leakage. The joints shall be monitored for an initial period of 30 minutes. After that an inspection shall be made every 12 hours for a period of at least 2 days.

   b. For hydrocarbon gas process systems: all joints subject to a service test must pass regular gas leak testing (i.e., with gas meter) during an initial monitoring period of no less than 30 minutes. Further tests shall be carried out every 12 hours for a period of at least 2 days. The acceptance criterion shall be reached when the gas detector indicates there is no hydrocarbon gas in the proximity of the joints.

## 3.6 Hydraulic Leak Testing

1. This form of leak testing involves the use of nitrogen as a means of pressurization of a pipe system filled with a liquid test medium (preferably water).

2. Hydraulic leak testing is only permitted for Category D fluid services (as defined in ASME B31.3) such as potable water.

3. For hydraulic testing the test should be conducted for a minimum of 30 minutes. A test shall be deemed successful if no significant reduction in pressure [no more than 10 psig (68.7 kPa)] is observed over the test period and all joints have been visually inspected for leakage. The acceptance criterion is zero leakage.

## 3.7 Soap Bubble Leak Testing

This method of leak testing involves the pressurizing of a system with nitrogen and then testing the joints in question with a soap solution.

1. This method is only suitable for leak testing of Category D (as defined in ASME B31.3) fluids and non-lethal hydrocarbon systems, liquid or gas, up to, and including, Class 300 rating. See "N2/He Leak Testing" criteria below for systems outside of this boundary.

2. The acceptance criterion for bubble leak testing is that there should be no continuous formation of bubble growth detected within 90 seconds of application.

---

## 3.8 N2/He Leak Testing

A 99 percent N2 and 1 percent He gas mixture may be used for the purposes of leak testing. Contractor shall measure and confirm the prescribed composition prior to testing by cross checking with a certified sample to allow any necessary corrections to be made to the calibration of the helium mass spectrometer. Company shall witness and note the measured helium content in the mixture.

1. This method is suitable for leak testing of lethal (i.e., H2S) systems and hydrocarbons above Class 300 rating.

2. The composition of the actual mixture to be used during testing shall be compared to a sample of known composition (i.e., from sample bottle) using a helium mass spectrometer with remote probe. The Company's Engineer shall witness and document that the readings taken from the test gas match those of the mixture in the pipe system.

3. The joints in question are to be tested using the helium mass spectrometer with a remote probe. If a leak is detected, the mass spectrometer shall be used to estimate the leak rate. The measured leak rate shall be expressed in units of Standard Cubic Feet Per Year (scf/yr). Leak rates shall not exceed the criteria listed in Table 1.

## 3.9 Leak Test Pressure

1. For the leak test methods described in this section, either of the following leak test pressure criteria shall be observed:

   a. Maximum of 90 percent of the pressure relief valve (PRV) set point, or

   b. Not to exceed 90 percent of the maximum design pressure (DP) or maximum allowable working pressure (MAWP) of the system. In the case where equipment of different pressure ratings is being tested together, in no case shall the leak test pressure exceed 90 percent of the lowest system DP or MAWP.

   c. With the exception of bubble testing, the minimum pressure shall not be less than the maximum normal operating pressure.

   d. For initial leak checks using bubble (i.e., Snoop®) testing (with air or N2), the pressure shall not exceed lesser of 101 psig or 90 percent of the maximum design pressure of the system.

2. In all cases, the entire system being leak tested shall be protected against overpressure by use of one or more PRVs. Where a relief valve is not permanently installed in the system being tested or in cases where a section of the system being tested is unprotected, a temporary PRV shall be installed

3. The system shall be pressurized in stages at no more than 25 percent increments of the ultimate test pressure, unless the test pressure is less than 200 psig, in which case 50 percent increments

---

may be used and held for not less than 10 minutes between stages. After the hold period, a line walk shall be performed to identify any audible leaks which may be present.

4. Upon successful completion of a TP, the system shall be depressurized to permit restoration of the pipe system to its final operational configuration (e.g. blind, spectacle, etc., removal).

5. When all leak testing has been completed, purging shall commence. Purging is complete when the oxygen content of the system has been reduced to the specified maximums (i.e., 4 percent for all hydrocarbon systems).

## 4 Piping System Cleanliness

1. During field storage, all covers shall remain on major pipe components (valves, pipe joints, large fittings, etc.). During field fabrication, all covers shall remain on pipe components as long as possible so foreign matter, rodents, etc., will not enter the piping system. Prior to flange joint assembly on suction lines to rotating equipment, the Owner's Engineer shall witness with recorded sign off of the removal of temporary covers and pipe internal cleanliness, which shall apply each and every disassembly and reassembly of a flange joint in piping to rotary equipment.

2. After completion of piping system pressure tests, all piping shall be cleaned by "flushing," "blowing" or "chemical cleaning." (High point vents and valved low point drains shall be accessible for these activities). An exception is large piping that can be cleaned internally by manual methods.

3. In general, hydrocarbon lines shall be flushed with clean potable water, unless water is not permitted in the piping system or is not available in sufficient quantity or at high enough pressure. Blowing with dry air is an acceptable alternative if air is available in sufficient pressure and volume.

---

4. The Owner's Engineer shall approve flushing fluids and flushing, draining and drying procedures, and schedules. These procedures shall include complete descriptions of flushing fluids, source of fluids, proposed use of de-watering devices such as plastic foam pigs and disposition of fluids. The requirements for temporary supports, items to be included or excluded, temporary strainers, filling, and venting of piping systems during flushing shall be specified. All flushing, draining and drying shall be witnessed and approved by the Owner's Engineer.

5. The Owner shall approve procedures and schedules for piping protection after testing, flushing, draining, drying, and pre-commission cleaning until start-up.

| 4 | Alkaline detergent wash for oil and grease removal. |
| 5 | Solvent cleaning with acids or chelants, for rust and mill scale removal. |
| 6 | Post cleaning passivation: Prevention of after-rusting of cleaned surfaces by chemical treatment. |
| 7 | Post cleaning protection: Vapor phase inhibitor coating with a lubricant compatible rust preventive except for non-lubricated compressors and those in refrigeration service, or nitrogen blanket. Systems constructed entirely of stainless steel do not require the above. Blind, plug, or cap all piping joints left temporarily disconnected. |

## 4.2 Restrictions and Precautions during Cleaning

1. Equipment containing austenitic materials shall not be contacted with:

   a. Acid solutions containing halides and chemicals such as hydrochloric acid. Alternative solvents are acceptable provided the chloride content does not exceed 50 ppm in acidic solvents and 250 ppm in neutral and alkaline solvents.

   b. Caustic soda (NaOH) solutions unless:

      i. Chloride level is less than 250 ppm to avoid chloride stress corrosion cracking.

      ii. Caustic solutions are diluted and flushed prior to startup to avoid caustic stress corrosion cracking.

   c. If degreasing is required, sodium carbonate and trisodium phosphate solutions may be used provided the chloride content does not exceed 250 ppm.

   d. Rinse water that exceeds 250 ppm chlorides.

   e. Hot air used to dry equipment such that metal temperatures will exceed 125°F (50°C).

---

2. Chemical solutions shall not be introduced into equipment unless high point vents and low point drains are available to ensure proper filling and complete removal of solutions.

3. Heat shall not be applied directly to equipment containing acid solutions. Boilers may be fired for degreasing, but acid solutions must be diluted and heated externally to the equipment.

4. Process systems may be cleaned by flushing out the lines with water at a minimum velocity of 10 ft./s.

## 4.3 Cleaning Fuel Lines to Burners

1. Fuel lines to burners may be cleaned by using steam and air, alternately, to dislodge scale.

2. When the lines are clean after the last air blowing, the burner connections shall be reinstalled and the piping blown with air through the burners.

3. Each burner shall be visually checked to ensure the orifices are not obstructed.

## 4.4 Centrifugal Compressor

1. Particular attention shall be given to the cleaning of compressor and blower. Generally, the cleaning methods listed in Table 2 may be used. Additionally, the piping cleanliness shall be inspected and approved by a Company rotating equipment specialist before final acceptance.

2. The final cleaning of all compressor and blower systems shall be performed after the hydrostatic test of the piping and as close as feasible to the initial run of the equipment.

3. After final assembly of the lubricating, seal, and control oil systems at the site, the filter casings, cooler shells, and other system components shall be opened and wiped clean.

4. Bearings shall be bypassed during initial flushing. Final flush shall be through the bearings.

5. The method of checking the cleanliness of the system shall be determined by the equipment manufacturer's representative, but in no case shall it exceed any one of the following criteria:

   a. Contain more particles per milliliter than AS4059 7B as per SAE AS 4059, or

   b. Contain no more than Scale number 8 on the 14 micron reference (3rd set of digits of the standard cleanliness designation), of BS BS ISO 4406, or

   c. No more than 6 non-metallic particles captured by a 100 mesh screen after 30 minutes of flushing at a normal lubrication design flow rate for the particular machine in question.

## 4.5 Positive Displacement Compressor Piping

1. Compressor suction piping shall be chemically cleaned. (Refer to Appendix B and C of this document for typical chemical cleaning procedure.)

---

2. The compressor piping and equipment to be cleaned includes:

   a. The first-stage suction line between the knockout drum or filter and the compressor

   b. Intercoolers

   c. Interstage suction lines

   d. Bypass lines

   e. Suction and interstage pulsation dampers.

3. Orifice plates and other potential obstacles to effective cleaning shall be removed from the piping system during the cleaning operation.

## Appendix A: Hydrochloric Acid Chemical Cleaning of Carbon Steel Piping

1. This procedure may be used to chemically clean suction piping to compressor inlets and discharge piping from compressor outlets to the non-return valve, recycle valve and other piping requiring chemical cleaning. The chemical operations shall be performed with hydrochloric acid in a continuous and rapid manner, particularly the rinsing and passivating stages following the acid cleaning cycle, because carbon steel is highly susceptible to corrosion during these critical stages. The set-up, quantities or agents, volumes of water and other factors are system dependent.

2. It is highly recommended to test the effectiveness of the corrosion inhibitor and passivation solution prior to cleaning the piping. This is done by exposing carbon steel test coupons to the complete chemical cleaning operation prior to the start of hydrochloric acid cleaning.

### A–1. Precleaning

1. Obtain all safe work permits and conduct safety meetings. Check personnel protection, such as eye washes, showers and other equipment.

2. Mechanically prepare system for flow, ensuring that system has low-point drains, high-point vents and circulation connections.

3. Establish circulation of potable water from pumping equipment via chemical circulating hoses and/or temporary piping.

    a. Circulate water through the piping and exit piping system via chemical hoses and/or temporary piping back to the circulation tank.

    b. Vent the system at high points to eliminate air pockets.

    c. Test hoses and connections for leaks.

    d. Make repairs as needed.

4. Hydrostatic test system with potable water at 100 psi prior to injecting chemicals. Place a representative sample (a piece of pipe or any small spool pieces with the same type of fouling system) in the circulation tank.

### A–2. Degreasing

1. The first step in chemical cleaning is degreasing of the piping surfaces which is performed to remove all traces of oil or grease adhering to pipe surfaces prior to acid cleaning:

    a. Degreasing shall be performed by the use of suitable heavy-duty alkaline or detergent cleaning solutions, maintained at temperatures between 180°F and 200°F for one or more hours, depending on the degree of contamination.

---

b. Products having cleaning power similar to a 3 percent solution of Houghton Clean 240 (E. F. Houghton & Co.) or Alconox (Alconox, Inc.) are satisfactory.

2. Piping surfaces shall be thoroughly rinsed or flushed with potable water for at least 15 minutes to remove all traces of the degreasing solution from the piping surfaces.

## A-3. Acidizing

1. A 5 to 10 percent (by volume) solution of inhibited hydrochloric acid (HCl), at a temperature not exceeding 159°F, shall be circulated in the piping for one hour or longer until the oxide scale is completely removed:

   a. A corrosion inhibitor with properties similar to Rodine 213 or 214 (Amchem Products, Inc.) or A-120 (Dow Chemical Service) is satisfactory.

   b. Inhibitor concentrations shall be in accordance with vendor recommendations.

2. The strength of the acid shall be monitored to maintain the required concentration.

## A-4. Flushing

1. The piping shall be thoroughly rinsed and/or flushed with potable water as rapidly as possible to rid the surfaces of all traces of excess acid or iron salts and to prevent the formation of rust.

2. All drains and blanked ends shall be opened for the rinsing process. Rinsing shall continue for at least 20 minutes after the pH of the effluent water equals the pH of the influent water.

3. Collection and proper disposal of acidized flush water is required.

## A-5. Passivation

1. Passivation of piping surfaces shall be performed as rapidly as possible after the rinsing cycle. Passivating solutions, conferring residual corrosion protection, equal to or better than a 1.0 percent solution of sodium nitrite (NaNO2), are satisfactory.

2. Following passivation, the piping shall be dried by purging with nitrogen gas or dry filtered air at a temperature maintained between 150°F and 170°F. This shall be followed by a visual inspection of internal piping surfaces to ensure that they are free from traces of contamination.

3. Following the drying process, a permanent nitrogen blanket is recommended.

## Appendix B: Ammoniated Citric Acid Chemical Cleaning

This procedure may be used to degrease, pickle, passivate and dry various carbon steel piping systems. The set-up, quantities or agents, volumes of water, etc., are system dependent.

### B-1. Precleaning

1. Obtain all safe work permits and conduct safety meetings.
2. Check personal protection, such as eye washes, showers, and other equipment.
3. Mechanically prepare system for flow, ensuring that system has low-point drains, high-point vents and circulation connections.
4. Establish circulation of potable water from pumping equipment via chemical circulating hoses and/or temporary piping:
   a. Circulate water through the piping and exit piping system via chemical hoses and/or temporary piping back to the circulation tank.
   b. Vent the system at high points to eliminate air pockets.
   c. Test hoses and connections for leaks.
   d. Make repairs as needed.
5. Hydrostatic test system with potable water at 100 psi prior to injecting chemicals. Place a representative sample (a piece of pipe or any small spool pieces with the same type of fouling system) in the circulation tank.

### B-2. Degreasing

1. Add sodium hydroxide to the circulating water until a 0.5 to 1.0 percent (by weight) solution is reached; add 0.4 to 1.0 percent (by volume) surfactant.
2. Heat solution to 160°F to 180°F while circulating through system.
3. Circulate chemical solution for two hours once the solution has reached temperature.
4. Stop circulation, close inlet and outlet valves on system.
5. Place chemical return hose (from system) into vacuum truck, reopen valves and drain or pressure solution from system.

### B-3. Flushing

Fill chemical circulator with potable water and flush system (minimum [2 ft./sec]) into vacuum truck until the pH of the effluent flush water equals the pH of the influent flush water.

---

## B–4. Acidizing

1. Establish circulation and hydrotest the system per steps 4 and 5 in Section C-1 of this Appendix.
2. Add acid corrosion inhibitor at a rate of 4 lbs. inhibitor per 100 lbs. citric acid or per vendor recommendation.
3. Add 3.0 percent by weight citric acid to circulating water.
4. Add aqueous ammonia to circulating solution until a pH of 3.0 to 4.0 is reached.
5. Elevate temperature of solution to 160°F to 170°F.
6. Circulate at minimum (2 ft/sec) at the elevated temperature for 6 to 8 hours or until titrations and/or a representative sample (see step 5 in Section C-1 of this Appendix) indicates complete removal of iron oxide, mill scale and weld slag.

## B–5. Passivation

1. Lower temperature of acid solution to 130°F to 140°F. Add 0.5 percent excess (by weight) citric acid to circulating solution.
2. Add aqueous ammonia to circulating citric acid solution until a pH of 9.5 is reached.
3. Add 20 lbs. of soda ash per 1000 gallons circulating volume to ensure that elevated pH remains high.
4. Add 1.0 percent sodium nitrate to solution and aerate solution with compressed air. Circulate for one hour at 130°F to 140°F.
5. Drain and/or purge solution from system per steps 4 and 5 in Section C-2 of this Appendix.

## B–6. Draining and Drying System

1. Following passivation, the piping shall be dried by purging it with nitrogen gas or dry filtered air at a temperature maintained between 150°F and 170°F.
2. This shall be followed by a visual inspection of internal piping surfaces to ensure that they are free from traces of contamination.
