3. For existing systems or post maintenance commissioning of liquid hydrocarbon systems, service testing is permitted only for non-lethal process fluids where there are adequate provisions to isolate and safely drain the contents of the system in the event of a leak.

4. The acceptance criterion for service testing shall be as follows:

   a. For liquid systems: a visual inspection of the broken joints during re-commissioning to ensure zero leakage. The joints shall be monitored for an initial period of 30 minutes. After that an inspection shall be made every 12 hours for a period of at least 2 days.

   b. For hydrocarbon gas process systems: all joints subject to a service test must pass regular gas leak testing (i.e., with gas meter) during an initial monitoring period of no less than 30 minutes. Further tests shall be carried out every 12 hours for a period of at least 2 days. The acceptance criterion shall be reached when the gas detector indicates there is no hydrocarbon gas in the proximity of the joints.

## 3.6 Hydraulic Leak Testing

1. This form of leak testing involves the use of nitrogen as a means of pressurization of a pipe system filled with a liquid test medium (preferably water).

2. Hydraulic leak testing is only permitted for Category D fluid services (as defined in ASME B31.3) such as potable water.

3. For hydraulic testing the test should be conducted for a minimum of 30 minutes. A test shall be deemed successful if no significant reduction in pressure [no more than 10 psig (68.7 kPa)] is observed over the test period and all joints have been visually inspected for leakage. The acceptance criterion is zero leakage.

## 3.7 Soap Bubble Leak Testing

This method of leak testing involves the pressurizing of a system with nitrogen and then testing the joints in question with a soap solution.

1. This method is only suitable for leak testing of Category D (as defined in ASME B31.3) fluids and non-lethal hydrocarbon systems, liquid or gas, up to, and including, Class 300 rating. See "N2/He Leak Testing" criteria below for systems outside of this boundary.

2. The acceptance criterion for bubble leak testing is that there should be no continuous formation of bubble growth detected within 90 seconds of application.
