3. Test packages (TPs) shall include a complete set of marked up Process and Instrumentation Diagrams (P&IDs). The TP system limits shall comprise as large a system as possible while maintaining a common relief pressure and shall clearly identify:

   a. Test boundaries

   b. Isolation valves

   c. Pressurization and depressurization points

   d. Primary and secondary vent paths

   e. Test and local pressure gauges

   f. Trapped gas opportunities and local bleed points, and

   g. Critical valves.

4. A valve checklist identifying all valves, within the limits of the leak test along with their required valve positions (open vs. closed), shall be contained within the TP. Line valves within the TP limits declared open shall be half open while testing for stem seal and/or packing leaks. All remotely actuated valves should be highlighted to permit adequate advance planning and preparation to open or close the valves as required.

5. The leak testing procedure shall include a detailed plan to safely facilitate re-use of large volumes of test medium in other systems.

## 3.4 Leak Test Medium and Inspection Method

The leak test medium to be utilized shall be approved by Company's Engineer. Flanged connections selected for leak checking shall be wrapped with tape to facilitate leak detection via a small opening in the tape. Leaks shall be clearly marked for repair. All witnessed joins shall be documented in the facility's permanent records. This includes a signature of a witnessed Joint Certificate by the Contractor and Company representative.

## 3.5 Service Testing

As used in this document, "service testing" is defined as leak testing using the process fluid as a medium.

1. For new, noncommissioned process systems, service testing is permitted only in Category D (as defined in ASME B31.3) fluid services such as water and instrument air piping.

2. For existing systems or post maintenance commissioning of gas systems, service testing is permitted only for non-lethal process systems in locations that are adequately ventilated or that can tolerate a hydrocarbon gas leak.
