b. Products having cleaning power similar to a 3 percent solution of Houghton Clean 240 (E. F. Houghton & Co.) or Alconox (Alconox, Inc.) are satisfactory.

2. Piping surfaces shall be thoroughly rinsed or flushed with potable water for at least 15 minutes to remove all traces of the degreasing solution from the piping surfaces.

## A-3. Acidizing

1. A 5 to 10 percent (by volume) solution of inhibited hydrochloric acid (HCl), at a temperature not exceeding 159°F, shall be circulated in the piping for one hour or longer until the oxide scale is completely removed:

   a. A corrosion inhibitor with properties similar to Rodine 213 or 214 (Amchem Products, Inc.) or A-120 (Dow Chemical Service) is satisfactory.

   b. Inhibitor concentrations shall be in accordance with vendor recommendations.

2. The strength of the acid shall be monitored to maintain the required concentration.

## A-4. Flushing

1. The piping shall be thoroughly rinsed and/or flushed with potable water as rapidly as possible to rid the surfaces of all traces of excess acid or iron salts and to prevent the formation of rust.

2. All drains and blanked ends shall be opened for the rinsing process. Rinsing shall continue for at least 20 minutes after the pH of the effluent water equals the pH of the influent water.

3. Collection and proper disposal of acidized flush water is required.

## A-5. Passivation

1. Passivation of piping surfaces shall be performed as rapidly as possible after the rinsing cycle. Passivating solutions, conferring residual corrosion protection, equal to or better than a 1.0 percent solution of sodium nitrite (NaNO2), are satisfactory.

2. Following passivation, the piping shall be dried by purging with nitrogen gas or dry filtered air at a temperature maintained between 150°F and 170°F. This shall be followed by a visual inspection of internal piping surfaces to ensure that they are free from traces of contamination.

3. Following the drying process, a permanent nitrogen blanket is recommended.
