## Appendix A: Hydrochloric Acid Chemical Cleaning of Carbon Steel Piping

1. This procedure may be used to chemically clean suction piping to compressor inlets and discharge piping from compressor outlets to the non-return valve, recycle valve and other piping requiring chemical cleaning. The chemical operations shall be performed with hydrochloric acid in a continuous and rapid manner, particularly the rinsing and passivating stages following the acid cleaning cycle, because carbon steel is highly susceptible to corrosion during these critical stages. The set-up, quantities or agents, volumes of water and other factors are system dependent.

2. It is highly recommended to test the effectiveness of the corrosion inhibitor and passivation solution prior to cleaning the piping. This is done by exposing carbon steel test coupons to the complete chemical cleaning operation prior to the start of hydrochloric acid cleaning.

### A–1. Precleaning

1. Obtain all safe work permits and conduct safety meetings. Check personnel protection, such as eye washes, showers and other equipment.

2. Mechanically prepare system for flow, ensuring that system has low-point drains, high-point vents and circulation connections.

3. Establish circulation of potable water from pumping equipment via chemical circulating hoses and/or temporary piping.

    a. Circulate water through the piping and exit piping system via chemical hoses and/or temporary piping back to the circulation tank.

    b. Vent the system at high points to eliminate air pockets.

    c. Test hoses and connections for leaks.

    d. Make repairs as needed.

4. Hydrostatic test system with potable water at 100 psi prior to injecting chemicals. Place a representative sample (a piece of pipe or any small spool pieces with the same type of fouling system) in the circulation tank.

### A–2. Degreasing

1. The first step in chemical cleaning is degreasing of the piping surfaces which is performed to remove all traces of oil or grease adhering to pipe surfaces prior to acid cleaning:

    a. Degreasing shall be performed by the use of suitable heavy-duty alkaline or detergent cleaning solutions, maintained at temperatures between 180°F and 200°F for one or more hours, depending on the degree of contamination.
