## 3.8 N2/He Leak Testing

A 99 percent N2 and 1 percent He gas mixture may be used for the purposes of leak testing. Contractor shall measure and confirm the prescribed composition prior to testing by cross checking with a certified sample to allow any necessary corrections to be made to the calibration of the helium mass spectrometer. Company shall witness and note the measured helium content in the mixture.

1. This method is suitable for leak testing of lethal (i.e., H2S) systems and hydrocarbons above Class 300 rating.

2. The composition of the actual mixture to be used during testing shall be compared to a sample of known composition (i.e., from sample bottle) using a helium mass spectrometer with remote probe. The Company's Engineer shall witness and document that the readings taken from the test gas match those of the mixture in the pipe system.

3. The joints in question are to be tested using the helium mass spectrometer with a remote probe. If a leak is detected, the mass spectrometer shall be used to estimate the leak rate. The measured leak rate shall be expressed in units of Standard Cubic Feet Per Year (scf/yr). Leak rates shall not exceed the criteria listed in Table 1.

## 3.9 Leak Test Pressure

1. For the leak test methods described in this section, either of the following leak test pressure criteria shall be observed:

   a. Maximum of 90 percent of the pressure relief valve (PRV) set point, or

   b. Not to exceed 90 percent of the maximum design pressure (DP) or maximum allowable working pressure (MAWP) of the system. In the case where equipment of different pressure ratings is being tested together, in no case shall the leak test pressure exceed 90 percent of the lowest system DP or MAWP.

   c. With the exception of bubble testing, the minimum pressure shall not be less than the maximum normal operating pressure.

   d. For initial leak checks using bubble (i.e., Snoop®) testing (with air or N2), the pressure shall not exceed lesser of 101 psig or 90 percent of the maximum design pressure of the system.

2. In all cases, the entire system being leak tested shall be protected against overpressure by use of one or more PRVs. Where a relief valve is not permanently installed in the system being tested or in cases where a section of the system being tested is unprotected, a temporary PRV shall be installed
