## 3.0 DEFINITIONS

**3.1** Contractor – Company or business that agrees to furnish materials or perform specified services at a specified price and/or rate to Owner.

**3.2** First Normally Closed Valve – The first valve that is closed (or intended to be closed) for the majority of the time during the operation of the line. It is opened, for only a brief period of time, a few times during the operational duration of the piping system. Examples of normally closed valves include the following:

- The first block valve off of a piping run intended to segregate piping that is connected to an open (atmospheric) system, such as a vent, drain, sample station, or a hose station.
- The first block valve off of a piping run intended to segregate piping that is used only for start-up, shut-down, decocking, chemical injection, or other intermittent services.

**3.3** Fluid Service – A general term concerning the application of a piping system for a given service, considering the combination of fluid properties, operation conditions, and other factors that establish the basis for design and operation of the piping system.

**3.4** Fluid Service and Service Classification – The design requirements in this REP and other associated piping REPs classify fluid service and service as follows:

**3.4.1** Category D Fluid Service (Non-Flammable, Non-Hazardous): a fluid/material service, as defined in accordance to ASME B31.3, in which all of the following apply:

1. The fluid/material handled is non-flammable, non-toxic, and not damaging to human tissues as defined in paragraph 300.2 of ASME B31.3.
2. The design gauge pressure does not exceed 150 psi.
3. The design temperature is from −20°F through 366°F.

Examples of fluids/materials meeting the above criteria include the following:

- Cooling water.
- Steam condensate < 366°F.
- Storm drains piping.
- Hydraulic fluid.
- Treated waste water.
- Nitrogen.
- Air.
- Non-hazardous chemical additives < 366°F.
- Fire water.
- Drinking water.
- Catalyst, spent catalyst < 366°F.
- Boiler feed water < 366°F.
- Steam < 366°F.
- Plumbing and sewers.

**3.4.2** Non-Flammable, Hazardous Fluid Service – A sub-set of Normal Fluid Service as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the potential for personnel exposure to a non-flammable fluid/material service is damaging to the human tissues due to the fluid/material's physical and/or chemical properties and/or its pH.

Examples of fluids/materials meeting the above criteria include the following:

- Acids.
- Caustics.
- Hazardous chemical additives (resulting in chemical burns).
- Process water (some residual HCs), stripped sour water.
- Catalyst, spent catalyst ≥ 366°F.
- Boiler feedwater and steam ≥ 366°F.
- Streams with design gauge pressure exceeding 150 psi but less than or equal to the corresponding gauge pressure allowed by the ASME B16.5 Class 1500 rating.

**3.4.3** Flammable and Combustible Fluid Service – A sub-set of Normal Fluid Service as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the fluid/material is readily ignitable when released to the atmosphere, including flammable liquids; hydrocarbon vapors; and other vapors, gases, or solids. Per NFPA 30, a liquid having a flash point below 100°F and having a vapor pressure not exceeding 40 psia at 100°F is known as a Class I liquid. Class I liquids are subdivided as follows:

- Class IA liquids – liquids with flash points < 73°F and boiling points < 100°F.
- Class IB liquids – liquids with flash points < 73°F and boiling points ≥ 100°F.
- Class IC liquids – liquids with flash points ≥ 73°F but < 100°F.

Per NFPA 30, a combustible liquid is one whose flash point is 100°F or higher, and they are classified as:

- Class II – flash point equal to or greater than 100°F, but less than 140°F.
- Class IIIA – flash point equal to or greater than 140°F, but less than 200°F.
- Class IIIB – flash point equal to or greater than 200°F.

Examples of flammable fluids/materials meeting the above criteria include the following:

- Any flammable and combustible fluids with operating temperatures above their flash points.
- Light hydrocarbons, lighter than 68 degrees API gravity (0.705 specific gravity).
- Flammable services that can auto-refrigerate and lead to brittle fracture.

**3.4.4** Dangerous Fluid Service – A sub-set of Normal Fluid Service as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the fluid/material is damaging to human tissue due to the fluid/material's chemical properties excluding pH.

Examples of fluids/material meeting the above criteria include materials such as the following:

- Phenol.
- Hydrogen sulfide.
- Chlorine.
- Others contained in a stream at concentrations above those where regulation and local health practice may put limits on personnel exposure.

**3.4.5** Aggressive Environmental Service (AES) – A fluid/material service, as defined in REP 10-2-1 and REP 10-2-2.

**3.4.6** Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) Alkylation Service – A service in which the main purpose is to produce a high-octane gasoline-blending component in presence of HF catalyst under conditions selected to optimize alkylate yield and quality. This document specifically refers to two specific processes: (a) the Industry Source 05 licensed process; (b) the UOP HF licensed process.

**3.4.7** High Pressure Fluid Service – A fluid/material service, as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the potential for personnel exposure is judged to be significant and in which Owner specifies the use of Chapter IX of ASME B31.3 for piping design and construction (pressure in excess of that allowed by the ASME B16.5 Class 2500 rating); (see also paragraph K300 of ASME B31.3).

**3.4.8** Normal Fluid Service – A fluid/material service, as defined in accordance to ASME B31.3.

**3.4.9** Fatigue/Cyclic Service – A service in which specific piping components or joints operate under conditions that will produce fatigue damage due to cycling condition. Sources of cycling loads on conditions include but are not limited to the following:

1. Pressure cycling.
2. Thermal cycling.
3. Mechanical load cycling not induced by pressure.
4. Loads associated with vibration that may arise from such sources as impact, pressure pulsation, turbulent flow vortices, resonance in compressors, and wind.
5. Any combination of loads described above.

Specific fatigue service design considerations are provided in ASME B31.3.

Examples of piping components operating in fatigue service include the following:

- Reciprocating compressor piping.
- Centrifugal compressor piping.
- High pressure to low pressure let-down valves.
- Relief valve piping operating below 100% of Relief Valve (RV) setting (relief valve chatter).
- Steam traps.
- Know piping circuits of water hammering.
- Drains with components without proper gussets.
- Piping containing two phase flow which induces cyclic stresses.

Other classifications of service are used in other associated piping REPs to classify or define a specific service condition that might affect design, selection, construction, and erection of piping systems.

**3.5** Inspector – A Industry Source 05 appointed engineer or Inspector.

**3.6** Manufacturer – The recipient of a direct or indirect purchase order for materials and/or equipment. In this context, a direct order is one issued to a manufacturer by a contractor or Owner. An indirect order is one issued to a manufacturer by a vendor (recipient of a direct order) for materials, fabricated components, or sub-assemblies.

**3.7** Owner – Industry Source 05.

**3.8** Owner's Engineer – A Industry Source 05 appointed engineer.

**3.9** Piping Service Class – Simplified classification of piping system as established in API 570, based on the risk classification and that is used to establish frequency and extent of inspection. This classification includes four piping service classes, 1 through 4.

## 4.0 GENERAL PIPING DESIGN

### 4.1 General

**4.1.1** Piping shall conform to the requirements of this Practice and ASME B31.3, ASME B31.1, or ASME B31.4, ASME B31.8, NFPA 13, NFPA 24, or NFPA 59A as applicable, except where superseded by more stringent local codes or regulations.

**4.1.2** (\*) This Practice supplements the piping requirements of the applicable ASME piping code. In case of conflict between these Practices and the ASME piping code, the most stringent requirements as determined by Owner's Engineer shall govern.

### 4.2 Design Pressure and Temperature

**4.2.1** The maximum design temperature for each line and its components is the metal temperature that represents the most severe condition along with the coincident pressure that requires the greatest pipe wall thickness and highest flange rating. The metal temperature for piping components shall be determined as follows:

1. For externally insulated piping components, the design metal temperature shall be the maximum design temperature of the contained fluid.

2. For uninsulated (externally) and non-refractory lined (internally) piping components with fluid temperatures 32°F and above, the design metal temperature of the contained fluid, reduced by the percentage shall be as shown in Table 1 of this Practice.

3. For uninsulated (externally) and non-refractory lined (internally) piping components with fluid temperatures less than 32°F, the design metal temperature shall be that of the contained fluid.

4. For lines and internally insulated piping, the design temperature shall be specified or calculated using maximum ambient temperature and zero wind.

**4.2.2** The Minimum Design Temperature (MDT) used in the design shall be the lowest temperature expected in service after considering all of the following:

1. The start-up and shut-down conditions shall be considered when determining the MDT. The MDT may be based on controlled start-up and shut-down procedures that ensure that the MDT and coincident pressure meet the requirements of the ASME piping code. If this alternative is used, the operating manual for the unit shall specifically address these procedures. Typically, the MDT can be set at a temperature where the coincident pressure during the start-up and shut-down conditions is equal to or less than 25% of the design temperature.

2. The MDT may be determined by computation or by measurement from equipment in service under equivalent operating conditions. Consideration shall be given to normal operating conditions, operational upsets, auto-refrigeration, and any other source of potential cooling.

3. If controlled start-up and shut-down procedures are not used (item a.) and the process design conditions do not result in special coding effects (item b.), the MDT shall be set to the lowest expected metal temperature for the site. See the "Isothermal Lines of Lowest One-Day Mean Temperatures" figure in API 650.

4. For piping constructed to ASME B31.3 the MDMT shall be established as the minimum permissible value based upon the selected material specification and furnished thickness based on Table 323.2.2 of ASME B31.3.

5. The MDMT shall be adjusted as applicable to include the restrictions on material toughness per REP 10-2-2.

**4.2.3** The MDT for each line is the lowest metal temperature expected in service. The minimum design metal temperature shall be determined by computation or by measurement from equipment in service under equivalent operating conditions. Consideration shall be given to the lowest expected metal temperature, operational upsets, auto-refrigeration, atmospheric temperature, and any other sources of cooling. The pressure specified at the minimum design metal temperature shall be the design pressure determined in the following paragraphs unless calculations or operating procedures ensure that a lower pressure is coincident with the minimum metal temperature.

**4.2.4** The design pressure for piping components is that pressure that represents the most severe condition along with the coincident temperature that requires the greatest pipe wall thickness and highest flange rating. The design pressure for piping shall be consistent with the design pressure for vessels and equipment to which it is attached, and the following:

**4.2.4.1** Protected by a Pressure Relief (PR) device: The design pressure shall be equal to the set pressure of the PR device protecting the system plus static head and the pressure differential required for flow to the PR device.

**4.2.4.2** Not protected by a PR device: The design pressure for any section of piping not protected by a PR device shall be equal to the maximum pressure that can be developed as a result of control valve failure, pump shut-off, or inadvertent valve closure and static head.

**4.2.5** Variations from design conditions: Variations of short duration in pressure and temperature from the design conditions are permitted by short-time, adjusted, pressure-temperature ratings of piping components in accordance with paragraphs 302.2.4 of ASME B31.3. The short-time design pressure and temperature shall be coexistent values based on variations (more severe than normal design conditions). Such variations may occur during a start-up, shut-down, normal operation of the process such as regeneration and decoking, emergency conditions or a major equipment failure, operating error, etc. Guidelines for establishing these pressures are as follows:

**4.2.5.1** For piping associated with pressure vessels and equipment that are protected by a PR device, the emergency pressure shall be equal to the set pressure of the PR device plus static head and the pressure differential required for flow to the PR device.

**4.2.5.2** For piping not protected by a PR device, the emergency pressure shall be equal to the maximum pressure that can be developed as a result of control valve failure, pump shut-off, or inadvertent valve closure, and static head.

**4.2.5.3** When pump curves are available, the pump shut-in pressure shall be the sum of the pump differential pressure (shut-in) plus normal suction pressure.

**4.2.5.4** When pump curves are not available for determining shut-in pressures, an acceptable estimate of the shut-in pressure shall be the greater of the following:

1. Normal pump suction pressure plus 120% of normal pump differential pressure.
2. Maximum pump suction pressure plus normal pump differential pressure.
3. If shut-in pressures are estimated in accordance with the sub-paragraphs above, they shall be re-evaluated when pump curves are obtained:
   - A lower design pressure than determined from the first sub-paragraph above shall be acceptable if the actual pump supplied has a shut-off differential pressure less than 120% of normal differential pressure.
   - Where the actual pump supplied has a shut-off differential pressure greater than 120% of normal differential pressure, the design pressure, if previously determined from the first sub-paragraph, above shall be corrected.

**4.2.6** (*) Unless otherwise specified by Owner's Engineer, the design pressure for piping components operating at positive pressures only shall be a minimum of 50 psig.

**4.2.7** (*) Unless otherwise specified by Owner's Engineer, the minimum design pressure for piping components operating at vacuum pressure shall be full vacuum (15 psia) and 50 psig internal pressure.

**4.2.8** When designing for external pressure (for example, in jacketed piping), the possible absence of internal pressure in the inner pipe shall be taken into consideration.

**4.2.9** Piping and components between double block valves shall be suitable for the more severe line classification on either side of the double valve installation.

**4.2.10** Piping downstream of such equipment as heat exchangers and control valves shall not be designed for the lower pressure resulting from pressure drop through the equipment if the pressure can increase because of downstream fouling or inadvertent closing of a valve. Valves that are locked or sealed open during operation and closed only for maintenance need not be considered.

**4.2.11** Supplemental design requirements and piping systems in HF service are covered in REP 5-10-1.

## 4.3 Sizing Requirements for Piping

**4.3.1** (*) The design flow quantity for oil, hydrocarbon, gas, and vapor lines shall be the maximum quantity for which the equipment upstream (pumps, vessels, furnaces, etc.) is designed, except that the design capacity of connections between towers and reboilers shall be subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer.

**4.3.2** (*) The design capacities for utility services piping shall be subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer.

**4.3.3** Line sizing criteria for liquids, gases and steam, fluid solids, and mixed phase flow shall be as shown in Table 2, Table 3, Table 4, and Table 5, respectively.

**4.3.4** For two-phase flow, the parameter:

$$\rho_M V_M^2 \leq 10{,}000$$

*With:*

$$\rho_M = \frac{M_T}{\dfrac{M_L}{\rho_L} + \dfrac{M_G}{\rho_G}}$$

$$V_M = \frac{M_T}{3600\,\rho_M A}$$

A = cross sectional area of pipe, ft²

$\rho_M$ = mixture density, lbs/ft³

$\rho_L$ = liquid density, lbs/ft³

$\rho_G$ = vapor density, lbs/ft³

$M_T$ = total mixture flowrate, lbs/hr

$M_L$ = liquid flowrate, lbs/hr

$M_G$ = vapor flowrate, lbs/hr

$V_M$ = mixture velocity, ft/sec

(Note: This erosion velocity equation is taken from API RP 14E, Paragraph 2.5).

**4.3.5** Piping in intermittent service (for example, start-up, pumping-out, bypasses) shall be sized on the basis of available pressure differentials and subject to the line sizing criteria of this Practice.

**4.3.6** Pipe size restrictions on bore and outside diameter shall be as specified in REP 5–2–1.

**4.3.7** Pump suction line sizes shall be determined as follows:

**4.3.7.1** (*) Pump suction lines shall be sized to provide a Net Pump Suction Head Available (NPSHA) not less than the Net Pump Suction Head Required (NPSHR) by the pump selected. NPSHA shall be at the rated pump capacity. The economics of increasing line size versus adjusting vessel elevation shall be considered in providing sufficient Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH). The line shall not be smaller than the pump nozzle and velocity shall not exceed 10 ft/sec. Line velocities higher than 6 ft/sec shall require approval of Owner's Engineer.

**4.3.7.2** The static head used in calculating the available NPSH shall be measured from the bottom tangent line for vertical vessels, or the bottom of horizontal vessels to one of the following:

1. The centerline of a horizontal centrifugal pump or rotary pump.
2. The suction nozzle on a vertical centrifugal pump or reciprocating pump.

**4.3.7.3** The design of storage tank suction lines shall be based on an NPSH taken from the lowest specified liquid level in the tank at which rated pump capacity is required. If vortex-breaking equipment is required, the pressure drop through the equipment shall be considered.

**4.3.7.4** In sizing suction lines for reciprocating pumps, acceleration head shall be considered.

**4.3.8** The pressure drop available for control valves shall be as specified in REP 5-3-17.

**4.3.9** In areas where the level of noise is a concern, the requirements of the *Industry Source 05 Engineering Services Recommended Environmental Guidelines* shall be followed.
