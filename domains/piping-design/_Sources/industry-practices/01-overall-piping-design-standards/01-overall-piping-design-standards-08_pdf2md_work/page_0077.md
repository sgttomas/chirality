#### 8.8.2.2 Stop flow; main or block valves

Piping classes specify which valves are to be selected for a particular service or duty, generally in accordance with the following guidelines:

[TABLE: Valve selection guidelines for stop flow / main or block valves]

| Ball valves, soft seated | DN 15 to DN 250 | General service < 200 °C |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ball valves, metal seated | DN 15 to DN 900 | General service and special applications > 200 °C |
| Gate valves | DN 15 and larger | General service |
| Butterfly valves, lined | DN 100 and larger | General (water) service, ASME rating class 150 |
| Butterfly valves, lined | DN 100 and larger | Corrosive service, ASME rating class 150 and 300 |
| Butterfly valves, soft seated or metal sealed (high performance) | DN 80 and larger | General service and special applications, e.g. cryogenic or high temperature |
| Diaphragm valves, lined | DN 15 to DN 300 | Corrosive service, ASME rating class 150 |
| Ball valves, lined | DN 15 to DN 150 | Corrosive service, ASME rating class 150 and 300 |
| Gate and globe valves, bellows-sealed, welded bonnet | DN 15 to DN 40 | Critical, very toxic service |
| Gate and globe valves, with special gland packing | DN 50 to DN 600 | Critical, very toxic service |

#### 8.8.2.3 Valves in steam service

All valves for steam service in ASME rating class 600 and higher shall be of the butt weld end type, except for equipment assembly valves, blow-down valves and instrument isolation valves, which may be flanged.

#### 8.8.2.4 Valves in hydrogen service

Valves for pure hydrogen or fluids containing hydrogen in piping classes 600 and higher should be butt welded except for check valves and instrument isolation valves, which may be flanged.

Valves in hydrogen service require special attention with regard to external leakage, which can be prevented by different methods depending on the required level of tightness and economics (see 8.8.2.7).

#### 8.8.2.5 Valves in low temperature service

Acceptable valve types for low temperature services are specified in BS 6364 and MESC SPE 77/200 and SPE 77/209.

Gate, globe, ball and butterfly valves in low temperature service shall have a vapour space sufficient to maintain the stem packing at a temperature high enough to permit actuation of the valve.

The vapour space of valves in services between 0 °C and minus 50 °C is defined in MESC SPE 77/209.

The vapour space of valves in services below minus 50 °C is defined in MESC SPE 77/200.

NOTE Depending on the valve design, this may require extended valve bonnets.

Systems which operate occasionally below minus 20 °C (e.g. during de-pressuring) and have a lower design temperature between minus 20 °C and minus 50 °C, do not require valves with extended bonnets. Such valves shall not be opened or closed at sub-zero temperatures.
