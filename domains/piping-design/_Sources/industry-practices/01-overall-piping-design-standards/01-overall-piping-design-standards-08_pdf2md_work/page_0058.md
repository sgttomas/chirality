## 5. UTILITY PIPING

### 5.1 FIRE WATER

Fire water piping systems shall comply with DEP 80.47.10.31-Gen.

### 5.2 COOLING WATER

Cooling water pipes ≤ DN 600 should have block valves at the plot limit so that they can be isolated for maintenance while the cooling water system remains in operation.

The Principal shall specify whether plot limit block valves are required for cooling water pipes larger than DN 600.

Cement-lined pipes DN 900 and larger shall be provided with inspection and maintenance manholes of minimum size DN 600. The distance between manholes should be no more than 100 m.

Cooling water pipes longer than 100 m shall be checked for potential surges (2.3.4).

Large cooling water pipes may require special supports to avoid subsidence.

The possibility should be considered of measuring and recording the flow for each unit or group of integrated units. Alternatively the plot limit block valves should be of the calibrated type, i.e. with pressure tappings which enable off-line flow measurement.

Sufficient pressure indicators shall be installed to determine the system pressure profile.

A slight over-pressure shall be maintained in cooling water systems, e.g. by means of a restriction orifice or an overflow system, in order to avoid vapour locks.

The open funnel pipe to the drain system shall have an extra capacity of at least 20%.

Where sub-zero temperatures may occur a closed cooling water system shall have a bypass with a globe valve around the upstream and downstream block valves for each unit or integrated group. Sufficient drain capacity shall be provided.

Cooling towers shall have isolation valves at the inlet to each cooling tower cell to allow access for maintenance.

If cooling water pipes may be subject to fouling the tie-off shall be taken from the top of the header in order to avoid fouling of piping and equipment. For cooling water design fouling data, see DEP 20.21.00.31-Gen.

Main distribution pipes shall have facilities at the lowest points to permit complete draining within 6 hours. Venting facilities shall be provided to relieve air pockets.

The piping materials shall be selected from the applicable piping classes in consultation with a materials and corrosion engineer.

Glass-reinforced unsaturated polyester (GRUP) should be used for cooling water pipes unless uneconomical. Particular advantages of GRUP pipes are corrosion resistance, lower fluid friction factors and lower susceptibility to fouling.

If economical, concrete pipe or channels may be used for atmospheric pressure cooling water return pipes.

### 5.3 FOR OTHER PURPOSES

A clear distinction shall be made between potable water (water of drinking water purity), industrial water and various kinds of cooling water, etc.

For potable and industrial water, non-metallic piping (8.2.2) shall be used wherever practical.
