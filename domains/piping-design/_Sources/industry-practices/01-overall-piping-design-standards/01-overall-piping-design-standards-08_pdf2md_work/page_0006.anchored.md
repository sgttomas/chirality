## 2. PIPE SIZING

### 2.1 GENERAL

The following pipe sizes shall be used:

DN 15, DN 20, DN 25, DN 40, DN 50, DN 80, DN 100, DN 150, DN 200, DN 250, DN 300, DN 350, DN 400, DN 450, DN 500 and DN 600 in accordance with the following limitations.

- Due to their vulnerability to damage and their limited mechanical strength, sizes DN 15 and DN 20 should not be used for long-run piping.
- Nominal pipe size in pipe tracks shall not be less than DN 50;
- Nominal pipe size in pipe racks shall not be less than DN 40;
- Pipe sizes between those given in the piping classes shall not be used.

For requirements of small bore piping see (3.9).

Unless economically justified otherwise, the range of pipe sizes above DN 600 shall be restricted to the following, in order to avoid the purchase of many different sizes of pipe and fittings:

DN 750, DN 900, DN 1050, DN 1200, DN 1400, DN 1600, DN 1800 and DN 2000.

The pipe numbering system shall be in accordance with Industry Source 08 Doc 31.10.03.10-Gen.

Appendix 8 gives dimensions and properties of pipe up to and including DN 750.

After the basic pipe routes, number of valves, control valves, fittings, etc., have been determined, the anticipated pressure drop for the preliminary pipe sizes shall be checked.

A calculation sheet for pumping data (Industry Source 08 Doc 31.29.01.85-Gen.) shall be prepared for each pump, showing calculations for suction and discharge conditions, static head, static pressure, vapour pressure at pumping temperature and minimum available NPSH (Net Positive Suction Head), including the calculated pressure drop in equipment, pipes, valves, fittings and control valves.

The Principal shall be consulted for piping systems for which the pressure drop calculations are not covered in (2.3).

The pipe sizes shall be listed on data sheet Industry Source 08 Doc 31.38.01.92 Gen. or if more economical a listing in electronic form may be delivered containing the same information.

### 2.2 PRELIMINARY PIPE SIZING

Appendix 1 may be used for preliminary pipe sizing of pump suction and discharge piping.

Appendix 2 gives the flow rates (m³/h) for various velocities (m/s), which may be used for both liquid and gas piping.

The following velocities should be used for preliminary pipe sizing for interconnecting piping:

- water and other liquids: 0.5 To 5 m/s;
- air and other gases: 10 To 20 m/s;
- saturated steam (dry): 15 To 30 m/s;
- superheated steam: 30 To 60 m/s;
- vacuum pipes: 10 To 100 m/s.

The above figures are not applicable to sludge, slurry, erosive fluids or two-phase flow. The Principal shall be consulted for those services.

For sizing of flare and vent systems see Industry Source 08 Doc 80.45.10.10-Gen.

To determine the flow pattern of two-phase flow see Industry Source 08 Doc 31.22.05.11-Gen.
