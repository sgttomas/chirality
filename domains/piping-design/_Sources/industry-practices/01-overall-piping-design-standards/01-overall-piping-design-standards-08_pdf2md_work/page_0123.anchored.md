## 10.6.3 "Wet H₂S" service (refinery/gas plant applications)

All carbon steel piping in refineries and gas plants exposed to wet H₂S process streams shall be designed and manufactured to resist the potential cracking mechanisms caused by the generation of free hydrogen in an aqueous corrosion reaction.

In refinery streams containing free water and H₂S, the pH is often around 6 or higher due to the presence of ammonia. Trace quantities of contaminants such as chloride or fluoride may also be present, which form ammonium salts (ammonium chloride and ammonium fluoride). Cyanides also play an important role in refinery streams as they can breakdown iron sulphide scale and possibly cause hydrogen pick-up. In high pH streams containing significant levels of sulphide and carbonate, the pressures shall be taken to prevent carbonate cracking, which is a form of alkaline stress corrosion cracking (ASCC).

Piping classes should be selected according to the potential damage mechanism(s) anticipated or experienced in the service and the severity of the process environment (potential level of hydrogen flux and/or carbonates).

Tables 1A, 1B and 1C should be used to assess the severity of the process environment as applicable to the type of damage mechanism being considered.

NOTE: Tables 1A, 1B and 1C assume that the environment being considered is present during normal operations. Short term upsets should only be considered if the damage mechanism anticipated or experienced is also likely to occur in the short term. If in doubt, the materials engineer of the Principal shall be consulted.
