### 2.3.4 Pressure Surge Calculations

Surge is caused by rapid velocity changes of the fluid, e.g. because of rapid valve closure, pump trip or start. The steep pressure gradients that may occur under some surge conditions and which cannot be avoided by appropriate means (e.g. non-slam check valves) should be evaluated with regard to the effects on the supports and the design pressure of the system.

Pressure surge calculations are relevant to pipes handling incompressible fluids (liquids). The magnitude of the pressure surge is determined by the velocity of the fluid in the pipe and the rate at which this velocity is changed. As a general rule a sudden velocity change of 1 m/s will generate a pressure surge of 10 bar. The effect of a sudden velocity change depends on the length of the pipe and of the closing speed of the end valve.

The designer shall ensure that the occurrence of a pressure surge can be tolerated; if in doubt a surge calculation shall be carried out. As a general rule pressure surges may be ignored if the pipe is shorter than 100 m and the closing time of the end valve is longer than 5 s.

Measures should be taken to avoid cavitation since the collapse of a cavity could generate a high pressure surge. Cavities could occur due to a sudden change in flow rate, which can be caused by a shut down of the supply pump(s) and the closure of the valve at the beginning of the pipe.

The location of the cavity depends on the elevation profile of the pipe and will most likely be directly downstream of the equipment causing the flow reduction (e.g. pump or valve) and at high points in the system.

After a piping system has been checked for surge potential by a full surge analysis and transient analysis the following shall be reported to the Principal for approval:

- selection of surge scenarios and operational conditions;
- final surge pressure analysis;
- selection of the most severe surge;
- selection of representative piping section;
- final static and dynamic stress analysis.

Surge scenarios shall include:

- rapid valve closure with and without pump tripping;
- pump tripping;
- pump start-up;
- pump trip followed by restart.

The study shall cover maximum possible flow rates. Flows in single-pipe operation of multiple pipe systems shall be assessed if pump capacity exceeds the normal pipe capacity.

- LNG loading and rundown pipes;
- LPG loading and rundown pipes;
