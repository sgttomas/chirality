2.22.3 Vents on vessels shall be blind flanged and drains generally piped to the sewer. Drains from process outlet piping may suffice if complete drainage of vessel will result. All valves and instruments shall be accessible from platforms.

## 2.23 Fuel and Pilot Gas Piping

2.23.1 The use of stainless steel "Braided Flexible" hose is acceptable for the final connection between the supply piping and the individual burner and/or pilot. The flexible section shall meet or exceed the pressure/temperature and hydrotest requirements of the respective line class in BP CHP STP 205.

2.23.2 The process fluid shall be routed to each heater pass by symmetrical piping design for equal flow distribution, and, if an all liquid phase flow, through the use of flow control valves.

2.23.3 Fuel and pilot gas piping shall be routed without pockets and supplied with drain valves at the low points. Individual take-offs shall connect to the top of the header and shall be supplied with two isolation valves. Valves that will be used to take the burners or pilots in and out of service shall be 1/4 turn valves. All valves in fuel or pilot gas service shall have a temperature range sufficient to allow for steaming out the piping system. Heater fuel and pilot gas piping shall comply with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-038.

2.23.4 The total fuel to each heater shall be measured and recorded.

2.23.5 Ahead of each pilot gas and burner manifold, a 1" valved connection shall be installed for methanol injection. Pressure gauges are required on the common fuel gas feed line and at each of the manifolds.

## 2.24 Heater Piping

Piping and supports at heaters shall be designed to provide clear access for manways, explosion doors, peepholes, heater tube replacement workspace, and similar items. Piping shall be designed so that thermal expansion loadings are not absorbed by the heater coils. Free access shall be afforded burner manifolds. Refer to 2.26.7 for heater snuffing steam requirements.

## 2.25 Flue Gas Sample Connections

Provisions for the sampling of the flue gas at the outlet of the radiant section should be provided. The draft indicator lead may be used. In any event, the sample line shall be piped to grade or to the draft gauge elevation. An eductor operating on plant air shall be installed to draw down the sample gas.

## 2.26 Steam and Condensate Piping

2.26.1 Steam piping shall be designed for maximum condensate elimination. Low points in steam headers shall be equipped with a drip leg and a steam trap. A trap is required at each collection point. Traps shall be installed ahead of all outlets, expansion joints, and dead ends of lines and headers. Steam piping and steam trap design shall comply with standard drawings LA-000-0100-0200-005, LA-000-0100-0200-006, LA-000-0100-0200-007, LA-000-0100-0200-008, and LA-000-0100-0200-009.

2.26.2 Exhaust steam lines discharging to the atmosphere shall be equipped with an exhaust head. The discharge should not create a hazard to personnel or equipment. As a minimum, exhaust heads shall be located 10' above the highest operating area of equipment within a 25' radius. Condensate carryover should be minimized by ample line sizing to reduce steam flow velocity.

2.26.3 Exhaust steam header lines shall be installed without line pockets. Any line condensation shall drain away from the exhaust header.
