- 2.26.4 Take off connections from either live or exhaust steam lines shall be from the top of the header. Header block valves shall be installed in the horizontal run of line at the high point above the header connection.

- 2.26.5 For relief valves in flammable services, snuffing steam shall be connected to all relief valve outlet piping which discharges to atmosphere. Minimum size snuffing steam lines shall be 2" for relief valves 4" and smaller, and 1-1/2" for relief valves larger than 4". Steam snuffing lines shall be self-draining and routed to avoid pockets and any collection of condensate. A 1/2" weep hole shall be provided at the low point of the atmospheric discharge piping from the relief valve. Snuffing steam shall be connected horizontally into the discharge pipe at the closest practical location near the relief valve. The snuffing steam line shall include a break-out union near the relief valve. Snuffing steam lines shall originate at battery limit manifolds clearly identified as to service. Manifolds shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-010. Relief valve snuffing steam manifolds shall be separate from heater snuffing steam manifolds (see section 2.26.7).

- 2.26.6 A steam out system, using 140# steam, shall be provided for all hydrocarbon vessels, towers, and exchangers, as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-003. (See Industry Source 01 CHP STP 40, for sizing of steam-out connection.)

- 2.26.7 Snuffing or smothering steam shall be connected to the fire box of heaters. The snuffing steam shall also include stab-in connections near the heater connections. Steam flow shall be regulated by a hand valve, properly identified and tagged, at the release manifold located 50' minimum from any fired heater. Snuffing steam lines shall be self-draining and routed to avoid pockets and the collection of condensate. If pockets are unavoidable, a 1/2" weep hole shall be provided at the low point. A low point bleed shall be provided to drain line condensate after snuffing steam use. Snuffing steam lines shall originate at a clearly identified snuffing steam manifold located at the battery limit. The heater snuffing steam manifold shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-010 and shall be separate from any relief valve snuffing steam manifold (see section 2.26.5).

- 2.26.8 All process steam lines shall tie-in directly at the steam header.

- 2.26.9 The design of all new condensate lines shall consider the potential for corrosion-erosion. If flashing or two-phase flow is possible, such as downstream of a control valve, orifice or other item causing significant pressure drop, consider using higher alloy fittings (elbows and reducers) in the zone where flashing or two-phase flow can occur. Alternatively, maximize the distance between the item creating the pressure drop and the downstream change in pipe direction, so as to allow the vapor to re-condense.

## 2.27 Steam Tracing and Jacketing

- 2.27.1 Electric tracing should normally be used for new installations instead of steam tracing or jacketing, except where steam is necessary for maintaining critical process temperatures. The necessity of steam tracing or jacketing shall be considered for the following conditions: where fluidity is required; where pipe metal temperature can drop below the dew point; where vapor condensation could occur; where protection against corrosive materials that can condense out; instrument leads in which any of the above conditions may affect the proper operation of instruments; and compressor suction lines in which condensation may occur on the piping.

- 2.27.2 Steam tracer material shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAHS and S-07. Steam jacketed piping shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAY and CXY.

- 2.27.3 Tracer tube fittings shall not be installed underneath insulation.
