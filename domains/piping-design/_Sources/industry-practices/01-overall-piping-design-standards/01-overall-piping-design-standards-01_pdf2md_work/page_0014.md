- 2.8.10 Integral attachments including lugs, ears, shoes, plates, and angle clips welded to the piping shall meet the same welding requirements as the piping.

- 2.8.11 Dummy pipe extensions, used as supports, shall be completely isolated from the active piping to prevent entry of the flowing medium. Dummy supports shall be provided with a 1/2" drain hole at the low point of the cavity, where there is potential for liquid or pressurized gas accumulation. Some dummy leg supports may be required to include a 1" by 1-1/2" inspection hole to accommodate in-service thickness examination of the pipe or elbow. At the approval of the piping design, the project engineer or designer shall consult with the BP CHP Inspector for designation of the specific dummy support locations where inspection holes are required. Inspection holes shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-042.

- 2.8.12 Insulated hot lines shall be provided with shoes sized so that the required insulation thickness will clear the pipe support. Insulated lines in low temperature service shall be supported on wrapper plates curved to fit the outside of the pipe line insulation.

- 2.8.13 Uninsulated piping subject to vibration shall be supported on pipe shoes. Other bare piping should rest directly on supporting steel.

- 2.8.14 Rigid adjustable base supports shall be provided for the inlet and outlet piping for all rotating equipment, where possible. The adjustable base supports as shown on drawings LA-000-0100-0200-040 and LA-000-0100-0200-041 are standard unless otherwise approved by BP CHP. Spring support may be considered if use of rigid support will impose unacceptable nozzle loads on the equipment due to thermal expansion.

- 2.8.15 See Section 2.1.10 for guidance on pipe support design for small diameter piping in services with potential for vibration and fatigue issues, such as reciprocating compressor trim piping.

## 2.9 Expansion and Flexibility

- 2.9.1 All piping shall be designed for thermal expansion from start-up, normal operations, regeneration, shutdown, steam out, solar heat, and/or ambient conditions without overstress of piping, valves, or connected equipment. Pipe spacing per Section 2.5.3 should assure that expanding lines will not touch or damage adjacent lines, conduit, structures, or similar items.

- 2.9.2 In general, BP CHP prefers that expansion shall be taken care of by flexibility in piping using elbows, bends, offsets, or loops. Packed type expansion joints and "cold spring" of piping shall not be used without written approval.

- 2.9.3 Piping to be stress analyzed as listed in Section 10.0.

- 2.9.4 Clamps or U-Bolts on small piping which has thermal movements shall not restrict the movement, unless they are designed to act as guides or anchors. The piping support drawings shall clearly indicate the intended purpose of the clamps or U-Bolts, and specify any special installation requirements.

## 2.10 Vibration

- 2.10.1 Careful attention shall be given to the design of piping connected to equipment, valves, or instruments, which may produce harmonic effects, pulsations, or excessive vibrations, which may result in improper operation or damage to piping, equipment, or instruments. When such conditions exist, consideration shall be given to the use of pulsation damping devices or other design modifications. For additional piping requirements related to vibration and reciprocating compressors see sections 2.1.10 and 4.1.5 in this standard, and applicable sections of BP PHP STP 10.
