## 2.11 Pressure Relief and Flare Headers

- 2.11.1 Equipment shall be protected by safety or relief valves in accordance with BP CHP STP 350 and the BP CHP STP CHP-GN 44-70. See section 2.26.5 of this standard for requirements for snuffing steam and weep holes at relief valve atmospheric discharge piping.

- 2.11.2 Relief valves shall be positioned to be accessible by mobile crane. If this is not possible, consult BP CHP Reliability Engineer for direction.

- 2.11.3 Block valves for flare header isolation or pressure-relief device isolation shall be capable of being locked or car-sealed open, and shall be designed and installed such that they cannot inadvertently close due to mechanical failure. Gate valves used for flare header isolation or pressure relief device isolation shall be installed with stems oriented horizontally to prevent partial or complete blockage due to a detached disc. When it is not possible to install a gate valve with the stem in the horizontal position, use a different type of valve, such as a full port ball valve or orbit valve.

- 2.11.4 Flare headers shall be provided with continuous gas purges, which enter the headers at the extreme upstream ends and are regulated to control the purge gas flow. See additional purge gas requirements in BP CHP STP CHP-GN 44-70. For corrosive gas streams, additional purge gas may be needed immediately downstream of the relief valve so that the entire relief valve discharge piping will be swept. Consult with the BP CHP Corrosion Engineer to determine where additional purges are needed for corrosive gas streams.

## 2.12 Blinds and Blanks

- 2.12.1 Permanent line blinds shall be spectacle (figure 8 type) blinds, in accordance with drawings LD-00-0100-21 and LD-00-0100-22. Permanent blinds shall be shown on P&ID's. When the weight of the spectacle blind will exceed 100 pounds, separate paddle type spacers and blinds shall be provided.

- 2.12.2 Where the engineering design specifies blinds for maintenance purposes, piping systems in the following table shall be designed with sufficient flexibility to permit insertion of temporary maintenance slip blinds.

[TABLE: Flange Rating (Class) vs Joint and Pipe Size for maintenance slip blinds]

| Flange Rating (Class) | Joint | Pipe Size |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 150 | F.F./R.F. | Up thru 6" |
| 300 | F.F./R.F. | Up thru 4" |
| 600 | R.F. | Up thru 4" |

For piping not included in the table above and where the engineering design specifies blinds for maintenance purposes, a permanent spectacle blind or spacer shall be installed in accordance with drawings LD-00-0100-21 and LD-00-0100-22.

- 2.12.3 Carbon and low alloy steel permanent line blinds shall have a corrosion allowance not less than 0.10". Austenitic stainless steel permanent line blinds shall have a minimum corrosion allowance of 0.063".

- 2.12.4 Blind and spacer gasket facings shall be serrated in accordance with ASME B16.5.

## 2.13 Valving

- 2.13.1 The rules contained in Sections 2.13.2 and 2.13.3 are intended to reduce excess valving. Piping designers are encouraged to propose additional simplifications for BP CHP approval.
