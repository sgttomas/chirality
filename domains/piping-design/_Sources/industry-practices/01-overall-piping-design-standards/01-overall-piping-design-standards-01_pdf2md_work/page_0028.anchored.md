4.1.6 For all piping systems containing H₂S streams with greater than 1000 ppm by volume for vapor stream or 1000 ppm by weight for liquid stream, joints are required to be welded. For vent, drain, and instrument branch connections or other branch connections to services which do not contain more than 1000 ppm H₂S, welded joints are also required at least up through the first block valve.

## 4.2 Branch Connections

4.2.1 Branch connections shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205, including the applicable branch connection charts referenced in the pipe classes.

4.2.2 Branch connections shall have adequate length to prevent valves and instruments from being covered by insulation.

4.2.3 Stub-in or stub-on branch connections shall not be used without prior written approval by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer, except for thermowell connections using LWN flanges as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-019.

4.2.4 Branch connections shall not be made by threading directly into the wall of the run pipe. Half couplings are not permissible for use as branch connections.

4.2.5 Hot tap connections, defined as the installation of a branch connection by welding and drilling on piping in service, shall be done in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP Safety and Health Policy 1620 Welding and Inspection in Service and Hot Tapping.

4.2.6 All integrally reinforced branch connection fittings (such as pipets and weldolets) shall be attached to the run pipe by a full penetration groove weld and a finish cover fillet weld as specified in ASME B31.3 or B31.1 as applicable, and as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-019.

## 4.3 Flanges

4.3.1 Flanges are to be manufactured in accordance with ASME B16.5 and the various Standards listed in ASME B31.3 or B31.1 for sizes up to and including 24". Flanges above 24" shall be in accordance with ASME B16.47 Series B (previously API 605), except ASME B16.47 Series A (previously MSS SP-44) flanges may be used where necessary to connect to existing piping or equipment. Since Series A and B flanges are incompatible with each other, designers must verify dimensions of existing flanges for mating to new flanges.

4.3.2 Socket weld and threaded flanges are not recommended for services above 500°F if several thermal gradients or thermal cycling are involved.

4.3.3 Slip-on flanges shall not be used in any flammable, hydrogen, or steam services, or any service above 500°F operating temperature. Slip-on flanges shall not be used where severe bending stresses are likely to be present, or where there is vibration or cyclic temperature conditions. Slip-on reducing flanges are allowed for steam jacketed piping in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAY and CXY.

4.3.4 Loose or optional-type flanges and lap joint flanges are not permitted.
