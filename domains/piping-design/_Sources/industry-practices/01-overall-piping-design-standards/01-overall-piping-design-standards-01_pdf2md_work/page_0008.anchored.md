2.3.7 Provisions should be made in the design to allow bleeding and/or draining of trapped pressures and volumes to achieve a Zero Mechanical State (ZMS) prior to maintenance. Installation of a bleed valve should be considered between check valves and block valves, as a means to ensure ZMS downstream of the check valves (reference Texas City Fatality Incident September 2, 2004). Alternately, installation of a bypass around the check valve could be considered.

Note: This requirement is intended to apply to both new and existing piping, but does not apply to the following services:

- Water (<100°F, such as CWS, CWR, firewater, process water)

- Air

- Special circumstances (such as all-welded systems), providing that a formal risk review is conducted.

2.3.8 The use of tubing warrants special attention during design, installation, operation, and maintenance, to ensure mechanical integrity. Tubing is often used for services such as instrumentation, pump seal flush, sample stations, and injection quills.

Incidents at Industry Source 01 CHP related to hazardous leakage from tubing and tube fittings include high pressure hydrogen service, rotating equipment (vibration) and injection quills. Contributing causes are often the inadequate fit up of joints and lack of proper support for the tubing.

Hazardous services for tubing are defined as hydrocarbons above autoignition temperature, toxic chemicals, all high pressure services (500 psig and up) and high temperature services (250°F and up). Some specific limitations on the use of tubing in hazardous services are as follows:

a) In general, the length of tubing and the number of compression fittings should be minimized as much as is practical. Each specific installation must be designed with this objective.

b) The use of intermediate compression joints for splicing in these services is not allowed without approval by Industry Source 01 CHP.

c) Injection quill systems in these services must be hard piped per the applicable pipe specification (such as pipe classes CJL and FJL).

Note: Pre-insulated tubing bundles are acceptable in hazardous services, provided that they are properly protected and supported, and installed without splices.

Refer to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 314 for instrument tubing requirements.

2.3.9 As applicable, Industry Source 01 ETP GP 44-10 *Plant Layout* shall be followed in the design of piping systems. References within GP 44-10 to other standards or codes only apply to the extent specified in GP 44-10.

2.3.10 To prevent high cycle fatigue failures in small piping joints at reciprocating compressors, see section 4.1.5 for special requirements for NPS 2 and smaller trim piping joints and connections.

2.3.11 Dissimilar metal welds are not allowed for pressure containing welds in wet H₂S or HF services, since hydrogen can be charged into the metal and result in hydrogen stress cracking. Exceptions apply for some clad pipe applications. See additional information in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 42 section 13.

2.3.12 Unless approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP metallurgist, 300 series stainless steel piping components shall not be welded to or installed in contact with galvanized or zinc coated components, particularly when the material may reach temperatures above 750°F. See the liquid metal embrittlement sections of ETP GP 42-10 for more information.
