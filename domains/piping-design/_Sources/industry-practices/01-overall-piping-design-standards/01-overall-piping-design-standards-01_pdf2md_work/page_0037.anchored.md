If dead legs cannot be avoided, alternate methods approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer and Corrosion Engineer shall be utilized to mitigate the risk of dead leg problems. Some potential alternate methods are upgrading the piping materials, applying internal protective coatings, or adding circulating lines with inhibitors.

## SECTION 13: INJECTION AND MIXING POINTS

### 13.1 General

Effective and reliable service from injection piping and quills depends on numerous factors. Failures at injection and mixing points may create high risk situations. Alternate designs need to be carefully and thoroughly evaluated by all applicable parties, including, but not limited to, Operations, Process Engineering, Corrosion Engineering, and Reliability Engineering.

All new and replacement injection and process mixing points shall comply with the requirements of NACE SP0114, with the exception that any requirements in Sections 13.2 through 13.3 below supersede the NACE requirements.

### 13.2 Design Criteria

#### 13.2.1 Orientation

Injection and mixing points shall be designed for concurrent flow (pointing downstream, parallel with bulk flow). Countercurrent (against the flow) and crossflow (through or across the flow) injection designs are rarely the correct designs and are not allowed without an approved Standard Deviation Request. Concurrent designs should consider the proximity to the downstream piping components, such as elbows, for erosion concerns. See NACE SP0114 for this and other concurrent flow design considerations.

Injection piping and quills, either retractable or fixed, shall be designed so that there is only one obvious way of installing the quill, with respect to the direction of the bulk flow into which they are being inserted. The design shall indicate with an arrow which direction the bulk flow is going, accompanied by the text "FLOW," and the discharge opening of the quill or nozzle shall inject in the same direction. Also, a secondary indication of direction shall be shown, such as a global coordinate like "NORTH," or with some other positive indicator like the downstream equipment number, etc.

The quill shall be designed so that there is physically only one way of installing it to achieve the above concurrent flow, or, shall have matching alignment tabs on the quill flange and mating piping flange that clearly indicate they should fit together to ensure correct flow direction. If alignment tabs are used, they should also be shown on the downstream side of the flanges. See Directional Detail Drawing LA-000-0100-0419-001 for an example of alignment tabs and detail that must accompany any quill.

#### 13.2.2 Documentation Requirements

If the quill or injection piping is retractable, the manufacturer's detail drawing should be stored in Equipment and Vendor Files, in Documentum, or as a Industry Source 01 piping detail drawing. Any new quill that is installed shall have a reference on the P&ID to the drawing containing these details.

Injection quills shall be assigned a 3200 series Location number and a GP number, per the Location Codes, Class, and Subclass tables in the Equipment Number Request form in Documentum.

### 13.3 Lifecycle Requirements

Injection and mixing points have increased inspection requirements and shortened intervals, which are already defined by Inspection Practices. Whenever a quill is pulled for inspection, it should be inspected for mechanical integrity (fatigue cracks, corrosion, obvious signs of packing glands leaks, etc.). When an injection or mixing point is added, deleted, or modified, the Inspection department shall be notified.
