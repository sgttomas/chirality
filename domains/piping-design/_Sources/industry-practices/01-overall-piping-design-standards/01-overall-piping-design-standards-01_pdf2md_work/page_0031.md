## 7.2 Site Re-Hydrotesting (Third Party)

Site re-hydrotesting of valves is not required if the valves have been procured per BP CHP STP 205 or the BP R&M Approved Manufacturers List (AML). Re-hydrotesting of valves procured through normal channels is optional and may be performed at the discretion of the BP CHP Engineer/Project Team. Critical valves and valves purchased outside of the approved lists above should be considered for local re-testing.

## SECTION 8: REQUIREMENTS FOR SPECIFIC VALVE TYPES

### 8.1 Butterfly Valves

8.1.1 Butterfly valves shall conform to BP CHP STP 205 and BP CHP STP 204.

8.1.2 Due to their short service life compared to gate valves, butterfly valves require specific BP CHP approval for each application. High performance designs are preferred.

8.1.3 Triple offset butterfly valves shall be installed per the manufacturer's instructions with the valve oriented to have the higher pressure on the shaft side of the disc when in the closed position. Triple offset butterfly valves are designed to be leak tight in both directions at the rated pressure and temperature of the valve, however best sealing performance will be achieved when pressure acts on the shaft side of the disc. Piping designers shall indicate the correct orientation of the valves on the piping design drawings.

### 8.2 Ball Valves

8.2.1 Ball valves shall conform to BP CHP STP 205 and BP CHP STP 203. Ball valves shall be designed to be fire safe and shall be certified to comply with the latest edition of API 607 or other equivalent fire test approved by the BP CHP Reliability Engineer.

### 8.3 Check Valves

8.3.1 Check valves shall be sized appropriately for the flow conditions, to assure that the valve will not operate in the nearly closed position and will not experience excessive wear due to disc chatter. It is not acceptable to use a check valve matched to the pipe size without reviewing the manufacturer's sizing criteria.

### 8.4 Globe Valves

8.4.1 Globe valves shall conform to BP CHP STP 205 and BP CHP STP 206. Globe valves are normally suitable for throttling when the disc is at least 20 % open; otherwise flow-induced vibration may result, leading to valve damage. For this reason, valve sizing is important to assure that the valve is not too large for its intended flow conditions. Typically, a bypass valve is too large if it is the same NPS as the control valve it bypasses. For bypass valve applications with high differential pressure or critical services, a standard globe valve may not be suitable. An engineered control valve design should be considered in such applications. The globe valve sizing should be reviewed by the BP CHP Instrument Engineer and Fixed Equipment Engineer during project design. If an engineered control valve design is selected, an upstream isolation block valve should be installed since control valves have an inherent seat leakage rate. The leak rate should be carefully considered in this application.
