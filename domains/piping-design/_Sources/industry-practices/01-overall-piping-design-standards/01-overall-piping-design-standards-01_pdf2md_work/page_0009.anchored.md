## 2.4 Layout and Arrangements

### 2.4.1 Crane Access

Equipment, structures, and piping shall be arranged to permit crane access to compressors, air coolers, and major equipment parts not provided with built-in maintenance facilities.

### 2.4.2 Dead Legs

Dead legs and low points should be avoided. Refer to section 12.0 for dead leg design criteria.

### 2.4.3 Pump Access

Clear access, both vertically and horizontally, will be provided under main pipeways for trucks or mobile equipment to service pumps.

### 2.4.4 Exchanger Access

Whenever possible, tube bundles shall be removed with a mobile crane and tube bundle extractor. Permanent monorail structures will be provided only where crane access is impractical. Where required, the monorail shall extend far enough beyond the exchanger's channel end to permit the use of the hoist to both pull and lower tube bundles to grade. Where built-in bundle handling facilities not provided, clear space for tube bundle removal by mobile crane shall be provided. Clearance 1-1/2 times the bundle length in front of the exchanger, measured from the tube sheet, and extending 20' on at least one side, shall be provided and free of piping.

### 2.4.5 Furnace and Boiler Access

#### 2.4.5.1

Furnaces and boilers shall be located near a plot boundary for easier access. Tube pulling shall be by mobile crane with adequate dropout and swinging area provided. External piping should be minimized. Road access shall be provided to the tube pulling area.

#### 2.4.5.2

Where practical and economical, fired equipment shall be grouped together. Common stack(s) are permitted only with prior Industry Source 01 CHP approval when they are more economical; however, dampers or suitable barriers must be provided in individual ducting to the stack. Such dampers and barriers shall provide isolation that will allow personnel entry into the shutdown equipment while the other units are still fired or operating.

### 2.4.6

Prevailing winds should be considered with vessels, pumps, compressors, and other equipment handling light hydrocarbons located downwind of fired heaters to minimize the possibility of vapors being carried toward an ignition source. Heater orientation and the use of wind boxes shall be considered if operation would be affected by the maximum winds anticipated in the area.

### 2.4.7

Piping shall be laid out in an orderly manner for economy, appearance, efficient operation, and maintenance.

### 2.4.8

Avoid compound angles in piping configuration unless absolutely necessary.

### 2.4.9

Piping runs outside of the main pipeways shall be parallel or perpendicular to pipeways wherever possible.

### 2.4.10

Piping and valves shall be arranged so as not to present a tripping, striking, or safety hazard.

### 2.4.11

Lines through tower platforms and structures shall be sleeved. The sleeve height above the platform, deck, or floor shall be 6".

### 2.4.12

Pockets in lines shall be held to a minimum and avoided whenever possible. Free draining lines shall be noted on the P&ID's.
