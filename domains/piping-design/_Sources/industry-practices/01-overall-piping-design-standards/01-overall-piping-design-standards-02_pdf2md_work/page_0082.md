- p) All jacketed lines.

- q) Lines that although not in themselves stress critical, tie into stress critical lines and have a significant impact on stress or flexibility.

- r) Lines connected to equipment or non-ferrous material, or equipment with brittle lining.

- s) Lines connected to equipment for which the manufacturer has specified low loadings.

- t) Any line that the Owner or the Designer designates as stress critical.

- u) Buried lines with a design temperature above 160°F (71°C).

- v) All lines in severe cyclic service as defined by B31.3 section 300.2.

5.17.2.2. The person(s) responsible for piping design and stress analysis shall be notified immediately when any changes that affect the design conditions are made to stress critical lines.

## 5.17.3. Stress Sketches and Analysis

### 5.17.3.1.

To ensure an efficient stress analysis the following information must be shown on the stress sketch:

- a) Branch points complete with line number.

- b) Co-ordinates and elevation of at least one reference point to assist in locating on orthographics.

- c) Critical clearances from piping and equipment.

- d) Design/upset and operating pressures and temperature.

- e) Dimensions +/- 2" (+/- 50 mm) where possible. Dimensions shall be shown from support to support, support to branch or fitting to fitting.

- f) Direction of flow

- g) Insulation type and thickness.

- h) Line numbers.

- i) Line sizes.

- j) Location and size of reducers.

- k) Location and type of all proposed pipe supports.

- l) Location of fixed end of horizontal vessels and heat exchangers.

- m) Location of nozzle centre lines relative to fixed points or anchors for horizontal vessels and bottom tangent lines for vertical vessels.

- n) Location of structural steel.
