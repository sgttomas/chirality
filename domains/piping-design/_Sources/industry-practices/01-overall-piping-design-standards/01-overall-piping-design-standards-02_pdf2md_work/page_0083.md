5.17.3.2. The piping stress analysis shall comply with the applicable Code for the service and location.

5.17.3.3. The piping stress analysis shall be done with a computer program, manual calculation, or with reference to pre-calculated tables of U-shape and L-leg configurations. The method used shall be recorded on the stress sketch.

5.17.3.4. Depending on the complexity of the project, and to avoid major relocation of equipment or rerouting of pipes during the detail design, preliminary stress sketches followed by preliminary stress analysis shall be made. This preliminary work may be done while some of the items listed in paragraph 5.17.3.1 are "assumed", and when required shall include alternate locations and routing.

5.17.3.5. The results of the piping stress analysis shall be marked-up on the stress sketches and shall include:

- a) Nozzle loadings on equipment.
- b) Loads on anchors and stops.
- c) Loads of over 1000 lb (454 kg) and movements of over 1" (25 mm) on solid pipe supports.
- d) All loads and movements on spring hangers.

5.17.3.6. It is the stress analyst's responsibility to ensure that pipe loadings on equipment comply with industrial standards, or are accepted by the equipment manufacturer.

5.17.3.7. If the mark-ups on a stress sketch include re-routes or relocation of supports, spring hangers, anchors, or stops, it is the Piping Designer's responsibility to include them in the final design. If that is not possible then alternatives must be developed and re-calculated.
