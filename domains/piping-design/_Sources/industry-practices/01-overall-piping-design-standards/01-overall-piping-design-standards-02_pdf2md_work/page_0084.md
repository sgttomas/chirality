5.17.3.8. Before the piping drawings are issued for construction the person(s) responsible for stress analysis and Piping Design shall double-check the construction drawings to ensure that all stress recommendations and requirements have been incorporated.

5.17.4. Field Work

5.17.4.1. Before any changes are made during fabrication or installations, the person(s) responsible for stress analysis and piping design shall be notified and shall decide whether those changes affect the piping stresses. Their agreement is required before the changes can be made.

5.17.4.2. Near the end of construction the stress analyst or his designate shall make site visits to ensure that the piping systems are properly installed. Particularly important are:

a) Spaces for thermal expansion and contraction

b) Expansion joints including their tie-rod and supports

c) Spring hangers including their "cold and empty" loading

5.17.5. Documentation and Records

5.17.5.1. The Document Control System shall have cross-references between the line number, the Stress Sketch(es), and the piping stress analysis calculations, Including the alternate sketches and analysis.

5.17.5.2. At the completion of the project the final versions of the stress sketch(es), and the piping stress analysis calculations, including any changes made during construction or start-up, shall be submitted to the Owner's Records Department.

## 5.18. Injection Points and Process Mix Points

5.18.1. Injection points are locations where chemicals or process additives are introduced into a process stream. Special attention is required in designing the point of injection of corrosion inhibitors, neutralizers, process antifoulants, antifoam, desalter demulsifiers, oxygen scavengers, caustic, and water washes to mitigate the potential for erosion, corrosion, fatigue or other mechanical damage and to also ensure proper mixing occurs.

5.18.2. The design the injection system shall consider the purpose, location and method of injection as well as materials selection for the injection device (quill/nozzle), injection delivery system and piping or equipment into which the material is being injected. High alloy materials are normally required.

5.18.3. Injection quills/nozzles shall be designed to be removable for inspection and/or cleaning. The need for in-service removal of injection quills shall be identified if required.

5.18.4. Mix points, where the joining of two or more process streams of differing composition and/or temperature takes place require attention to design. Risks such as cyclic service (due to thermal cycling or flow induced vibration for example), differential thermal stress, localized erosion, localized corrosion, environmental cracking, and mechanical loads shall be addressed in the design.
