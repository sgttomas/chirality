## 5.17. Stress Analysis

5.17.1. The purpose of this Section is to establish guidelines and procedures that must be followed in order to provide a uniform approach to piping stress analysis, starting with stress critical lines, and ending with field inspection after installation and start-up. Stress analysis is done mostly for thermal expansion or contraction, but also included are lines where weight or fluid dynamics causes excessive stresses.

5.17.2. Stress Critical Lines

5.17.2.1. The following minimum criteria shall be used for selecting lines to be stress analyzed, and for which stress sketches shall be produced.

a) Lines of NPS 3 and 4 with design temperature above 500°F (260°C).

b) Lines of NPS 6 and larger with design temperature above 250°F (121°C).

c) All lines with design temperature below minus 20°F (-29°C).

d) Non-ferrous lines with design temperature above 212°F (100°C).

e) Non-metallic lines with design temperature above 122°F (50°C).

f) All lines with very long horizontal or vertical straight runs that have lower design temperature limits than stated in paragraphs a) thru e) above. Lines which require assessment shall be determined by the Piping Stress Engineer.

g) All lines NPS 24 and larger.

h) Lines with wall thickness less than schedule 10/10s or greater than extra strong.

i) All lines subject to any mechanical, fluid, or 2-phase flow induced impact, vibration, or dynamic loads.

j) All PRV inlet and discharge piping to ensure adequate supporting and restraint for all moments, forces, shock loads, and stresses on the valve body, the connection flanges, the piping, and the supporting structure.

k) All pump, compressor and turbine piping, excluding instrument or auxiliary piping.

l) All fired heater, steam generator, Industry Source 08 and tube exchanger with bellows, and air cooler piping, excluding instrument, fuel gas or auxiliary piping.

m) Piping connected to vessels, tanks or equipment subject to different settlement (or upheaval), or to thermal expansion.

n) Lines containing expansion joints, or quick disconnect couplings.

o) Lines with special non-standard fittings or supports that locally reduce the strength.
