5.4.9.4. In addition to the Code requirements mentioned above, hot bending shall be in accordance with the International Tube and Pipe Association Standard TPA-IBS-98. A written procedure is required.

5.4.9.5. In addition to the Code requirements mentioned above, cold bending shall be in accordance with International Tube and Pipe Association Standard TPA-CBS-98. A written procedure is required.

5.4.9.6. Carbon and Low Alloy Steel

a) Pipe bending in accordance with ASME B31.3 is acceptable where design permits with the exceptions/cautions listed below:

   i. Hardness's after bending shall not exceed limits specified in relevant Industry Source 10 or industry standards such as Industry Source 10 0903, 0202, NACE MR0103 or NACE MR0175

   ii. Carbon steel piping for service temperatures of less than -20°F (-29°C) (Charpy impact tested) generally is not a candidate for bending without special precautions and additional testing.

5.4.9.7. 300 Series Stainless Steels

a) Cold bending of 300 series stainless steel is acceptable where there is no risk of stress corrosion cracking. Where there is a risk of stress corrosion cracking, cold bending followed by solution annealing is acceptable. Prior to hot bending of 300 series stainless steel the fabricator shall show, by qualification tests including a 400x micrograph and ASTM A 262 Test Practices A and E, that the base material will not be damaged during bending.

5.4.9.8. Other Materials

a) A qualified materials/fabrication/piping engineer shall be consulted for advice concerning bending of materials not covered in this Standard.

5.4.9.9. Bending shops/fabricators shall be involved at the piping design stage and suitable specifications agreed on so that understanding is reached on all pertinent design and fabrication requirements and issues.

5.4.9.10. Bends shall be used to change direction when:

a) The bend radius required is not standard for factory made fittings.

b) Flow requirements dictate their use.

5.4.9.11. Miter bends shall be used for lines NPS 26 and larger in Category "D" fluids, if cost effective when compared with pipe bends or factory made buttwelding fittings. Miter bends shall be in accordance with the requirements and limitations of ANSI/ASME B31.3. Exceptions require written approval by the Owner's Engineer.
