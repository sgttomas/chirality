d) Branch connections shall be made to the top side of the header to ensure drainage into the main. Branch block valves shall be installed in the horizontal to provide drainage away from a closed gate.

#### 5.9.14. Fired Heaters

**5.9.14.1.** Heater piping and supports vary with the type of heater. Heaters can be roughly classified as cylindrical and rectangular.

**5.9.14.2.** Cylindrical heaters have vertical tubes located concentrically inside a thin steel Industry Source 08 with nozzles at the top. Piping from the nozzles drops down the outside, usually in a flat bank. Due to large heater diameter and thin Industry Source 08, all supports from heater Industry Source 08 require reinforcing.

**5.9.14.3.** For rectangular (box type) heaters with horizontal tubes, nozzles are usually located at about pipe rack levels and can take only small (500 lb force average) (2224 N) forces from connecting piping. Special anchors are usually required to protect these nozzles and internal piping.

**5.9.14.4.** Where pigging will be required for furnace tube cleaning or inspections, furnace piping shall be designed with removable spools or swing elbows to facilitate pigging of the furnace tubes.

#### 5.9.15. Blowdown Piping

**5.9.15.1.** Blowdown and flare lines shall be designed, if possible, without low points or pockets which collect condensate, and in such a manner that they shall drain by gravity. Where this is impractical, a valved drain shall be provided at the low point.

**5.9.15.2.** Where process systems are provided with blowdown facilities, the blowdown valves shall be so located or provided with remote manual operating devices, such that their operation shall not be impeded by a fire in the area of the equipment being blown down.

#### 5.9.16. Vacuum Seal leg Piping

**5.9.16.1.** All seal leg piping shall be vertical with no horizontal runs. 45° pulled bends shall be utilized with legs run at 45° or less to the vertical.

**5.9.16.2.** The length of a barometric leg, which depends on the specific gravity of the liquid and the depth of vacuum, shall be taken into account.

#### 5.9.17. Furnace transfer lines.

(Vacuum and Atmospheric Distillation)

**5.9.17.1.** Consideration shall be given to solid particle erosion when designing furnace outlet transfer line piping. The use of pulled bends and/or metallurgy changes in the piping shall be considered at the discretion of the Owners Engineer.

**5.9.17.2.** Straight inlet line process requirements might set the minimum distance between the furnace and the tower. Usually 50 feet (15.25m) minimum is provided between last pipe loop entry into the transfer line and the tower inlet nozzle.
