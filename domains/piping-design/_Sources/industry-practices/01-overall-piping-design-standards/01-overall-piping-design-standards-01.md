# SECTION 1: ADMINISTRATION AND REFERENCES

## 1.1 Purpose

1.1.1 This standard is intended to establish the minimum requirements for the design, materials, and testing of all piping systems for the Industry Source 01 (hereafter referred to as Industry Source 01 CHP in this Standard). The safety and integrity of the piping systems form the primary basis for the requirements contained herein. Consideration also has been given to efficient operation and economy of costs.

1.1.2 This standard is not intended to replace good engineering or to impede the development of new designs and methods which will produce better or more economical piping systems. As with any standard, sound engineering judgment must guide its use.

## 1.2 Scope

1.2.1 Piping systems consistent with the scope of ASME B31.3, Chemical Plant and Petroleum Refinery Piping and the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section 1, shall be in accordance with the latest issues of those Codes, including latest published addenda. This Standard is intended to either supplement or to emphasize portions of the Codes.

1.2.2 Piping systems excluded from the scope of the above mentioned Codes shall be installed in accordance with this Standard.

1.2.3 Where jurisdictional authorities have requirements exceeding or in conflict with this Standard, such requirements shall supersede this Standard.

1.2.4 This Standard is also applicable to piping furnished with packaged equipment, including the lube and seal oil systems of rotating equipment.

1.2.5 Other specifications cover the special requirements for sewer and drainage systems. Special requirements may also apply to fire protection systems. Heating, ventilating, water, and air systems servicing buildings, rather than plant or process areas, may conform to applicable heating, ventilating, plumbing, or refrigeration codes.

1.2.6 The P&ID drawings shall show the limits of the piping governed by the Boiler Code and indicate which code or requirements, other than the Refinery Piping Code, apply to the various parts of the piping systems.

## 1.3 Deviations

1.3.1 Any deviations to this standard shall be resolved as discussed in Industry Source 01 CHP STP P001.

1.3.2 Codes, Standards, or Publications referenced in this Standard shall be the latest approved version of such Code, Standard, or Publication unless otherwise indicated. Any deviations from or questions about the above shall be brought to the attention of Industry Source 01 CHP. A Industry Source 01 CHP written approval is necessary prior to the completion of the design.

1.3.3 Any conflicts between this standard and other Industry Source 01 CHP standards or applicable codes shall be brought to the attention of Industry Source 01 CHP for resolution.

## 1.4 Review and Approvals

1.4.1 Industry Source 01 reviews or approvals required by this Standard shall be by the Industry Source 01 CHP Engineer or designated alternate.

## 1.5 Supplementary Standards

1.5.1 Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 contains the detailed specifications for all piping and tubing components.

1.5.2 Several standard piping drawings supplement this document and shall be complied with where applicable. These drawings can be found and accessed in DocumentUM with the search function using partial drawing number "LA-000-0100-0200-." These drawings are

subject to ongoing revision, and the latest version should always be obtained prior to each use.

## SECTION 2: DESIGN

### 2.1 Miscellaneous Technical Criteria

**2.1.1** Material selection for piping in hydrogen service shall be in accordance with the section of Industry Source 01 ETP GP 38-10 covering high temperature hydrogen attack. Hydrogen service is defined as any service in which the partial pressure of hydrogen in the fluid handled is 50 psia or greater. When the operating conditions exceed 400°F and 50 psia hydrogen partial pressure, carbon steel material is not allowed without written approval by the responsible Industry Source 01 engineer. If carbon steel material is approved for piping operating above 400°F and 50 psia hydrogen partial pressure, the following additional requirements shall be included in the design: install temperature indicator instrumentation located at the highest practical process temperature point, with output to the operator control board; establish the safe operating temperature limit for the piping; and set up an appropriate operations monitoring program to assure safe operation.

**2.1.2** Where a service is designated as "General Hydrocarbons" (without having specific quantities of either sulfur or hydrogen sulfide specified), it shall be understood that these lines may contain up to 0.2 Wt.% sulfur and/or 0.01 mole% hydrogen sulfide.

**2.1.3** Piping design shall be based on a minimum service life of 12 years and a maximum corrosion rate of 10 mils/year. The design corrosion allowance shall not exceed 0.125" unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. Piping systems expected to exceed the maximum corrosion rate or maximum corrosion allowance shall be upgraded to a higher alloy or less corrosive material.

**2.1.4** In general, piping designed to this standard shall be hydrotested to the test pressure of the flanges, unless the pipe wall thickness would limit the test pressure to a lower value. If it can be shown that a significant savings can be realized in certain piping systems, the test pressures may be lowered with Industry Source 01 CHP approval, provided the applicable code or legal requirements are met.

**2.1.5** Electric Heat tracing is preferred when required for maintaining process temperature or for freeze protection, except for sulfur services.

**2.1.6** Industry Source 01 ETP GP 42-10 section 301.15 line velocity criteria shall be followed unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP.

**2.1.7** Care shall be taken to avoid sealed voids in piping in hydrogen and wet H2S service where diffused hydrogen can accumulate. When sealed voids cannot be avoided, 1/4" threaded vent/weep holes shall be provided in all such voids, such as piping reinforcement pads, piping anchor saddles, and similar attachments. These holes shall be positioned for best drainage of the cavity, and shall be packed with heavy grease after pressure testing of the pads.

**2.1.8** Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) shall be used for all root pass welds for seal oil and lube oil piping, for alloy and stainless steel piping, for acid piping, for turbine inlet piping, and carbon piping between knockout drums and compressors. The GTAW requirement for these root pass welds shall be noted on the applicable piping isometric drawings.

**2.1.9** For pipe wall thickness requirements, see the line classes in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 for established services and materials. For pipe services or materials not currently included in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205, new line classes will need to be developed, including determination of pipe wall thickness. As far as possible, new line classes should be patterned after existing similar line classes. New line class names will be assigned by the designated Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer. The minimum required pipe wall thickness for new line classes shall be the corrosion allowance plus the greater of the following: (a) wall

thickness required by ASME Code calculations for the design pressure and temperature; or (b) the minimum required mechanical/structural thickness for pipe span, stress analysis, or other considerations. The mechanical/structural thickness shall at least comply with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 92.

2.1.10 Small diameter trim piping at reciprocating compressors and in similar situations with the potential for vibration shall be designed and supported to prevent piping fatigue problems. Consideration should be given to piping designs which improve the resistance to pipe fatigue, such as providing heavier wall thickness at equipment connections, using full penetration welds, minimizing the unsupported length of the piping, and minimizing the mass of unsupported valves and instruments. See related requirements for trim piping at reciprocating compressors in section 4.1.5. For designing piping for or avoiding fatigue issues, piping should be supported such that the natural frequency of a given span is at least 5 times greater than the pulsating flow frequency. For additional guidance on this subject, see the Energy Institute publication Guidelines for the Avoidance of Vibration Induced Fatigue Failure in Process Pipework.

2.1.11 Materials for new or replacement piping systems exposed to high temperature sulfidation shall be 9Cr-1Mo, or a higher alloy such as austenitic stainless steel. 5Cr-¼Mo or carbon steel material is not permitted. High temperature sulfidation corrosion occurs in hydrocarbon services containing sulfur compounds operating at temperatures above 450°F in refinery process units including Crude Distillation, Vacuum Distillation, Coker, FCC, Visbreaker, and Hydroprocessing.

## 2.2 Design Pressure and Temperature

- 2.2.1 The maximum operating pressure and maximum operating temperature shall be the most severe condition of coincident pressure and temperature expected in service.

- 2.2.2 The design pressure shall be determined per the following table, except as specified herein, or otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. For 140# steam the design pressure shall be 170 psig. For fire water the design pressure shall be 150 psig, limited by Industry Source 01 CHP STP 285 line class CAM. For instrument air, utility air, and utility nitrogen the design pressure shall be 150 psig to maintain Category D service per ASME B31.3.

> **Table - Maximum Operating Pressure / Design Pressure table** - see [XLSX](tables/01-overall-piping-design-standards-01_p0006_tbl01_max-operating-pressure-design-pressure.xlsx) and [source crop](tables/01-overall-piping-design-standards-01_p0006_tbl01_max-operating-pressure-design-pressure.png)

| Maximum Operating Pressure | Design Pressure |
| :--- | :--- |
| Less than 100 psig | 150 psig |
| 100 - 250 psig | Maximum Operating Pressure + 50 psi |
| Greater than 250 psig | 1.20 x (Maximum Operating Pressure) |

- 2.2.3 The design temperature shall be at least 50°F higher than the maximum operating temperature, except as specified herein, or otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. For 140# steam the design temperature shall be 500°F, limited by ASME B16.5 flanges and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line class CAH. For superheated 140# steam with design temperatures above 500°F, use pipe class DAH. For cooling water return the design temperature shall be 150°F, limited by line classes CA3 and CAX. For potable water the design temperature shall be 73°F at 150 psig, limited by the rating of the HDPE pipe in line class BW1. For utility air the design temperature shall be 150°F limited by line class CAL. For instrument air the design temperature shall be 100°F limited by line class CAN. For oily water sewer the design temperature shall be 150°F limited by line class A2A.

- 2.2.4 Every system's design shall be reviewed to insure that the design pressure and temperature are based upon the most severe condition of coincident pressure and temperature as specified in the codes cited in Section 1.2.1. For example, where piping is subject to:

    a) High temperature and relatively low pressure during catalyst regeneration, and/or

b) A higher pressure but somewhat lower temperature during the normal operational cycles, and/or

c) Steam-out conditions for cleaning, warm-up, or leak testing of piping during shutdown, start-up, or maintenance periods (see 2.2.8).

It is unnecessary to design the piping to the temperature of a and the pressure of b. The proper procedure is to calculate a, b and c requirements separately, and select the result requiring the greater pipe and component thickness.

2.2.5 Design temperature for steam-traced piping will be the fluid temperature or 20°F below the tracing steam's saturation temperature, whichever is greater.

2.2.6 The piping design temperature and MDMT for a fluid with an operating temperature below minus 20°F will be the fluid's normal operating temperature. Additionally, the piping design shall be suitable at 100°F for the design pressure.

2.2.7 The piping design MDMT for a fluid with an auto-refrigeration temperature below minus 20°F will be equal to or less than the auto-refrigeration temperature.

2.2.8 In most cases, piping in hydrocarbon services needs to be designed to include steam-out. An operations representative for the applicable process unit shall be consulted to verify which piping systems require steam-out capability and which steam system would be used. Steam-out conditions shall be based on full blocked-in pressure and temperature for the steam system to be used, unless unit operating procedures or other positive means are established to control the steam-out to lower conditions. When other than full steam pressure and temperature are proposed to be used for the steam-out design conditions, justification shall be submitted to the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer for approval.

## 2.3 Safe Practices

2.3.1 Special precautions must be taken in handling oxygen both in permanent piping and in temporary connections to oxygen supply trailers. Refer to Compressed Gas Association CGA G-4.4 Oxygen Pipeline and Piping Systems, and ASTM G88 Standard Guide for Designing Systems for Oxygen Service. See also engineering data sheets Industry Source 01 Doc 335 for additional requirements. Mechanical and chemical cleaning requirements shall be noted on the piping isometric drawings.

2.3.2 Plastic safety shields shall be provided for flanged joints and valve bonnets on lines carrying acids, caustics, or other hazardous materials in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205.

2.3.3 Safety showers and eyewash fountains per drawing LA-000-0100-0200-039 shall be provided in areas where personnel are subject to sprays or spills of acids, caustics, or other hazardous chemicals.

2.3.4 For hot tapping or welding on piping in service, refer to Industry Source 01 CHP Safety and Health Policy 520.

2.3.5 Where periodic disassembly is required for multiple piping components of similar configuration but different metallurgy, flanged piping spools or other removable components shall not have symmetrical design. The removable portions shall be designed with differing shapes or lengths to prevent mistakenly interchanging carbon steel or lower alloy parts with higher alloy parts.

2.3.6 Special precautions must be taken in services where Iron Sulfide (FeS) can come into contact with hydrogen and/or hydrocarbons. For example, during the design of process water filters, provision for purging with inert fluid shall be considered (reference Texas City Fatality Incident January 14, 2008).

2.3.7 Provisions should be made in the design to allow bleeding and/or draining of trapped pressures and volumes to achieve a Zero Mechanical State (ZMS) prior to maintenance. Installation of a bleed valve should be considered between check valves and block valves, as a means to ensure ZMS downstream of the check valves (reference Texas City Fatality Incident September 2, 2004). Alternately, installation of a bypass around the check valve could be considered.

Note: This requirement is intended to apply to both new and existing piping, but does not apply to the following services:

- Water (<100°F, such as CWS, CWR, firewater, process water)

- Air

- Special circumstances (such as all-welded systems), providing that a formal risk review is conducted.

2.3.8 The use of tubing warrants special attention during design, installation, operation, and maintenance, to ensure mechanical integrity. Tubing is often used for services such as instrumentation, pump seal flush, sample stations, and injection quills.

Incidents at Industry Source 01 CHP related to hazardous leakage from tubing and tube fittings include high pressure hydrogen service, rotating equipment (vibration) and injection quills. Contributing causes are often the inadequate fit up of joints and lack of proper support for the tubing.

Hazardous services for tubing are defined as hydrocarbons above autoignition temperature, toxic chemicals, all high pressure services (500 psig and up) and high temperature services (250°F and up). Some specific limitations on the use of tubing in hazardous services are as follows:

a) In general, the length of tubing and the number of compression fittings should be minimized as much as is practical. Each specific installation must be designed with this objective.

b) The use of intermediate compression joints for splicing in these services is not allowed without approval by Industry Source 01 CHP.

c) Injection quill systems in these services must be hard piped per the applicable pipe specification (such as pipe classes CJL and FJL).

Note: Pre-insulated tubing bundles are acceptable in hazardous services, provided that they are properly protected and supported, and installed without splices.

Refer to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 314 for instrument tubing requirements.

2.3.9 As applicable, Industry Source 01 ETP GP 44-10 *Plant Layout* shall be followed in the design of piping systems. References within GP 44-10 to other standards or codes only apply to the extent specified in GP 44-10.

2.3.10 To prevent high cycle fatigue failures in small piping joints at reciprocating compressors, see section 4.1.5 for special requirements for NPS 2 and smaller trim piping joints and connections.

2.3.11 Dissimilar metal welds are not allowed for pressure containing welds in wet H₂S or HF services, since hydrogen can be charged into the metal and result in hydrogen stress cracking. Exceptions apply for some clad pipe applications. See additional information in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 42 section 13.

2.3.12 Unless approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP metallurgist, 300 series stainless steel piping components shall not be welded to or installed in contact with galvanized or zinc coated components, particularly when the material may reach temperatures above 750°F. See the liquid metal embrittlement sections of ETP GP 42-10 for more information.

## 2.4 Layout and Arrangements

### 2.4.1 Crane Access

Equipment, structures, and piping shall be arranged to permit crane access to compressors, air coolers, and major equipment parts not provided with built-in maintenance facilities.

### 2.4.2 Dead Legs

Dead legs and low points should be avoided. Refer to section 12.0 for dead leg design criteria.

### 2.4.3 Pump Access

Clear access, both vertically and horizontally, will be provided under main pipeways for trucks or mobile equipment to service pumps.

### 2.4.4 Exchanger Access

Whenever possible, tube bundles shall be removed with a mobile crane and tube bundle extractor. Permanent monorail structures will be provided only where crane access is impractical. Where required, the monorail shall extend far enough beyond the exchanger's channel end to permit the use of the hoist to both pull and lower tube bundles to grade. Where built-in bundle handling facilities not provided, clear space for tube bundle removal by mobile crane shall be provided. Clearance 1-1/2 times the bundle length in front of the exchanger, measured from the tube sheet, and extending 20' on at least one side, shall be provided and free of piping.

### 2.4.5 Furnace and Boiler Access

#### 2.4.5.1

Furnaces and boilers shall be located near a plot boundary for easier access. Tube pulling shall be by mobile crane with adequate dropout and swinging area provided. External piping should be minimized. Road access shall be provided to the tube pulling area.

#### 2.4.5.2

Where practical and economical, fired equipment shall be grouped together. Common stack(s) are permitted only with prior Industry Source 01 CHP approval when they are more economical; however, dampers or suitable barriers must be provided in individual ducting to the stack. Such dampers and barriers shall provide isolation that will allow personnel entry into the shutdown equipment while the other units are still fired or operating.

### 2.4.6

Prevailing winds should be considered with vessels, pumps, compressors, and other equipment handling light hydrocarbons located downwind of fired heaters to minimize the possibility of vapors being carried toward an ignition source. Heater orientation and the use of wind boxes shall be considered if operation would be affected by the maximum winds anticipated in the area.

### 2.4.7

Piping shall be laid out in an orderly manner for economy, appearance, efficient operation, and maintenance.

### 2.4.8

Avoid compound angles in piping configuration unless absolutely necessary.

### 2.4.9

Piping runs outside of the main pipeways shall be parallel or perpendicular to pipeways wherever possible.

### 2.4.10

Piping and valves shall be arranged so as not to present a tripping, striking, or safety hazard.

### 2.4.11

Lines through tower platforms and structures shall be sleeved. The sleeve height above the platform, deck, or floor shall be 6".

### 2.4.12

Pockets in lines shall be held to a minimum and avoided whenever possible. Free draining lines shall be noted on the P&ID's.

#### 2.4.13 Valve Access

- **2.4.13.1** Preferred locations for valves are specified on drawings LA-000-0100-0200-001 and LA-000-0100-0200-002.

- **2.4.13.2** Valves which are used during routine operations or an emergency shall be located so that they are accessible from grade or a suitable platform. The valves shall be installed such that the bottom of the handwheel is no more than 4' 9" above grade or platform. When this requirement becomes impractical (such as awkward location or inadequate space) a deviation may be granted with approval from the operations representative for alternatives such as allowing the use of gear operators with extended stems or chainwheels.

- **2.4.14** Chainwheels shall not be used on bypass valves at all control valve stations.

- **2.4.15** Unless otherwise specified, a Battery Limit valve shall be installed on each branch of a loop serving two or more units. Battery Limit valves shall be accessible from a suitable platform. A bleed valve is required on the unit side of Battery Limit process valves and Battery Limit utility valves. Chainwheel operators are not allowed on Battery Limit block valves. Handwheel extensions are acceptable. Spectacle blinds shall be provided at battery limits. The items above are the minimum Battery Limit requirements. Other requirements may be applicable from the Industry Source 01 CHP Safety and Health Manual Isolations Policy 5001, or to meet the needs of the Operations Department.

- **2.4.16** All chainwheel operators shall be specified with safety retaining cables. Chainwheel operators on 6" and larger gate and globe valves shall be the impact type for non-gear operated valves, using the type mounted directly on the yoke bushing, Roto Hammer series "M" or equal. For Orbit valves, gear operated valves, and valves 4" and smaller with handwheel diameters 4" and larger, use non-impact type direct mount chainwheel operator, Roto Hammer Series "E" or equal. For valves with handwheel diameters less than 4" use clamp-on type chainwheel operator, Roto Hammer series "CL" or equal. Clamp-on type chain operators shall not be used on 6" valves and larger, gear operated valves, or valves with handwheel diameters 4" and larger.

- **2.4.17** Valves on small branch connections shall be installed as close to the header as possible to reduce stresses, dead legs and corrosion between the connection and valve.

## 2.5 Above Grade Piping

- **2.5.1** All piping within and adjacent to process units, except drains, shall be run overhead and routed as directly as possible, preferably in established rack pipeways. Outside of piped pipeways, utility lines shall be routed on the upper support unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineering.

- **2.5.2** Piping shall run at different elevations on designated north-south and east-west racks and will change elevation (minimum 3') when changing direction. Flat turns in main pipeways must have prior Industry Source 01 CHP approval and are only permissible when the status of the layout is sufficiently advanced to insure that the line concerned will be the ultimate outer line on the rack.

- **2.5.3** The spacing of bare and insulated pipe in pipe racks shall be determined using the required insulation thicknesses contained in Industry Source 01 CHP Standard Industry Source 01 Doc 000-001 to meet the following minimum pipe clearances shall be determined after consideration of any expected pipe movements addressed in section 2.9.1. For bare and insulated lines at temperatures 450°F and lower, a minimum 1" clearance shall be provided between all parts of adjacent lines, including flanges and insulation coverings, and a minimum 6" clearance shall be provided between all parts of lines and adjacent electrical conduit. For bare and insulated lines at temperatures above 450°F, the respective clearances defined above shall be increased to 2" and 7". A minimum 2" clearance shall be provided between all parts of pipe and adjacent pipe rack structural members, not including the beams or members directly supporting the pipe. For heat traced lines, the

diameter of the heat tracing material shall be added to the insulation thickness before confirming pipe clearances. At orifice flanges, larger pipe spacing shall be provided as needed to provide adequate clearance from the connected orifice tap valves, tubing, and instruments.

Note: Pipe flanges operating at temperatures above 500°F shall not be insulated, to keep the bolts exposed to atmospheric cooling and reduce the potential for flange leaks. Personnel protection in accordance with section 2 of Industry Source 01 CHP STP 100 shall be applied to uninsulated hot flanges where required.

2.5.4 Piping in offsite areas such as tank farms may be run on sleepers with Industry Source 01 CHP approval. A minimum clearance of 1' between flange and grade is required for maintenance and painting. Storage tank isolation valves shall be provided outside of tank dike walls.

2.5.5 When new pipe rack space is required for a project, only enough pipe rack space shall be added to support the current work scope including a minimum 20% design contingency, unless Industry Source 01 CHP Refinery Management specifically approves the inclusion of additional spare capacity for future refinery use. Where future pipe rack expansion is feasible, the main pipe supports shall be designed to carry one additional deck. The Engineer or Designer shall obtain direction from Industry Source 01 CHP regarding additional spare pipe rack capacity before starting the design. For design calculations, assume any future space to be occupied by 8" lines filled with water. Piping shall be arranged to accommodate the future use of any additional space provided. See Industry Source 01 CHP STP 807 for additional pipeway design criteria.

2.5.6 Adequate clearance shall be provided for operation and maintenance of control valves, including sufficient elevation for plug and trim removal without removing the valve from the line.

2.5.7 Manifold piping shall be arranged to provide working clearance for industrial type, hand, air-powered, or hydraulic wrenches used for bolt tightening. Restricted access should be avoided.

2.5.8 Valves, flanges, unions, and other breakout connections shall NOT be located inside vessel skirts.

## 2.6 Below Grade Piping

2.6.1 Assurance must be provided that no underground obstructions or interferences exist within the footprint of the proposed construction work. Assurance may be provided by potholing, hydro excavation of perimeter, utility location tools, verified accurate drawings, ground-penetrating radar, or similar methods.

2.6.2 Buried steel lines shall have an exterior protective coating applied in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 503.

2.6.3 Buried steel lines requiring insulation above grade shall be insulated below grade with a foam glass system conforming to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 100.

2.6.4 For sleeve requirements for underground piping, see Industry Source 01 CHP STP 801.

2.6.5 Trenches should be avoided. Any trench deemed necessary shall be constructed of concrete with open grating covers and provided with firebreaks consisting of concrete walls, packed at the pipe openings, to contain spills. Low-point drains shall be provided as the trenches must be self-draining. Solid trench covers shall not be used unless an approved standard deviation request is obtained from Industry Source 01 CHP, and trenches with solid covers must be supplied with vents as well as firebreaks. Also refer to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 801 for additional trench requirements.

2.6.6 There shall be a minimum of a 1' clearance between piping or edges of piping flanges installed in trenches and the floor. The piping shall be flanged to permit removal from

trench without cutting. A 6" clearance shall be provided between piping or edge of flange and grating or cover plate. The grating cover should be capable of supporting the expected traffic loading.

2.6.7 All buried lines shall maintain clearances or barriers from potable water lines per Department of Health (DOH) requirements.

2.6.8 Below grade piping shall have a minimum 30" pipe cover as shown on drawing LB-000-0100-0801-008, unless approved otherwise by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer.

2.6.9 Underground valves shall be installed in valve boxes or vaults for maintenance access, unless otherwise approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer. Typical details for underground valve boxes and vaults may be found on drawings LD-00-0100-302, LD-000-0100-0402-004, LD-000-0100-0402-005, LD-000-0100-0402-005, LD-00-4000-0000-2315, and LD-000-4000-2861-002. Alternate details may be submitted to the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer for approval.

## 2.7 Clearance and Accessibility

### 2.7.1 Overhead Clearances

The following minimum vertical clearance under overhead piping, pipe supports, instrument tubing, and instrument/electrical trays shall be maintained:

- a) Over railroads, top of rail to bottom of any obstruction – 22.5'
- b) Over plant roads – 17.5' minimum
- c) At pump row access way (under pipeway) to nearest obstruction - 12'
- d) Over pumps and turbines from high point of grade - 8'
- e) Overhead walkways, passageways, and platforms to nearest obstruction (except at dead ends) – 7.5'

### 2.7.2 Horizontal Clearance Guidelines

Equipment, structures, platforms, piping, and their supports shall be arranged to provide the following minimum horizontal clearances:

- a) Fired heaters to pump and other flammable-containing equipment (exclusive of compressors) - 50'
- b) Fired heaters to equipment handling nonflammable liquids - 25'
- c) Fired heaters to compressors handling flammable vapors - 100'
- d) Minimum aisle spacing between large compressors - 8' or rod pulling length
- e) Compressors handling flammable vapors to non-associated process pumps, vessels, or similar equipment - 25'
- f) Width of aisle way under the main pipe rack of process units – 12.5' (If pumps are mounted on the pipe rack perimeter, see k below.)
- g) Back of control panels - 4'
- h) Operating aisles - 4'
- i) At driver end of pumps where truck access is required - 10'
- j) At driver end of pump where truck access is not required - 5'
- k) Under pipeways, between driver end of pumps, where access is required - 18'
- l) At Industry Source 08 cover end of exchangers at grade, for access way - 4' (Exclusive of bundle pulling requirements)

- m) Maintenance platform at channel end of elevated exchangers - 3'
- n) Maintenance platform at Industry Source 08 cover ends of elevated exchangers – 3.5'
- o) In front of manways - 3'
- p) Minimum passageway at grade and on elevated platforms – 2.5'
- q) Between extremities, including piping of adjacent pumps – 2.5'
- r) Between extremities, including insulation of paired exchangers or vessels - 1.5'

## 2.8 Support and Anchorage

**2.8.1** Pipe supports, guides, anchors and insulation supports shall be shown on the piping drawings.

**2.8.2** All piping shall be adequately supported, guided, or anchored so as to prevent excessive vibration, deflection, or stress on equipment. Loads imposed on equipment by the piping shall not exceed that allowed by the equipment manufacturer and the applicable Industry Source 01 CHP STPs 10, 12, 14, 20, 21, 70, 71, and CHP-GIS-34-201.

**2.8.3** A listing showing predicted thermal movement and proposed anchor locations for all lines crossing the process unit battery limits shall be submitted for approval by Industry Source 01 CHP and included in the Mechanical Equipment Data Books. The intent is to handle on-site piping expansion within the battery limits. When significant movement due to thermal changes is anticipated on an overhead line entering or leaving a process unit, the line shall be anchored at the process unit battery limit.

**2.8.4** See standard pipe support, shoe, and anchor drawings LA-000-0100-0200-040, LA-000-0100-0200-041, LA-0100-80, LA-0100-81, LA-0100-84, LA-0100-85, LA-0100-86, LA-0100-87, LA-0100-88, LA-0100-89, LA-0100-90, LA-0100-91, LA-0100-93, LA-0100-94, LA-0100-95, LA-0100-97, LA-0100-98, LA-0100-223, LA-0100-224, LA-0100-257, and LD-0100-20. These drawings shall be used where applicable unless Contractor's or supplier's alternate detail drawings have been approved for use by Industry Source 01 CHP. Note that clamp on type shoes are not permitted without prior Industry Source 01 CHP approval.

**2.8.5** Pipe shall not be welded to its support (other than a shoe) for either anchorage or vibration damping.

**2.8.6** Piping shall be arranged and supported to permit removal of equipment such as pumps and exchanger bundles for routine maintenance without having to install temporary supports. Equipment shall not be used as a pipe support.

**2.8.7** Above grade piping shall be supported by structural members. Use of dummy pipe supports shall be minimized per section 12.3, but dummy supports are preferred over use of rod hangers. Rod hangers may be used only where no other means of support is practicable. Rod hangers shall be assigned an RH number by submitting a request form to Document Control, and a detail drawing shall be created for each hanger using the Industry Source 01 CHP rod hanger drawing template.

**2.8.8** All piping shall be supported in a manner suitable for hydrostatic test loadings unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. In larger diameter piping handling gas during normal operation, it may be economically advantageous to design the permanent structural support for gas loading and install temporary support for hydrotest loads. The Contractor shall bring this to Industry Source 01 CHP's attention for consideration. If approved by Industry Source 01 CHP, the temporary supports shall be prominently noted on the engineering drawings.

**2.8.9** Spring hangers and spring supports shall be provided with permanent scales marked with hot and cold positions, position indicators, and limit stops. Spring hangers/supports shall be assigned an SH number by submitting a request form to Document Control, and a detail drawing shall be created for each spring hanger or support using the Industry Source 01 CHP spring hanger/support drawing template.

- 2.8.10 Integral attachments including lugs, ears, shoes, plates, and angle clips welded to the piping shall meet the same welding requirements as the piping.

- 2.8.11 Dummy pipe extensions, used as supports, shall be completely isolated from the active piping to prevent entry of the flowing medium. Dummy supports shall be provided with a 1/2" drain hole at the low point of the cavity, where there is potential for liquid or pressurized gas accumulation. Some dummy leg supports may be required to include a 1" by 1-1/2" inspection hole to accommodate in-service thickness examination of the pipe or elbow. At the approval of the piping design, the project engineer or designer shall consult with the Industry Source 01 CHP Inspector for designation of the specific dummy support locations where inspection holes are required. Inspection holes shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-042.

- 2.8.12 Insulated hot lines shall be provided with shoes sized so that the required insulation thickness will clear the pipe support. Insulated lines in low temperature service shall be supported on wrapper plates curved to fit the outside of the pipe line insulation.

- 2.8.13 Uninsulated piping subject to vibration shall be supported on pipe shoes. Other bare piping should rest directly on supporting steel.

- 2.8.14 Rigid adjustable base supports shall be provided for the inlet and outlet piping for all rotating equipment, where possible. The adjustable base supports as shown on drawings LA-000-0100-0200-040 and LA-000-0100-0200-041 are standard unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. Spring support may be considered if use of rigid support will impose unacceptable nozzle loads on the equipment due to thermal expansion.

- 2.8.15 See Section 2.1.10 for guidance on pipe support design for small diameter piping in services with potential for vibration and fatigue issues, such as reciprocating compressor trim piping.

## 2.9 Expansion and Flexibility

- 2.9.1 All piping shall be designed for thermal expansion from start-up, normal operations, regeneration, shutdown, steam out, solar heat, and/or ambient conditions without overstress of piping, valves, or connected equipment. Pipe spacing per Section 2.5.3 should assure that expanding lines will not touch or damage adjacent lines, conduit, structures, or similar items.

- 2.9.2 In general, Industry Source 01 CHP prefers that expansion shall be taken care of by flexibility in piping using elbows, bends, offsets, or loops. Packed type expansion joints and "cold spring" of piping shall not be used without written approval.

- 2.9.3 Piping to be stress analyzed as listed in Section 10.0.

- 2.9.4 Clamps or U-Bolts on small piping which has thermal movements shall not restrict the movement, unless they are designed to act as guides or anchors. The piping support drawings shall clearly indicate the intended purpose of the clamps or U-Bolts, and specify any special installation requirements.

## 2.10 Vibration

- 2.10.1 Careful attention shall be given to the design of piping connected to equipment, valves, or instruments, which may produce harmonic effects, pulsations, or excessive vibrations, which may result in improper operation or damage to piping, equipment, or instruments. When such conditions exist, consideration shall be given to the use of pulsation damping devices or other design modifications. For additional piping requirements related to vibration and reciprocating compressors see sections 2.1.10 and 4.1.5 in this standard, and applicable sections of Industry Source 01 PHP STP 10.

- 2.10.2 Design models for piping systems for pressure relief valves sized greater than 2"x3", which include more than 30 feet pipe length and three or more elbows in the inlet piping,

shall be reviewed for potential vibration problems resulting from relief valve chatter. This review should identify whether additional guides and supports are needed for the piping.

## 2.11 Pressure Relief and Flare Headers

- 2.11.1 Equipment shall be protected by safety or relief valves in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 350 and the Industry Source 01 CHP STP CHP-GN 44-70. See section 2.26.5 of this standard for requirements for snuffing steam and weep holes at relief valve atmospheric discharge piping.

- 2.11.2 Relief valves shall be positioned to be accessible by mobile crane. If this is not possible, consult Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer for direction.

- 2.11.3 Block valves for flare header isolation or pressure-relief device isolation shall be capable of being locked or car-sealed open, and shall be designed and installed such that they cannot inadvertently close due to mechanical failure. Gate valves used for flare header isolation or pressure relief device isolation shall be installed with stems oriented horizontally to prevent partial or complete blockage due to a detached disc. When it is not possible to install a gate valve with the stem in the horizontal position, use a different type of valve, such as a full port ball valve or orbit valve.

- 2.11.4 Flare headers shall be provided with continuous gas purges, which enter the headers at the extreme upstream ends and are regulated to control the purge gas flow. See additional purge gas requirements in Industry Source 01 CHP STP CHP-GN 44-70. For corrosive gas streams, additional purge gas may be needed immediately downstream of the relief valve so that the entire relief valve discharge piping will be swept. Consult with the Industry Source 01 CHP Corrosion Engineer to determine where additional purges are needed for corrosive gas streams.

## 2.13 Valving

- 2.13.1 The rules contained in Sections 2.13.2 and 2.13.3 are intended to reduce excess valving. Piping designers are encouraged to propose additional simplifications for Industry Source 01 CHP approval.

2.13.2 Unless specifically required for operation, block valves will not be provided at vessel nozzles when the line to or from the vessel is self-draining.

2.13.3 Normally a block valve will not be provided at the vessel nozzle for the following line services:

a) Overhead vapor to condensers

b) Thermosiphon reboilers; liquid and vapor

c) Overhead condensers to accumulator

d) Inter-connecting exchanger units in series

## 2.14 Vents, Drains and Bleeds

2.14.1 Complete venting and draining of all piping, either thru equipment or separate connections, is required. Minimum size for vents and drains in piping shall be 3/4". Design and installation of vents and drains shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-034.

2.14.2 All drains periodically blown down, including instrument, piping drains, and drains from major equipment, shall be independently routed to the sewer with discharge visible from the drain valve location. Hazardous material shall be released at a safe distance from the drain valve and operator.

2.14.3 Funnels with a splash rim should be used at drain point on elevated platforms with the funnel drains routed to a sewer at grade.

2.14.4 For vents and drains intended for use only for hydrotesting, it is preferred to have permanent valves installed per the pipe line class. If temporary threaded valves are used, they shall be removed and the threaded openings shall be plugged and seal welded per Industry Source 01 CHP STP 211 paragraph 14.4.4. Threaded pipe caps shall not be used in place of round solid plugs at vents and drains. Seal welding of the plugs is not required for pipe line classes which specify threaded fittings in sizes below 2".

2.14.5 All vent and drain locations shall be shown on the isometrics.

2.14.6 Vessels, exchangers, centrifugal pump casings, and other equipment shall be provided with a drain line not smaller in size than the drain connection on the equipment.

2.14.7 A closed pump-out system shall be provided for units which cannot be adequately cleared using system pressure. Pump-out lines shall be 2" minimum in size with a blind and double block and bleed arrangement as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-003.

2.14.8 A positive means of isolation shall be provided for all vents and drains in VOC services and hazardous services. In these services, vents and drains that are **expected to be routinely opened** while the unit is operating shall be provided with double block valves. Vents and drains subject to this requirement include installations in process piping, utility piping, instrument piping and tubing, sight glasses, vessel trim, pump trim, and compressor trim. For all other vents and drains in VOC and hazardous services, where the valves are not expected to be routinely opened, other means of positive isolation besides double block valves are acceptable as noted below.

Note: Other acceptable means of positive isolation are: (1) a single valve with a threaded round head solid plug; or (2) a single valve with a blind flange.

Services considered to be hazardous include all process hydrocarbons, 600# steam and condensate, boiler feed water, caustic, acid, nitrogen, oxygen, natural gas, fuel gas, ammonia, sour gas, sour water, and other services containing toxic chemicals. Services considered to be non-hazardous include low pressure steam and condensate, lube oil.

seal oil, instrument air, process air, utility air, cooling water, fire water, potable water and utility water.

Note: The piping downstream of the final valve may have threaded joints if it is open to the atmosphere. In that case, the final valve is preferred to be a socket weld by threaded end valve. Otherwise, the piping joints shall be as specified in the applicable line class.

- 2.14.9 Drain valves shall be provided at low points on both sides of control valve manifolds between the control valves and the upstream and downstream block valves as shown on drawings LA-000-0100-0200-030 and LA-000-0100-0200-032.

- 2.14.10 Drain valves shall have at least 1'-0" clearance from grade level to allow for cleaning or unplugging.

## 2.15 Utility Stations

- 2.15.1 Utility stations consisting of 1" steam, water, and air connections with a hose rack shall be provided at convenient points at grade, exchanger platform levels and operating platforms which have stairway access. The utility stations shall be located so that with a single 50' length of hose per station, coverage is provided for all equipment. Utility stations and hose racks shall be installed as shown on drawings LA-000-0100-0200-013 and LA-000-0100-0200-014.

- 2.15.2 Utility stations with air connections only shall be furnished at each vessel platform servicing a manway and at the top platform of vertical heaters and reactors unless accessible from a grade station with a 50' hose.

- 2.15.3 Utility water only stations, without hose rack, shall be located on towers and vessels for manway coverage using a single 50' hose. Piping to service water connections on tall structures should have a valve manifold at column base for draining when not in service.

- 2.15.4 Utility stations with steam, air, and water service plus hose racks for steam and water shall be provided as follows:

  - 2.15.4.1 At grade and on elevated exchanger platforms to permit complete area coverage with a 50' length of hose from each station.

  - 2.15.4.2 At loading racks for coverage with a 50' length of hose.

- 2.15.5 Where nitrogen utility stations are specified, they shall be designed and installed in accordance with standard detail drawing LB-00-0100-264.

## 2.16 Equipment Piping

- 2.16.1 Warm-up piping for hot and heavy oil pumps shall be piped to a point on the bottom of the pump casing.

- 2.16.2 Temporary conical strainers and removable spool pieces for strainer removal shall be provided for all turbine inlets unless permanent strainers are indicated on the P&ID. Strainers shall be installed between the turbine and inlet block valve. The apex of the conical screen shall be pointed upstream.

- 2.16.3 Flanged block valves must be provided on all 1-1/2" and smaller connections on equipment.

- 2.16.4 Pipe flanges which mate to equipment flanges shall be welded in the field unless otherwise approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Rotating Equipment Engineer assigned to the project.

## 2.17 Pump Piping

- 2.17.1 The piping configuration shall permit removal of the suction screen, impeller, pump or driver without the removal of the block valves, or temporary supports. Provide removable spools if required. Adequate flexibility and support in the piping shall be provided so that

the piping loads imposed on pump nozzles are within those allowed by Industry Source 01 CHP's Pump Standards. If expansion joints are required, they shall be sleeved and tied as a minimum. Pipe supports at the pumps shall be adjustable per drawings LA-000-0100-0200-040 and LA-000-0100-0200-041.

2.17.2 The suction and discharge block valves and check valve for centrifugal pumps shall be line sized if the line is equal to or one size larger than the pump nozzle. If the line is two or more pipe sizes larger than the pump nozzle, the valves shall be the intermediate size between the line and nozzle.

2.17.3 Suction and discharge block valves shall be accessible for hand operation.

2.17.4 Centrifugal and rotary pump discharge lines shall be provided with a check valve between the pump and the first block valve where there is the possibility of reverse flow. Minimum flow bypasses where required for pump protection shall take off between the pump discharge nozzle and check valve.

2.17.5 Any size reduction in horizontal runs of suction lines shall be made with eccentric reducers installed with flat side up.

2.17.6 Any centrifugal and rotary pumps that are not equipped with permanent strainers shall be provided with temporary conical screens and removable spool pieces for strainer removal. The screens shall be installed between the suction valve and the pump to permit removal without a system shutdown. The apex of the conical screen shall be pointed up stream.

2.17.7 Pressure gauges shall be provided on the discharge side of all centrifugal and rotary pumps, between the pump and the block valve. A single pressure gauge shall be provided on the common suction line between the normal and spare of centrifugal and rotary pumps and on the suction and discharge of reciprocating pumps. The gauges shall be visible from either pump.

2.17.8 All vents and drains shall be piped individually to a sewer at the front of the pump base. This includes the pump baseplate drain. Vents and drains shall be 3/4" minimum except for the connections furnished on the pump which may be smaller. Drains shall be provided for all low points in the pump or piping that can trap liquid, and between the discharge block valve and check valve when separated by a spool piece. Drains shall also be provided between the discharge block valve and the check valve for pumps handling hydrocarbons, toxic, or hazardous fluids. Spools shall be provided between the block and check valves for accommodating the drains as required.

2.17.9 Provide warm up lines from above (downstream of) discharge check valve to pump drain if required.

2.17.10 Suction piping symmetry shall be maintained to parallel pumps with the piping arranged to avoid short radius elbows, tees, crosses, and similar items so that the flow is as smooth (nonturbulent) as practicable at the suction nozzle.

2.17.11 Seal oil piping shall be of a material compatible with the process piping. Seal oil lines shall not be common to vents. Cooling water piping shall be arranged routed to the various pump parts in a parallel arrangement from a 1-1/2" minimum header terminating with a plugged tee for clean out. Copper tubing and brass fittings shall not be used on process pumps. This piping, if field routed, shall not impede seal and coupling access.

2.17.12 Pumps shall not be located under main pipeways. Pumps may be located at the edge of the pipeway to allow pipe supports such as spring hangers for the pump suction and discharge piping to be cantilevered away from the pipeway.

## 2.18 Seal Oil and Lube Oil Piping

Piping used for seal oil and lube oil services shall be 304 or 316 stainless steel per STP 205. Low carbon grades 304L and 316L, and dual grade are acceptable but not required.

Normally the low carbon grade material is required for welded stainless steel piping systems; however for lube oil and seal oil services it is not necessary due to the low risk of cracking, since water, carbon dioxide, or chlorides are not expected to be present. For additional seal oil and lube oil piping requirements see paragraph 2.1.8 of this standard, and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 13.

## 2.19 Steam Turbine Piping

- **2.19.1** Turbines shall be supplied with steam from the top of the steam header line, and shall include a block valve (located at the header). Connections shall be at least 10' upstream of the end of the header. The steam supply piping shall have a condensate leg and trap immediately ahead of the turbine where the piping configuration would permit condensate to collect. The supply line for turbines shall be run beyond the turbine and into a trapped condensate leg.

- **2.19.2** Main valves shall be provided on the steam supply and exhaust lines. See also Section 2.26.4.

- **2.19.3** A relief valve shall be installed in the turbine's exhaust steam line between the block valve and turbine that can be blocked in, in addition to sentinel relief valve, unless the turbine case can withstand the full inlet steam pressure. A full size 3-way valve on the exhaust venting one flow path to atmosphere may be substituted for the relief valve.

- **2.19.4** Standby turbines equipped for automatic or remote starting shall be provided with a warm-up line around the control valve assembly, consisting of a minimum 1" gate valve followed by a flanged restriction orifice. The control valve must be installed in a horizontal run to ensure all condensate buildup will be trapped away.

- **2.19.5** Turbines shall also be provided with a steam trap on the case drain. Condensing turbines which exhaust upwards require vacuum traps.

- **2.19.6** Block valves upstream of trip throttle valves on multistage turbines shall be provided with a 1" minimum warm-up bypass and block valve.

- **2.19.7** Piping loads transmitted to turbines shall be within those allowed by Industry Source 01 CHP's turbine standards. If the piping configuration does not naturally achieve flexibility, then expansion joints must be used. Sleeved, pressure balanced joints shall be used for 140 psig and higher steam. Expansion joints for lower pressure steam must be sleeved and tied as a minimum.

- **2.19.8** Steam turbine supply piping shall be dedicated to the turbine, and shall not have process steam takeoffs.

## 2.20 Compressor Piping

- **2.20.1** Suction piping between knockout drums and reciprocating compressors shall be chemically cleaned and protected with a rust preventative. All cleaning procedures shall be approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. The piping shall be given final inspection and acceptance by Industry Source 01 CHP engineers prior to installation. This cleaning and preparation shall not be done "in place"; therefore the suction piping shall be designed so removal is possible and can be cleaned before installation. Chemical cleaning requirements shall be noted on the piping isometric drawings.

- **2.20.2** Block valves shall be installed on both inlet and outlet lines of the compressor. Suction and discharge pressure and temperature gauges shall be provided. All drains shall be piped to sewer, including interstage case drains. On reciprocating compressors, a full capacity relief valve from each stage shall be installed.

- **2.20.3** Provision for a temporary start-up conical suction strainer shall be supplied between the suction block valve and the compressor, as near to the compressor as possible. The apex of the cone shall point upstream. Valved connections shall be provided on the inlet and outlet sides of the strainer for differential pressure taps.

- 2.20.4 Piping shall be designed to allow for removal of compressor parts and for adequate operating and maintenance clearances. Valves, compressor controls, and accessories requiring operating checks and maintenance shall be accessible from grade or platforms. Piping leads on compressor nozzles shall be held to within the allowable established by Industry Source 01 CHP's compressor standard.

- 2.20.5 Cooling water shall be supplied as required from parallel manifolds and furnished with block valves on both inlet and outlet. The manifolds shall dead end with a plug or blind for cleanout purposes. Cylinder jacket water shall be provided with steam-water tempering spargers designed to operate silently while discharging between 90°F to 140°F unless separate dedicated cooling systems are supplied.

- 2.20.6 Distance pieces on reciprocating machines in hydrocarbon service shall be vented with pipe runs terminating at least 10' above grade or operating platforms.

- 2.20.7 Connections for pressure gauges and thermowells shall be installed in the nozzles (on the compressor side) of all pulsation bottles. The connections shall conform to the materials specification of the attached piping. They shall not interfere with maintenance of the machine. Pressure gauges shall be installed in piping downstream of discharge pulsation bottles.

- 2.20.8 Reciprocating compressor pipe supports and braces recommended by the equipment manufacturer shall be installed. These supports and braces will be identified by an Analog Study performed by the equipment manufacturer. Every deletion is to be justified by the engineering contractor and approved by Industry Source 01 CHP. Equipment reactions/loadings shall encompass the first vessel or exchanger located upstream of the suction pulsation damper and downstream of the discharge pulsation damper.

- 2.20.9 Recycle lines for centrifugal compressors shall be full discharge line size to the control valve and full suction line size downstream of the control valve.

- 2.20.10 Unless otherwise specified, all suction lines from the knockout pot to the cylinders of reciprocating compressors, including the suction pulsation dampers, shall be heat traced and insulated.

- 2.20.11 Check valves in centrifugal compressors which are installed in piping systems with ASME flange class 600 and lower shall be special non-slam, slow-closing check valves. Mokveld TKZ, Cameron Entech Non-Slam Nozzle Check Valve, Curtis-Wright Enertech NozzleCheckValve, or approved alternate. Check valves at centrifugal compressors which are installed in piping systems with ASME flange class 900 and higher, and check valves at all reciprocating compressors, shall be approved by Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineering. Review and selection of check valves at compressors shall include both the Fixed Equipment and Rotating Equipment Engineers.

- 2.20.12 For additional piping requirements related to reciprocating compressors see Sections 2.1.10 and 2.5.6 in this standard, and applicable sections of Industry Source 01 CHP STP 10.

## 2.21 Product Sample Draw-Off Piping

Product sample piping shall be as short and direct to the sample cooler or sample outlet as much as possible to minimize product loss. Product sample systems shall be closed loop type. Consult with Industry Source 01 CHP Engineer for specific sample station requirements.

## 2.22 Vessel and Exchanger Piping

- 2.22.1 Coolers and condensers shall have inlet and outlet valves on the water sides with drains, vents, pressure gauges, and a thermal relief valve in between the block valves.

- 2.22.2 Piping shall be designed for the removal of bundles without additional temporary supports. Good access to exchanger flange bolting and pulling lugs on the sides of exchangers for rigging gear shall be provided. Adequate bundle removal space should be provided.

2.22.3 Vents on vessels shall be blind flanged and drains generally piped to the sewer. Drains from process outlet piping may suffice if complete drainage of vessel will result. All valves and instruments shall be accessible from platforms.

## 2.23 Fuel and Pilot Gas Piping

2.23.1 The use of stainless steel "Braided Flexible" hose is acceptable for the final connection between the supply piping and the individual burner and/or pilot. The flexible section shall meet or exceed the pressure/temperature and hydrotest requirements of the respective line class in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205.

2.23.2 The process fluid shall be routed to each heater pass by symmetrical piping design for equal flow distribution, and, if an all liquid phase flow, through the use of flow control valves.

2.23.3 Fuel and pilot gas piping shall be routed without pockets and supplied with drain valves at the low points. Individual take-offs shall connect to the top of the header and shall be supplied with two isolation valves. Valves that will be used to take the burners or pilots in and out of service shall be 1/4 turn valves. All valves in fuel or pilot gas service shall have a temperature range sufficient to allow for steaming out the piping system. Heater fuel and pilot gas piping shall comply with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-038.

2.23.4 The total fuel to each heater shall be measured and recorded.

2.23.5 Ahead of each pilot gas and burner manifold, a 1" valved connection shall be installed for methanol injection. Pressure gauges are required on the common fuel gas feed line and at each of the manifolds.

## 2.24 Heater Piping

Piping and supports at heaters shall be designed to provide clear access for manways, explosion doors, peepholes, heater tube replacement workspace, and similar items. Piping shall be designed so that thermal expansion loadings are not absorbed by the heater coils. Free access shall be afforded burner manifolds. Refer to 2.26.7 for heater snuffing steam requirements.

## 2.25 Flue Gas Sample Connections

Provisions for the sampling of the flue gas at the outlet of the radiant section should be provided. The draft indicator lead may be used. In any event, the sample line shall be piped to grade or to the draft gauge elevation. An eductor operating on plant air shall be installed to draw down the sample gas.

## 2.26 Steam and Condensate Piping

2.26.1 Steam piping shall be designed for maximum condensate elimination. Low points in steam headers shall be equipped with a drip leg and a steam trap. A trap is required at each collection point. Traps shall be installed ahead of all outlets, expansion joints, and dead ends of lines and headers. Steam piping and steam trap design shall comply with standard drawings LA-000-0100-0200-005, LA-000-0100-0200-006, LA-000-0100-0200-007, LA-000-0100-0200-008, and LA-000-0100-0200-009.

2.26.2 Exhaust steam lines discharging to the atmosphere shall be equipped with an exhaust head. The discharge should not create a hazard to personnel or equipment. As a minimum, exhaust heads shall be located 10' above the highest operating area of equipment within a 25' radius. Condensate carryover should be minimized by ample line sizing to reduce steam flow velocity.

2.26.3 Exhaust steam header lines shall be installed without line pockets. Any line condensation shall drain away from the exhaust header.

- 2.26.4 Take off connections from either live or exhaust steam lines shall be from the top of the header. Header block valves shall be installed in the horizontal run of line at the high point above the header connection.

- 2.26.5 For relief valves in flammable services, snuffing steam shall be connected to all relief valve outlet piping which discharges to atmosphere. Minimum size snuffing steam lines shall be 2" for relief valves 4" and smaller, and 1-1/2" for relief valves larger than 4". Steam snuffing lines shall be self-draining and routed to avoid pockets and any collection of condensate. A 1/2" weep hole shall be provided at the low point of the atmospheric discharge piping from the relief valve. Snuffing steam shall be connected horizontally into the discharge pipe at the closest practical location near the relief valve. The snuffing steam line shall include a break-out union near the relief valve. Snuffing steam lines shall originate at battery limit manifolds clearly identified as to service. Manifolds shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-010. Relief valve snuffing steam manifolds shall be separate from heater snuffing steam manifolds (see section 2.26.7).

- 2.26.6 A steam out system, using 140# steam, shall be provided for all hydrocarbon vessels, towers, and exchangers, as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-003. (See Industry Source 01 CHP STP 40, for sizing of steam-out connection.)

- 2.26.7 Snuffing or smothering steam shall be connected to the fire box of heaters. The snuffing steam shall also include stab-in connections near the heater connections. Steam flow shall be regulated by a hand valve, properly identified and tagged, at the release manifold located 50' minimum from any fired heater. Snuffing steam lines shall be self-draining and routed to avoid pockets and the collection of condensate. If pockets are unavoidable, a 1/2" weep hole shall be provided at the low point. A low point bleed shall be provided to drain line condensate after snuffing steam use. Snuffing steam lines shall originate at a clearly identified snuffing steam manifold located at the battery limit. The heater snuffing steam manifold shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-010 and shall be separate from any relief valve snuffing steam manifold (see section 2.26.5).

- 2.26.8 All process steam lines shall tie-in directly at the steam header.

- 2.26.9 The design of all new condensate lines shall consider the potential for corrosion-erosion. If flashing or two-phase flow is possible, such as downstream of a control valve, orifice or other item causing significant pressure drop, consider using higher alloy fittings (elbows and reducers) in the zone where flashing or two-phase flow can occur. Alternatively, maximize the distance between the item creating the pressure drop and the downstream change in pipe direction, so as to allow the vapor to re-condense.

## 2.27 Steam Tracing and Jacketing

- 2.27.1 Electric tracing should normally be used for new installations instead of steam tracing or jacketing, except where steam is necessary for maintaining critical process temperatures. The necessity of steam tracing or jacketing shall be considered for the following conditions: where fluidity is required; where pipe metal temperature can drop below the dew point; where vapor condensation could occur; where protection against corrosive materials that can condense out; instrument leads in which any of the above conditions may affect the proper operation of instruments; and compressor suction lines in which condensation may occur on the piping.

- 2.27.2 Steam tracer material shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAHS and S-07. Steam jacketed piping shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAY and CXY.

- 2.27.3 Tracer tube fittings shall not be installed underneath insulation.

- 2.27.4 Steam tracers shall originate at the highest point in the system and drain toward traps. Each tracer branch shall have a block valve at the source. For a typical steam tracing

supply station see drawing LA-000-0100-0200-011. Tracer lines may split into branches but must not reconnect back. Tracer lines shall be banded to their companion pipe using 16 gauge stainless steel straps spaced on 4' centers.

## 2.28 Water Piping

### 2.28.1 Utility Water

**2.28.1.1** Utility Water is general purpose water used throughout the refinery with a nominal pressure of 120 psig. The connection of utility water to process vessels or piping shall be made in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-003.

**2.28.1.2** The source of water to be used in water wash systems for fractionating towers or other process equipment as required by the plant design shall be confirmed with Industry Source 01 CHP Process Engineers.

### 2.28.2 Fire Water (Nominally 125 psig)

**2.28.2.1** Generally fire water shall be routed underground. Fire water underground headers shall be 8" minimum.

**2.28.2.2** Fire hydrants, hose reels, fire monitors, and hose racks shall be installed as directed by Industry Source 01 CHP. In general, fire hydrants shall be spaced 125' to 150' apart around the periphery of process units and 250' to 300' apart in tank farms and other open areas. Fire monitors will also be installed around the process units, readily accessible and strategically placed, to cover the critical areas of high fire potential.

**2.28.2.3** Fire water spray systems shall be provided for pumps and compressors in accordance with recommendations in API RP 2030 *Application of Fixed Water Spray Systems for Fire Protection in the Petroleum and Petrochemical Industries*. The spray systems shall be designed in accordance with drawings LA-000-0100-0200-015 and LA-000-0100-0200-016, and shall consist of spray nozzles located over the equipment to provide full spray coverage. Water flow shall be regulated by a clearly identified hand valve at a centralized water spray manifold located a safe distance from the equipment. The minimum line size shall be 1" (1-1/2" in the pipeways) but hose reels shall govern minimum size. Spray nozzles shall be minimum 1/2" nominal size, 316 stainless steel, Spraying Systems Co. type HHSJX SpiralJet with full cone spray pattern and extra-large free passage. Water piping supplying multiple spray nozzles shall be symmetrical in design to provide balanced flow to each nozzle. Consider the need for clean-out connections in the piping system and provide where prudent.

**2.28.2.4** Design of water deluge protection should generally provide a water rate of 0.5 GPM/sq.ft. over pumps and 0.3 GPM/sq.ft. over compressors as well as areas 2' around so as to also cool connecting piping, vessels, fin fan bays, and similar items. Where motors or drivers are expensive and/or difficult to replace, they should also be protected along with the pump or compressor.

### 2.28.3 Cooling Water

**2.28.3.1** Cooling water piping within the process unit areas shall be routed overhead. Connections 2" and smaller from the main header shall be valved at the branch take-off. The minimum size connection to the main header shall be 1". Connections shall take-off from the top of the main header.

**2.28.3.2** The supply and return headers for a process unit area shall be provided with both temperature and pressure indicators on the unit side of the battery limit valves.

2.28.3.3 See additional cooling water installation requirements under specific equipment piping sections.

### 2.28.4 Potable Water

Potable Water piping shall conform to applicable Washington State Regulations. Special attention shall be paid to the project reports mandated in WAC 246-290-110, with exclusions allowed by WAC 246-290-125. NSF 61 requirements shall be followed as specified in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 (POPV). All correspondence (written and verbal) between Industry Source 01 CHP and the regulatory agencies shall be directed through the Industry Source 01 CHP Potable Water Specialist, to the DOH and to the Birch Bay Water Authority.

## 2.29 Emergency Isolation Valves (EIVs)

EIVs (some referred to as Emergency Block Valves) are defined as a means to isolate flammable or toxic substances, in the event of a leak or a fire.

EIVs shall be considered where required by API 553 (Section 7). A risk review shall be conducted with the appropriate site personnel to determine if EIVs are required and to specify details of installation.

Consideration shall be given to the installation requirements of API 553.

### 2.29.1 Service Applicability for Pumps (also see Industry Source 01 ETP GP 42-10)

Service Requirements are as follows:

- for flammable liquid service; and
- on piping systems located downstream of vessels (such as tanks; towers; reactors) and upstream of pumps; and
- when vessel volume is greater than 100 bbl; or
- when vessel volume greater than 50 bbl if temperature greater than 410 F; or
- when vessel volume greater than 50 bbl if temperature greater than auto ignition temperature; or
- when vessel volume greater than 50 bbl if the contents are light ends.

*Note: Excluded from requirements that require EIVs are installations with glandless or sealless pumps.*

Definition of Dact is as follows:

- in the case of a single pump, the actual horizontal distance between the pump and the upstream EIV; or
- in the case of a double pump/single EIV arrangement, the actual horizontal distance between a pump and the single EIV located upstream of the branch to the second pump; or
- in the case of a double pump/double EIV arrangement, the actual horizontal distance between each pump and the EIV located on each branch line.

*Note: The preference is to use a single EIV for double pump arrangements.*

Dmin is 10 feet, and is the minimum allowable horizontal distance between the pump and the EIV for the above three cases.

Remote actuation is required for Dact less than 50 feet. The actuating device (such as local push button) must be more than 50 feet away from the pump.

Fireproofing of the EIV is required for Dact less than 25 feet.

### 2.29.2 Service Applicability for Compressors, Heaters, Vessels and for Toxic Substances

Where EIVs are required, consideration shall be given to the installation requirements of API 553.

### 2.29.3 Mitigating Factors

In determining the final requirements, consideration should be given to factors such as:

- pump seal type
- installations where the pump seal flush is connected to the flare system
- corrosion history

## 2.30 Electrical Isolation Kits and Cathodic Protection

#### 2.30.1 Flange Isolation Kits

Flange isolation kits shall be applied and specified in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205, General Notes section 18. Flange isolation kits are intended to electrically isolate underground sections of a piping system from aboveground portions, or to electrically isolate metal piping of dissimilar materials which are connected together in either underground or above ground installations. Electrical isolation will prevent the underground piping from becoming a sacrificial anode for the above ground piping, and will eliminate the potential for corrosion due to galvanic activity between dissimilar metal piping.

#### 2.30.2 Cathodic Protection and Protective Coatings

To protect underground steel pipe from corrosion, cathodic protection and protective coatings shall be provided. Protective coatings shall meet the requirements of Industry Source 01 CHP STP 503. Cathodic Protection shall meet the requirements below.

a) Cathodic protection may be provided by either impressed current or sacrificial anodes.

b) Consult with the Industry Source 01 CHP corrosion engineer to review the cathodic protection design. In addition, consult with the Industry Source 01 CHP electrical engineer for impressed current systems.

c) Installation details for sacrificial anodes are shown on Industry Source 01 CHP drawings LA-000-0100-0414-001 through -004 and LA-000-0100-0415-001 through -014.

d) Sacrificial anode installations shall be recorded on the Industry Source 01 CHP Passive (Sacrificial) Cathodic Protection Schedule, LD-000-0100-0410-001. Sacrificial anode installation numbers are issued by Industry Source 01 CHP Document Control.

e) Sacrificial anode locations shall be shown on the applicable underground piping plan.

## 2.31 Anhydrous Ammonia Service

- 2.31.1 For anhydrous ammonia service, see the special requirements of WISHA and OSHA for piping systems and equipment. Special certification or labeling by nationally recognized laboratories is required for many of the material items used in this service, including valves, pumps, compressors, PSVs, level instruments, and pressure gauges. Installation requirements are also included for use of shut off valves, excess flow valves, and back pressure check valves for specific applications.

- 2.31.2 For non-refrigerated anhydrous ammonia systems, pipe with welded or welded and flanged joints shall be schedule 40 minimum, and pipe with threaded joints shall be schedule 80 minimum. Brass, copper, and galvanized piping and tubing materials are prohibited in non-refrigerated systems.

- 2.31.3 Flexible metal connections for permanent anhydrous ammonia installations shall have a minimum working pressure of 250 psig.

2.31.4 Threaded joint compound shall be ammonia resistant.

### 2.32 Utility Air Piping

2.32.1 The utility air piping system shall not be used for instrument air supply. The utility air piping and the instrument air piping shall be kept segregated.

2.32.2 Utility air piping shall be designed with no pockets for good drainage for water removal. Drain valves shall be provided at all low points.

2.32.3 Branch connections shall take off from the top of the line with a block valve provided for each take off.

2.32.4 The number of take offs per line shall be as follows:

- a) For a 1/2" line, 1 to 5.
- b) For a 3/4" line, 6 to 10.
- c) For a 1" line, 11 to 25.

### 2.33 Hoses in LPG Service

2.33.1 Hoses used in LPG services must be designed and manufactured in accordance with API 2510. Hoses shall be designed for a minimum working pressure of 350 psig and a minimum bursting pressure of 1750 psig, regardless of the hose class in which they will be installed. Prior to being put into service, hoses shall be hydrotested to 1.5 times the maximum pump discharge pressure or the relief valve setting, whichever is greater. Hoses are to be marked "LPG" at max. 10 ft. intervals, and marked with maximum working pressures as well as the products for which they are suitable.

2.33.2 Hoses, including the related couplings and valve assemblies, shall be hydrotested at least annually to the maximum pump discharge pressure or the relief valve setting, whichever is greater. It is the responsibility of the project engineer to request that the above equipment be added to a Preventative Maintenance plan. Thereafter, it is the responsibility of the appropriate Industry Source 01 CHP personnel (i.e. maintenance & reliability coordinator) to ensure that the required testing is completed.

2.33.3 Hoses that are intended for permanent use shall be assigned appropriate SP numbers and/or equipment numbers.

## SECTION 3: COMPONENT LIMITATIONS

### 3.1 General

Piping materials selection shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205, including additional components limitations listed in the General Notes of that standard.

### 3.2 Size Prohibitions

Piping smaller than 1/2" and pipe sizes 1-1/4" (except in plumbing systems), 2-1/2", 3-1/2", 4-1/2", 5", and 7" shall not be used. If equipment is furnished with connections in the above sizes, the transition to a Standard pipe size shall be made as close as possible to the equipment.

### 3.3 Pipe Caps

Threaded or socket weld pipe caps shall not be used, unless use of a round solid pipe plug or other piping alternative is not possible. For cases where the designer believes there is no alternative except use of a threaded or socket weld pipe cap, provide justification to and obtain approval from the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer before proceeding.

### 3.4 Welded Austenitic Stainless Steel

Austenitic (300 series) stainless steel materials which will be welded during fabrication shall be dual grade (such as 316/316L) or low carbon grade (such as 316L), except for seal oil and lube

oil piping in section 2.18, or unless otherwise approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer. Dual grade material should normally be specified since it fully meets the requirements of both grades and provides the combined benefits of both grades. Low carbon grade provides better resistance to carbide precipitation and intergranular corrosion of welds and heat affected zones. The dual grade then combines the better corrosion resistance with the higher mechanical properties of the standard grade material.

## SECTION 4: PIPING JOINTS

### 4.1 Acceptable Joint Types

4.1.1 For piping 1-1/2" and smaller handling hydrocarbons, toxic, or hazardous fluids, typically socket weld fittings will be used, except where full penetration welds are required for special conditions as specified in 4.1.5 for reciprocating compressors, or as specified in 4.3.2 for severe thermal gradients or thermal cycling, or as specified in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 pipe classes. With approval of the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer, threaded fittings may be allowed in place of socket weld fittings where the piping requires frequent disassembly or where flanges are impractical, such as fuel gas and fuel oil burner manifolds, pump gland plates, and similar applications. Socket welds shall be in accordance with drawing LA-000-0100-0200-018.

4.1.2 Pipe, fittings, flanges and valves may be threaded in sizes 1-1/2" and smaller for air and water service within the temperature-pressure limitations of Class 150 ASME B16.5 flanges. Threaded piping shall not be used underground unless approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer.

4.1.3 Threaded pipe and fittings may be used downstream of 1-1/2" and smaller block valves (the last valve in the case of double blocks) which bleed, vent, or drain to atmosphere.

4.1.4 Pipe, fittings, flanges and valves shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205. Except as previously permitted in this section, steel and alloy piping components in line classes with ASME B16.5 Class 150 or higher flanges shall be joined as follows:

a) Nominal Pipe Size 2" and Larger - Piping components shall be butt welded or flanged, except as described in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 General Notes section 9.2.2 for special circumstances for 2" size only.

b) Nominal Pipe Size 1-1/2" and Smaller - Piping components will normally be socket welded, except where flanged valves or flanged joints are needed at flanged equipment connections, blinding locations, or similar situations. Socket welds may be upgraded to butt welds where needed for special situations.

4.1.5 At reciprocating compressors in hydrocarbon, flammable, toxic, hazardous, sour, or corrosive services, all NPS 2 and smaller piping vents and piping branch connections for new or redesigned trim piping systems shall be full penetration welds or weld neck flanges. This is for the purpose of preventing high cycle fatigue failures at joints in small piping. Trim piping can be in any service and includes but is not limited to piping connections, instrument connections, vent/drain connections, lube oil connections, vents to flare, and similar items connecting to the following compressor system components:

a) Suction liquid knock-out vessels.

b) Suction piping within 50 pipe diameters of compressor.

c) Compressor.

d) Interstage piping within 50 pipe diameters of compressor.

e) Intercoolers and associated separators.

f) Aftercoolers.

g) Pulsation suppression devices.

h) Discharge piping within 50 pipe diameters of compressor.

4.1.6 For all piping systems containing H₂S streams with greater than 1000 ppm by volume for vapor stream or 1000 ppm by weight for liquid stream, joints are required to be welded. For vent, drain, and instrument branch connections or other branch connections to services which do not contain more than 1000 ppm H₂S, welded joints are also required at least up through the first block valve.

## 4.2 Branch Connections

4.2.1 Branch connections shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205, including the applicable branch connection charts referenced in the pipe classes.

4.2.2 Branch connections shall have adequate length to prevent valves and instruments from being covered by insulation.

4.2.3 Stub-in or stub-on branch connections shall not be used without prior written approval by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer, except for thermowell connections using LWN flanges as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-019.

4.2.4 Branch connections shall not be made by threading directly into the wall of the run pipe. Half couplings are not permissible for use as branch connections.

4.2.5 Hot tap connections, defined as the installation of a branch connection by welding and drilling on piping in service, shall be done in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP Safety and Health Policy 1620 Welding and Inspection in Service and Hot Tapping.

4.2.6 All integrally reinforced branch connection fittings (such as pipets and weldolets) shall be attached to the run pipe by a full penetration groove weld and a finish cover fillet weld as specified in ASME B31.3 or B31.1 as applicable, and as shown on drawing LA-000-0100-0200-019.

## 4.3 Flanges

4.3.1 Flanges are to be manufactured in accordance with ASME B16.5 and the various Standards listed in ASME B31.3 or B31.1 for sizes up to and including 24". Flanges above 24" shall be in accordance with ASME B16.47 Series B (previously API 605), except ASME B16.47 Series A (previously MSS SP-44) flanges may be used where necessary to connect to existing piping or equipment. Since Series A and B flanges are incompatible with each other, designers must verify dimensions of existing flanges for mating to new flanges.

4.3.2 Socket weld and threaded flanges are not recommended for services above 500°F if several thermal gradients or thermal cycling are involved.

4.3.3 Slip-on flanges shall not be used in any flammable, hydrogen, or steam services, or any service above 500°F operating temperature. Slip-on flanges shall not be used where severe bending stresses are likely to be present, or where there is vibration or cyclic temperature conditions. Slip-on reducing flanges are allowed for steam jacketed piping in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 line classes CAY and CXY.

4.3.4 Loose or optional-type flanges and lap joint flanges are not permitted.

## 4.4 Flange Bolting

4.4.1 Flange bolting shall meet the requirements of ASME B31.3 along with the following additional criteria:

4.4.1.1 Carbon steel bolts are only allowed where Code requires them to be used with cast iron flanges, and shall be machine bolts conforming to ASTM A307 Grade B. Carbon steel stud bolts are not permitted.

4.4.1.2 Stud bolts shall be threaded full length and shall be furnished with heavy hexagon pattern nuts. Thread fit shall be Class 2A-2B. Unless otherwise

approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer, bolting shall be Coarse Thread Series for nominal sizes 1" and smaller, and 8-Pitch Thread Series for all larger sizes.

4.4.1.3 The unthreaded points on the ends of stud bolts shall be either flat and chamfered, or rounded.

4.4.1.4 Bolting exposed to the process fluid shall be of a material at least as corrosion resistant as the flange material.

4.4.2 Unless otherwise approved by Industry Source 01 CHP, the design temperature for the bolts shall be at least the maximum allowable working temperature of the piping with no credit taken for cooling.

Stud bolts shall be installed centered in the flange joint with nearly equal lengths protruding at both ends. **4.5 Orifice Flanges**

4.5.1 Orifice flanges shall be specified and installed without jacking bolts/screws to prevent interference during installation which may inadvertently hinder proper bolt-up and gasket seating. Orifice flanges shall be specified per the applicable piping class in Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205. Separation of flanges for installation of orifices can be performed with separate wedging tools.

## SECTION 5: FLANGE FACINGS AND GASKETS

**5.1 Flange Facing**

5.1.1 Flange facings shall be in accordance with dimensions established in ASME B16.5 or ASME B16.47 as applicable. Raised face flanges are preferred for new installations. ## SECTION 7: VALVES – GENERAL

**7.1 Valve Standards and Service Definition**

7.1.1 Where no specific applicable design Standard exists, the design of all pressure containing components shall conform to the requirements of ASME B31.3 and ASME B16.34.

## SECTION 8: REQUIREMENTS FOR SPECIFIC VALVE TYPES

### 8.1 Butterfly Valves

8.1.1 Butterfly valves shall conform to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 204.

8.1.2 Due to their short service life compared to gate valves, butterfly valves require specific Industry Source 01 CHP approval for each application. High performance designs are preferred.

8.1.3 Triple offset butterfly valves shall be installed per the manufacturer's instructions with the valve oriented to have the higher pressure on the shaft side of the disc when in the closed position. Triple offset butterfly valves are designed to be leak tight in both directions at the rated pressure and temperature of the valve, however best sealing performance will be achieved when pressure acts on the shaft side of the disc. Piping designers shall indicate the correct orientation of the valves on the piping design drawings.

### 8.2 Ball Valves

8.2.1 Ball valves shall conform to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 203. Ball valves shall be designed to be fire safe and shall be certified to comply with the latest edition of API 607 or other equivalent fire test approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer.

### 8.3 Check Valves

8.3.1 Check valves shall be sized appropriately for the flow conditions, to assure that the valve will not operate in the nearly closed position and will not experience excessive wear due to disc chatter. It is not acceptable to use a check valve matched to the pipe size without reviewing the manufacturer's sizing criteria.

### 8.4 Globe Valves

8.4.1 Globe valves shall conform to Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205 and Industry Source 01 CHP STP 206. Globe valves are normally suitable for throttling when the disc is at least 20 % open; otherwise flow-induced vibration may result, leading to valve damage. For this reason, valve sizing is important to assure that the valve is not too large for its intended flow conditions. Typically, a bypass valve is too large if it is the same NPS as the control valve it bypasses. For bypass valve applications with high differential pressure or critical services, a standard globe valve may not be suitable. An engineered control valve design should be considered in such applications. The globe valve sizing should be reviewed by the Industry Source 01 CHP Instrument Engineer and Fixed Equipment Engineer during project design. If an engineered control valve design is selected, an upstream isolation block valve should be installed since control valves have an inherent seat leakage rate. The leak rate should be carefully considered in this application.

## SECTION 9: INSTRUMENT PIPING

### 9.1 Scope

It is the intent of this section to supplement Industry Source 01 CHP STP 314.

### 9.2 Definitions

9.2.1 The terms "close coupled" and "remote mounted" used in conjunction with the installation of differential pressure transmitters signifies that the orifice taps have line class valves which are not an integral part of the three valve manifold.

9.2.2 Close coupled further signifies that the transmitter is line mounted with short impulse lines that connect the line class valves with the three valve manifold. Remote mounted signifies the transmitter is located at grade, or similar far removed location relative to the orifice isolation valves.

9.2.3 For typical sketches of close coupled, and remote mounted transmitters see API RP 551, Process Measurement Instrumentation, Section 3.

9.2.4 The term "service condition" as used in the following Sections shall refer to a Piping Class utilized within the design parameters stated, for a specific service (such as steam or hydrogen). See Industry Source 01 CHP STP 205.

9.3.1 Control valves shall be located near their respective instrument indicators. If this is not feasible, remote indicators shall be provided. Control valve manifolds and bypasses shall be designed in accordance with drawings LA-000-0100-0200-030, LA-000-0100-0200-031, LA-000-0100-0200-032, and LA-000-0100-0200-033.

9.3.2 All regulating, throttling, or control valves shall be accessible for hand operation. When a local indicator, which responds to the operation of the valve is provided, it shall be observable from the operating position at the valve.

9.3.3 Where a high pressure drop condition exists across a control valve, sonic flow with high noise levels may be expected. When this situation exists, the piping should be designed to lessen vibration and noise.

9.3.4 Instruments mounted in shop fabricated control panels or racks need no field piping. Complete prefabrication of piping and electrical wiring is generally done by the control panel fabricator. If the control panel or rack is fabricated in the field, Industry Source 01 CHP STP 314 applies.

9.3.6 Piping design and the layout for field instruments shall be in accordance with Industry Source 01 CHP STP 314.

9.3.7 Industry Source 01 CHP philosophy towards special instruments, such as on-stream analyzers, is to incorporate the vendor recommended piping configuration into the specified service condition. ## SECTION 10: FLEXIBILITY (STRESS) ANALYSIS OF PIPING SYSTEMS

### 10.1 General

In general, a flexibility analysis shall be performed for new piping systems and modifications to existing systems by a "qualified designer", as defined in Section 301.1 of ASME B31.3. The analysis may be informal, or formal, at the discretion of the qualified designer and in accordance with ASME B31.3 sections 319.4.1 and 319.4.2 respectively. Formal analysis includes simplified, approximate, and comprehensive methods. The applicable method to be used shall be determined by the qualified designer.

It is essential that the analysis consider the full extent of the piping "system", defined as the assembly of piping and equipment between at least two adjacent "logical" anchor points (where displacements are known or can be defined).

Primary stresses (pressure and other sustained loads such as weight) and secondary stresses (thermal expansion - stress range from the design minimum to design maximum temperatures) must be within code allowances.

All lines shall be designed and supported to provide adequate flexibility to handle thermal expansion from ambient temperature fluctuations, and all known operating conditions and expected transients.

ETP GP 42-10 Section 319 may also be used for guidance, but is non-mandatory.

Design life shall be part of the analysis where any node in the piping system is in the creep range for the material (normally temperatures above 850 F).

### 10.2 Critical Lines

As a minimum requirement, a formal flexibility analysis shall be performed for all critical lines as defined in this paragraph. Critical lines include all piping systems with conditions shown on the right side of the curve in Figure 1, and all piping systems regardless of pipe size or design temperature which meet any of the following criteria:

a) Piping connected to rotating equipment such as pumps, turbines, compressors, and blowers, or subject to slug flow, water hammer or shock loading.

b) Process and steam lines connected to air coolers, fired heaters, and steam boilers.

- c) Major suction and fill lines connected to storage tanks
- d) Piping connected to other equipment with special loading limitations
- e) Piping subjected to severe pressure or thermal cyclic loading (such as coke drum piping)
- f) Relief lines connected to discharge of PSVs and rupture discs if reaction forces are significant
- g) Branch lines connected to headers that are subject to significant movement
- h) All piping potentially subject to auto refrigeration and/or thermal shock (such as flare headers or LPG lines)

  **Note:** Ensure sufficient flexibility to allow for thermal contraction without exceeding stresses to where brittle fracture may occur.

- i) All piping requiring expansion bellows, spring hangers or proprietary support mechanisms.

## 10.4 Information Required for Analysis

The following information is typically required to perform a flexibility analysis of piping systems:

a) Complete isometric drawing (or equivalent collection of 3D model plus 2D drawings) of the proposed piping, up to "logical" anchor points (where displacement is known or can be defined) including:

## SECTION 12: DEAD LEG DESIGN CRITERIA

### 12.1 Definition

Dead legs may be defined as areas, segments or sections of piping systems with little or no flow where fluids can become stagnant. Dead legs produce potential corrosion, fluid quality and bacterial problems and, therefore, should be eliminated or minimized whenever possible. Dead legs are particularly detrimental in water or wet fluid systems as they provide sites for the accumulation of water and water wet solids, aggravating the problems outlined above.

### 12.2 Corrosion

Corrosion rates in dead legs can be significantly higher than that measured in free flowing segments of the system because 1) they collect water and solids, causing concentration cell corrosion and 2) they are difficult, if not impossible to protect in chemically inhibited systems as the stagnant condition prevents the chemical from circulating to the pipe walls. Failures often occur in dead legs in systems that show low corrosion rates at monitoring locations in the free flowing stream.

### 12.3 Design Criteria

During the 3D CAD review of piping, special attention shall be given to minimizing dead legs. The ideal piping system would be a single pipe from point A to point B with no branches or side connections. Since this is seldom possible, the following guidelines should be followed to minimize potential dead leg problems.

#### 12.3.1 Future Connections

Piping branches for future tie-ins should be made up fitting to fitting without nipples or other extensions. When possible, such connections should be at the top of a horizontal pipe rather than at the bottom. Top connections in gassy or oily waters will fill with gas or oil, minimizing the dead leg effect.

#### 12.3.2 End of Header

When possible, headers should end with an elbow rather than a tee. When a header is designed for future expansion (or ended in a blind flange) the flange connection should be located at the last branch and not at the end of an extended pipe.

#### 12.3.3 Pipe Support

When a piping system changes direction and the elbow is short of a pipe support, the pipe should not be extended to the pipe support. Rather, there should be an elbow with a welded dummy to provide support for the pipe. However use of dummy supports shall also be minimized, where other piping design options are available.

#### 12.3.4 Bypass or Recirculation Line

Piping should be made up fitting to fitting with valve installed as close to main line as possible. Also, bypass connections should be oriented (top or side rather than bottom) to minimize solids and water accumulation.

#### 12.3.5 Drain Headers

Drain headers on dirty vessels should be provided with valves for flushing header after vessel cleaning.

#### 12.3.6 Hot Vacuum Residual Lines (Vacuum Bottoms/Coker Feed)

This section applies to carbon steel and chrome alloy (<17% chrome) pipe lines in Vacuum Residual service operating above 450°F with branch line flow rates less than 1 ft/sec. For branch connections that meet these conditions, the piping shall be designed to avoid thermally induced flow and the potential to trap corrosive vapor in the dead leg. The three piping arrangements listed below are acceptable designs. Branch distance requirements listed in the three design options are measured from the outside of the main line. If other design options are necessary, approval by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer is required.

a) A branch connection that comes off the bottom of the main line, must drop vertically down a minimum of 5 pipe diameters before any change in direction to a horizontal or upward path.

b) A branch connection that comes horizontally off the side of the main line shall slope downward a minimum 1/16" per foot away from the main line, and run for a minimum distance of 150 pipe diameters with no pockets where vapor can collect. Within this minimum distance, downward or horizontal turns are acceptable if the minimum slope is maintained. If the horizontal branch line turns down vertically for a minimum distance of 5 pipe diameters, the 150 pipe diameter distance specified above is no longer required.

c) A branch connection that comes vertically off the top of the main line shall have a normally closed block valve installed within a maximum straight distance of 4 pipe diameters measured to the valve joint.

#### 12.3.7 Nitrogen Connections

Close attention to detail shall be given to nitrogen connections to process lines, such as the inlet to a hydrotreating reactor. Tie-ins to the process lines shall be made to the top of horizontal lines or to the side of vertical lines. The piping shall be self-draining with no pockets where water can collect. Piping shall be designed fitting to fitting with the block valve installed as close to the process line as possible, but without creating a pocket at either side of the block valve when in the closed position. This is intended to prevent collection of hydrotest water, which if left undiscovered can result in a freeze induced leak.

## 12.4 Alternative Action

If dead legs cannot be avoided, alternate methods approved by the Industry Source 01 CHP Reliability Engineer and Corrosion Engineer shall be utilized to mitigate the risk of dead leg problems. Some potential alternate methods are upgrading the piping materials, applying internal protective coatings, or adding circulating lines with inhibitors.

## SECTION 13: INJECTION AND MIXING POINTS

### 13.1 General

Effective and reliable service from injection piping and quills depends on numerous factors. Failures at injection and mixing points may create high risk situations. Alternate designs need to be carefully and thoroughly evaluated by all applicable parties, including, but not limited to, Operations, Process Engineering, Corrosion Engineering, and Reliability Engineering.

All new and replacement injection and process mixing points shall comply with the requirements of NACE SP0114, with the exception that any requirements in Sections 13.2 through 13.3 below supersede the NACE requirements.

### 13.2 Design Criteria

#### 13.2.1 Orientation

Injection and mixing points shall be designed for concurrent flow (pointing downstream, parallel with bulk flow). Countercurrent (against the flow) and crossflow (through or across the flow) injection designs are rarely the correct designs and are not allowed without an approved Standard Deviation Request. Concurrent designs should consider the proximity to the downstream piping components, such as elbows, for erosion concerns. See NACE SP0114 for this and other concurrent flow design considerations.

Injection piping and quills, either retractable or fixed, shall be designed so that there is only one obvious way of installing the quill, with respect to the direction of the bulk flow into which they are being inserted. The design shall indicate with an arrow which direction the bulk flow is going, accompanied by the text "FLOW," and the discharge opening of the quill or nozzle shall inject in the same direction. Also, a secondary indication of direction shall be shown, such as a global coordinate like "NORTH," or with some other positive indicator like the downstream equipment number, etc.

The quill shall be designed so that there is physically only one way of installing it to achieve the above concurrent flow, or, shall have matching alignment tabs on the quill flange and mating piping flange that clearly indicate they should fit together to ensure correct flow direction. If alignment tabs are used, they should also be shown on the downstream side of the flanges. See Directional Detail Drawing LA-000-0100-0419-001 for an example of alignment tabs and detail that must accompany any quill.

#### 13.2.2 Documentation Requirements

If the quill or injection piping is retractable, the manufacturer's detail drawing should be stored in Equipment and Vendor Files, in Documentum, or as a Industry Source 01 piping detail drawing. Any new quill that is installed shall have a reference on the P&ID to the drawing containing these details.

Injection quills shall be assigned a 3200 series Location number and a GP number, per the Location Codes, Class, and Subclass tables in the Equipment Number Request form in Documentum.

### 13.3 Lifecycle Requirements

Injection and mixing points have increased inspection requirements and shortened intervals, which are already defined by Inspection Practices. Whenever a quill is pulled for inspection, it should be inspected for mechanical integrity (fatigue cracks, corrosion, obvious signs of packing glands leaks, etc.). When an injection or mixing point is added, deleted, or modified, the Inspection department shall be notified.
