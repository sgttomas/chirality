# 1 Introduction

This Piping Design Criteria is intended to be site specific for the Industry Source 03 projects on the Industry Source 03 Facility of Alaska. Listed are the minimum requirements for the design, materials, fabrication and testing of In-Plant Piping materials and furnished with packaged equipment. These design guidelines ensure uniformity and consistency of the design by describing the following:

- Piping materials specification
- Piping design and layout requirements
- Piping stress analysis and pipe support requirement
- Definition of design deliverables

## 1.1 Objectives

The purpose of this criteria define the minimum technical requirements for the piping design and layout of process and utility piping system for module piping installation, including the following:

- Plot Areas
- Equipment
- Piping
- Piperacks
- Platforms
- Roads
- Other miscellaneous items

These design criteria does not include the topics of spacing and layout consideration for minimizing losses due to fire.

The plant layout is concerned with the spatial arrangement of the infrastructure and process equipment interconnections within the plant boundaries. The requirement of battery limit beyond the plant boundary is outside the scope of these criteria. If there are local legislative regulations on plant layout in which the site is located, these must be complied with (such as statutory requirements based on local environment and security restrictions).

[Industry Source 03 logo]

## 1.2 Scope

Plant layout shall identify and adopt a layout that is safe and cost effective and which meets the requirements of the process design, safe operation/maintenance, emergency response action and evacuation. The following items shall be used to guide piping engineering design:

- Piping and Instrument Diagram (P&ID's) shall be the basis for piping design and shall show piping schematics, line numbers, process equipment, valve, piping specialties, controls, and instruments. Changes to these diagrams after they are issued for design shall have prior approval from the Company project representative.

- Piping and fittings supplied integrally with vessels, compressors, pumps, and other equipment shall be in accordance with requirement of these criteria concerning limitations on type of piping connection.

- Separation distance for common items of equipment shall be regarded as a maximum for economic layout purposes. Any proposal to increase these separation distances shall be subject to cost benefit analysis.

- Space for probable future equipment shall be designated and located adjacent to related equipment. The allocation of such space should be addressed during the initial project risk assessment in order to identify potential hazards associated with probable future equipment.

- Piping layout shall allow for protection of piping systems, where necessary, to prevent freezing or to prevent difficulties resulting from high viscosities or wax formation.

# 4   Design Parameters

## 4.1   Applicable Codes

- In-Plant Process and Utility piping shall be built in accordance with ASME B31.3 piping code, except as follows:
- Drill Site/Well Pad manifold piping may be in accordance with ASME B31.4 and /or B31.8 piping codes
- Pumping facility, tank farm, and terminal piping may be in accordance with ASME B31.4.
- Gas Compression facilities may be designed in accordance with ASME B31.9 or B31.8 using a 0.5 design factor.

## 4.2   Severe Cyclic Design

The following piping shall meet the ASME B31.3 requirements for Severe Cyclic Service (including piping built to B31.4 and B31.8).

- Reciprocating compressor or reciprocating pump downstream piping within 100 feet of the equipment discharge flange, or up to the first piping vibration isolation hanger/support - if that distance is shorter.

- Piping that is exposed to conditions meeting the B31.3 definition of severe cyclic conditions.

## 4.3 Design Conditions

All piping designs, including ASME B31.4 and/ or B31.8, shall consider ASME B31.3 Sections 301.4 through 301.10.

Piping systems operating at less than atmospheric pressure shall be designed for 15 psi external pressure.

## 4.4 Corrosion Allowance

A corrosion allowance shall be added to the design wall thickness of carbon and low alloy steel piping. The corrosion allowances specified in the individual line class are nominal. Reduction of the specified corrosion allowance must be approved by the Piping Materials Engineer. Lower allowances shall not be used without the approval of the Company representative.

No corrosion allowance is required where corrosion resistant alloys or internal claddings are specified.

## 4.5 Sour Service

NACE International MR0175 definitions for sour service apply, except process streams with at least 100 ppm H2S shall be considered sour as well. The definition also applies to piping that is not initially in sour service but which is expected to become sour sometime during the life of the piping system. This includes all systems containing production fluids.

For piping in sour service, NACE International MR0175/ISO15156 shall be used to determine appropriate materials for all wetted items.

Hydrogen induced cracking (HIC) resistance shall be considered for pipe and piping components made from rolled plate or strip (e.g., DSAW or HF-ERW).

## 4.6 Nonmetallic Piping

Nonmetallic piping systems shall have a rated burst pressure of no less than three times the system hydrostatic test pressure.

## 4.7 Pipe Sizing

When appropriate, computer models or other analytical techniques will be used to size lines for vapor, liquid and multiphase flow. Size lines to avoid slug flow regions whenever practical.

Consult Process Design Criteria – Industry Source 03 Doc PRCRT-000001 for line sizing and fluid velocity recommendation.

## 4.8 Wall Thickness

The Line Classes list specific wall thickness for each pipe diameter. It is the responsibility of the Piping Material Engineer to verify these dimensions for compliance with the Specification and Code of Construction.

For ASME B31.3, the nominal wall thickness (t) is calculated by factoring the mill tolerance into minimum wall thickness (t_m) including corrosion allowance (i.e., for ASTM A106: t_1 = t_m/0.875, where t_m is the minimum calculated wall plus any corrosion allowance). For ASME B31.4 and B31.8 the mill tolerance is accounted for in the respective design factors used in wall thickness calculations.

## 4.9 Branch Connections

Due to the complexities of design and installation, stub–on pipe branches may be used as branch connections only where the design and fabrication have the specific approval of the Industry Source 03 project representative. Stub-on and stub-in branch connections with full encirclement reinforcement saddles are not allowed without the approval of the Company project representative.

Miter bends shall not be used in hydrocarbon, chemical, erosive or corrosive services.

## 4.10 Supports

Flanged connections at equipment should be capable of accepting maintenance slip blinds.

Dead legs for supporting pipe shall be avoided on all piping. Dummy legs may be provided when pipe runs fall short of supports.

Spring-loaded pipe support elements shall be designed to prevent complete release of piping load in the event of spring failure or misalignment. Hydrostatic load shall be included in design loads. Lines subject to blowdown and impulse loadings shall be identified by the process engineer and the layout and its support shall be designed to avoid high loading and vibration.

## 4.11 Piping Stress Analysis

The In-Plant Piping stress analysis shall be carried out by contractor in compliance with the Industry Source 03 specification. Specific engineering codes, standards and practices shall be applied in the area where the project is developed. This will apply to module piping, in-plant piping and pipelines.

## 4.12 Electrical Isolation

Flange electrical isolation kits shall be used whenever a galvanic corrosion cell would be created by connecting dissimilar metals, joining flanges like SS to a CS flanges. This mainly used to avoid electrochemical reaction to prevent from galvanic corrosion.

Isolation kits are typically not required in non-electrolyte environment.

## 4.13 Thermowells

Thermowells process connection shall be heavy wall, and typically will be threaded, or flanged type per the applicable piping class specification. The immersion length ("U") shall be defined by instrument engineer for the required minimum insertion depth.

Thermowells shall be constructed from drilled barstock, tapered and the material shall be 316 Stainless Steel (SS) minimum. Refer to Instrumentation Temperature Measurement Specification (Industry Source 03 Doc ICSPE-000008) for thermowells specification and recommendation.

## 4.14 Insulation

Insulation shall be provided for personnel protection on hot surfaces (with a normal operating temperature greater than 140°F) within 3 feet of an established work space of a fixed access area. Protection should extend to a height of 7 feet above platforms and not less than 3 feet beyond handrails.

Outdoors piping other than stainless steel, with continuous operating temperature <300°F, shall be externally coated beneath the insulation to protect from corrosion under insulation. Coating shall be completely extended through the module wall penetration.

When any insulation other than foamed-in-place polyurethane is used on exterior piping, care shall be taken to ensure that the insulation segments and the jacketing are oriented to allow free draining.

# 5 General Design Consideration

## 5.1 Plot Layout

Plot layout shall be an economical design to meet the process and licensors objective reflecting the need for safety to life and property, and allowing adequate access for maintenance, operation and emergency action. To adopt the safest and most effective layout that meets the requirement of process design, the following will be considered:

- Separation distance – It is necessary to define probable maintenance and access needs and the effect of such activities on adjacent equipment.

- Plot drainage – The location of spillage collection areas depends on the likely spillage, volatility of liquid and position of permanent ignition sources and the effect of pool fire on the equipment.

- Special fire risk area – The need for special fire risk areas shall be reviewed to decide if improved equipment design can justify their reduction or elimination.

- Construction consideration – Risk from construction activity should be considered and included in the final risk assessment. Movement of construction equipment and especially operation of cranes and lifting gear near operating units can be a hazard.

- Blast Effect – Consideration should be given to effects of blast where there is a significant risk of unconfined gas/vapor explosion.

## 5.2 Access Ways and In-Plant Roads

Access ways within each unit shall be provided for maintenance and for firefighting from the roads around the plot. The design of road layout shall consider the plant complexity and the type of fire-fighting equipment likely to be utilized during an emergency. Minimum widths for personnel access ways, stairways, elevated walkways, platforms and vehicular access ways shall be considered.

- Routing – Access ways may be run longitudinally under pipe racks, provided that all relevant clearance factors have been considered.

- Access to Platforms – Access to platform shall meet minimum clearance requirement. Platform escape routes shall not be adjacent to each other. Platform escape routes shall not be adjacent to the source of hazard.

## 5.3 Piping

Requirements for process piping system are covered in Plant Piping Design Specification (Industry Source 03 Doc PISPE-000005) that includes information on layout, access, pipe clearances, pipe support, piperacks, valve operation, spades, sample points and battery limit isolation.
