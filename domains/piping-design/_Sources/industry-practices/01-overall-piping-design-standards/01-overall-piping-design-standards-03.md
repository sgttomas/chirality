## 1.0 GENERAL

### 1.1 Scope

This specification describes the basis for the overall design of process and utility piping and shall apply to all piping systems and parts of such systems.

### 1.2 Intent

The intent of this specification is to provide for a complete package in accordance with standard practice. This is a general specification, and therefore subject to variations in materials, design and construction, provided that the requirements of standard practice are met. Prior approval shall be obtained from the Owner before making any change to these specifications.

### 1.3 Conflicting Requirements

Should a conflict arise between this specification and other specifications or government regulations, the Vendor shall contact the Owner and a decision reached in writing before any work is done.

### 1.4 Vendor Responsibility

The Vendor shall be responsible for conformance to governing codes, Owner's specifications, drawings, legal and contractual requirements. In the event of on compliance with the foregoing, the Owner may correct any deficiencies, and the cost of such repairs shall be charged to the Vendor.

## 2.0 CODE REQUIREMENTS

### 2.1 Codes and Standards

Minimum piping requirements are to be equal to or shall exceed requirements of latest editions of the following applicable codes. Any mandatory federal, provincial or local codes in effect at the project site are to be complied with:

- Local pressure piping regulating authority
- ABSA, Alberta Boilers Safety Association
- API 520, Design and Installation of Pressure-Relieving Systems
- API 521, Guide for Pressure-Relieving and Depressurizing Systems
- ASME, B31.3, Chemical Plant & Petroleum Refinery Piping
- ASME, Section IX, Welding & Brazing Requirements
- CSA Z662, Oil & Gas Pipeline Systems
- Department of Labour, Boilers Branch for the applicable province
- OHSA, Occupational Health and Safety Act
- Regulations of the applicable province and Canada

## 3.0 DESIGN CONDITIONS

### 3.1 Operating Conditions

3.1.1 Normal design conditions of pressure and temperature shall be the most severe conditions expected to co-exist under long-time operating conditions. These usual operations include all manipulation and control functions such as throttling, blocking and bypassing likely to be used for operation and control.

3.1.2 Usual operating conditions do not include more severe temporary conditions, such as those incidental to start-up, shutdown, steam-out or abnormal operations. Temporary conditions govern as design conditions only when there is clear evidence they definitely exceed time and severity limits in paragraph 302.2.4 of Refinery Piping Code, ANSI B31.3.

### 3.2 Design Temperature

3.2.1 Design temperature is the most severe sustained fluid temperature, subject to conditions of Section 3.1 above. Design temperature of internally insulated or non-insulated components may be the metal temperature rather than the fluid temperature.

3.2.2 Design temperature for steam traced piping shall be the fluid temperature or 10° C below saturation temperature of tracing steam, whichever is greater.

3.2.3 Design temperature for piping with a fluid operating temperature below -29° C, shall be the minimum fluid operating temperature. In addition, design shall be suitable for design pressure at 38° C.

### 3.3 Design Pressure

3.3.1 Design pressure shall be the most severe condition of internal or external fluid pressure, subject to conditions of Section 3.1 above.

3.3.2 Minimum positive design pressure shall normally be 175 kPA.

3.3.3 Design pressure for unrelieved piping systems subject to pump discharge pressure, shall be pump differential pressure at shutoff of stalling conditions, plus normal pump suction pressure and hydrostatic head.

3.3.4 Design pressure for piping systems operating under vacuum shall be full vacuum. Exception may be taken where suitable protection against vacuum failure is provided.

3.3.5 For piping systems protected by relief valves, the relief valve set pressure shall be equal to the design pressure minus the hydrostatic head below the relief valve.

### 3.4 Other Conditions

3.4.1 Allowable stress values apply to total loads imposed on piping materials. Design shall provide for all loading significantly affecting pipe material stresses. See sections 301.4 through 301.7 of Refinery Piping Code for loadings in addition to fluid pressure, which may affect piping stresses.

## 4.0 LINE SIZING

### 4.1 General

4.1.1   Piping shall be sized for normal operating conditions. Provision for future increased capacity shall be made only when specifically required.

4.1.2   Each line shall be sized individually in accordance with good engineering practice and economical service. The following velocity and pressure values shall be used as a guide for line sizing. Consideration shall also be given to length of line, working pressure, availability of pressure drop, vapour pressure of fluid, required pump NPSH and any other considerations of significance. In instances where normal operations requires a large pressure drop between the terminals of line, the line shall be sized to take an appreciable amount of the drop rather than through a valve.

**4.2 Velocities**

Velocities in lines shall not exceed the following:

4.2.1

Light hydrocarbons and water:

Pump discharge    1.5 - 2.5 m/s    (5-8 fps)
Pump suction      0.9 m/s          (3 fps)
Gravity flow      0.76 m/s         (2.5 fps)

4.2.2

Gases:

100 psig              18-24 m/s    (60-80 fps)
200 psig              15-18 m/s    (50-60 fps)
300 psig              12-15 m/s    (40-50 fps)
500 psig              9-12 m/s     (30-40 fps)
1000 psig             3-6 m/s      (10-20 fps)
Compressor suction    15 m/s       (50 fps)
Compressor discharge  18 m/s       (60 fps)

4.2.3

Steam:

0 - 30 psig (saturated)            30 m/s    (100 fps)
30 - 150 psig (saturated or superheated)  50 m/s    (167 fps)
150 psig and up (superheated)       75 m/s    (250 fps)

In corrosive service line velocities shall be reduced to 80% or less of the maximum.

### 4.4 Other Limitations

4.4.1 NPS 3/8, 1-1/4, 2-1/2, 3-1/2, 5, and 7 pipe sizes shall not be used, except where required to connect to equipment. Conversion to standard piping shall be as near the equipment as possible.

4.4.2 Minimum pipe size run shall be NPS 3/4, except for connections to equipment or instrument services.

4.4.3 In pipeway runs the minimum size for process and utility piping shall be NPS 2 or as otherwise governed by length of span.

4.4.4 All piping size NPS 2 and larger shall be flanged/butt-welded unless otherwise specified.

4.4.5 In threaded piping, swage nipples shall be used for change in pipe size. Bushings are not permitted.

## 5.0 EQUIPMENT PIPING

### 5.1 Compressor Piping

5.1.1 Large centrifugal compressors may be elevated sufficiently for piping and auxiliaries to be located below main operating platforms. Smaller compressors shall be grade mounted with piping and auxiliaries arranged for convenient access and maintenance.

5.1.2 Particular consideration shall be given to design of piping subject to vibration from dynamic loading associated with reciprocating compressors. Volume bottles shall be provided as required at the suctions and discharges. Suction and discharge lines shall be securely clamped. Small piping around compressors and on the same supports as suction and discharge lines shall be well braced to reduce vibration. Reciprocating compressor suction and discharge piping shall be run on substantial supports near grade if at all possible.

5.1.3 The piping drawings shall show the limits of compressor suction piping where special internal cleaning is required. Temporary screens or strainers shall be provided.

5.1.4 Removable spool pieces shall be provided at compressors where needed to permit maintenance without major piping disassembly.

## 6.0 PRESSURE RELIEF SYSTEMS

### 6.1 General

6.1.1 A schedule showing the location, type, marking size, flange rating, orifice identification, area and setting pressure of all pressure relief devices on each unit shall be supplied to Owner. This shall also include the type of fluid, gravity or molecular weight, maximum operating inlet pressure and temperature and relieving capacity as well as the presence of any corrosive agent.

6.1.2 Consideration shall be given to providing pressure relief devices between block valves where thermal expansion due to ambient temperature changes or steam tracing may cause damage.

[Industry Source 06 logo]

6.1.3  A pressure relief device shall be placed on the low-pressure side of pressure reducing valves where the piping and/or equipment in the low-pressure side are not suitable for the conditions which exist on the high-pressure side.

6.1.4  Compliance with Owner's General Specification GS 4.0 Instrumentation is required.

## 6.2 Piping

6.2.1  The size of the inlet line shall not be less than the size of the relief valve inlet. The size of the outlet line shall not be less than the size of the relief valve outlet.

6.2.2  The length of the inlet piping shall be kept to a minimum.

6.2.3  At maximum flow, the pressure drop due to entrance losses and pipe friction shall not exceed three percent of the set pressure of any valve.

## 6.3 Discharge to Atmosphere

6.3.1  A pressure relief valve discharging vapours to atmosphere shall be provided with outlet piping as follows:

6.3.1.1  For hydrocarbons, shall be vented a minimum of two metres above building peak. Regardless of height or conditions the riser shall be at least 15 m horizontally from any furnace.

6.3.1.2  For steam, to atmosphere at a safe location.

6.3.1.3  A pressure relief valve in liquid service and discharging to atmosphere shall do so at a visible safe place.

## 6.4 Piping to Closed System

6.4.1  Any pressure relief valve discharging to a closed system shall be installed so that the discharge piping slopes into the header.

6.4.2  Blowdown headers shall be installed so that the piping slopes into the blowdown drum. Where this is not practical, the low points in the header shall be traced and insulated, and a NPS 3/4 valved drain tie shall be provided.

6.4.3  The bonnet vent of a relief valve with bellows shall be piped to the discharge line.

## 6.5 Block Valves

6.5.1  Where a block valve is installed to permit removal of a relief valve during unit operation it shall be located immediately below the relief valve, and be car-sealed open.

6.5.2  Where a pressure relief valve discharges into a header and an outlet block valve is installed, this valve shall be car-sealed open.

6.5.3  Car-sealed open valves shall be numbered on the engineering flow plans and a list of numbers and locations shall be supplied to Owner.

6.5.4  Block valves at relief valves and in blowdown lines shall be positioned so that the block valve remains open if any component of the valve accidentally fails.

6.5.5  Any block valve in the relief path shall have a full port and shall have a clear path from inlet to

outlet (i.e. ball or gate). It shall have a means of readily identifying valve position (i.e. handle, position indicators, etc.).

6.5.6 An NPS 3/4 bleeder valve shall be provided between any block and pressure relief valve.

6.5.7 The size of the inlet valve shall not be less than the size of the relief valve inlet. The size of the outlet valve shall not be less than the size of the relief valve outlet.

**6.6 Location**

6.6.1 Relief valves may be located on the piping instead of on the equipment and shall be located at the lowest possible elevation that may be economically justified.

## 7.0 FLEXIBILITY

### 7.1 General

Piping shall be designed with sufficient flexibility to absorb stress due to the following:

7.1.1 Line expansion or contraction as a result of operating or ambient conditions.

7.1.2 Expansion or contraction of connected equipment.

7.1.3 Settlement of equipment or tankage.

### 7.2 Design

7.2.1 Stresses due to thermal expansion or contraction shall be analyzed by the methods in ANSI B31.3 or ANSI B31.1 as applicable.

7.2.2 Piping shall normally be laid out to provide sufficient flexibility without the use of expansion loops or joints.

7.2.3 Anchors and guides shall be provided where necessary to minimize the movement of lines across supports.

### 7.3 Provision for Expansion

Where the layout does not provide sufficient flexibility the following may be used:

7.3.1 Expansion loops with guides and anchors.

7.3.2 Cold springing.

7.3.3 Expansion joints with guides and anchors, when approved by Owner.

7.3.4 Spring loaded supports or hangers where rigid supports restrict line movement or cause over stressing of the line.

## 8.0 PIPE SUPPORTS

### 8.1 Design

8.1.1 Supports shall normally be designed to transmit all piping loads directly to foundations.

8.1.2 Loadings to be considered in design calculations include:

- Weight of pipe and insulation
- Weight of the line contents based on water
- Wind loading
- Lateral loading due to movement of lines on supports, guides and anchors
- Vibrations due to pulsations from reciprocating pumps, compressors and possible line surges.

8.1.3 The material for pipe supports shall be structural steel or reinforced concrete, unless otherwise approved in writing by Owner.

## 8.2 Requirements

8.2.1 Supports shall be located so that the force transmitted to connections on machinery such as pumps, compressors, turbines, etc. does not exceed that allowed by the machinery manufacturer. The design of such supports shall be detailed and not left for field improvisation.

8.2.2 Supports shall be located to minimize the load transmitted to connections of equipment such as vessels, exchangers, etc.

8.2.3 Piping to equipment requiring removal for maintenance, including control valve assemblies shall be supported so that temporary supports are not required during removal.

## 8.3 Shoes and Guides

8.3.1 Shoes shall be installed on insulated pipe at supports.

8.3.2 Non-insulated lines shall rest directly on the supports but shoes shall be provided on lines subject to excessive or continuous movement.

8.3.3 Guides shall be installed on supports to restrict lateral movement.

## 8.4 Spring Supports and Hangers

8.4.1 Spring supports may be either variable or constant load type provided that overstressing of line or nozzles is not produced.

8.4.2 Variable sprint hangers and supports shall be provided with a scale showing the range of working load and a means of adjustment.

8.4.3 Constant support spring hangers shall be provided with the calibrated load clearly indicated, a scale showing the range of movement and an accurate means of adjustment.

8.4.4 All spring supports and support shall be adjusted in the field outer unit start-up and in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

# 9.0 PIPING FOR INSTRUMENTS

9.1 Unless otherwise approved by Owner, control valves shall be located high enough to permit valve stem removal, but not higher than 900 mm above grade or a platform.

9.2 Where vaporization occurs in control valves, they shall be placed as close as practical to the

destination of the stream.

9.3 Control valve by-pass piping shall provide sufficient clearance for the removal of the control valve from the loop.

9.4 All control valves shall be provided with block, and by-pass valves rated for the same design pressure as the upstream piping.

9.5 By-pass valves shall be designed for a capacity at least equal to the capacity of the control valve.

9.6 Flanged end reducers or spools shall be used between flanged control valves and block valves. The length of the reducers or spools shall be sufficient to permit removal of bolts, and the installation of drain or vent connections.

9.7 Valved NPS 1 drains shall be provided upstream of control valves.

9.8 All control valves in socket weld piping shall be flanged.

9.9 The only devices which may be mounted on control valves shall be valve positioners, volume boosters, lock-up relays and pressure controllers. These devices shall be installed so as to be easily accessible for servicing. The devices shall not be so mounted when vibration may cause malfunction.

9.10 Orifice flanges shall be located adjacent to walkways, platforms, or structures and shall be installed in horizontal pipe runs, wherever possible. Orifice flanges shall have socket weld taps except where screwed connections are specifically permitted in Owner's Specification GS 2.3 Line Class Detail.

9.11 Instrument air distribution headers shall be NPS 2 minimum. A block valve shall be provided for each take-off connection from these headers.

9.12 All gauge glasses and instruments in process services shall be provided with NPS 1/2 minimum drain connections, piped to their respective closed drain systems. They shall be supplied complete with isolation valves in addition to the gauge cocks.

## 10.0 THERMAL TRACING

10.1 All equipment, piping and instrumentation which may be subject to freezing shall be heat traced per Owner's General Specification Industry Source 06 Doc 5005 Winterization.

## 11.0 SAMPLE CONNECTIONS

### 11.1 Requirements

**11.1.1** When specified, double valved sample connections (using a NPS 3/4 main block valve and a NPS 1/2 bar stock needle valve for regulation) shall be provided as required to permit full evaluation without disconnecting piping or instruments during plant performance tests. Every line containing a process flow meter shall be provided with a sample connection. Liquid sample connections shall be taken off the side of the pipe.

## 12.0 SOUR SERVICE REQUIREMENTS

Piping in sour service shall be designed per Owner's General Specification Sour Service, Industry Source 06 Doc 5001 Piping Material Service Index and Industry Source 06 Doc 5002 Piping Material Line Class Detail. Design for critical sour service is outside the scope of this specification.

### 12.1 Threaded Piping and Instrument Connections

**12.2.1** Threaded connections shall be classified according to the following definitions, and use of threaded connections shall comply with Table 12-2:

- (a) Primary threaded connections are connections made directly to a process stream, without capability for isolation from the process by means of upstream flanged, socket welded, or socket welded by screwed block valves connected directly to the process by welds, flanges, or welded fittings.

- (b) Secondary threaded connections are connections with capability for isolation from the process by means of upstream flanged, socket welded, or socket welded by screwed block valves connected directly to the process by welds, flanges, or welded fittings.

**12.2.2** When threaded connections are used for sour service, the following conditions apply:

- (a) Pipe diameter shall be less than NPS 2.

- (b) Pipe thickness shall be Schedule 80 minimum.

- (c) Threading shall be only be accomplished by a cutting operation. Rolled threads shall not be used without the written concurrence of the Owner engineer. If rolled threads are considered, a suitable heat treatment shall be applied after thread rolling, so that the microhardness at all thread roots is below 248 HV₁₀₀. Microhardness test data shall be supplied to support any request for use of rolled threads.

- (d) Connections shall be located away from areas where liquid water may accumulate.

- (e) Requirements of applicable codes, standards and specifications shall be satisfied.
