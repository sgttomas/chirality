## 110 Codes and Industry Practices

The various piping codes (such as ASME/ANSI B31.1 and B31.3 in the United States) contain practices necessary for safe piping systems, but are not intended to be complete specifications for all phases of design. The Company recognizes this fact, and the guidelines and specifications in this manual provide the supplemental requirements normally needed to obtain economical systems for basic fluid services. A few requirements—where experience has shown them to be better choices—are more stringent than code minimums. Engineers responsible for design and construction of piping systems are expected to be familiar with and to comply with the appropriate codes even though some of their provisions may not be specifically included in this manual.

## 111 Piping Code History

The American Standards Association Code for Pressure Piping, ASA B31.1, 1942 through 1955, was for many years a guide for design, fabrication, erection, and testing of industrial piping. Early on, the Company recognized this code as an acceptable basic standard. The Operating Companies and the Engineering Department subsequently developed numerous standards, specifications, and design practices to supplement and extend the Code provisions.

In 1955, the ASA recognized that a single volume of code provisions would no longer meet the growing and divergent needs of industry. It was decided to issue separately bound sections of the ASA Piping Code—each section covering the piping requirements for a major industry. In 1966, the ASA was reorganized as the United States of America Standards Institute (USASI), and in 1969 the name was changed to the American National Standards Institute (ANSI).

Plans for a separate Chemical Industry Process Piping Code (B31.6) were discontinued when by mutual industry agreement both requirements were incorporated into ANSI B31.3. In December 1978, American National Standards Committee B31 was reorganized as the ASME Code for Pressure Piping, B31, Committee under procedures developed by The American Society of Mechanical Engineers and accredited by ANSI. The code designation was also changed to ANSI/ASME B31.

The applicable sections of ASME/ANSI B31 are:

- B31.1 Power Piping
- B31.2 Fuel Gas Piping
- B31.3 Chemical Plant and Petroleum Refinery Piping
- B31.4 Liquid Petroleum Transportation Piping Systems
- B31.5 Refrigeration Piping
- B31.8 Gas Transmission and Distribution Piping Systems
- B31.9 Building Services Piping
- B31.11 Slurry Pipelines
- B31G Manual for Determining the Remaining Strength of Corroded Pipelines: A Supplement to B31, Codes for Pressure Piping
