## 123 Pipeline Applications

U.S. Government Code of Federal Regulations, Title 49, Part 192 (49CFR192) and Part 195 (49CFR195), cover interstate oil piping and essentially all gas transportation piping. The minimum design requirements for oil and gas pipelines are defined in 49CFR192 and 49CFR195. However, ASME/ANSI B31.4 and B31.8 provide supplemental design information, and their use is recommended. In case of conflict, the applicable part of 49CFR governs. These requirements are detailed in the *Pipeline Manual*.

## 124 Foreign Applications

For installations outside the United States and Canada, the engineer responsible for piping design and construction should determine if there are national or local regulations pertaining to piping design. For consistency in engineering and fabrication ASME/ANSI B31 is preferred over local rules, unless local rules are more stringent. The ASME/ANSI codes remain good guidelines where no other regulations exist.

# 130 Company Practices

## 131 Corporation Piping Specification

Piping components are designed and fabricated according to current standards and codes established by national bodies such as the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) and the American National Standards Institute (ANSI). The Corporation Piping Specification, developed and used by design groups and operations units within the Company, is based on these standards and codes.

Piping codes prescribe minimum practices necessary for safety. They are not complete specifications. The Corporation Piping Specification adds requirements normally needed to obtain safe and economical piping systems for basic fluid services.

The Corporation Piping Specification (GB135169) was developed for process facilities. The specification covers services encountered in most of the Company's operating facilities.

The Corporation Piping Specification is based on experience, judgment, and economics; use it as a guide for the design of safe and economical piping systems. It is not mandatory.
