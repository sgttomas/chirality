- What employee amenities are required (change rooms, showers, lockers, food facilities, etc.)

- Where the new facilities will be integrated into any existing facilities. This has major impact on layout studies

- What unable infrastructure exists (utilities, off-plot systems, maintenance facilities, administrative facilities, laboratory facilities, transport systems, etc.)

An acceptable operating factor (percent of the time the facility is in operation) needs to be agreed upon with the operator. Many facilities are designed to run for several years between major, scheduled shutdowns; at other times the unit will be shut down only in extreme emergencies. Parallel systems that allow load-sharing (thus decreasing the system duty), require piping for easy, efficient, and safe switching. Other facilities can be brought down as needed, quickly repaired and brought back on stream in a few hours. The operating factor and maintenance provisions of a system affect the extent of sparing and redundancy of equipment, piping, and instrumentation. It also influences the quality level of piping and other equipment. The designer may want to specify higher quality for piping and equipment that must operate unspared for several years at a time, such as critical valves, sampling systems and piping having seal welding.

Infrastructure (off-plot and utilities) requirements need to be established early in the project and a close review conducted with the operators to determine what may already be available at the selected site. For instance, start-up and shutdown systems often are dependent on existing infrastructure; new facilities may require the addition of parallel or larger piping in the existing plant.

Early attention should be given to environmental permits; frequently they control the project schedule. Environmental conditions should be considered that could bear on operating and maintenance personnel safety and health. Examples include noise levels and exposure to hazardous materials.

Finally, variations in operating styles from one location to another must be recognized, such as the extent of plant supervision and the experience and capabilities at all levels of the operating staff. Availability of support facilities also differs. Some organizations have internal shop, lab, and administrative services readily available; others rely heavily on outside and contract services that may or may not be able to respond quickly.
