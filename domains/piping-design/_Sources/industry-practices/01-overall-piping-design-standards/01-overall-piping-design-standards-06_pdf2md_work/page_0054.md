### 4.3.2 Bushings

Use swages in process flowing conditions where possible. Socket-welded swages are preferred but threaded swages are acceptable when the flow direction is from the smaller diameter component to the larger diameter component. Swages offer gradual flow transitions, minimizing erosion potential, while bushings create step changes resulting in very poor flow transitions and increased erosion potential. Additionally, from an inspection standpoint, bushings are difficult to inspect without disassembly. Whereas there is some potential to radiograph a threaded swage connection, a bushing is much more problematic to inspect with radiography.

Where swages and other preferred piping component installations are impractical, bushing installation is acceptable under the following process service conditions:

- Process service conditions with no flow allowed: dirty water, produced water, seawater, sandy crude, hydrocarbons, corrosive hydrocarbons, wet gas, sump discharge and gravity drains.

- Process service conditions with flow allowed: lube oil, seal oil, hydraulic oil, fuel gas, dry gas, diesel fuel, methanol, nitrogen, instrument air, plant air and chemical injection.

As a general rule, flow direction should only be from the smaller diameter component to the larger diameter component. Exceptions may be made when the responsible engineer closely reviews the system design and approves of the installation.

Due to the product's high affinity for leaching through threaded connections, threaded piping components should not be used in heating glycol, cooling glycol, and Halon service.

Bushing material (i.e. carbon steel, stainless steel) shall be specified on a case-by-case basis for the respective piping system. Careful consideration should be given, but not limited to the following:

- Galvanic issues with dissimilar materials

- Potential erosion

- Process flow should never be from large diameter to small (i.e., typical usage is for gauge connections no flow condition)

- Potential for thread galling (i.e., rolled or cut threads)

- Potential for hex head to contact fitting shoulder before threads are tight (i.e., loose tolerances on bushing and/or fitting)

Hex head bushings shall be the only bushing types allowed. Hex head bushings and swages accommodate a pipe wrench for tightening or loosening the fitting insuring the user knows which joint is being worked on. As a common rule, threaded pipe construction requires a minimum of 5 threads engaged.
