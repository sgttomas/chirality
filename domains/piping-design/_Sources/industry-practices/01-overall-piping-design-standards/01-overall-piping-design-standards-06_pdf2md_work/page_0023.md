## 2.6 Piping Support

### 2.6.1 Support Guidelines

Supports should normally be located at line direction changes. Whenever practical, anchors should be at the midpoint between expansion loops.

Adjustable supports should be provided for both inlet and outlet piping connected to pumps, compressors, heat exchangers, large meters, and similar equipment on which the manufacturer has imposed limits on nozzle loads, and/or elsewhere when growth or movement from thermal expansion is required. The piping supports should be designed so that forces and moments transmitted to the equipment by weight, misalignment, vibration, or thermal expansion are within the equipment manufacturer's limits from ambient to operating temperature. Use of expansion joints requires Company approval.

Relief valve discharge piping and supports should be designed so that stresses induced by connecting lines should not overstress the relief valve body. The reactive forces during discharge should also be considered. Supports and connections should be arranged to facilitate removal and replacement of the relief valve.

The use of cold spring for reducing forces is discouraged and should only be used with approval of the stress engineer.

Piping should be supported on both sides of control valves such that the control valve may be removed for maintenance without providing additional supports for the piping.

Piping and pipe support structures should be designed for the most severe conditions of coincident loads. The following are the types of loads which must be considered:

- Dead Load: The dead load should consist of the weight of the pipe, pipe fittings, attachments and components, and insulation.

- Process Loads: Gravity load from the weight of the operating contents of the pipe. Longitudinal stresses in the pipe due to the operating pressure. Piping analysis shall include sufficient load cases to account operating scenarios as defined through discussion as needed by the Process department.

- Thermal Loads: The stress of growth effects due to the change in temperature from ambient condition (or installation temperature) to the operating temperature.

- Momentum Loads: The momentum load should include the reaction forces due to the change in momentum in surge and slug flow, and the reaction due to the opening of relief valves or snap-acting control valves.
