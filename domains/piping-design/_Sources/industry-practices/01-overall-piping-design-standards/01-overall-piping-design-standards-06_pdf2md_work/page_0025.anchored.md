Proper pipe supports and hangers should be used for all piping. No pipe supports or hangers shall be attached from handrails.

For outdoor service, all insulated hot lines should have cradles at support locations. For indoor service, saddles or pipe shoes may be specified. Small diameter lines, less than 1½ inches may rest directly upon the supports with the insulation cut back to clear the support. Cradles, cradle anchor shoes, guides, and anchors should be indicated on drawings as shown on piping standards.

All acoustically insulated lines, such as are commonly found in gas compression facilities, should have cradles at support locations. Cradles, shoes, guides, and anchors should be indicated on drawings as shown on piping standards.

Lines on vertical vessels should be supported and guided from the Industry Source 08 wherever practical.

All lines in modules should be tied down for shipping to minimize the excessive forces encountered during transport.

Spring loaded pipe support elements should be designed to prevent complete release of piping load in the event of spring failure or misalignment. Hydrostatic load should be included in design loads. Springs should be secured for shipment.

Many standard piping support designs allow the use of elbow-mounted supports at the bottom of vertical piping runs. This design, however, limits the NDT methods available to the inspection crews and alternative standard pipe supports are preferred.

The pipe structural support element should conform to the following:

- All corners shall be rounded.
- Support should rest on platform beams or solid foundation, not on deck plate.
