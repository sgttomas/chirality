## 2.4 Piping Sizing Requirements

Lines should be sized for design condition and governed by service conditions and economics. Satisfaction of technical requirements to meet the service conditions establishes the basic line size. Application of allowable stress (span), structural and economic considerations to this analysis establishes the line size selected for the final design. Line sizing selection and wall thickness shall be based on the following considerations:

- Process condition

- Available pressure drop and horsepower

- Velocity limitations (noise, erosion, vibration, slugging, pump/compressor suction etc.)

- Support spacing, allowable stresses and line deflection

- Material availability and economics

- Consolidation of sizes and wall thicknesses based on material ordering (mil run)

No changes in pipe diameter or wall thickness in pipe diameter or wall thickness may be made without consideration of all the above items. Deviation from initial parameters based on process conditions (i.e., increasing in line size for span) may be carefully coordinated with the process engineer prior to selecting a final pipe diameter and wall thickness.

Piping smaller than ¾-inch nominal is not to be used except in instrument or other auxiliary utility services, such as chemical injection. This size limitation does not apply to miscellaneous piping located on equipment and vessels. A minimum line size of 2 NPS should be considered for lines located in the piperacks.

Pipe sizes of ¾, 1¼, 2½, 3½, 4½, 5, 7, and 9 inches should not be used except where equipment connections contain these sizes. In such cases, transitions to other commercial pipe sizes are to be made as close as possible to the equipment connections.

With due consideration to economy, pipe sizing should match piping materials that are in regularly produced commercial sizes. Because of the high material cost, special care should be given to the sizing of internally coated carbon steel, stainless steel, and alloy lines.
