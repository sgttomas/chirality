### 3.4.3 Elastomer Characteristics

The sensitivities of different types of elastomers to CO₂ decompression damage is as follows:

[TABLE: Table 5: Elastomer Sensitivities to CO₂ Decompression Damage]

| Sensitivity | Elastomer Type | Typical Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Least | EPDM, EP, Atlas™ | lean gas, CO₂, steam |
| Medium | Buna-N, Hydrin® | Gas, oil, water |
| Most | Viton®, Fluorel®, Kalrez® | Gas, oil, water |

There are specific formulations of elastomers within each group that will perform better or worse than shown above. There is also no one single elastomer choice to use in all cases where high pressure CO₂ is present because other failures causes such as temperature, abrasion and water and oil resistance must also be considered. In general, for all elastomers, seals with cross section diameters larger than 0.15-inch and dynamic seals are more susceptible to decompression damage than smaller or static seals.

### 3.4.5 Seal Assembly Design and Clearances

Particular designs of seal assemblies, particularly the ones with no extrusion pathways and ones that limit the elastomer's expansion work better than other designs in CO₂ decompression. This factor can cause one Manufacturer's equipment to fail with EPDM O-rings while another manufacturer is successful in the same service employing Buna-Nitrile O-rings.

### 3.4.6 Number of Decompressions

CO₂ decompression damage is physical damage rather than a chemical attack and will not occur if the seals are not depressurized. The number of decompressions required to fail a seal range from one to many. A safe rate of depressurization is not known as a function of seal assembly design, elastomer type, pressure, and CO₂ concentration. It can range from 1,000 psi/day to 1,000 psi/10 minutes for very unfavorable situations.
