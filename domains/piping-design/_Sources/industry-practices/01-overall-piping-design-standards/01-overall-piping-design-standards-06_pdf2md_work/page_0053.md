When guaranteed minimum wall thicknesses are being specified, any included mill tolerances indicated in respective tables should be deducted.

Whenever technically and economically justifiable, calculations should be performed according to the appropriate ASME B31 to use lesser wall thicknesses to match any lower design pressures. The Line classes list specific wall thickness for each pipe diameter. It is the responsibility of the Piping Material Engineer to verify these dimensions for compliance with the Specification and Code of Construction.

With due consideration to economy, wall thickness selection should match piping materials which are available in the quantities required. Line classes may list calculated and standard wall thickness or only standard wall thicknesses. In either case, consolidation of sizes and wall thickness across line classes based on material ordering (mill run) requirement may be required. Wall thickness changes should be shown on the drawings and considered in the design. It is desirable that wall thickness changes include fittings and flanges as well as the associated line class.

Increase in wall thickness above that specified in the line class is generally acceptable without approval. However, other than as noted above, use of pipe with thicker wall than the specified is limited to the next heavier schedule or weight designation. Additional increase in wall thickness must have approval by the Company or design representative. Should an even thicker material be needed (i.e. due to increased corrosion allowance), a more appropriate piping line class should be used and the effect of additional weight on the design may need to be re-evaluated.

### 4.2.3 Socket Weld Piping

Socket weld couplings are the preferred method for joining 1½-inch and smaller straight run piping in socket weld line classes.

### 4.2.4 Screwed Piping

Straight run piping in screwed line classes should be joined with couplings supplied with pipe.

### 4.2.5 Stress Relieved Piping

Piping to be stress relieved for process reasons should be identified on the process line list and "SR" marking in the isometric drawings.

## 4.3 Fittings

### 4.3.1 Elbows

Welding elbows shall be long radius unless otherwise approved on a case-by-case basis. Reducing butt-weld elbows may be used instead of elbow-reducer combinations only where a shorter connection is needed.
