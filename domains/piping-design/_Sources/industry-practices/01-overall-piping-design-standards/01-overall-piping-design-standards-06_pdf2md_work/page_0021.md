## 2.5 Piping Flexibility

### 2.5.1 Flexibility Investigations

The piping system should be designed to have adequate flexibility to prevent pipe movement from causing overstress of pipe or support material, leakage at joints, or distortion of connected equipment due to excessive forces or moments. Piping should be supported to minimize vibration and piping stresses by using anchors, guides, supports, and structural bracing.

The effect of vibration, natural frequency and uncertainty prediction in temperature from equipment on connecting piping shall be assessed and considered in flexibility analyses.

### 2.5.2 Method of Analysis

Piping systems, when required, can be analyzed by simplified, approximate, or comprehensive methods of analysis.

Approximate or simplified methods can only be used in the range of configurations for which their adequacy has been demonstrated. Approximate methods will still be used for rough checks, since the majority of pipes, in going from A to B, will be inherently flexible.

Computer solution (such as Auto-Pipe or Caesar II or other equivalent stress analysis computer program) should generally be used when a comprehensive analysis is required due to the complexity of the systems involved.

### 2.5.3 Flexibility Guidelines

Pipe stress analysis should normally be performed for critical lines in accordance with the calculation of piping criticality. Flexibility analysis should be categorized giving consideration to strength, reaction hazard (e.g., nozzle loads) and service hazards.
