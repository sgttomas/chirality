# General Piping Design

**4.2.5.2** For piping not protected by a PR device, the emergency pressure shall be equal to the maximum pressure that can be developed as a result of control valve failure, pump shut-off, or inadvertent valve closure, and static head.

**4.2.5.3** When pump curves are available, the pump shut-in pressure shall be the sum of the pump differential pressure (shut-in) plus normal suction pressure.

**4.2.5.4** When pump curves are not available for determining shut-in pressures, an acceptable estimate of the shut-in pressure shall be the greater of the following:

1. Normal pump suction pressure plus 120% of normal pump differential pressure.
2. Maximum pump suction pressure plus normal pump differential pressure.
3. If shut-in pressures are estimated in accordance with the sub-paragraphs above, they shall be re-evaluated when pump curves are obtained:
   - A lower design pressure than determined from the first sub-paragraph above shall be acceptable if the actual pump supplied has a shut-off differential pressure less than 120% of normal differential pressure.
   - Where the actual pump supplied has a shut-off differential pressure greater than 120% of normal differential pressure, the design pressure, if previously determined from the first sub-paragraph, above shall be corrected.

**4.2.6** (*) Unless otherwise specified by Owner's Engineer, the design pressure for piping components operating at positive pressures only shall be a minimum of 50 psig.

**4.2.7** (*) Unless otherwise specified by Owner's Engineer, the minimum design pressure for piping components operating at vacuum pressure shall be full vacuum (15 psia) and 50 psig internal pressure.

**4.2.8** When designing for external pressure (for example, in jacketed piping), the possible absence of internal pressure in the inner pipe shall be taken into consideration.

**4.2.9** Piping and components between double block valves shall be suitable for the more severe line classification on either side of the double valve installation.

**4.2.10** Piping downstream of such equipment as heat exchangers and control valves shall not be designed for the lower pressure resulting from pressure drop through the equipment if the pressure can increase because of downstream fouling or inadvertent closing of a valve. Valves that are locked or sealed open during operation and closed only for maintenance need not be considered.

**4.2.11** Supplemental design requirements and piping systems in HF service are covered in REP 5-10-1.

## 4.3 Sizing Requirements for Piping

**4.3.1** (*) The design flow quantity for oil, hydrocarbon, gas, and vapor lines shall be the maximum quantity for which the equipment upstream (pumps, vessels, furnaces, etc.) is designed, except that the design capacity of connections between towers and reboilers shall be subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer.

**4.3.2** (*) The design capacities for utility services piping shall be subject to the approval of Owner's Engineer.
