Examples of fluids/materials meeting the above criteria include the following:

- Acids.
- Caustics.
- Hazardous chemical additives (resulting in chemical burns).
- Process water (some residual HCs), stripped sour water.
- Catalyst, spent catalyst ≥ 366°F.
- Boiler feedwater and steam ≥ 366°F.
- Streams with design gauge pressure exceeding 150 psi but less than or equal to the corresponding gauge pressure allowed by the ASME B16.5 Class 1500 rating.

**3.4.3** Flammable and Combustible Fluid Service – A sub-set of Normal Fluid Service as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the fluid/material is readily ignitable when released to the atmosphere, including flammable liquids; hydrocarbon vapors; and other vapors, gases, or solids. Per NFPA 30, a liquid having a flash point below 100°F and having a vapor pressure not exceeding 40 psia at 100°F is known as a Class I liquid. Class I liquids are subdivided as follows:

- Class IA liquids – liquids with flash points < 73°F and boiling points < 100°F.
- Class IB liquids – liquids with flash points < 73°F and boiling points ≥ 100°F.
- Class IC liquids – liquids with flash points ≥ 73°F but < 100°F.

Per NFPA 30, a combustible liquid is one whose flash point is 100°F or higher, and they are classified as:

- Class II – flash point equal to or greater than 100°F, but less than 140°F.
- Class IIIA – flash point equal to or greater than 140°F, but less than 200°F.
- Class IIIB – flash point equal to or greater than 200°F.

Examples of flammable fluids/materials meeting the above criteria include the following:

- Any flammable and combustible fluids with operating temperatures above their flash points.
- Light hydrocarbons, lighter than 68 degrees API gravity (0.705 specific gravity).
- Flammable services that can auto-refrigerate and lead to brittle fracture.

**3.4.4** Dangerous Fluid Service – A sub-set of Normal Fluid Service as defined in accordance with ASME B31.3, in which the fluid/material is damaging to human tissue due to the fluid/material's chemical properties excluding pH.

Examples of fluids/material meeting the above criteria include materials such as the following:

- Phenol.
- Hydrogen sulfide.
- Chlorine.
- Others contained in a stream at concentrations above those where regulation and local health practice may put limits on personnel exposure.

**3.4.5** Aggressive Environmental Service (AES) – A fluid/material service, as defined in REP 10-2-1 and REP 10-2-2.

**3.4.6** Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) Alkylation Service – A service in which the main purpose is to produce a high-octane gasoline-blending component in presence of HF catalyst under conditions selected to optimize alkylate yield and quality. This document specifically refers to two specific processes: (a) the Phillips HF licensed process; (b) the UOP HF licensed process.
