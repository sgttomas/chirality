**EXHIBIT 3-10** Planning Piping with a Process Flow Diagram

[FIGURE: Three-part exhibit illustrating how a process flow diagram is subdivided into subsystems and then translated into a physical arrangement.

**a. Subdivided Process Flow Diagram** — A hand-drawn process flow diagram bounded by dashed-line rectangles that group related equipment into four subsystems labeled A, B, C, and D (each label shown in a circle).
- Subsystem A (upper left): A feed stream enters from the left and is routed through a small pump set on a base, a heat exchanger (with diagonal arrow symbol indicating heat transfer), and a small horizontal drum.
- Subsystem B (middle left): Receives a stream from A. Contains a heat exchanger feeding a horizontal drum, a pump, a mixing/junction symbol, and a second heat exchanger that discharges a stream out to the lower left.
- Subsystem C (center): The largest subsystem. Contains a tall vertical tower with an overhead heat exchanger and overhead reflux drum, a reflux/bottoms pump, and a side reboiler/exchanger. Streams from B feed C, and C discharges streams toward D. A leader line from this dashed boundary points to the handwritten label "TYPICAL SUBSYSTEM."
- Subsystem D (right): Contains two parallel vertical towers (columns), each with an overhead line exiting to the right and bottoms outlets shown with valve symbols at grade. A return stream from D flows back to the upper right.

**b. Subsystem Arrangement** — A plan-view sketch (within a dashed boundary) showing the physical arrangement of the equipment that makes up subsystem B. Two small vertical vessels are shown at the top, with a pipe rack/header running horizontally across the upper portion of the plot. Below, a horizontal drum and adjacent vertical exchangers/columns are arranged with interconnecting piping and pumps. The subsystem is labeled B (in a circle) at the lower right of the plan.

**c. Interconnection of Subsystems** — A schematic plan view showing the four subsystem plots (A upper left, D upper right, B lower left, C lower right), each drawn as a dashed rectangle. Inter-subsystem flows are shown as arrows: a feed arrow enters from the left into A, streams pass between A–B, B–C, C–D, and D–A across the central interconnecting corridor, and product arrows exit to the left from B and from the upper right of D.]
