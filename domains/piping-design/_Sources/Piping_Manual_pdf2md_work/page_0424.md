**Rotating Equipment Nozzle Allowable Loads** As discussed earlier, the designer should use the equipment manufacturer's published allowable loads. However, these loads may not be available at the "planning" stage of piping layout. For this example, we will make the following assumption: The maximum allowable force on steel rotating equipment nozzles is (200 lb) × (the nominal nozzle size). The maximum limit for the allowable load is 2,000 lb.

*Example of Allowables Calculation*

2-in, 300# RF, CS nozzle 200 × 2 = 400 lb
18-in, 150# RF, CS nozzle 200 × 18 = 3,600 lb, exceeds the allowable limit. Use 2,000 lb.

The maximum allowable force on cast iron equipment nozzle is (50 lb) × (nominal nozzle size). The limit is 500 lb.

*Example of Allowables Calculation*

4-in, 125# FF, CI nozzle 50 × 4 = 200 lb
12 in, 250# FF, CI nozzle 50 × 12 = 600 lb, exceeds the allowable limit. Use 500 lb.

**Rotating Equipment Nozzle Example Calculation** This section describes how to determine the pipe length required for achieving a flexible pump layout. (Reference Exhibit 16-16 for example diagram.)

Calculate the allowable load on the pump.

(10-in pump nozzle) × (200) = 2,000 lb maximum force allowed

*Step 2.0*

Calculate the expansion in the north-south direction. The 15 ft 6 in leg is the only run in the north-south direction. (Reference Exhibit 16-15 for thermal growth table.)

(15 ft 6 in) × (0.046) = 0.713 in expansion

*Step 3.0*

Calculate the total absorbing legs that are 90° to the 15 ft 6 in leg. These legs are the east-west leg (11 ft) plus the vertical leg (7 ft 3 in). Due to the rigid valves and nozzles in the system, neglect the 6"-8" vertical leg.

total available absorbing legs = (11 ft) + (7 ft 3 in)
