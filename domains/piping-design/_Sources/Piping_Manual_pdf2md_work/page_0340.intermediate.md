As noted, the minimum inside diameter of sewer boxes is 48 in. The formula used to size sewer boxes depends on the inlet line configuration—a 90° entry and a 45° entry are shown in Exhibit 13-16.

For the 90° entry sewer box, the sum of one half the diameter of each of the largest two lines adjacent to each other is added to 12 in. That sum is then multiplied by 4 and divided by π (3.1416 is used here):

$$\frac{(9 \text{ in} + 6 \text{ in} + 12 \text{ in}) 4}{3.1416} = 34.37 \text{ in}$$

For the 45° entry sewer box, the sum of one half the diameter of each of the largest two lines adjacent to each other is added to 12 in. That sum is then multiplied by 8 and divided by π (3.1416 is used here):

$$\frac{(9 \text{ in} + 7.5 \text{ in} + 12 \text{ in}) 8}{3.1416} = 72.57 \text{ in}$$

## CHEMICAL AND PROCESS (CLOSED) SEWERS

Many industrial plants have multiple process or chemical drain systems. These systems are designed to collect all corrosive or toxic chemical wastes as well as surface drainage around the equipment bearing these materials. Exhibit 13-17 displays a typical piping and instrumentation diagram for a chemical drain system. Depicted on this flow diagram are those pieces of equipment bearing the material to be collected; the actual number of drains is determined by the low point in each piping configuration. Exhibit 13-18 shows a plan of the entire system.

Because many of these systems are of PVC, carbon, stainless steel, or fiberglass reinforced pipe, the key elevations are set by working point centerlines.
