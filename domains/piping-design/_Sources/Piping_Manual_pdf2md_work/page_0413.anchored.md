## Conceptual Studies

The piping layout designer will generate conceptual orthographic drawings (planning studies) based on good layout practice and the recommendations of the pipe stress engineer. The plant design supervisor circulates these documents to the other disciplines. The stress engineer will often write requests on this document for additional steel, additional nozzle reinforcement, critical pipe support locations, and other items. Refer to Exhibit 1-9 for an example of a "planning study." Feedback from the different engineering design groups concerning stress analysis requests may change the designer's piping layout.

Up to this point the stress engineer has reviewed only the critical lines. The conceptual study contains layouts for noncritical as well as critical lines. Therefore, the layout designer may receive a stress engineer's feedback on the noncritical lines at this stage. The authors designed the tools given in this chapter to reduce this feedback to a minimum.

## Three-Dimensional (3-D) Modeling

The 3-D designer builds the 3-D models based on the approved conceptual studies. The designer includes the pipe supports in the model. At this point, revisions to layout due to stress problems are unusual.

## Isometric Extraction and Approval

Plant design extracts the isometrics from the 3-D electronic model automatically. The isometric checker reviews each isometric. The stress engineer then signs off on the isometric. The extracted isometric also includes pipe supports.
