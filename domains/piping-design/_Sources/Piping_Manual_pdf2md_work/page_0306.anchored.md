**Pipe load** Pipe load refers to the weight of all piping, valves, fittings, and insulation, including the weight of contents during test or regular operation, whichever is greater.

**Earthquake load** This is the specific seismic data for a particular site.

**Wind load** Measured in miles per hour, wind load is the force applied to a structure at a given site.

**Exchanger bundle removal load** This is half the weight of the tube bundle.

**Thermal expansion load** The effects of thermal expansion must be accommodated by equipment support structures and elements.

**Pipe anchor load** The force calculated to resist loading in anchored piping systems, the pipe anchor load is added to the thermal expansion loads of the equipment.

**Dynamic loading** This is the response of structures and their components to the cyclical loading produced by rotating or reciprocating equipment.

## STRUCTURAL DETAILS

A typical ladder detail is shown in Exhibit 12-1. Ladders with elevations of a maximum of 20 ft (6,100 mm) above grade do not require a safety cage. An 8-in (200-mm) open area behind the ladder should be maintained for toe clearance. Any obstruction within this distance is a potential tripping hazard.

The preferred positions for ladders, stairways, and ramps are illustrated in Exhibit 12-2. It is recommended that ladders be run vertically to a maximum angle of 15°. Stairways should be limited to a maximum angle of 50° off the horizontal plane, but they should not be used for less than a 15° incline. Ramps should be limited to a 15° maximum incline.
