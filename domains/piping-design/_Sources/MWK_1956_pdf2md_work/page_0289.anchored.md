## VIBRATION: PREVENTION AND CONTROL

1. Consider a pulsation dampener as a "cavity" resonator (one cavity):

$$V = \frac{\pi}{4} \times 1^2 \times 2 = 1.57 \text{ ft}^3$$

$$L = 0;$$

$$A = \frac{\pi}{4} \times \left(\frac{3.5}{12}\right)^2 = 0.07 \text{ ft}^2,$$

$$\mu = 2\sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{D^2}{\pi}} = D = \frac{3.5}{12} = 0.29 \text{ ft},$$

$$C = 2075 \text{ ft/sec.}$$

Hence, for one cavity,

$$f = \frac{2075}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{0.29}{1.57}} = 142 \text{ cps}$$

A second cavity will not change this result materially. We have also

$$\lambda = C/f = 2075/142 = 14.6 \text{ ft} \gg 2 \text{ ft}$$

(typical chamber dimension),

thus satisfying a condition for using the above equations.

The damping coefficient due to communication with outside fluid is:

$$\zeta = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu/\lambda = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.29/14.6 = 0.01.$$

For lower frequencies, ζ is even smaller, as will be shown later.

2. Consider now a snubber with a 3 ft long neck (distance from compressor). Then

$$L = 3 \text{ ft};$$

$$\mu = \frac{A}{L + \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A}}; \quad A = 0.07 \text{ ft}^2;$$

$$\mu = \frac{0.07}{3 - \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{0.07\pi}} = 0.022 \text{ ft.}$$

$$f = \frac{2075}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{0.022}{1.57}} = 40 \text{ cps}$$

$$\lambda = \frac{2075}{40} = 52 \text{ ft} \gg 3 \text{ ft.}$$

$$\zeta = \tfrac{1}{2} \frac{0.022}{52} = 2 \times 10^{-4}.$$

It is seen that in this case ζ is quite small and the influence of this damping is, in general, of no consequence.

3. Consider *BCDH* as a chamber with two entrances (Fig. 9.39).

![Fig. 9.39 Pulsation dampener system parallel configuration.](figures/MWK_1956_p0289_fig01_fig-9-39-pulsation-dampener-system-paral.png)

$$\text{Volume} = 2 \times 1.57 + (5 + 10 + 5) \times 0.07 = 4.5 \text{ ft}^3$$

$$A \text{ (per neck)} = 0.022 \text{ ft. Therefore}$$

$$f = \frac{2075}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.022}{4.5}} = 32 \text{ cps}$$

$$\lambda = \frac{2075}{32} = 65 \text{ ft} \geq 30 \text{ ft}$$

Hence, the above calculations will not be greatly in error. ζ is very small.

4. On the other hand consider the system above as shown with flow in one direction (Fig. 9.40).

![Fig. 9.40 Pulsation dampener system in-line configuration.](figures/MWK_1956_p0289_fig02_fig-9-40-pulsation-dampener-system-in-li.png)

Then it is found from the equations given in Subsection 9.6b that:

$$f_1 = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{V}}; \quad f_2 = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mu + 2\mu_2}{V}}$$

*a.* μ ≠ 0

Then

$$f_{\min} = \frac{a}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{V}} = \frac{2075}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{0.022}{1.57}} = 40 \text{ cps as in case 2.}$$

*b.* Suppose, however, that the end entrances are closed, then μ = 0 and

$$f_{\min} = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2\mu_2}{V}}$$

$$\mu_2 = \frac{0.07}{20 + \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{0.07\pi}} = 0.0034 \text{ ft}$$

and

$$f = \frac{2075}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.0034}{1.57}} = 27 \text{ cps;}$$

$$\lambda = \frac{2075}{27} \gg 24 \text{ ft;} \quad \zeta \text{ is small.}$$
