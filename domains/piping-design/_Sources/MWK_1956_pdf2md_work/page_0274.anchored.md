where

$$\mu = \frac{A}{V(L + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A})} \quad (9.69a)$$

has been designated by Rayleigh as an "acoustic conductivity."

Thus,

$$f_n = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{A}{V(L + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A})}} \quad (9.70)$$

If $L \gg \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A}$ then this result reduces to that given by eq. 9.68. If, however, if $L \ll \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A}$ then

$$f_n = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \frac{A}{V}} \quad (9.71)$$

This is the case of a "cavity resonator."

For a circular opening of diameter $d$

$$A = \pi d^2/4$$

the cavity resonator equation becomes

$$f_n = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{d}{V}} \quad (9.72)$$

The last result holds provided, $t \ll d/4$, where $t$ is the cavity wall thickness. Finally, for a spherical cavity resonator, the result reduces to

$$f_n = \frac{C}{2\pi D} \sqrt[6]{\frac{d}{D}} = 0.22 \frac{C}{D} \sqrt[6]{\frac{d}{D}} \quad (9.73)$$

$d$ and $D$ being the diameters of the cavity opening

$$f_n = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\mu_1 + \mu_2}{V_1} + \frac{\mu_2 + \mu_3}{V_2}\right) \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\mu_1 + \mu_2}{V_1} - \frac{\mu_2 + \mu_3}{V_2}\right)^2 + \frac{4\mu_2^2}{V_1 V_2}}} \quad (B.8)$$

and spherical chamber respectively, provided of course, $d/D \ll 1$. In general, in a Helmholz resonator, a representative dimension of the chamber is supposed to be much smaller than a representative dimension of the cavity or opening. An important quantity in connection with the above results is the "wave length":

$$\lambda = \frac{C}{f_n} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{V}{\mu}} \quad (9.74)$$

All results given above hold so long as the wave length $\lambda$ is greater (by at least a factor of 2 or 3) than a representative dimension of the chamber (say the greatest linear dimension). Otherwise the results given in eqs. 9.68 to 9.74 are in error.
