## B.4 Simplified Surge Filter Analysis

Derivation of Results (see Fig. B.4).

Let $V$ = volume of tank, ft$^3$.

$a$ = effective orifice area, ft$^2$.

$Q_1(t)$ = mass flow rate from compressor, time variable, slugs/sec.

$Q_2(t)$ = mass flow rate from tank into piping system, time variable, slugs/sec.

$p(t)$ = pressure in tank, time variable, lbs/ft$^2$.

$p_0$ = pressure in pipe, considered constant, lbs/ft$^2$.

If smoothing is to be effective then the assumption that $p_0$ is constant will be valid.

$\rho(t)$ = density in tank, time variable, slugs/ft.

$T$ = temperature of gas in tank, degrees Rankine.

An *isothermal* process is assumed so that $T$ is constant.

$C$ = speed of sound, constant for an isothermal process, ft/sec.

$\bar{R}$ = gas constant, ft·lb/slugs $\times$ degrees F.

$\gamma$ = ratio of specific heats.

$$C = \sqrt{\gamma \bar{R} T} \quad (B.27)$$

It is assumed that the pressure drop across the effective orifice is moderate so that, at all times, the flow is well in the subsonic range. In this case, instead of the theoretically exact, but cumbersome, relation between pressure drop and flow rate through an orifice in the subsonic case, an approximate but simpler version of the Bernoulli type may be written:

$$Q_2(t) = a\sqrt{\rho(t)[p(t) - p_0]} \quad (B.28)$$

where $a$ = effective orifice area, including contraction coefficient, $\times \sqrt{2}$.

Then, by virtue of the gas law,

$$p = \bar{R}\rho T \quad (B.29)$$

and the definition of speed of sound $C$, eq. B.27,

$$Q_2(t) = \frac{a\sqrt{\gamma}}{C}\sqrt{p(t)[p(t) - p_0]} \quad (B.30)$$

From continuity:

$$Q_1(t) - Q_2(t) = V\dot{\rho}(t) \quad (B.31)$$

$$(\dot{\rho} = d\rho/dt)$$

But, on the assumption of an isothermal process,

$$\dot{\rho}(t) = \frac{\dot{p}(t)}{\bar{R}T} = \frac{\gamma}{C^2}\dot{p}(t) \quad (B.32)$$

Substituting the last result into eq. B.31 gives:

$$Q_1(t) - Q_2(t) = \frac{\gamma V}{C^2}\dot{p}(t) \quad (B.33)$$
