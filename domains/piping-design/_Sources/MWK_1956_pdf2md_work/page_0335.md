Substituting now the expression for $Q_2(t)$ from eq. B.30 into eq. B.33 and dividing through by a factor yields the following:

$$\dot{p}(t) + \frac{aC}{\gamma V} \sqrt{p(t)[p(t) - p_0]} = \frac{C^2}{\gamma V} Q_1(t) \quad (B.34)$$

$Q_1(t)$, being the output from the compressor, is a known time function. Hence, eq. B.34 is a first order differential equation for the instantaneous tank pressure $p(t)$. This differential equation, however, is non-linear.

It is supposed now that the pulsations are moderate in amplitude relative to their average values. Let the average values, being time independent, be represented by bars above the corresponding symbols. Thus:

$$p(t) = \bar{p} + \Delta p; \quad \dot{p}(t) = \Delta \dot{p} = d(\Delta p)/dt$$
$$Q_1(t) = \bar{Q}_1 + \Delta Q_1 = \bar{Q}_1 + \Delta Q_1$$
$$Q_2(t) = \bar{Q}_2 + \Delta Q_2 = \bar{Q}_1 + \Delta Q_1 \quad \text{i.e., } \bar{Q}_1 = \bar{Q}_2$$

On the assumption that the increments $(\Delta\text{'s})$ are small, relative to the average values, the expression $\sqrt{p(t)[p(t) - p_0]}$ may be approximated by a binomial expansion to first order terms in the increment $\Delta p$, as follows:

$$\sqrt{p[p - p_0]} \approx \sqrt{\bar{p}(\bar{p} - p_0)} \left[1 + \frac{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}}{1 - p_0/\bar{p}} \cdot \frac{\Delta p}{\bar{p}}\right] \quad (B.35)$$

Also, in terms of the average values only, eq. B.34 reduces to:

$$\frac{a\sqrt{V}}{C} \sqrt{\bar{p}(\bar{p} - p_0)} = \bar{Q}_1 \quad (B.36)$$

By virtue of eqs. B.35 and B.36, eq. B.34 reduces, after some simplification, to:

$$\frac{\gamma V}{C^2} \bar{Q}_1 \left(\frac{\Delta \dot{p}}{\bar{p}}\right) + \frac{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}}{1 - p_0/\bar{p}} \cdot \frac{\Delta p}{\bar{p}} = \frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}_1} \quad (B.37)$$

Likewise, by virtue of eq. B.35, it follows from eq. B.30 that

$$\frac{\Delta p}{\bar{p}} = \frac{1 - p_0/\bar{p}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}} \cdot \frac{\Delta Q_2}{\bar{Q}_2} \quad (B.38)$$

Substituting the last relation into eq. B.37 gives:

$$\frac{\gamma V}{C^2} \bar{Q}_1 \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}} \left(\frac{\Delta \dot{Q}_2}{\bar{Q}_2}\right) + \frac{\Delta Q_2}{\bar{Q}_2} = \frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}_1} \quad (B.39)$$

Equation B.39 is a first order *linear* differential equation expressing the variation in outflow as a function of the variation in inflow (i.e. into the tank), relative to average values. If:

Inflow variation with respect to mean flow rate $\quad \dfrac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}_1} = x$

Outflow variation with respect to mean flow rate $\quad \dfrac{\Delta Q_2}{\bar{Q}_2} = y$

Time contact $\quad \dfrac{\gamma V}{C^2} \dfrac{\bar{Q}_1}{\bar{p}} \dfrac{1 - p_0/\bar{p}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}} = \tau$

$$(B.40)$$

then eq. B.39 assumes the form:

$$\tau \dot{y} + y = x \quad (B.41)$$

The last first order linear non-homogeneous differential equation is the familiar differential equation of a first order filter, $\tau$ being the "time constant" of the filter, having the dimension of time.

Since,

$$C^2 = \gamma \bar{R} \bar{T} = \frac{\gamma \bar{p}}{\bar{\rho}}$$

Then, if

$$Q^*_1 = \frac{\bar{Q}_1}{\bar{\rho}}$$

represents the average volume flow rate, relative to average tank density per unit time, the expression for $\tau$ becomes:

$$\tau = \frac{V}{Q^*_1} \cdot \frac{1 - p_0/\bar{p}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}p_0/\bar{p}} \quad (B.42)$$

The output from a reciprocating compressor is pulse-like and all harmonics are contained in it. However, if the fundamental harmonic is attenuated by the filter, the higher harmonics can surely be disregarded and it is thus sufficient to consider $\Delta Q_1/\bar{Q}_1 = x$ of a purely sinusoidal form

$$x = x_0 e^{i\omega t}$$

Then the steady state solution for $y$ is of the form $y = y_0 e^{i\omega t}$, and from the differential eq. B.41:

$$y_0 (i\tau\omega + 1) = x_0 \quad (B.43)$$

The attenuation factor is the absolute value of the ratio $x_0/y_0$. Or,

$$\text{A.F} = \sqrt{1 + (\tau \omega)^2} \qquad i = \sqrt{-1} \quad (B.44)$$

Consider a reciprocating compressor, with $N = $ rpm and $m = $ number of cylinders, uniformly spacing of cylinders being assumed, then,

For single action,

$$\omega = 2\pi \frac{N}{60} m$$
