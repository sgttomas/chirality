## 9.8g Magnitude and Direction of Forces on Piping Bends.

At a pipe bend, forces are acting during flow as a result of change in direction of the stream which is equivalent to a change in momentum.

![Fig. 9.36 Forces at pipe bend.](figures/MWK_1956_p0285_fig01_forces-at-pipe-bend.png)

The magnitude and direction of these forces as indicated in Fig. 9.36 may be obtained from the relation:

$$F = 2QU \cos \theta \qquad (9.93)$$

where $Q$ = mass flow rate, slugs/sec.
  $U$ = velocity of fluid, ft/sec.
  $F$ = force on bend, lb.

In the case of pulsating flow, e.g. from a reciprocating compressor, the mass flow rate $Q$ is a periodic time function leading to a cyclic bend force $F$. The resultant effect is then a periodic excitation, with magnification in the case of structural resonance with the pulsation.
