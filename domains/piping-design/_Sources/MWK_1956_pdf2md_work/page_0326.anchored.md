Referring again to Table A.8, the lower six equations, derived from Table A.6, relate the various reacting forces and moments to the rotations and deflections at point $N$. Since there is no external moment at point $N$:

$$M_{xN} = F_{aN}y_N - F_{yN}x_N$$
$$M_{yN} = F_{zN}x_N - F_{xN}z_N \tag{A.15}$$
$$M_{zN} = F_{yN}z_N - F_{zN}y_N$$

Furthermore, the deflections of the supports, stops, etc. $\delta_{xN}$, $\delta_{yN}$, $\delta_{zN}$ are expressable in terms of the forces which produce them, $F_{xN}$, $F_{yN}$, $F_{zN}$ (for the usual rigid supports, $\delta_{xN}$, $\delta_{yN}$, $\delta_{zN}$ are commonly taken equal to zero). Hence, the 12 equations of Table A.8 contain but 12 unknowns and consequently can be solved.

In the case of a branch line $BN$ connected to the main point $N$ as shown in Fig. A.22, the rotations, $\theta_{xN}$, $\theta_{yN}$, $\theta_{zN}$, as well as the deflections, $\delta_{xN}$, $\delta_{yN}$, $\delta_{zN}$, are affected by the flexibility of the branch. Thus the rotations at point $N$ cannot be considered unrestrained as they were in the treatment of stops. The junction moments in the branch at point $N$, $M'_{xN}$, $M'_{yN}$, $M'_{zN}$, expressed in terms of the terminal moments,$^b$ $M_{xB}$, $M_{yB}$, $M_{zB}$, are given by:

$$M'_{xN} = M_{xB} + F_{yB}z_N - F_{zB}y_N$$
$$M'_{yN} = M_{yB} + F_{zB}x_N - F_{xB}z_N \tag{A.16}$$
$$M'_{zN} = M_{zB} + F_{xB}y_N - F_{yB}x_N$$

$^b$ Referred to the origin.

![Fig. A.22 Three-anchor configuration.](figures/MWK_1956_p0326_fig01_three-anchor-configuration.png)

In the development of Table A.8, the moments $M_{AN}$, $M_{yN}$, $M_{zN}$, were understood to result only from *forces* applied at point $N$ since it was assumed that at that point no external moments were applied so that rotations were unrestrained. Consideration of the more general case leads to redefinition of the moment equations (A.15) as follows:

$$M_{xN} = M'_{xN} + F_{zN}y_N - F_{yN}z_N$$
$$M_{yN} = M'_{yN} + F_{xN}z_N - F_{zN}x_N \tag{A.17}$$
$$M_{zN} = M'_{zN} + F_{yN}x_N - F_{xN}y_N$$

But $F_{xN} = F_{xB}$, $F_{yN} = F_{yB}$, $F_{zN} = F_{zB}$, therefore, $M'_{xN} = M_{xB}$, $M'_{yN} = M_{yB}$, $M'_{zN} = M_{zB}$. Hence, with appropriate changes to the subscripts and limits of Table A.8 the 12 equations for the branched system are derived, appearing as shown in Table A.9.

The influence of normal forces and shears is negligible in most piping calculations except that on rare occasions for thermal expansion effects it may warrant investigation. Hence, the development of these constants is given only for the case of concentrated loads (or thermal expansion).

The energy of shear is given by the following:

$$U_s = \int_0^L \frac{k_s \lambda F_s^2 \, dl}{2GA} \tag{A.18}$$
