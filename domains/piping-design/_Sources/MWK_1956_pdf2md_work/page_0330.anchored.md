$$f = \frac{C}{2\pi} \sqrt{\eta}$$

with $\eta$ being a root of:

$$\eta_3 - \alpha_1 \eta^2 + \alpha_2 \eta - \alpha_3 = 0 \quad \text{(B.10)}$$

The 3 roots of the cubic equation are real and positive, corresponding to 3 physical modes.

$$m = 1, 2, \ldots n$$

### B.2 General Characteristic Equation for a Branched Piping System

The hydrodynamic equations of motion for one-dimensional non-steady flow without friction or heat transfer are:

$$\rho \frac{\partial U}{\partial t} + \rho U \frac{\partial U}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} \quad \text{(momentum)}$$

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\rho U) = 0 \quad \text{(continuity)}$$

where $\rho$, $p$, $U$ are pressure, density, and velocity respectively. In addition, for an isentropic process for a gas.

$$p = \text{Const.} \, \rho^\gamma \qquad (\gamma = C_p / C_v)$$

If barred symbols represent mean values, while $\Delta$'s represent increments, then the equations assume the form:

$$(\bar{\rho} + \Delta \rho) \frac{\partial (\bar{U} + \Delta U)}{\partial t} + (\bar{\rho} + \Delta \rho)(\bar{U} + \Delta U) \frac{\partial (\bar{U} + \Delta U)}{\partial x}$$

$$= -\frac{\partial (\bar{p} + \Delta p)}{\partial x}$$

$$\frac{\partial (\Delta \rho)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\Delta \rho)}{\partial x} (\bar{U} + \Delta U) + (\bar{\rho} + \Delta \rho) \frac{\partial (\Delta U)}{\partial x} = 0$$

$$\bar{p} + \Delta p = \gamma \frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{\rho}} \Delta \rho = \bar{p} + C^2 \Delta \rho$$

$$C = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma \bar{p}}{\bar{\rho}}} = \text{speed of sound, considered constant.}$$

Subtracting the mean values, considering the $\Delta$'s infinitesimal and neglecting terms of higher order than 1st in the infinitesimals:

$$\bar{\rho} \frac{\partial (\Delta U)}{\partial t} + \bar{\rho} \bar{U} \frac{\partial (\Delta U)}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial (\Delta p)}{\partial x}$$

$$\frac{\partial (\Delta \rho)}{\partial t} + \bar{U} \frac{\partial (\Delta \rho)}{\partial x} + \bar{\rho} \frac{\partial (\Delta U)}{\partial x} = 0$$

$$\Delta p = C^2 \Delta \rho$$
