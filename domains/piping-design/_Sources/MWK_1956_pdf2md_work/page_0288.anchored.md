$$\text{Period} = \frac{4L}{C} = \frac{4 \times 33}{2075} = 0.0635 \text{ sec.}$$

and the fundamental frequency is

$$f_0 = \frac{1}{0.0635} = 15.7 \text{ cps}$$

Due to the existence of branch $DHG$, the actual fundamental acoustic frequency is different. The effect of the second branch will now be estimated from relations given in Subsection 9.6 assuming that end $G$ is acoustically closed, as assumed for end $A$. The branches are $ABCID$, $GIID$, and $DEF$. Since the areas of all branches are the same, the general relation given in the above subsection reduces in this case, for joint $D$, to:

$$\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{f}{f_0} \cdot 33 + \beta\right) + \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{f}{f_0} \cdot 33 + \beta\right) + \tan\left(\frac{5\pi}{33}\frac{f}{f_0} + \beta\right)$$

$$\underbrace{\hspace{2em}}_{\text{Branch } ABCD} \quad \underbrace{\hspace{2em}}_{\text{Branch } GIID} \quad \underbrace{\hspace{2em}}_{\text{Branch } DEF}$$

In view of the assumption that ends $A$ and $G$ are acoustically closed, and since the pipe areas are constant,

$$\beta \text{ (Branch } ABCD) = 0, \quad \beta \text{ (Branch } GIID) = 0$$

So that the relation is:

$$\tan\left[\frac{10\pi}{33}\frac{f}{f_0} + \beta_1\right] = \tan\left(\frac{10\pi}{33}\frac{f}{f_0}\right) + \tan\left(\frac{5\pi}{33}\frac{f}{f_0}\right) \quad (9.94)$$
$$(DEF)$$

Since the area in branch $DEF$ is constant, "phase" $\beta_1$ is constant for the entire branch. On the other hand, $F$ was assumed acoustically "open," whence

$$\beta_1 = \frac{\pi}{2}\left(m - \frac{f}{f_0}\right), \quad m = 1, 3, 5 \cdots$$

Hence, from the relation above, and for the fundamental mode ($m = 1$)

$$\tan\left[\frac{10\pi}{33}\frac{f_1}{f_0} + \frac{\pi}{2}\left(1 - \frac{f_1}{f_0}\right)\right] = \tan\left(\frac{10\pi}{33}\frac{f_1}{f_0}\right) + \tan\left(\frac{5\pi}{33}\frac{f_1}{f_0}\right)$$

This is a transcendental equation for the ratio $f_1/f_0$ where $f_0 = 15.7$ cps as calculated above for a simple line $ABCDEF$ with $A$ acoustically closed and $F$ acoustically open.

It is seen at a glance that $f_1/f_0 = 1$ cannot be satisfied by the last relation, so that due to branch $GHD$ the frequency is indeed changed. By trial and error it is found that $f_1/f_0 = 0.9$ is a root of that relation satisfying it about approximation, so that

$$f_1 = 0.9 \times 15.7 = 14.1 \text{ cps}$$

It is seen that the fundamental acoustic frequency based on all branches is less than the frequency which results when branch $DHG$ is omitted.

### 9.9b Estimate of Acoustic Frequency of the System Corresponding to its First Harmonic (2nd Mode).

It may be of interest sometimes to estimate the acoustic natural frequency of the first harmonic corresponding to the second acoustic mode. In accordance with the results given in Subsection 9.6c

$$\tan\left(\frac{3\pi}{2} - 1.3\frac{5\pi f_2}{33 f_0}\right) = \tan\left(\frac{10\pi f_2}{33 f_0}\right) + \tan\left(\frac{5\pi f_2}{33 f_0}\right)$$

By trial and error it is found that a root of this transcendental equation is $f_2/f_0 = 2.16$, so that $f_2 = 2.16 \times 15.7 = 34$ cps. The ratio of the frequency of the fundamental acoustic frequency is $f_2/f_1 = 34/14.1 = 2.4$. In a single branch with constant cross sections the ratio would be 3 : 1. In general, it appears then that the effect of branch $GHD$ is to reduce the fundamental acoustic frequency considerably.
