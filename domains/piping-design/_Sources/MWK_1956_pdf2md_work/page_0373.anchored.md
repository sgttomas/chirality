$$
EI \begin{vmatrix} \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \alpha^{88} \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} A^1 + A^2 + A^4 + A^5 + A^7 & A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & (A^2 + A^4)K_{15} \\ A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^3 + A^4 + A^6 + A^7 & A^4 + A^7 & A^4K_{56} \\ A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^4 + A^7 & A^4 + A^6 + A^7 & (A^4 + A^6)K_{56} \\ K'_{15}(A^2 + A^4) & K'_{56}A^4 & K'_{56}(A^4 + A^6) & K'_{56}(A^4 + A^6)K_{56} + A^x \end{vmatrix} \begin{vmatrix} F^a \\ F^a \\ F^a \\ F^{aa} \end{vmatrix}
$$

(5A.36)

where $A^* = \text{diag.} \, [1/x_1, 1/x_2, 1/x_3, 1/x_4, 1/x_5, 1/x_6]$, and $x_p$ ($p = 1, \ldots, 6$) are spring constants of moments and forces for the orthogonal elastic stops.

In the case of a skew force-stop which has direction cosines with the fixed coordinate axes as $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$ and $\beta_3$, Eq. 5A.36 will be modified to be:

$$
EI \begin{vmatrix} \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \delta^7 \\ \alpha_{\text{skew}} \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} A^1 + A^2 + A^4 + A^5 + A^7 & A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & (A^2 + A^4)K_{15}\overline{\beta} \\ A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^3 + A^4 + A^6 + A^7 & A^4 + A^7 & A^4K_{56}\overline{\beta} \\ A^2 + A^4 + A^7 & A^4 + A^7 & A^4 + A^6 + A^7 & (A^4 + A^6)K_{56}\overline{\beta} \\ \overline{\beta}^T K'_{15}A^4 & \overline{\beta}^T K'_{56}A^4 & \overline{\beta}^T K'_{56}(A^4 + A^6) & \overline{\beta}^T K'_{56}(A^4 + A^6)K_{56} + A^x\overline{\beta} \end{vmatrix} \begin{vmatrix} F^a \\ F^a \\ F^a \\ F_{\text{skew}} \end{vmatrix}
$$

(5A.37)

where $F_{\text{skew}}$ is the skew force and $\alpha_{\text{skew}}$ the skew displacement. They bear the following relationships with $\overline{F}^{88}$ and $\alpha^{88}$:

$$
F_{\text{skew}} = \overline{F}^{88}, \quad (5A.38)
$$
$$
\alpha_{\text{skew}} = \overline{\beta}^T \alpha^{88},
$$

where

$$
\overline{\beta}^T = [0, 0, 0, \beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3]. \quad (5A.39)
$$

## 5A-4 An Example

Figure 5A.9 shows a sketch of a reactor coolant loop representing a piping system with two anchors, one closed loop, and four elastic stops. Thus, a total of sixteen unknown moments and forces has to be determined.

Page 375 shows the input to the machine. By using the table marked "Key to Input Data", the input is self-explanatory. It should be noted that the flexibility factor (k) and the stress intensification factor (i) for curved members are automatically calculated if not specified.

Page 375 also shows the check of coordinates of the end points of each branch.

Page 376 (at top) shows the restraining moments and forces on anchors and stops as well as on additional points specified on the SUM-line on the input sheet A. The total actions on the anchor at the reactor are obtained by adding those for points 1 and 101. The maximum stress in the system is also automatically recorded.

Page 376 also gives the stresses, moments, deflections and rotations at each point of the system. Since a curved member in the input is denoted by a radius only, the machine calculates the tangent points which are indicated as A and B.

Finally page 377 gives the moments, forces and coordinates referred to the origin at the anchors, stops and intersections. These moments and forces are actually those from the solution of the equations. A complete print-out of the equations and the inversion of the same may be obtained by substituting an IND-card for an END-card. Utilizing this device, it is possible to split a complicated piping system into two or more parts if the capacity of the machine is insufficient to solve the required number of equations.
