The plastic behavior of flat plates having a circular cut-out reinforced by a pipe collar was also investigated [57]. The analysis was restricted to ideally plastic materials (no strain hardening) which obey the maximum shear-stress flow condition. It was found that for a "full-strength" reinforcement (load at fully plastic condition in reinforced plate equal to or greater than that referring to intact plate), the dimensions of the pipe collar, as shown in Fig. 3.12, must satisfy the equations:

$$\frac{H}{L} = \frac{1 + t_n/R}{t_n/R} \qquad \text{for } R/t_n \leq \tfrac{1}{2}$$

$$\frac{H}{L} = \frac{1 + t_n/R}{\sqrt{1 + 2(t_n/R)^3} - 1} \qquad \text{for } R/t_n \geq \tfrac{1}{2} \qquad (3.20)$$

These equations bear resemblance to the results of the elastic analysis, indicating that, for a given amount of reinforcement ($t_n H = $ constant), maximum effectiveness is achieved when the reinforcement is concentrated near the opening (small $t_n$ and large $H$). Naturally these results can be considered to retain their validity only within consistent limits.<sup>13</sup> Furthermore, for strain-hardening materials or large plastic strains exp. 3.20 can be used, at best, only as a rough guide. This plastic analysis was recently extended [58] to reinforcements of various cross sections.
