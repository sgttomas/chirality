## 9.9j Estimate of Volume and Pressure Drop Requirement of Hydraulic Filters (Bottles) in the Compressor Discharge Lines.

It is thought that an attenuation factor of 5%, corresponding to a reduction of 5%, to compressor pulsations from an average flow rate, is a minimum requirement.

As shown in Table 9.2, the maximum flow rate at intake per compressor is 40 cfm at 250 rpm. Now, the average density ratio on discharge side vs. intake is approximately

$$\frac{\rho_{\text{discharge}}}{\rho_{\text{intake}}} = \frac{1100}{415 + 665} \times \frac{100 + 460}{220 + 460} = 0.84$$

Therefore, maximum discharge = 40/1.7 = 23.5 cfm. Hence, for a double-acting single piston compressor the discharge volume flow rate per stroke per piston is approximately

$$V^* = \frac{23.5}{2 \times 250} = 0.047 \text{ cu ft}$$

Consider a "bottle" 12 in. in diameter and 2 ft long. The volume is

$$V = \frac{\pi}{4} \times 1^2 \times 2 = 1.57 \text{ cu ft}$$

On the basis of uniform gas density, the ratio of "bottle" volume to volume flow rate per stroke per piston is

$$\frac{V}{V^*} = \frac{1.57}{0.047} = 33.4$$

Figure 9.30 in the text or eq. B.48 (Appendix B) shows that for an attenuation factor of 20, the pressure drop required between the bottle and the piping system is $2\frac{1}{2}\%$ of mean tank pressure, or $0.025 \times 1100 = 27.5$ psi.

For the same attenuation factor (95% reduction of pulsation amplitude), and a 4 ft long "bottle" of 12-in. diameter the pressure drop required is only 1.2% of mean tank pressure or only 13 psi out of 1100.
