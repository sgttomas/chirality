The effective damping depends not only upon the viscous friction coefficient but also upon the mass and spring constant. The overall effect is expressed in terms of a *viscous damping coefficient* or *damping ratio*:

$$\zeta = c/c_c \tag{9.38}$$

where $c_c$ is defined as *critical damping*, pertaining to the particular system.

The critical damping of an effective spring-mass-dashpot system is defined as follows: If less damping is present, the system will damp out in an oscillatory manner when disturbed by a passing transient; for damping greater than critical the system will damp out in a nonoscillatory manner, as illustrated in Fig. 9.15.

The critical damping for a given system is immediately determined from the differential equation as follows:

For free vibration the differential equation is: $m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = 0$. This differential equation is satisfied by the expression $x = Ae^{\lambda t}$ provided that $\lambda$ is a root of the quadratic $m\lambda^2 + c\lambda + k = 0$, or

$$\lambda^2 + (c/m)\lambda + \omega_n^2 = 0, \quad \omega_n^2 = k/m, \quad m \neq 0,$$

from which

$$\lambda = -\frac{c}{2m} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{c}{2m}\right)^2 - \omega_n^2}$$

Critical damping occurs when the expression under the radical is zero. That is:

$$c_c = 2m\omega_n \tag{9.39}$$

By the use of eqs. 9.38 and 9.39, eq. 9.37, after dividing by $k$, may be written in the form:

$$\frac{\ddot{x}}{\omega_n^2} + \frac{2\zeta}{\omega_n}\dot{x} + x = \left(\frac{F}{k}\right)e^{i\omega t} = \delta_{st}e^{i\omega t}$$

The steady state solution for $x$ is of the form:

$$x = Re^{i\omega t}$$

$R$ being in general a complex quantity, the absolute value (modulus) of which represents maximum displacement (spring deflection), i.e. $|R| = \delta_{dyn}$. From the differential equation:

$$\frac{R}{X_{st}} = \frac{1}{1 + 2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}i - \left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}\right)^2}$$

so that the magnification factor is:

$$\text{M.F.} = \left|\frac{R}{\delta_{st}}\right| = \left|\frac{\delta_{dyn}}{\delta_{st}}\right|$$

$$= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\left[1 - \left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}\right)^2\right]^2 + \left[2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}\right]^2}} \tag{9.40}$$

Plots of M.F. vs $\omega/\omega_n$ for several values of $\zeta > 0$ are shown in Fig. 9.14, in addition to the "pure" resonance curve for $\zeta = 0$.

The maxima of the M.F. are given by:

$$1 \leq (\text{M.F.}) \max = \frac{1}{2\zeta\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}} \tag{9.41}$$

and the maxima occur at:

$$\frac{\omega}{\omega_n} = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2} \tag{9.42}$$
