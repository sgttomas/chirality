and the energy of normal force be

$$U_n = \int_0^l \frac{k_n F_n^2 \, dl}{2EA} \quad (A.19)$$

where $A_k$ = flexibility factor in shear = 1.

$\lambda$ = shear distribution factor which depends on the shape of the cross section ( = 2 for thin-walled cylinders).

$F_t$ = shearing force.

$A$ = cross-sectional area.

$k_n$ = flexibility factor in tension or compression = 1.

$F_n$ = normal force.

The shear equation may be written

$$U_s = \int_0^l \left( \frac{F_t^2 + F'_t{}^2}{GA} \right) dl \quad (A.20)$$

where shearing force is expressed in components $F_s$ and $F'_s$, respectively in and transverse to a reference plane containing the particular portion of the line.

If the subside, inside, and mean radii of the pipe cross section are designated by $r_o$, $r_i$, $r_m$ respectively, and the wall thickness by $t$, the properties of the section and their approximate relationship may be expressed as follows:

$$A = \pi(r_o^2 - r_i^2) = 2\pi r_m t$$

$$I = \frac{\pi}{4}(r_o^4 - r_i^4) = \pi r_m^3 t$$

$$\frac{I}{A} = \frac{r_m^2}{2}$$

Substituting these values into eqs. A.19 and A.20

and introducing the stiffness factor $Q$, the total energy due to shear and normal forces becomes

$$U_{sn} = \frac{1}{2EI} \int Q(2.6F_t^2 + 2.6F'_t{}^2 + 0.5F_n^2) r_m^2 \, dl \quad (A.21)$$

Applying Castigliano's theorem, the displacement in the z-direction is given by

$$\delta_z = \frac{1}{EI} \int_0^l Q r_m^2 \left( 2.6F_s \frac{\partial F_s}{\partial F_z} + 2.6F'_s \frac{\partial F'_s}{\partial F_z} + 0.5F_n \frac{\partial F_n}{\partial F_z} \right) dl \quad (A.22)$$

Similar equations can be written for $\delta_y$ and $\delta_x$ by substituting for $\partial F_z$ the partial differential of the applicable force.

The shear and normal forces for any point in the z-plane are

$$F_s = F_x \cos \psi - F_y \sin \psi$$
$$F'_s = F_z$$
$$F_n = F_y \cos \psi + F_x \sin \psi$$

Similarly for any point in the y-plane

$$F_s = F_x \cos \phi - F_z \sin \phi$$
$$F'_s = F_y$$
$$F_n = F_z \cos \phi + F_x \sin \phi$$

and for any point in the x-plane

$$F_s = F_y \cos \theta - F_z \sin \theta$$
$$F'_s = F_x$$
$$F_n = F_z \cos \theta + F_y \sin \theta$$
