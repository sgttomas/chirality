So that

$$\Delta p_3 = \frac{2(\bar{p} - p_0)}{\bar{Q}} \Delta Q_1 = \frac{2 \Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{Q}} \Delta Q_1$$

Hence

$$\Delta \bar{p} = \bar{p} - p_0$$

$$(\Delta p_3)_1' = \frac{2 \Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{Q}} (\Delta Q_1) \quad (B.18)$$

Differentiating B.15, substituting B.17 and B.18, and simplifying:

$$\ddot{x} + \frac{C^2 A}{V L^2} \dot{x} - 2 \frac{\Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{p} L^2} \left( \frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}} \right) = 0 \quad (B.19)$$

Also from continuity:

$$\frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}} + \frac{\Delta Q_2}{\bar{Q}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\bar{Q}}$$

Or substituting from B.14:

$$\frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}} + \frac{\dot{x} A}{\bar{Q}} \dot{x} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\bar{Q}} \quad (B.20)$$

Equations (B.19) and (B.20) are two linear differential equations in terms of $\Delta Q_1 / \bar{Q}$ and $x$, $\Delta Q / \bar{Q}$ being given as a function of time. For steady state harmonic oscillation let:

$$\frac{\Delta Q}{\bar{Q}} = R e^{i \omega t}, \quad \frac{\Delta Q_1}{\bar{Q}} = R_1 e^{i \omega t}, \quad x = x_0 e^{i \omega t}, \quad i = \sqrt{-1}$$

$R$, $R_1$, and $x_0$ being, in general, complex. Substituting in B.19 and B.20 gives, after some rearrangement:

$$(\omega^2 - \omega_n{}^2) x_0 + \frac{2 \Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{p} L^2} R_1 = 0 \quad (B.21)$$

$$R_1 + i \omega \frac{\bar{p} A}{\bar{Q}} x_0 = R \quad (B.22)$$

Where $\omega_n{}^2 = C^2 A / V L^2$ is the square of the resonator natural frequency, in accordance with the results given previously.

From B.21 and B.22:

$$\frac{R_1}{R} = \frac{1}{1 + \left[ \frac{2 A \Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{Q} L^2} \right] \omega} \cdot \frac{1}{(\omega_n{}^2 - \omega^2)} i$$

whence the magnification factor (M.F.) for $\Delta Q_1 / \bar{Q}$ becomes

$$\left| \frac{R_1}{R} \right| = \text{M.F.} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \left[ \frac{\left( \frac{2 A \Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{Q} L^2} \right) \omega}{(\omega_n{}^2 - \omega^2)} \right]^2}} \quad (B.23)$$

Now let

$$\tau = \frac{2}{\bar{p}} \frac{\Delta \bar{p}}{\bar{Q}} \frac{V}{\bar{p}} \quad (B.24)$$

where

$$\bar{V} = \frac{V}{\bar{p}} = \text{average volume flow rate}$$

Then $\tau$ is a time constant of the resonator orifice combination, considered merely as a filter. Since $C^2 = \bar{p}/\bar{\rho}$ and $\omega_n{}^2 = C^2 A / V L^2$,

eq. B.23 gives the final expression for the magnification factor of $\Delta Q_1 / \bar{Q}$:

$$\text{M.F.} = \left| \frac{R_1}{R} \right| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \left[ \frac{\tau \omega}{1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2} \right]^2}} \quad (B.25)$$

It is seen from eq. B.24 that $\tau$ is proportional to the pressure drop across the orifice and the volume of the resonator. The greater the value of $\tau$ the smaller the M.F. and the better the smoothing. However, even for a very small value of $\tau$ (say a very small pressure drop across orifice), the magnification factor still tends to zero if the resonator frequency is *chosen so as to coincide with the forcing frequency $\omega$*. This then is the "tuning" phenomenon.

Since an ideal case is considered, the M.F. drops to zero at the tuning frequency. In a physical case this will not happen, of course, although in the neighborhood of the tuned frequency, the M.F. will drop sharply. The effect of the orifice pressure drop and resonator capacity, as reflected via the time constant $\tau$, is to maintain a lower value of the magnification factor in a *band* of frequencies around the tuned frequency; the larger $\tau$ the wider the band. If there exists no orifice, then instead of a concentrated pressure drop there will be a continuous pressure drop due to pipe friction. The smoothing will then occur in a finite length of pipe in the neighborhood of the resonator. A curve of the magnification factor for the outflow pulsations $\Delta Q_1 / \bar{Q}$ vs. $\omega/\omega_n$ is shown in Fig. B.4.

$$\tau = 1/\omega_n$$

Finally, from the previous results it is found that the magnification factor for the flow into the resonator

$$\left| \frac{\Delta Q_2 / \bar{Q}}{\Delta Q_1 / \bar{Q}} \right| = \left| \frac{R_2}{R} \right|$$
