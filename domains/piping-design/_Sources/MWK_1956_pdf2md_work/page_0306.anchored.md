The integration limits are from the point of load application (lower limit) to the fixed end, or to the point where the loading is discontinuous. Thus, the limits for the moments and forces applied at the free end are from $A$ to $O'$. For the uniform load, two sets of limits are needed, $F$ to $N$, and $N$ to $O'$, since the load application begins at $F$, accumulates linearly to $N$, and then acts with constant effect to $O'$.

By inspection of the integrals it is seen that they all are functions of the following basic expressions called shape coefficients:

$$s = \int kQ \, dl$$

$$u = \int (k \cos^2 \psi + 1.3 \sin^2 \psi) Q \, dl$$

$$v = \int (1.3 \cos^2 \psi + k \sin^2 \psi) Q \, dl \quad (A.5)$$

$$p = \int (1.3 - k) \sin \psi \cos \psi Q \, dl$$

These expressions are conveniently employed in deriving a further series of constants also termed shape coefficients. With reference to concentrated loads these are:

$$s_x = \int x s \, ds \qquad u_x = \int x \, du = \int y \, dq$$

$$s_{xx} = \int x^2 \, ds \qquad u_{xx} = \int x^2 \, du = \int xy \, dq$$

$$s_y = \int y \, ds \qquad v_x = \int y \, dv = \int x \, dq \quad (A.5)$$

$$s_{yy} = \int y^2 \, ds \qquad v_{yy} = \int y^2 \, dv = \int xy \, dq$$

$$s_{xy} = \int xy \, ds$$

With reference to uniform loads:

$$w_x = + \int (\bar{x}' - x) l \, ds \qquad w_y = - \int (\bar{y}' - y) l \, ds$$

$$w_{ux} = + \int y(\bar{y}' - y) l \, ds \qquad w_{ux} = + \int x(\bar{y}' - y) l \, dr$$

$$w_{vx} = - \int x(\bar{x}' - x) l \, ds \qquad w_{vx} = - \int y(\bar{x}' - x) l \, dr$$

$$w_x = - \int (\bar{x}' - x) l \, du + \int (\bar{y}' - y) l \, dq$$

$$w_y = - \int (\bar{y}' - y) l \, dv - \int (\bar{x}' - x) l \, dq$$

$$w_{ux} = -\int x(\bar{x}' - x) l \, du + \int y(\bar{y}' - x) l \, dq$$

$$\qquad - \int y(\bar{y}' - y) l \, dv + \int x(\bar{y}' - y) l \, dq \quad (A.5)$$

When the equations (formulas A.4) are expressed in terms of the shape coefficients (formulas A.5) and the coefficients are presented in tabular form under their respective unknowns, the results appear as shown in Table A.1.

By referring to the origin the moments and deflections at the free end in accordance with the following transfer formulas, the simplified form shown in Table A.2 is obtained.

$$M'_x = M_x + F_y z_A - F_z y_A$$

$$M'_y = M_y + F_z x_A - F_x z_A \quad (A.6)$$

$$M'_z = M_z + F_x y_A - F_y x_A$$

$$\delta^*_x = \delta_x + y_A \theta_z - z_A \theta_y$$

$$\delta^*_y = \delta_y + z_A \theta_x - x_A \theta_z \quad (A.7)$$

$$\delta^*_z = \delta_z + x_A \theta_y - y_A \theta_x$$

Note that each of the columns headed by $w_xL$, $w_yL$, and $w_zL$ in Table A.1 has been divided into two, the first two columns representing the moment at the origin due to the accumulated load, and the last column giving that load. By this arrangement the recurrence of the shape coefficients can easily be observed in each column having moments and forces with the same subscripts.
