Substituting Eq. 5A.27 in matrix $A$ which is defined in Eq. 5A.18, it is easy to verify that:

$$A = \begin{vmatrix} L' & O \\ N' & L' \end{vmatrix} \begin{vmatrix} B & D \\ D' & C \end{vmatrix} \begin{vmatrix} L & N \\ O & L \end{vmatrix} \quad (5A.28)$$

where $O$ represents a $3 \times 3$ null matrix. If we let:

$$X = \begin{vmatrix} B & D \\ D' & C \end{vmatrix}$$

$$Y = \begin{vmatrix} L & N \\ O & L \end{vmatrix} \quad (5A.29)$$

be the submatrices of the matrix $A$, Eq. 5A.28 can then be written as:

$$A = Y'XY \quad (5A.30)$$

The following observations may be made on the submatrices in Eq. 5A.30:

1. The $3 \times 3$ matrices $B$, $C$, and $D$ (Eq. 5A.26) in $X$ matrix have identical meanings in the $\lambda$-system as $|W_{ij}|$, $|U_{jl}|$ and $|V_{il}|$ (Eq. 5A.13) in the $x$-system. In fact, if the local origin is taken as the common origin, and the $\lambda$-system identical to the $x$-system, $N$ would become a null matrix as can be seen from Eqs. 5A.21 and 5A.26, and $L$ would become a diagonal unit matrix as can be seen from Eqs. 5A.3 and 5A.26. Consequently, $A$ simply becomes $X$. Therefore, $X$ may be called the shape coefficient matrix, but referred to the $\lambda$-system. It is implicit here that $X$ can be computed for every segment of a pipe irrespective of the $x$-system.

2. The $\lambda$-system, or the coordinates at the local origin, can be chosen so as to make the construction of the $X$ matrix as simple as possible. The correct choice of the $\lambda$-system for common piping configurations is not difficult to obtain as will be demonstrated in the illustrative examples in Section 5A-2.4.

3. The $Y$ matrix, consisting of terms which are functions of the position vector $\bar{q}$ and the local coordinate $\bar{\lambda}_i$ only, can be computed for every piping segment irrespective of its geometrical configuration. Once $\bar{q}$ and $\bar{\lambda}_i$ are given in the $x$-system, $Y$ follows from Eq. 5A.29.

When matrix $A$ becomes available for every segment of the pipe, the shape coefficient matrix for the entire pipe may be obtained by summation. Equation 5A.18 can again be used to obtain the redundants and Eq. 5A.19 the internal forces at any point along the pipe, except that $\alpha_{ij}$, $\beta_{ij}$ are now defined by Eq. 5A.24.
