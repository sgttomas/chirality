## B.3 Tuned Resonator Relations

Consider the schematic below representing a piping system with a (tuned) resonator and orifice (Fig. B.2). The entire pressure loss in the pipe may be considered as an orifice effect. This is the case which will be elucidated in somewhat more detail later.

[FIGURE: Fig. B.2 Tuned resonator]

Let bars represent mean values while $\Delta$'s represent increments. Thus let the mean values of flow rate, pressure and density be $\overline{Q}$, $\overline{p}$, $\overline{\rho}$. Let the incremental values of flow rates and pressures be: $\Delta Q$, $\Delta Q_1$, $\Delta Q_2$ (as indicated on the schematic), $\Delta p_1$, and $\Delta p_2$. In accordance with procedure that a certain amount of mass oscillates as a rigid body in the neck; let the effective length of this mass column be $L^* = L + \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi A}$ where $L$ and $A$ are the length and cross-sectional area of the neck, and suppose that at a given instance this mass penetrates the resonator chamber a distance $z$, measured from an initial position. The distance $z$ is supposed to be small, of the same order as the increments. Then:

$$\Delta Q_2 = (\overline{p} + \Delta p_1) A \dot{z} = A \dot{z} \quad \text{(B.14)}$$

From the equilibrium of the vibrating mass:

$$\overline{\rho} L L^* \ddot{z} + A \Delta p_2 = A (\Delta p_1) \quad \text{(B.15)}$$

and:

$$\Delta Q_2 = V \overline{\rho} \dot{z} = \dfrac{V}{C^2} \dot{p}_2 = \dfrac{V}{C^2} (\Delta \dot{p}_2) \quad \text{(B.16)}$$

where $C_2 = dp/d\rho$ = speed of sound, considered constant.

From B.14 and B.16:

$$\overline{\rho} A \dot{z} = \dfrac{V}{C^2} (\Delta \dot{p}_2) \quad \text{or} \quad (\Delta p_2) = \dfrac{C^2}{V} \overline{\rho} A \dot{z} \quad \text{(B.17)}$$

For a small pressure drop through the orifice we have

$\overline{Q} + \Delta Q_1 = k\sqrt{\overline{p} + \Delta p_1 - p_0}$

$= k\sqrt{\overline{p} - p_0}\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta p_1}{\overline{p} - p_0}\right)$

While

$\overline{Q} = k\sqrt{\overline{p} - p_0}$

From which

$\dfrac{\Delta Q_1}{\overline{Q}} = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta p_1}{\overline{p} - p_0}$
