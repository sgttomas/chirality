# HISTORY AND DERIVATION OF PIPING FLEXIBILITY ANALYSIS

$$\delta_p = \frac{1}{EI} \bigg[ \int_0^L 4M_b \frac{\partial M_b}{\partial F_p} Q\, dl + \int_0^L 4M'_b \frac{\partial M'_b}{\partial F_p} Q\, dl + \int_0^L 1.3M_t \frac{\partial M_t}{\partial F_p} Q\, dl \bigg] \tag{A.2}$$

in which $EI/Q$ represents stiffness.

Figure A.1 illustrates a pipe line in space assumed completely fixed at one end, point $O'$, free to rotate and deflect at the other end, point $A$. The origin of the standard coordinate system is located at an arbitrary points, $O$. The line is considered weightless, but one member denoted as $NF$ is acted upon by positive unit loads, $w_x$, $w_y$, $w_z$, acting in the three coordinate directions, the total loading being $w_xL$, $w_yL$, $w_zL$. The letters $N$ and $F$ indicate respectively the point nearest and farthest away from the fixed end $O'$.

Due to the applied loading, the pipe will deform, causing rotations $\theta_x$, $\theta_y$, $\theta_z$, and deflections $\delta_x$, $\delta_y$, $\delta_z$, at the free end, $A$. These deformations can be altered by applying the moments $M'_x$, $M'_y$, $M'_z$, and the forces $F_x$, $F_y$, $F_z$ at the end $A$, thus making it possible to represent any degree of constraint.²

----

²By constraint is meant the external relation between the deformations at the point of reactions, and the reactions themselves.

----

With suitable values of these moments and forces, the free end can be brought to a position of zero deformation thereby conforming to the constraint of a completely fixed end. Moments and forces thus determined will constitute the reactions of an anchor upon the pipe. Similarly any system, however complex, can be treated as a fictitious cantilever loaded by known loads and loads (reactions) which result from known displacements. Constraint may be partial or even elastic as long as its variation can be expressed analytically.

Let $P(x, y, z)$ be an arbitrary point on the member $NF$ whose length is $L$. The length of the portion of the member from $F$ to $P$ is denoted as $l$ and its inclination³ at $P$ is measured positive in the counterclockwise direction from the positive horizontal axis and is denoted as $\phi$. The coordinates of the center of gravity of member $l$ are $\bar{x}'$, $\bar{y}'$, $\bar{z}'$. The internal moments in the pipe at the point $P$ are functions of the moments and forces at $A$ and the uniform loading from $F$ to $P$. For an arbitrary point between $O'$ and $N$ the internal moments are functions of the moments and forces at $A$ and the loading on $NF$.

----

³While the descriptor $NF$ is shown in Fig. A.1 as an inclined straight member, the relationships are developed so as to apply equally well to a curved member. Hence, $\phi$, the inclination at $P$, is treated as a variable.
