Suppose now that $n$ identical snubbers are connected at the same point. This is equivalent to an increase of $f$ by the factor $n$, which changes the expression for $F_S$ to:

$$F_S = kR_0 \frac{\left[1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2\right]}{\sqrt{1 + \left[\frac{1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2}{2nf'\omega/\omega_n}\right]^2}} \quad (9.48)$$

It is seen that the total maximum snubber force increases considerably less than proportionally with the number of snubber units. Indeed as $n \to \infty$, the max. total snubber force tends to a *finite* limit value given by:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} F_S = kR_0[1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2] \quad (9.49)$$

An infinitely stiff shock absorber ($\zeta = \infty$) is a rigid connection; hence, eq. 9.49 represents the force transmitted to the ground when the point in question is rigidly connected. The above is true from a dynamic point of view. However, shock absorbers of sufficiently large $\zeta$ may still be used to allow flexibility of piping equilibrium positions in view of thermal expansion as well as serve to counteract resonant vibration amplitudes.

From the preceding equations the following, perhaps more useful, relations can also be obtained:

If $R_n$ = amplitude for $n$ shock absorbers
$(R_1$ = amplitude for 1 shock absorber
$(R_0$ = amplitude without shock absorbers
$F_n$ = total max. force transmitted to $n$ shock absorbers
$(F_1$ = total max. force transmitted by 1 shock absorber

Then:

$$\frac{F_n}{F_1} = \left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)\left(\frac{R_1}{R_0}\right)^2\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}} \quad (9.50)$$

$$\frac{R_n}{R_1} = \frac{1}{n}\left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)\left(\frac{R_1}{R_0}\right)^2\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}} \quad (9.51)$$

So that

$$\frac{F_n}{F_1} = n \frac{R_n}{R_1} \quad (9.52)$$

Thus if $R_1/R_0 \leq \frac{1}{2}$ say, then $F_n = F_1$ from eq. 9.50 and $R_n = R_1/n$ by eq. 9.52.

### 9.5b Elastic Foundations for Rotating Machinery.

The installation of rotating machinery on elastic foundations is a fairly standard means for preventing vibration. The basic features of such an installation may be illustrated on the basis of the simple spring-mass model, Fig. 9.18. If $\omega$ (rad/sec)

![Fig. 9.18 Rotating machinery on elastic foundation.](figures/MWK_1956_p0271_fig01_rotating-machinery-on-elastic-foundation.png)

is the rotational speed, then the centrifugal force $F^*$ (lb) due to the unbalanced rotor mass $m$ (lb sec$^2$/ft) at radius $r$ (ft) is

$$F^* = mr\omega^2 \quad (9.53)$$

Let $k$ (lb/ft) be the overall spring constant of the system. Then the static deflection of the spring under force $F^*$ is

$$\delta_{st} = mr\omega^2/k \quad (9.54)$$

Since the undamped natural frequency $\omega_n$ (rad/sec) of the mass $M_0$ (lb sec$^2$/ft) is

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{k/m_0}$$

we have

$$k = m_0\omega_n^2 \quad (9.55)$$

and

$$\delta_{st} = (m/m_0)r(\omega/\omega_n)^2 \quad (9.56)$$

When the rotational speed is equal to the undamped natural frequency, the static deflection and the force $F^*$ will be independent of the natural frequency

$$\delta^*_{st} = (m/m_0)r \quad (9.57)$$

In the absence of damping, the maximum dynamic deflection is

$$\delta_{max} = \frac{\delta_{st}}{\left|1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2\right|} \quad (9.58)$$

and the maximum dynamic force in the spring is

$$F_{max} = k\delta_{max} = m_0\omega_n^2\delta_{max} = \frac{m_0\omega_n^2\delta_{st}}{\left|1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2\right|} \quad (9.59)$$

Substituting the expression for $\delta_{st}$ from eq. 9.56 into eq. 9.58 and eq. 9.59 gives:

$$\delta_{max} = \frac{m}{m_0}r\frac{(\omega/\omega_n)^2}{\left|1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2\right|} \quad (9.60)$$

$$F_{max} = \frac{mr\omega^2}{\left|1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2\right|} \quad (9.61)$$

or, in view of equations 9.55 and 9.57

$$\delta_{max}/\delta^*_{st} = [1 - (\omega_n/\omega)^2]^{-1} \quad (9.62)$$

$$F_{max}/F^* = [1 - (\omega_n/\omega)^2]^{-1} \quad (9.63)$$
