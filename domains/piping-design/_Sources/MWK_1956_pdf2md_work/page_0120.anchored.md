## DESIGN OF PIPING SYSTEMS

![Fig. 5.1 The z-plane.](figures/MWK_1956_p0120_fig01_fig-5-1-z-plane.png)

Single-plane systems are usually drawn and calculated in the z-plane (Fig. 5.1). The sketch is made and the given data recorded on Form A in accordance with Steps 1 through 5 described for the Simplified General Method. If there is expansion in the plane only, the following steps are taken.

*Step 6.* On Form B-1 enter the following as indicated:

Member number.

Shape (horizontal, vertical, or inclined).

Length of member $L$, ft.

Distances $a$ and $b$, ft, i.e. the $x$- and $y$-coordinates respectively, of the midpoint of the straight member.

Value of $L^2/12$.

*Step 7.* Have Steps 1 to 6 checked.

*Step 8.* Compute the shape coefficients for each member in accordance with these formulas:

> **Table - Shape coefficients for horizontal, vertical, and inclined members** - see [XLSX](tables/MWK_1956_p0120_tbl01_shape-coefficients-horizontal-vertical.xlsx) and [source crop](tables/MWK_1956_p0120_tbl01_shape-coefficients-horizontal-vertical.png)

| Shape Coefficient | Horizontal Member | Vertical Member |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $s$ | $kQL$ | $kQL$ |
| $s_a$ | $a \times s$ | $a \times s$ |
| $s_b$ | $b \times s$ | $b \times s$ |
| $s_{ab}$ | $b \times s_a$ (or $a \times s_b$) | $b \times s_a$ (or $a \times s_b$) |
| $s_{aa}$ | $a \times s_a + sL^2/12$ | $a \times s_a$ |
| $s_{bb}$ | $b \times s_b$ | $b \times s_b + sL^2/12$ |

Sum the shape coefficients, $s$, $s_a$, $s_b$, etc., across to obtain the final coefficients.

*Step 9.* Enter the final coefficients and the constants into the equation as indicated on the form. For complete fixation at the ends, end rotation $\delta_z$ is zero and the constant in the moment equation is therefore zero. The constants $EI^*\Delta_x$ and $EI^*\Delta_y$ are transferred from Form A, the term $EI^* = E_b I/144$ denoting stiffness expressed in lb-ft².

*Step 10.* Have Steps 8 and 9 checked.

*Step 11.* Solve the simultaneous equation and substitute the values obtained back into the equation to check the answer, as explained in Section 5.4.

*Step 12.* Compute the moments at the various points, find the point of maximum stress, and tabulate results.

*Step 13.* Have Step 12 checked.

The foregoing procedure is exemplified in Sample Calculation 5.1. The same system is calculated in Chapter 4 as Sample Calculation 4.10, and a comparison between the two shows that identical results are obtained. While the Simplified General Method has the advantage of ease of computation for the uninitiated, the method in the present chapter is more fundamental and hence, more versatile. Both methods involve the same amount of work.

A single-plane system with expansion *perpendicular* to the plane only, requires the solution of two rotation and one displacement equations. These equations are entirely independent of those for expansion in the plane. For a line such as that shown in Sample Calculation 5.1, if there is expansion in the *z*-direction, $M_z$, $M_y$, and $F_z$ must be found. This case can be solved by following the procedure and using the form sheets described for multiplane systems in Section 5.12.

### 5.6 Inclined Members and Changes in Stiffness

The procedure for calculating a line having straight members which are inclined to the coordinate axes departs but little from that described in Section 5.5. The only difference lies in the use of the general formulas in the calculation of the shape coefficients.

$$s = kQL$$
$$s_a = a \times s \qquad s_{ab} = (b \times s_a) + s(L^2/12) \cos \alpha \sin \alpha$$
$$s_b = b \times s \qquad s_{aa} = (a \times s_a) + s(L^2/12) \cos^2 \alpha$$
$$s_{bb} = b \times s_b + s(L^2/12) \sin^2 \alpha$$

where $\alpha$ is the angle of inclination measured from the positive horizontal axis to the member as shown in Fig. 5.2, positive in counterclockwise and negative in clockwise direction.

Sample Calculation 5.1 is calculated for 10 in. standard pipe, A-106, Grade A material. It is apparent that for the same expansion the magnitude of the moment and the forces is in direct proportion to the stiffness $EI'$. Hence, for any pipe with a stiffness of $E_2 I_2$ the moment and forces may be attained by dividing those shown in the example by $Q = EI/E_2 I_2$, or multiplying the summation coefficients by $Q$ and solving the equations.
