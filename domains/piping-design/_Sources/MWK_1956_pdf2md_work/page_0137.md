Figure 5.9 shows a pipe line in which members 2-5 are shown in a plane rotated $\alpha_1°$ off the standard $y$-axis, and members 8-A are shown in a plane rotated $\alpha_2°$ off the standard $z$-axis. The entire system is shown broken up into working planes. Since two different skews are involved, they must be handled separately. It is possible to set up skewed members in each of two skewed planes. To illustrate this, members 2 to 5 are shown in both $z'$- and $y'$-planes, depending upon the selection of the prime axes and of the corresponding direction of the angle $\alpha_1$.<sup>5</sup> Members 8-A are shown only in a $z'$-plane, but the reader will see they could have been set up in a $z'$-plane also. However, the shape coefficients are computed only once in one or other of the two possible planes.

The $c$-value for members $O'-2$ is readily apparent by inspection as $K_1$. For members 8-A, remember-

ing that $c$ is the perpendicular distance of the projected plane from the parallel plane at the coordinate origin, $c$ is equal to $K_2$ sin $\alpha_2$ shown on Fig. 5.9. The signs of $K_1$ and $K_2$ sin $\alpha_2$ indicate the direction of these distances from the coordinate origin in their auxiliary planes. It will be noted that member 2-3 could have been placed in the $x$-plane with members $O'-2$ as an inclined member. The choice is arbitrary. Likewise member 8-9 could have been placed with members 6-8. Also member 12-A could have been placed in a $z$- or $x$-plane, but here, in the interest of keeping down the number of working planes, it is not advisedly calculated as part of the $z'$-plane.
