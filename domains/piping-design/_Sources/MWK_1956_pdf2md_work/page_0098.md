According to assumptions 3 and 4 the individual legs absorb the following portion of the thermal expansion in the $x$-direction:

$$\delta_x = \frac{L^3}{\Sigma L_x{}^3 - \Sigma L_x{}^3} \Delta_x \tag{4.4}$$

where  $\delta_x$ = lateral deflection in the $x$-direction for the leg under consideration, in.

$L$ = length of the leg in question, ft.

$\Delta_x$ = overall thermal expansion of system in $x$-direction, in.

$\Sigma L_x{}^3 - \Sigma L_x{}^3$ = sum of cubed length of all legs perpendicular to the direction considered (in this case meaning the legs parallel to the $y$- and $z$-directions).

Similar equations can be written for the lateral deflections in the $y$- and $z$-directions. The schematic distribution of thermal expansions to the various members of a space-bend is shown in Fig. 4.5.

The deflection capacity of a cantilever of the type stipulated by assumption 5 (and shown in Fig. 4.4) can be given as:

$$\delta = \frac{48L^2 S_A}{ED} \tag{4.5}$$

A first (preliminary) approximation requires now the calculation of $\delta_x$, $\delta_y$, and $\delta_z$ from eq. 4.4 and $\delta$ from eq. 4.5 (or Chart C-13) for each leg. If $\delta_x$, $\delta_y$, and $\delta_z$ are all less than $\delta$, every leg possesses a sufficient deflection capacity, and the system can be regarded to be adequately flexible.

This comparison is most conveniently carried out on Form R as shown in Sample Calculations 4.9, 4.10, and 4.11. The stepwise process of the analysis is clearly indicated in these forms, which are self-explanatory in conjunction with the foregoing discussion.

In Sample Calculations 4.9, 4.10, and 4.11, the condition that $\delta > \delta_m$ (where $\delta_m$ denotes the largest of $\delta_x$, $\delta_y$, and $\delta_z$ in any leg) is satisfied for all legs save the one next to the terminal. In this case a further refinement is warranted in recognition of the actual rotation which takes place at intersections. This refinement is accomplished through the use of a correction factor $f$, which allows for the reduction of bending moment, due to the rotation of the leg adjacent to the one considered. The value of $f$ for the appropriate case is obtained from Chart C-14, depending on the position of the leg and its length relative to adjacent members. If the corrected deflection capacity of the leg, $f\delta$, is larger than $\delta_m$, the leg is considered to be sufficiently flexible. The use of the correction factor is shown in Steps 9 and 10 of the Sample Calculations.
