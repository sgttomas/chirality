## Expansion Loop Stress and Anchor Force

As an expansion loop expands, forces are transmitted to the two anchor points. To calculate the anchor force and maximum pipe stress of the loop, refer to Figure 5-8 and the following example.

This example assumes use of LR elbows and is restricted to line sizes of 4" through 14".

For force at each anchor:

$F = C \times E \times Ip$

Maximum stress:

$S = K \times E \times D$

- $C$ = Constant to be obtained from Table 5-2
- $E$ = Expansion to be absorbed by the loop, in inches (should be limited to 10")
- $Ip$ = Moment of inertia, inches⁴
- $D$ = Outside diameter of pipe in inches
- $K$ = Constant to be obtained from Table 5-3

### Need for Formal Stress Analysis

The need for formal stress analysis should be determined by designers with extensive flexibility experience. Hot piping closely connecting to strain-sensitive equipment such as pumps, compressors and turbines shall be closely reviewed for possible full analysis. For other systems full stress analysis is required when the following criteria is not satisfied:

*Example:* Expansion loop, see Figure 5-7:

Pipe = 12" std. wt.;
$D_o$ = 12.75 so use 13";
$T$ = 520° – 70° = 450°F;
$L$ = 200'

To solve for minimum *h*:

$h^2 = 0.0004 \times 13 \times 200 \times 450$

$h = \sqrt{468}$

$= 21.6'$

Minimum $W = 0.5 \times 21.6$

$= 10.8'$

To test for minimum $h = \dfrac{1.88 \times 13}{1.25}$

$= 19.5'$

use the larger 21.6'.
