# Course 11 — Leading Complex Projects

[Series](../index.html) · [Northbank case](../case-study.html) · [Glossary](../glossary.html) · [References](../references.html)

Complex projects connect technical work, commercial commitments, organizations, and people. A decision in one part can change the conditions elsewhere.

Leadership must keep those relationships visible. It must also preserve the authority, evidence, and responsibilities needed for sound decisions.

This course teaches diagnosis and direction when ordinary controls no longer give a credible account of the undertaking.

## Prerequisites and study plan

Complete Courses 01 to 10. Bring your Northbank project basis, execution forecast, interface records, and assurance findings.

Read Packets A to D. Allow six hours for reading, discussion, practice, and the assessed assignment.

Use an independent reader for the assignment. For individual study, ask a mentor to question your diagnosis and recommendation.

## Learning outcomes

After this course, you can:

- Explain coupled effects and feedback in a project.
- Examine incentives, interests, organizational boundaries, and decision rights.
- Distinguish known failures from uncertainty and unsupported claims.
- Compare continuation, recovery, reduction, suspension, and termination.
- Prepare a bounded decision with evidence and conditions for reconsideration.

## Lesson 1 — Understand the relationships that create complexity

A large list of activities does not explain a complex project. The important question is how changes pass between activities and people.

For example, a motor change can affect electrical protection, controls, equipment delivery, installation, commissioning, and operating information. These effects can return to the original decision: a delayed selection can reduce the available supplier choices, which can require another design change. This return is feedback. Its effects can increase or reduce the original difficulty.

Consider schedule pressure. Pressure can reduce review time. Missed defects can then cause rework. Rework creates further schedule pressure. That account is a hypothesis until evidence supports the connections. Identify observations for each connection before treating it as the cause.

A corrective response could protect the necessary review and resolve the controlling interface earlier. Adding people without resolving that interface may increase coordination demands.

Use three questions to examine a proposed action:

1. What conditions must exist for this action to work?
2. Which other activities or people will it affect?
3. How can those effects change the original problem?

Separate a dependency map from a feedback explanation. The map shows conditions needed for work. The explanation shows effects that return over time.

For each connection, state the mechanism. “Procurement affects commissioning” is incomplete. Equipment identity affects test procedures and spare parts, which affect readiness.

Keep time visible. An intervention can help today while creating a problem during delivery or operation.

## Lesson 2 — Work across interests and organizational boundaries

People can agree on the project purpose while facing different incentives. Procurement may protect price. Operations may protect availability. Engineering must preserve technical adequacy.

An incentive can make an incomplete report attractive. It does not prove dishonesty. Examine the reporting arrangement and the evidence it rewards.

D02 links a bonus to the reported handover date. It also records missing readiness evidence. This creates a reason to examine the handover measure.

Document counts can show activity. They cannot establish that interfaces are resolved or operating conditions are satisfied.

Replace an unsuitable measure with questions about the intended result. Ask which claims are established, which remain open, and which decisions are needed.

Examine who could bear the consequences of an unsupported report. Consider workers, operators, nearby people, and the environment where relevant.

Identify possible effects as questions when evidence is missing. Do not invent a hazard finding or assume that commercial benefit justifies exposure.

Organizational boundaries add another difficulty. A supplier's delivery promise may depend on a configuration that the site has not accepted.

Define what each party must supply. Name the receiving party, examination conditions, required timing, and decision authority.

Different decision rights can apply to one issue. The sponsor can change the Northbank budget. That act cannot establish motor compatibility or legal applicability.

The technical authority can decide within its competence and remit. Its decision cannot create expenditure authority beyond A01.

Prepare connected decisions together when possible. Preserve their separate conditions and authorities. An apparent agreement may still contain an unresolved technical or commercial boundary.

## Lesson 3 — Decide under uncertainty without concealing it

Uncertainty can concern facts, performance, cost, schedule, or consequences. Name the uncertainty before selecting its treatment.

An electrical survey establishes an observed supply. It does not establish spare capacity. A test establishes observed behavior within stated conditions.

An important distinction concerns reversibility. Preparing alternatives is often reversible. Placing an unsuitable order can create commitments that are costly to change.

Use early work to acquire useful information before such commitments. This approach needs a limit, a responsible person, and a decision it can inform.

For example, request a project-specific supplier configuration and a corrected selection basis. This request can inform procurement without authorizing the order.

Delay also has consequences. Compare the cost of waiting with the consequences of proceeding on an unsupported basis.

Do not label every unresolved matter as an acceptable risk. Some conditions are requirements that the proposed activity must satisfy.

A decision owner can accept tradeoffs within their remit. Risk acceptance does not expand that authority or establish unexamined technical performance.

A budget approval cannot excuse an unresolved legal requirement. A commercial reservation cannot establish technical suitability.

For a consequential decision, prepare:

- The decision question and its authority.
- The applicable requirements and present evidence.
- The options, including continued investigation where useful.
- The uncertainties and their possible consequences.
- The work permitted under each option.
- The conditions that require another decision.

A condition must be observable. “Proceed if safe” leaves the decision basis undefined. State the required examination and its responsible authority.

## Lesson 4 — Diagnose a troubled undertaking

Start with the present result and its basis. Establish what exists, what was examined, what is authorized, and what people now need.

Avoid treating every difficulty as poor execution. The purpose may be unclear. The design basis may be incomplete. The reported status may be unsupported.

Use a short diagnosis table.

| Question | Useful evidence | Possible consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the approved scope still address the intended need? | Approved basis and current user needs | A scope decision may be required. |
| Can the proposed result satisfy its conditions? | Design basis, interface records, and checks | A technical investigation or redesign may be required. |
| Is the execution plan credible? | Readiness, capacity, commitments, and forecast | Resequencing or a commitment decision may be required. |
| Does reported progress support reliance? | Identified outputs and examination evidence | The report or the result may require repair. |
| Can people raise adverse evidence? | Findings, meeting decisions, and responses | Leadership or governance may require correction. |

Test explanations against competing explanations. A delivery delay may follow late ordering, an unresolved selection, supplier capacity, or several connected causes.

Locate the next useful result. Ask who can produce it and which condition prevents it. This question helps avoid expanding work around an unresolved premise.

Keep useful, unaffected work moving. A voltage discrepancy can prevent equipment acceptance while allowing source indexing and test planning to continue.

Recovery must be a credible route to a useful result. It needs defined scope, resources, evidence, decision rights, and receiving responsibilities.

Compare at least three directions. Continuation, reduced scope, and suspension can expose consequences that a single optimistic plan conceals.

Reduction requires an authorized scope decision. Suspension requires arrangements to preserve equipment, information, responsibilities, and any continuing site obligations.

Termination also requires a controlled disposition. It can leave contractual, physical, financial, and professional obligations after production stops.

## Lesson 5 — Govern and learn from the response

Governance establishes who decides, who must be consulted, and how unresolved matters reach that decision.

A useful escalation describes a specific question, its evidence, the consequences of delay, and a recommendation. Sending an unexplained issue list transfers confusion.

Protect the reporting of adverse evidence. Address the missing changeover test in D02. Ask what evidence or authorized disposition justified its recorded closure.

Report readiness truthfully, including inconvenient limits. Explain any personal or commercial interest that could affect the recommendation or its reception.

Give the concern a competent owner. Correct the present status where the evidence requires it. Examine the effect on the acceptance claim.

The junior engineer's concern does not become correct because it is unpopular. Its grounds deserve examination under the same standard as other claims.

Treat confidential information through its assigned access and disclosure conditions. Supply necessary evidence to authorized reviewers through an appropriate route.

Confidentiality must receive explicit consideration when reporting a significant concern. Seek the responsible reporting route instead of quietly omitting the evidence.

These are general ethical questions. A06 leaves Northbank's actual legal applicability unresolved. Do not present an assumed law as their justification.

Institutional learning requires a change in practice. An additional report has little value if the next project repeats the same unsupported acceptance.

Identify a specific improvement, its owner, and the activity that will use it. For Northbank, readiness reporting could include unresolved acceptance conditions.

Check whether that change improves the next decision. Retain the original episode as evidence with its limits. One case cannot prove universal effectiveness.

## Worked Northbank example

The forecast, technical gaps, and handover limits now inform a decision about the project's direction. The leadership group must consider them together while preserving their different evidence and decision requirements.

The leadership group receives D01 after C03. It wants a continuation decision for the approved 18 litres per second scope.

The forecast is 265,000 against an authorized 240,000. The difference is 25,000 training currency units. No budget increase is recorded.

Release at the end of Week 6 gives delivery at the end of Week 11. Sequential installation, commissioning, and handover reach the end of Week 15.

Week 12 remains the target. Neither the forecast nor the leadership brief changes that commitment.

The group identifies three proposals: another supplier slot, activity overlap, and temporary operation. None has sufficient supporting conditions in D01.

It recommends a bounded investigation before the continuation decision. The supplier must identify the offered configuration, delivery conditions, and price.

The engineering team must assess the proposed overlap against installation and commissioning prerequisites. Operations must prepare an assessed basis for any temporary use.

The sponsor receives the forecast and options. The technical and legal gaps remain explicit. The proposal does not declare equipment acceptance or operating readiness.

This response advances the decision while preserving its limits. It does not promise recovery by Week 12 before a credible route exists.

## Transfer example

A hospital software replacement has a contractual launch date. The supplier reports complete modules, but the hospital has unresolved data migration and user access checks.

The manager examines the combined service and its readiness conditions. Module counts do not establish that staff can use the service safely.

A staged launch may be an option. Its boundaries, examination, support arrangements, and decision authority must be established before reliance.

## Practice — Examine the reporting system

Use A01, A02, C03 to C05, D01, and D02. Supply no additional approvals or test results.

1. Draw one feedback explanation for a Northbank difficulty.
2. Cite the evidence for each connection.
3. Mark connections that remain hypotheses.
4. Compare the document-count measure with three proposed readiness questions.
5. Prepare a response to the junior engineer's concern.
6. Identify one irreversible commitment that needs better evidence.
7. Explain which unaffected work can continue.
8. Identify who could be affected by an unsupported readiness claim.
9. Explain how to preserve truthful reporting and appropriate information sharing.

Exchange your work with an independent reader. Ask the reader to identify unsupported causal claims and unclear decision authority.

## Knowledge check

1. How does a feedback explanation differ from a dependency map?
2. Why can a document-count report conceal delivery risk?
3. Which decision rights remain separate when the sponsor approves expenditure?
4. How should reversibility affect the timing of an uncertain decision?
5. What evidence is needed before the proposed activity overlap can support a Week 12 recovery claim?
6. What would make the response to D02 an institutional improvement rather than another report?

## Assessed assignment — Continuation recommendation

Prepare a leadership memorandum of 1,000 to 1,500 words. Use Packets A to D and your earlier portfolio.

Diagnose the present undertaking. Compare continuation, scope reduction, and suspension. You can add termination if its consequences require consideration.

Recommend the next direction. Identify the required decisions, permitted work, unresolved conditions, and points for reconsideration.

Attach a one-page map of coupled effects. Attach a decision table that names authorities and the evidence each decision needs.

Obtain independent examination. Repair confirmed defects. Present a five-minute defense of the revised recommendation.

The assignment must meet these five criteria:

1. The diagnosis distinguishes authorized scope, forecasts, observations, proposals, and unsupported claims.
2. The coupled-effects map gives mechanisms, time effects, evidence, and explicit uncertainties.
3. The option comparison addresses technical, commercial, operating, and continuing obligations.
4. The recommendation preserves decision rights and states observable conditions for action or reconsideration.
5. The revised memorandum addresses adverse evidence, affected people, incentive conflicts, truthful reporting, and a usable improvement in practice.
