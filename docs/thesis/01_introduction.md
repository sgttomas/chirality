# Chapter 1 — Introduction

## 1.1 The problem

An agent's capacity to produce a convincing answer is not yet a capacity to carry
someone's work well. Work has a purpose, a history, a subject and consequences.
What counts as progress depends on those relations. A technically correct
contribution can answer the wrong question; an impressive implementation can
leave the intended user unable to act; an elaborate account of progress can
obscure an unresolved commitment.

The human–agent relationship therefore concerns more than expressing a complete
request to an obedient machine. The person may discover what matters through
investigation. An agent may expose an assumption, produce an alternative or make
a previously impractical action possible. Purpose guides this exchange without
specifying every step in advance. The person's judgment and the agent's
reckoning participate differently in the same undertaking.

The Root research question is: **what makes agent capability into purposeful,
accountable work, and why should its supporting environment develop through
revisable roles, methods, tools and artifacts? Why can those means support
different applications without prescribing one way of organizing every
undertaking?**

Chirality Root is one practical response. It is the shared environment in which
participants develop and use the means of human–agent work. Those means include
instructions, roles, methods, tools and representations of the work. They make
context accessible, actions possible and consequences open to examination. They
also become objects of work themselves when experience reveals their limits.

This creates a tension. Shared structure can preserve distinctions and learning
across contributions; it can also replace attention to the undertaking with
attention to its own categories. A framework that prescribes every route can
frustrate the initiative it seeks to enable. An arrangement that leaves every
relation implicit can lose purpose, evidence and responsibility as work expands.
The thesis examines how a shared environment can sustain useful distinctions
without pretending to settle the organization of work in advance.

Professional engineering motivates this inquiry because plausible production
and accountable reliance are plainly different there. Chapter 6 retains a
bounded interpretation of professional obligations. Root itself is a development
environment, not a means of conferring professional adequacy on its products.

## 1.2 The approach

Four connected perspectives structure the inquiry:

- **Ontology:** distinguish the objective, work product, need, source, operation,
  finding and acceptance from the files, indexes or messages representing them.
- **Epistemology:** make the grounds, assumptions and limits of material claims
  available where participants need to assess them.
- **Praxeology:** carry authorized work through investigation, production,
  examination, repair and integration, including revisions to the means of work.
- **Axiology:** assess usefulness, consequences, human responsibility and the
  costs of control in attention, delay and maintenance.

These perspectives concern one undertaking rather than four independent
compliance tasks. The object matters through its relationships; a claim matters
because someone may act upon it; action exposes what the prior understanding
missed; values determine which consequences demand attention. Each perspective
can correct a partial account supplied by another.

This relation explains why the means must be revisable. A failed operation may
reveal an inadequate distinction between intention and outcome. A new consequence
may change the grounds required for reliance. A control may obscure the very
question it was meant to clarify. The framework gives participants questions to
bring to those encounters, not a mechanism that settles them beforehand.

A durable artifact provides a shared object of attention across a conversation
or interruption. A role names a responsibility that could otherwise disappear
between contributors. A method preserves learning about how to proceed. A tool
makes a useful operation repeatable. Their value lies in these contributions,
not their mere existence. The current Root implementation supplies examples of
those relations. Project commitments, design and needs remain in deliverable
folders. A query exposes the
relevant inputs and consumers; an agent assesses whether an input meets its
condition. Selected roles and methods supply context without requiring every
participant to read the library. Assignment and host permissions bound action.
Continuing ownership carries findings into repairs and the shared result. Git
and PRs preserve changes without a second authored account of routine progress.

Root components need not be put into a product deliverable hierarchy. A change
to a resolver, workflow or instruction starts from that shared component and its
actual consumers. The [Root entry](../../execution/_Coordination/LOOP_INIT.md)
and [repository instructions](../../AGENTS.md) define this working distinction.

Agents extend **reckoning** through sophisticated analysis, criticism, planning
and adaptive action. **Judgment** is the person's situated and committed
engagement with what the work means, what matters and what they will stand
behind. It shapes purpose and choices throughout the undertaking. A final
acceptance is one expression of it; neither that event nor its record exhausts
judgment. Appendix D retains the shared conjecture and formal argument with
light definitions. The implementation argument does not attempt to deduce a
repository architecture from its notation.

## 1.3 Contributions and their standing

This Root thesis makes five connected contributions:

1. **An argument for a revisable generative environment.** The means of work
   shape what can be perceived and done. Making those means accessible to
   examination permits learning to change the environment as well as its outputs.
2. **An implementation of relevant context.** Current artifacts and consumer
   needs support selective queries; observed facts remain distinct from semantic
   suitability, completion and acceptance.
3. **A responsibility model without compulsory staffing.** Agent 0 holds purpose
   and continuity. Other roles supply useful contributions. Assignment-specific
   permissions and independent scrutiny remain distinct from role identity.
4. **An account of integration and controlled change.** A shared change examines
   actual consumers, preserves valid evidence and carries repairs into use. This
   applies to instruction and tool changes as well as product implementation.
5. **A longitudinal case of architectural revision.** The October reset coupled
   changed duties with changed enforcement. It demonstrates that some earlier
   mechanisms were dispensable in the examined work; it does not isolate a
   productivity effect or establish universal superiority.

These contributions have different standing. Normative commitments state the
adopted values and authority boundaries. Design hypotheses propose means that
may serve them. Empirical findings report what an identified implementation,
review or use established. Coherence, implementation and effectiveness are
separate claims. The literature supplies comparisons without establishing
historical priority for the whole arrangement.

Root, App and SWBPIPE address three related but bounded research questions:

| Thesis | Question and subject |
|---|---|
| Root, this thesis | How can shared human–agent development means generate useful work and remain open to examination and revision? |
| [App v4](../../projects/chirality-app-v4/docs/thesis/README.md) | How can an application host agents and workflow authoring through intelligible operations, permissions and outcomes? |
| [SWBPIPE](../../projects/chirality-piping/docs/thesis/README.md) | How can that participation be instantiated in piping design and stress-model authoring with defensible domain and numerical meaning? |

The App comes first in this explanatory sequence because its subject is the
human–agent method itself. Workflow authoring makes intentions and ways of acting
available for trial and revision. SWBPIPE then poses a harder transfer question:
can that relationship enter a specific practice without flattening its meanings
into generic agent commands? Proliferation is useful participation in such
application categories, with the domain's distinctions and consequences intact.
A shared protocol or service does not establish that result.

## 1.5 Scope and limitations

This is an architectural argument and a longitudinal case account, not a
controlled study, formal safety proof or regulatory endorsement. Instructions
can be disobeyed, tests can omit important cases, and participants can share a
mistaken premise. Root makes selected sources and operations available. Their
availability cannot guarantee informed judgment or a usable product.

The owner supplies direction, domain experience and consequential decisions.
Transfer without that continuing interpretation remains unproved. Root may
produce internally consistent tools that still serve the wrong question; its
own application authors may share assumptions that another practitioner would
challenge. A second application and a second practitioner test different parts
of the generalization.

The case does not treat a PRD as a deterministic compiler. Current requirements,
prior code, observed failures and owner interpretation inform each derivation.
App v4's separation from earlier Apps is evidence of design revision; its value
depends on retaining useful learning while replacing unsuitable means.

Current authority comes from the conversation, instructions and adopted product
commitments. This thesis creates no new development duties. Its
[status and source basis](README.md#warrant-status) limit what the explanatory
account authorizes and establishes.
