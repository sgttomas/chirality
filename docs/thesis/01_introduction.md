# Chapter 1 — Introduction

## 1.1 The problem

Work has a purpose, a history and consequences. A technically correct
contribution can answer the wrong question; an impressive implementation can
leave its intended user unable to act. Agent capability becomes useful through
its relationship to the work.

That relationship develops through inquiry: the person may discover what
matters while investigating, and an agent may expose an assumption, produce an
alternative or make a previously impractical action possible. Purpose guides the
exchange but does not specify every step. The person's judgment and the agent's
reckoning contribute differently to the same work.

**What makes agent capability into purposeful, accountable work? Why should its
supporting environment develop through revisable roles, methods, tools and
artifacts?**

Chirality Root is one response. It provides shared means for making context
accessible, action possible and consequences open to examination. When
experience shows the limits of those means, they also become objects of work.

Shared structure can preserve distinctions and learning, but it can also draw
attention away from the work and toward the structure's own categories.
Prescribing every route can frustrate initiative; leaving every relationship
implicit can lose purpose, evidence and responsibility as work expands.

## 1.2 The approach

Four perspectives help examine this tension:

- **Ontology:** what the work consists of and how its parts relate.
- **Epistemology:** what supports a claim, and where its grounds end.
- **Praxeology:** how intent becomes usable work through action and correction.
- **Axiology:** what matters, to whom, and with what consequences.

The four perspectives concern the same work. An object matters through
its relationships, a claim matters because someone may act on it, action shows
what the earlier understanding missed, and values determine which consequences
demand attention. Each perspective can correct a partial account given by
another.

The means must stay open to revision because experience can change the
understanding that justified them. A failed operation may reveal confusion
between intention and outcome. A new consequence may change the evidence needed
for reliance. A control may obscure the question it was meant to clarify.

Artifacts provide shared objects of attention across conversations and
interruptions. Roles name responsibilities that might otherwise disappear
between contributors. Methods preserve learning about how to proceed. Tools
make useful operations repeatable. Each earns its place through what it makes
possible.

Agents extend **reckoning** through analysis, criticism, planning and adaptive
action. **Judgment** is the person's situated, committed engagement with what the
work means, what matters and what they will stand behind. It shapes the work
throughout; a final acceptance is one expression of it.

## 1.3 Contributions and their standing

Root opens the means of production to the same inquiry as their outputs: a
role, method or tool can be examined and changed when it no longer serves its
purpose. Learning can improve the working environment as well as the product.

The repository gives this idea concrete form. Commitments, design and needs
remain in current artifacts, and queries expose relevant relationships.
Participants assess whether the available evidence meets the need. Continuing
ownership carries findings into repairs and contributions into a usable whole.
Changes to shared instructions must reach the tools and products that use them.

These contributions have been exercised through software development and
redevelopment. Chapter 8 traces working predecessor behavior through successor
definition, deliverable allocation and native use of the rebuilt App. It finds
preserved core meanings, functional extensions and corrections that reached
their consumers. The method's own execution approach also changed through
human steering and agent work. These are observed achievements in this case.

Their wider value remains open to examination. A record can support continuity
or become a competing body of work; a method can retain learning or repeat a
cost whose purpose has disappeared. Chapter 8 assesses both results and limits;
[Appendix E](appendix_e_development_history.md) retains the development history.
Neither the presence of a mechanism nor its removal establishes an improvement
in total effort and reliability.

## 1.4 Thesis structure

The [introduction](INTRODUCTION.md) introduces the question and its application cases. The chapters here develop
that account in four steps. Chapters 2–3 establish its precedents and
philosophical distinctions. Chapters 4–5 examine how architecture can make those
distinctions useful in work. Chapters 6–7 assess how those distinctions are
interpreted for professional practice, and the systems-engineering methods
proposed to support them. Chapters 8–10 then distinguish what the development
evidence supports from the claims that remain open.

The App and SWBPIPE cases in Chapter 8 examine particular applications. In
the App, authoring workflows makes method itself a subject of work; in
SWBPIPE, piping design tests whether shared means preserve engineering meaning,
numerical grounds and the conditions of use. Experience in either application
can show a limitation in Root's methods or framing. So application
development is part of the inquiry into the shared means, not a demonstration
whose success can be assumed from the framework.

## 1.5 Scope and limitations

This is a case followed over time, joining one developer's purpose, judgment
and steering with agent-led production and orchestration. That human contribution
is foundational to the framework and its evidence. Additional practitioners
would bring their own direction and judgment to further cases.

The evaluation separates transfer of development methods across the App and
SWBPIPE, preservation of App meaning across implementations, embedded-agent
product integration, and adoption in further settings. The first two have
observed results; the latter questions need their own evidence. Comparative
cost and reliability likewise require more than code volume or internal
consistency.

The practical argument concerns inspectable work and its consequences. The
philosophical conjecture in Appendix D preserves the argument's deeper origin;
the argument does not require assent to the conjecture. The thesis index gives
the source basis and standing.
