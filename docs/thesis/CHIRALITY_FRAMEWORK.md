# The Chirality Framework

Chirality AI Ltd. (explanatory research, revised October 2026).

**An account of professional accountability in agent-assisted work.**

This compact account can be read on its own. The
[Root thesis](README.md#reading-the-root-thesis) develops its sources,
architecture and evidence; the [shared introduction](INTRODUCTION.md) relates
it to the App and Piping theses.

---

## 1. Purpose

Chirality supports purposeful work by making relevant objects and relationships
understandable, the grounds and limits of claims inspectable, action and
correction practicable, and consequential commitments attributable. The mechanisms
that provide these conditions remain open to revision through use.

Professional accountability concerns what may be relied on, by whom, on what
grounds and under what commitment.

Three kinds of statement must remain distinct:

- **Normative commitments** identify values and responsibility boundaries the
  work chooses or is obliged to preserve.
- **Design hypotheses** propose mechanisms that might support those commitments.
- **Empirical findings** report what observation establishes under identified
  conditions, including failures, costs and limits of transfer.

A coherent design is not evidence that it works. A successful check supports
its tested claim, not every claim about the design.

## 2. The Nature of Knowledge in Professional Practice

### 2.1 Knowledge Belongs to a Knower

Information can be recorded, transmitted, copied, cited, compared and identified
without being the knowledge of any particular person. Knowledge, in this
framework's usage, is a situated achievement of a knower.

The framework does not use *knowledge* as a guarantee of truth. A person may
know wrongly, incompletely or provisionally, and may revise what they know.
This differs from accounts that reserve the word for true knowledge. The
practical argument can also be read in terms of situated understanding: evidence
and review discipline a person's understanding without transferring it into an
artifact.

`Claim` and `Warrant` identify an assertion and its grounds. They make a potential
basis for knowing open to inspection. Neither is a knower, and taken together
they do not define knowledge.

### 2.2 Configurational Multiplicity

Identical information may occasion different knowledge for different people,
or for one person in another context, purpose or time. Knowing is situated; a
variation in knowing need not imply a change in the information.

Chirality calls this **configurational multiplicity**. Schemas, taxonomies,
semantic lenses and graphs can preserve distinctions and direct inquiry, but
they do not exhaust what a person may perceive in the work. This openness does
not make interpretations equally well supported, and it does not relieve a
person of the need to answer to evidence.

### 2.3 Authentication and Accountable Reliance

Professional authentication is an attributable act concerning identified
information: the accountable person examines a defined work product and accepts
responsibility for reliance within an identified scope and purpose. The subject
must be identifiable. A specific content revision or hash can establish identity;
the applicable professional and product requirements determine the binding.

Authentication confers **accountable-reliance status** within that relationship.
It does not create knowledge, make the information true or determine what
another person must know from it. A record can be evidence of the act, but the
presence of a signature, approval field or hash does not establish that
sufficient examination or committed engagement occurred.

Judgment also shapes the question, intended use, relevant distinctions and
choice of evidence before acceptance. It is not exhausted by a final approval.

### 2.4 The Chirality of Knowledge

The framework uses *chirality* primarily to name the **accountability gap**
between externalizable information and accountable knowing. An artifact,
provenance chain or approval record is not the same as the person's knowing.
The metaphor marks this non-identity; it supplies neither a geometry of knowledge
nor a new chiral structure for every architectural distinction.

The gap is workable: information, grounds, uncertainty and attributable acts
can support a person's engagement without replacing it. Another citation or a
more capable model does not, by itself, make that person's commitment.

This gap differs from the operational `Gap`: a missing warrant or unresolved
input that further inquiry can address. The accountability gap is not a missing
field to be completed.

### 2.5 Agency, Delegation, and Accountability

An artificial agent combines a model, instructions, context and tools through a
host. It can investigate, infer, compare, propose, assess, plan, check and act
within authority, and these activities can be sophisticated, adaptive and
sustained.

The framework describes these capabilities as **reckoning**, following Smith's
distinction between reckoning and **judgment**. Judgment is the person's
situated, committed, world-involving engagement with what the work means, what
matters and what they will stand behind. It operates throughout purpose, framing
and action, not only at approval.

How professional responsibility is allocated is a different question from
capability. An agent may do extensive authorized work without occupying the
licensed professional's role. In this framework, it does not authenticate a
work product or assume duty of care. This states the allocation in the present
professional setting; it is not a proof about the possible cognition or
institutional standing of every future machine.

Greater capability can change what delegation, supervision and verification are
appropriate, but it does not itself transfer accountable judgment. Conversely,
reserving judgment does not require Agent 0 to be read-only or every ordinary
design refinement to receive a new approval.

### 2.6 Teams, Project Management, and Governance

Work requires continuity of purpose, ownership of integration and a way to
coordinate dependencies, but it need not require several agents. Roles describe
contributions; responsibilities do not prescribe a staffing chart.

In the current repository, the agent working with the owner holds Agent 0's
responsibilities. Managers and bounded executors are engaged when useful. TASK
does not delegate. An owner of work continues through ordinary repairs, within
the assignment and the host's actual permissions.

Current commitments, design and dependency conditions live with the deliverables.
Tools derive relevant relationships and observed facts. The conversation supplies
present intent; Git and PRs retain changes and reasons. A recorded dependency
does not prove readiness, and a missing edge does not prove independence.
Additional records are useful only where review, use, acceptance or recovery
needs them.

The normative, operative and evaluative perspectives concern the same work.
Commitments guide action; operations make action possible; evaluation examines
what occurred and what it supports. The relationship among them is iterative: a
finding can revise an operation, a method or an adopted criterion through the
appropriate authority. These are overlapping perspectives, not a prescribed
hierarchy of agents or a fixed sequence of approval stops. The
[shared introduction](INTRODUCTION.md) develops this relationship across the
three theses.

### 2.7 What the Framework Does and Does Not Cover

Professional reliance involves agency, organization and authority as well as
information. A person may need to know who acted, what changed, what evidence
was examined and which limitations remain.

The framework addresses these relationships. It neither claims a complete theory
of human life nor establishes that one implementation is sufficient for regulated
practice. Legal and professional requirements apply in their own jurisdictions
and contexts. Product contracts and actual examination must establish the
conditions for a particular use.

### 2.8 Normalization Table

| Element | Kind | Contribution or standing | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI agent | Artificial operative participant | Reckoning and action within authority | Does not acquire professional responsibility from capability |
| Accountable professional | Person under applicable professional obligations | Judgment, supervision and attributable reliance | Approval does not guarantee correctness |
| Claim | Assertion about a subject | Makes content available for examination | Plausibility is not a warrant |
| Warrant | Grounds relevant to a claim | Supports assessment within its actual scope | Citation presence does not establish relevance or adequacy |
| Ruling | Attributable decision | Settles a choice within the issuer's authority | Does not manufacture evidence or resolve a factual conflict by decree |
| Work product | Artifact used for a purpose | Contains or points to relevant content and grounds | Completion and technical validation do not establish acceptance |

## 3. What the Architecture Must Provide

### 3.1 The Generative Principle

A person needs access to the material grounds and limits of the reliance they
are asked to accept. Architecture can make these easier to find, examine and
relate to the actual work. That is a design obligation within this framework,
not a guarantee that the person will understand or examine them adequately.

Inspectability concerns useful access, and it does not require a separate record
of every activity. A current artifact, a test result or an existing change
record may already supply what is needed. Repeated representations can obscure
decisions that matter and draw attention away from the work.

<a id="32-the-four-pillars"></a>

### 3.2 The Four Perspectives

| Perspective | Enduring question | What it can expose |
|---|---|---|
| **Ontology** | What kind of thing or relationship is this? | Confusion between a proposal, an operation, a result and acceptance |
| **Epistemology** | What supports the claim, and what are its limits? | Unsupported inference, stale evidence or inadequate examination |
| **Praxeology** | How does intent become usable work through action and correction? | Unowned integration, blocked independent work or uncertain outcomes |
| **Axiology** | What matters, to whom, and with what consequences? | A narrow optimization that neglects safety, agency or useful attention |

The questions correct one another. A failed recovery attempt can reveal an
outcome that the ontology left out. A newly identified consequence can require
different verification. Experience can show that a supposed requirement was an
implementation habit. The four questions need no fixed document set, review
sequence or permanent process topology.

Control costs belong inside the evaluation. Attention, delay, maintainability
and user agency matter alongside reliability. A control earns its place when
its contribution justifies those costs. Applicable obligations and adopted
constraints govern what can be accepted; negotiable preferences support choices
among permissible alternatives. Uncertain premises require examination rather
than a favourable ranking. A local improvement can impose costs on construction,
operation, maintenance or another person's work. No universal score settles
these relationships. Judgment concerns which consequences matter in the actual
work and which mechanisms serve its obligations.

### 3.3 The Epistemic Architecture

Epistemology receives particular attention because plausible outputs can conceal
weak grounds, but it does not make the other perspectives subordinate to it.
Purpose determines which uncertainty matters; representation determines what can
be examined; practice determines whether examination and correction can affect
the result.

#### 3.3.1 The Epistemic Ontology

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | An assertion about a subject |
| **Warrant** | Relevant grounds, such as a source, observation, calculation, test or argument; model confidence alone is not a warrant |
| **Status** | An explicitly scoped classification useful to examination; not a substitute for the grounds |
| **Gap** | Missing grounds or information, with its effect on the current work made visible |
| **Conflict** | Incompatible claims whose scope, sources or meanings require examination |
| **Ruling** | A decision by the relevant authority where a choice is needed |

FACT, ASSUMPTION, PROPOSAL and TBD remain useful labels in contexts that adopt
them. They are not an exhaustive account of knowledge or a universal requirement
to annotate prose. An observation requires context; an assumption remains
provisional; a proposal is not an adopted commitment; TBD identifies missing
information. A ruling by a person can select a course of action under
uncertainty without turning the uncertain premise into a fact.

#### 3.3.2 Mechanisms and Their Enforcement Limits

Provenance, explicit uncertainty, conflict surfacing and proportional examination
are useful mechanisms, and how they are implemented varies with the consumer and
the consequences. Some are instructions; others are schema or runtime checks. A
check for a source field establishes that a field exists, not that the source
supports the claim. A prohibition on invention does not ensure that fabrication
never occurs.

#### 3.3.3 Evidence and Acceptance

Evidence may be strengthened or contradicted, or it may become inapplicable.
Review has a subject, method and scope. Acceptance is an authorized act
concerning an identified subject and purpose; professional authentication has
additional obligations in its applicable setting. These relationships do not
form a universal progression.
[Appendix E](appendix_e_development_history.md) discusses how they were
historically combined in a warrant lifecycle.

The practical questions are: what grounds support this claim; what examination
has addressed it; and what reliance, if any, has a person accepted? No general
deliverable status is required to answer them. Authentication does not automatically
release a product, approve later changes or broaden its scope of use.

### 3.4 The Fractal Property

The same questions can recur at several scales: one numerical result, a work
product, an application or the agent-assisted work itself. When they recur, they
call for attention to the particular subject, not a prescribed set of forms.

Recurrence is valuable when it reveals a distinction that matters at the scale
being examined. It is not proof of completeness or a reason to reproduce the
same forms throughout a repository.

### 3.5 The Generative Relationship to Systems Engineering

Systems engineering supplies candidate methods for preserving these distinctions:
configuration management can identify a subject; verification and validation
can test specified claims; interface design can constrain operations; human
factors can assess how a person interprets and controls the work.

The perspectives guide how these methods are selected and evaluated, but they do
not uniquely derive them. Each mechanism requires a reason tied to the work,
then evidence of its adequacy and cost. Changes in model capability can make
some supervision mechanisms unnecessary while leaving the underlying
responsibility unchanged.

## 4. What the Architecture Cannot Do

### 4.1 The Boundary

Architecture can organize information, perform operations and preserve evidence,
but it cannot perform a particular person's knowing or commitment on their
behalf. Neither a complete form nor a passing check establishes adequate
professional engagement. The person still interprets what matters and stands
behind the reliance they accept.

### 4.2 The Human Responsibility

An agent can extend reckoning across investigation, design, implementation,
examination and repair, but the current professional allocation retains judgment
and duty of care with the accountable person. This boundary grants no new
permission and imposes no fixed agent hierarchy.

Systems must not attribute an approval that the person did not give. Where an
approval is required, its scope and subject must remain clear. The retained
product boundary K-AUTH-1 applies to its adopted consumers; this framework
explains the reason without changing that contract.

## 5. The Central Distinction

Useful output and accountable reliance are related but different achievements.
A productive tool can improve quality and reduce effort. An accountable work
arrangement also enables a person to examine what matters and identify what
they will stand behind.

These aims can support each other: removing redundant records can improve both
throughput and examination. Conversely, faster output can increase the review
burden or conceal uncertainty. Neither the volume of administration nor the speed
of production alone establishes the quality of the arrangement.

---

## 6. The Axiom

*To know what you know, why you know it, and why you know why.*

The first clause directs attention to the subject of knowing, and the second
asks for its grounds. The third opens the framework's own grounds and
commitments to examination. It invites reflexive inquiry, not an infinite chain
of required records.

So Chirality must distinguish its own commitments, hypotheses and findings.
Its arrangements must answer to evidence about usable results, failures, recovery
and total effort. A coherent alternative or successful local correction cannot
establish superiority in every application or professional setting.

---

## 7. The Closing Principle

AI can extend professional reckoning without inheriting professional judgment
or responsibility merely by becoming more capable. People can grant extensive
authority for action while retaining consequential commitments.

The framework's coherence lies in preserving relevant distinctions and
responsibilities while examining how best to support them. Its implementations
remain open to challenge, and their adequacy must be assessed through actual
work, including the costs they impose and the failures they permit.
