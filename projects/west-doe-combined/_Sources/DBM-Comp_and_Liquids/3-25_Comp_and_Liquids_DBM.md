## Facility Overview

The 03-25 facility receives Doe field sour wellstream fluids, separates gas, condensate, and produced water, compresses sour gas, dehydrates compressed sour gas, and forwards process streams to the correct downstream facilities. Compressed sour gas is exported to the 04-25 Deep Cut Gas Plant inlet system. Raw 03-25 condensate is routed to 04-25 for MPFS/stabilization. Stabilized condensate then returns to the 03-25 Liquids Hub for storage, non-regenerative caustic mercaptan treating, product handling, and custody-transfer interface to the NRM NEBC Connector.

The Liquids Hub also receives stabilized condensate from 04-25, 06-29 Canlin, and future third-party sources. The liquids hub stores sour and product condensate, treats C5+ condensate for mercaptan reduction, manages produced water and H2O2 treatment, provides VRU service for tank vapours, and supports truck loading. Sales condensate LACT equipment is third-party NRM scope; the 03-25 scope includes the facility-side tie-in up to and including the tie-in flange.

# SEC-13 - Control System Basis

## Control System Overview

The control system provides centralized monitoring and control for the 03-25 Compressor Station and Liquids Hub. The Basic Plant Control System (BPCS) is the primary process control system for facility process inputs and outputs, except for compression unit controls, which are handled by standalone Unit Control Systems and integrated for monitoring and alarming.

## Topology and Networks

The control system uses a multi-level network architecture. The Process Control Network connects Level 1 industrial equipment to Level 2 and Level 3 networking equipment and servers. Server-to-server communication occurs on the PCN, with outside access restricted through an Industrial Demilitarized Zone. Field-mounted PCN switches use dual uplinks with link aggregation to reduce interruptions.

The I/O Network is segregated from other networks and is dedicated to I/O node communication. It shall use redundant Ethernet communications with Parallel Redundancy Protocol. Multiple I/O networks may be required depending on final topology.

The IDMZ and Enterprise Network are client/IT-OT interfaces. The IDMZ isolates the industrial production control network from enterprise networks. Final IDMZ layout, policies, firewall rules, IP addressing, and VLAN segmentation remain detailed-design and client IT/OT coordination items.

## Servers and Virtualization

The control system uses at least two physical servers for primary server functions. Servers shall be designed for fault-tolerant operation and host multiple virtual machines according to application requirements. The primary host is located in the control room; the secondary host is located in the low-voltage MCC room.

The virtual-machine set includes FactoryTalk Directory, primary and secondary FactoryTalk HMI servers, primary and secondary FactoryTalk Alarm and Event servers, primary and secondary FactoryTalk Data servers, engineering workstation server, operator workstation server, and historian server functions as defined by the KTY-13-06 artifacts. Hardware model, hypervisor, memory, CPU, storage, power supply, VM allocation, failover triggers, and VLAN assignments remain TBD.

## BPCS and Remote I/O

The BPCS controller platform is Allen-Bradley ControlLogix 1756-L8x series. The BPCS is simplex in the current basis, with a dedicated rack or chassis for process controller and network interface modules and separate racks for I/O modules. Controller quantity and sizing will be determined during detailed design.

Remote I/O uses Allen-Bradley Flex5000 I/O. RIO connects to the I/O Network via PRP network configuration. Each remote distribution centre includes a Remote I/O node to reduce wiring between the control system and remote distribution centres. Remote I/O nodes may also support building exhaust fan and heater controls.

All process and safety devices associated with the BPCS system are wired to the nearest Remote I/O control panel where practical. This includes ESD buttons, fire detection, LEL detection, H2S detection, and other process-related safety items.

## Unit Control Systems and Package Interfaces

Compression unit controls are excluded from direct BPCS control and are handled by standalone Unit Control Systems. Unit Control System values and general alarms are replicated to the BPCS for operation at the primary operations console. Final package data maps, permissive logic, trip interfaces, and alarm priorities shall be resolved during vendor integration.

Package and third-party interfaces shall use defined communication protocols and hardwired signals according to criticality. # SEC-14 - Instrumentation, Fire and Gas Detection, and Shutdown Interfaces

## Instrumentation and Safety Interface Basis

Instrumentation, fire and gas detection, and shutdown interfaces shall support safe operation of the 03-25 Compressor Station and Liquids Hub. Fire, gas, H2S, LEL, methyl mercaptan, ESD, and unit shutdown interfaces shall be coordinated with the BPCS, Remote I/O architecture, package controls, electrical area classification, and HAZOP/SIL outcomes.

## Detector Manufacturer

Fire, gas, and H2S detection shall use MSA/General Monitors as the approved manufacturer basis. Final model selection, environmental ratings, certification details, range, mounting, and accessories remain detailed-design items.

## LEL, H2S, Methyl Mercaptan, and Fire Detection

LEL, H2S, methyl mercaptan, and fire detection devices shall be placed according to process hazards, building layouts, ventilation, equipment spacing, truck loading, tankage, caustic/DSO systems, VRU, flare/vent interfaces, compressor buildings, TEG package, and operator access areas. Detector quantity, tag list, set points, voting logic, placement, and calibration requirements remain TBD pending detailed design and safety studies.

Detector signals associated with the BPCS system shall be wired to the nearest Remote I/O control panel where practical. This follows the control-system basis that safety devices including fire detection, LEL detection, H2S detection, and ESD buttons are wired to nearby Remote I/O to reduce field wiring.

## Audible and Visual Alarms

Audible and visual signals shall differentiate emergency conditions. The current source basis identifies red beacon association with Fire Detection, LEL Detection, and ESD activation. Horn tone selection shall distinguish ESD trip scenarios, including fire, LEL, H2S, and ESD trip conditions. Final alarm philosophy, tone mapping, beacon layout, and operator notification logic shall be confirmed during detailed design.

## Emergency Shutdown Pushbuttons

ESD pushbuttons shall be located outside all building exterior doors. Quantity and building-by-building placement remain TBD until the final building list and layout are frozen.

| ESD Item | Current Basis |
|---|---|
| Location | Outside all building exterior doors |
| Wiring | To nearest Remote I/O control panel where practical |
| BPCS/RIO platform | Allen-Bradley ControlLogix BPCS with Flex5000 RIO over PRP |
| Visual alarm association | Red beacon for ESD activation |
| Enclosure/classification/SIL | TBD by detailed design, HAZOP, and SIL assessment |

ESD pushbutton activation shall initiate the defined emergency shutdown function. Facility-level, zone-level, package-level, reset, bypass, and permissive logic remain to be defined in the cause-and-effect matrix and shutdown philosophy.

## Unit Shutdown Interfaces

Unit shutdowns for compressor packages, VRUs, TEG package, condensate treating, produced-water systems, truck loading, and utility interfaces shall be coordinated between package PLCs, BPCS, RIO, ESD logic, and electrical systems. Compression Unit Control Systems remain standalone but shall replicate required values and alarms to the BPCS. Final trip lists, shutdown levels, cause-and-effect actions, and reset responsibilities remain detailed-design deliverables.

# SEC-15 - Environmental, Regulatory, Specifications, Codes, and Standards

## Environmental and Regulatory Basis

The current DBM basis relies on facility electrification and current permit status for the 03-25 Compressor Station and Liquids Hub. The BC environmental assessment exclusion is based on facility electrification. Liquids hub permitting has been granted through BCER application 100120203. Further permit amendments are required for the truck rack and sour-water treating scope.

## BC Water Regulations

The Province of British Columbia owns water resources. Project water use, watercourse interaction, diversion, discharge, produced-water handling, stormwater management, and related activities shall comply with applicable BC water legislation and regulator requirements. The current KTY identifies the regulatory basis but does not close every act, permit, trigger, or deliverable; detailed regulatory review remains required before final issue.

## Consultation and Notification

Consultation and notification shall be completed in accordance with the BCER Consultation and Notification Manual before BCER application milestones where applicable. The current source set does not fully resolve manual version, responsible party, affected-party list, trigger register, or submission deliverables. These shall be confirmed by the regulatory team.

## Waste Management Permit

A waste management permit is required. The application is expected to be ready to post at the start of construction. The specific regulation, application format, submission agency workflow, and required supporting attachments remain TBD in the current source set.
