# SEC-01 - Project and Document Basis

## Purpose and Applicability

This Design Basis Memorandum establishes the current design basis for the West Doe Deepcut expansion, facility identifier 04-25. It is the governing basis-of-design document for the Tourmaline West Doe 300 MMSCFD Deepcut Gas Plant Expansion and is intended to define the facility identity, project objectives, scope limits, interface basis, construction responsibility, and terminology used by the downstream engineering sections.

The published facility shall be referred to as the West Doe Deepcut expansion. It is a largely greenfield deep cut sour gas processing facility at LSD 04-25-80-15W6, located approximately 22.2 km north of Dawson Creek, British Columbia. The expansion is part of the broader West Doe Complex, which also includes the existing NorthRiver Midstream West Doe Gas Plant equipment at LSD 2-25-80-15W6 and 3-25-80-15W6, and the 03-25 West Doe Compressor Station and Liquids Hub.

This section provides the document and project basis only. Process design values, equipment duties, safeguards, discipline design criteria, utility demands, regulatory requirements, and detailed package requirements are governed by the applicable later DBM sections. Where later sections publish section-specific values for a system or package, those section-specific values govern the detailed design basis for that system or package.

## Project Objectives

The project objective is to provide a deep cut sour gas processing expansion capable of processing contracted sour raw gas, condensate, and water volumes from Tourmaline and NorthRiver Midstream, while integrating with existing and new sales gas and liquids infrastructure.

The 04-25 expansion basis includes a largely greenfield 300 MMSCFD deep cut sour gas processing facility with NGL treating, condensate stabilization, product storage, and supporting utilities. The broader West Doe Complex also includes the 03-25 brownfield compressor station and liquids hub, which provides an 80 MMSCFD compressor station and a condensate storage and treating hub with produced water treating and storage.

The design objectives include:

- process sour raw gas through inlet separation, compression, sweetening, dehydration, cryogenic C3+ recovery, and sales gas compression;
- recover C3+ liquids using a cryogenic turbo-expansion process;
- stabilize inlet liquids and recover stabilization vapours for recompression and comingling with inlet gas;
- compress and sequester recovered acid gas in an active off-site disposal reservoir;
- provide NGL treating, NGL dehydration, NGL storage, condensate stabilization, and product handling consistent with the current-scope DBM basis;
- minimize energy consumption and emissions through electrification, electric-driven compression, efficient compression, selective MDEA amine treating, process-gas TEG dehydration, heat integration, BAHX-based cryogenic processing, multi-temperature heat medium service, and acid gas sequestration;
- maintain clear independent metering and mass-balance accountability for facility interfaces and any streams integrated between facilities.

The UltraTEF cryogenic process is the stated deep cut technology basis and carries an expected propane recovery of 99+ percent. This performance statement is an overview-level basis; detailed recovery, product, and operating cases are governed in the process and data-reference sections.

## Process and Commercial Basis

Prior to processing at the 04-25 facility, raw sour inlet gas is expected to have undergone off-site separation, free water and hydrocarbon liquid separation, compression, and dehydration into the plant inlet gathering system. Sour natural gas may partially condense in the inlet pipeline. At the facility inlet, condensed inlet liquids are separated and stabilized for further processing, while stabilization vapours are recovered and recompressed to the inlet gas stream.

The sour plant inlet gas is compressed, sweetened, dehydrated, and processed to recover C3+ hydrocarbons as a liquid. Sweet sales gas is compressed to pipeline. Recovered acid gas, consisting of H2S and some CO2, is compressed and disposed to an active NorthRiver Midstream disposal well through a new pipeline.

The commercial and product disposition basis is:

## Existing-Facility Interfaces and Metering

The design basis assumes very limited equipment or utility sharing between the existing facility and the 04-25 expansion unless sharing is explicitly stated. Significant direct integration of the existing facility with the expansion is not part of the basis except where defined by a specific interface.

The existing acid gas disposal well/reservoir is shared between facilities. Incremental acid gas volume may require modifications at 02-25 for acid gas injection scope; that modification requirement is to be confirmed during detailed engineering.

Power and utilities, specifically fuel gas and instrument air, are shared between 03-25 and 04-25. Associated piping, electrical, instrumentation, and controls are included in the shared-utility interface basis.

The sales gas pipeline to the Enbridge meter station is a key facility interface. Metering systems for both facilities shall remain independent. Any stream integrated between facilities shall be independently metered so the mass balance for each facility remains accurate.

If overall inlet capacity is reduced below nameplate, Tourmaline and NorthRiver Midstream are to optimize during detailed engineering whether existing-facility compressed inlet gas is processed in the existing facility or routed to the expansion. That future make-up gas scenario is not a confirmed operating case for normal design; if used, the stream shall be independently metered and treated as a receipt to the expansion facility and a disposition from the existing facility mass balance.

# SEC-04 - Inlet, Separation, Stabilization, and Stabilizer Overheads Basis

## Purpose and Process Boundary

This section defines the West Doe Deepcut expansion front-end process basis from the facility inlet through inlet separation, inlet/TEG heat exchange, medium-pressure flash/feed separation, inlet stabilization, and stabilizer overheads compression. The section starts at the first aboveground flange within the 04-25 lease boundary where pipeline scope transfers to the facility fabricator/construction contractor. It ends at the interfaces from this process block to amine treating, NGL mercaptan treating, VRU, fuel gas, heat medium, flare, drains, and downstream utility or product systems.

The process arrangement receives inlet gas and condensed hydrocarbon liquids, separates sour gas, sour raw condensate, and sour water, heats and flashes condensed liquids through the MPFF and stabilizer flash/feed systems, stabilizes C5+/NGL liquid product, and compresses flashed/stabilizer overhead vapours through the stabilizer overheads compressor (SOC) for recycle to the amine plant inlet.

## Functional Configuration

### Inlet Pipeline and Facility Inlet

The 04-25 facility receives gas from multiple upstream sources, including the 06-29 Compressor Station, Vermillion Mica Compressor Station, 03-18 Sunrise, Doe 02-11 acid gas, and the 03-25 Compressor Station. The 03-25 stream is high-pressure wet sour gas with a stated delivery pressure range of 4,482 to 5,516 kPag (650 to 800 psig). The relationship between that stream-specific pressure range and the general Inlet Gathering pressure table remains to be confirmed.

Final inlet pipeline configuration is to be determined during detailed design after the gathering system design is complete. Current basis assumes tie-ins to the common plant gathering pipeline occur in the upstream field. Any alternate future gas supply is assumed to enter the facility on an independent pipeline, or alternatively to be commingled with appropriate metering offsite.

The plant inlet pipeline gas enters through one 610 mm OD (24 in.) pig receiver on a dedicated structural steel non-enclosed skid. Skid-mounted isolation valves or ESDVs upstream of the pig receiver shall be full port for pigging. Barred tees shall prevent pigs from entering facility piping. Low-pressure fuel gas is provided downstream of the manual isolation valve for sweet-gas purge of the pig receiver before opening, and the pig receiver vents to the HP flare system.

### Inlet Separation

The inlet separator system separates vapour, light liquid, and heavy liquid as sour natural gas, sour raw condensate, and sour water. The upstream pipeline holdup volume is estimated at 67 m3, and slug processing over six hours by downstream MPFF, SOC, and stabilizer equipment is assumed. Downstream MPFF and stabilizer systems must be operational to process two-phase inlet gas that condenses as it approaches the facility.

Inlet piping from the rack to the inlet separator packages shall account for normal gas/liquid distribution to each separator and minimize uneven distribution. The design relies on piping symmetry, with detailed distribution review to be completed during detailed engineering.

## Controls, Protection, and Safeguards

The facility inlet includes a full-port piggable ESDV with position transmitters on the pig receiver inlet skid. The inlet separator shut ESDV pressure shutdown basis is 1360 psig; delivery point ESDV pressure shutdown is TBC. If inlet pipeline MAOP exceeds facility inlet design pressure, a HIPPS may be required to protect the inlet separators. HIPPS requirements, independent pressure control/shutdown configuration, and shutdown setpoints are to be confirmed during detailed engineering. Shutdown setpoints must account for high-pressure gas volume between plant inlet ESDVs and inlet separator inlet PCVs.

Inlet separator drive gas is used when separator pressure cannot be maintained at normal conditions. The source is sales gas immediately upstream of the sales gas splitter, with inlet compressor discharge as an alternate. Drive gas pressure maintains inlet separator pressure above the downstream MPFF drum so liquids can continue to be processed. Because drive gas enters upstream of plant inlet gas meters, it must be separately metered to each inlet separator package. Plant recycle may be required during start-up if the sales pipeline will not accept temporary total-sulphur deviations; the cryogenic plant can then cool down and produce near-spec gas internally before sales. LPG recycle from storage for cooling support is obsolete under current NGL/depropanizer-retired scope and is not a current design claim.

MPFF, stabilizer flash/feed separator, and stabilizer tower overhead systems are pressure regulated to the appropriate SOC suction stages. Relief and blowdown from the stabilizer flash/feed separator and stabilizer tower route to the HP flare. MPFF and SOC packages include automated blowdown provisions. SOC package blowdown is controlled by the compressor unit control panel, while SOC suction-header blowdowns upstream of side-stream suction PCVs are wired and controlled by the balance-of-plant control system. Specific locations for SOC suction-header blowdown valves are to be reviewed during detailed engineering.

SOC suction pressure control valves are required for each side-stream service in addition to first-stage suction inlet pressure control. These valves shall allow <2 psid differential pressure when 100% open, use ET-type control valves as the preferred type, and fail closed. SOC suction scrubbers are provided upstream of each stage, sized for off-design operating conditions. Capacity range and the 0.61 SG inlet-liquid-density assumption are to be reviewed during detailed engineering. SOC suction scrubbers shall include mist pads, not mesh/vane assemblies, and shall be sized with a maximum imperial K factor of 0.25 plus pressure deration.

# SEC-05 - Compression and Acid Gas Handling Basis

## Purpose and Scope

This section defines the process design basis for compression and acid gas handling systems in the West Doe Deepcut expansion. The section covers the inlet/sales multi-service compressors, the sales gas booster compressor, acid gas injection compression, and the acid gas disposal well interface. Cryogenic recovery, product treating, product storage, and plant-wide utilities are addressed only where they form direct interfaces to these compression and disposal systems.

## Compression Configuration

The compression system is arranged as four related service groups:

| Service group | Configuration | Primary role | Sparing basis |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Inlet / sales multi-service compressors | Five identical parallel multi-service reciprocating packages | Compress sour inlet gas to the amine treating inlet and compress sweet sales gas in two sales stages | Five x 20%; no installed spare package |
| Sales gas booster compressor | One separable reciprocating compressor package | Boost treated sales gas to pipeline delivery pressure | One x 100%; no installed spare package |
| Acid gas injection compressors | Two identical acid gas compressor packages, each designed for 100% capacity; one spare compressor included | Compress amine acid gas for injection/disposal | Two x 100% operating basis plus one spare; potential three x 50% alternative remains TBD |
| Acid Gas Disposal Well interface | Existing nearby disposal well/reservoir shared with the existing 02-25 facility | Receive compressed acid gas for injection | Well pressure characteristics and shared-facility modification scope remain TBD |

The inlet/sales compressor packages are multi-service induction-motor-driven separable reciprocating compressor packages. Each package provides one sour inlet gas compression stage and two sweet sales gas compression stages on a common compressor frame. This arrangement is intended to balance loading across the process plant, limit the production loss associated with one package being down to approximately 20%, and improve flow stability compared with a conventional split inlet-compressor/sales-compressor arrangement.

The sales gas booster compressor is a single sweet-gas reciprocating compressor package downstream of the sales compressor units. It receives sweet sales gas and delivers high-pressure gas to the downstream high-pressure sales gas coalescer and splitter for delivery to the TC sales gas pipeline.

The acid gas injection compression system compresses the acid gas stream from amine regeneration for disposal. The current detailed design basis is two 100% acid gas compressor packages with one spare compressor included; a possible three x 50% arrangement remains unresolved and is not treated as confirmed design basis.

## Inlet / Sales Compressor Basis

The inlet service receives sour gas from the inlet/TEG heat exchanger, combined with West Doe sour gas from the 03-25 Compressor Station, and compresses it for delivery to downstream amine sweetening. # SEC-06 - Treating, Dehydration, and Cryogenic Recovery Basis

## Purpose and Scope

This section defines the process basis for the West Doe Deepcut expansion gas treating, process-gas dehydration, molecular-sieve dehydration, mercury removal, and UltraTEF cryogenic recovery systems. The section covers the current design basis for:

- Amine treating for removal of H2S and partial CO2 slip control.
- Process-gas TEG dehydration downstream of amine treating and upstream of molecular-sieve dehydration.
- Molecular-sieve dehydration, regeneration, dry-out provisions, mercury removal, and downstream dust filtration.
- UltraTEF cryogenic recovery, including the cold separator, J-T operating path, turbo-expander path, BAHX, methanol injection, propane absorber, deethanizer, reflux system, reboilers, and associated pumps/coolers.

Product storage, downstream NGL treating, downstream NGL dehydration, sales-gas delivery, acid-gas compression/disposal, plant-wide utilities, and general controls architecture are addressed elsewhere except where they form a direct process interface for the systems in this section.

## Functional Configuration

High-pressure sour gas from upstream separation and compression is treated in the amine system, cooled and dehydrated in the process-gas TEG system, dried to cryogenic service quality in the molecular-sieve system, protected against mercury and particulate carryover, and then routed to the UltraTEF cryogenic unit for NGL recovery and sales-gas production.

The current processing sequence is:

1. High-pressure gas enters the amine inlet gas filter/coalescers.
2. Filtered gas flows upward through the amine absorbers, where MDEA-based solvent removes H2S and some CO2.
3. Sweet, water-saturated gas exits the amine absorbers and flows to the TEG inlet cooler and TEG inlet filter/coalescer.
4. TEG contactors dehydrate the gas before it enters the inlet/TEG dehydration cross-exchanger.
5. The gas enters molecular-sieve inlet filter/coalescers, molecular-sieve adsorbers, molecular-sieve dust filtration, the mercury recovery unit, and MRU dust filtration.
6. Water-free, particulate-free, mercury-protected gas flows to the UltraTEF cryogenic unit.
7. The UltraTEF unit operates either in expander mode for normal high-recovery service or in J-T mode for start-up/off-design service.

## Amine Treating Basis

### Process Description

The amine treating unit removes H2S from the high-pressure inlet gas and allows controlled CO2 slip using an MDEA-based solvent. High-pressure inlet gas from the first stage inlet-service compressor aftercooler outlet is routed through amine inlet coalescers before entering two 50% amine absorbers in upflow gas service. Lean amine contacts the gas counter-currently, absorbing H2S and some CO2. The sweet gas leaves the absorber overheads through demisting equipment and flows to the downstream TEG inlet cooling and dehydration system.

Rich amine from the absorber bottoms is level controlled to the amine flash drum. Flash gas is pressure controlled to the stabilizer overheads compressor first-stage suction header. Hydrocarbon liquid separated in the flash drum is manually drained to the amine slop tank. Rich amine is filtered, preheated in lean/rich amine exchangers, and fed to Tray 3 of the amine regenerator, where acid gas is stripped with steam generated in the amine reboiler. Acid gas overheads are partially condensed, separated in the reflux accumulator, and routed to the acid-gas compressor first-stage suction scrubber.

## Process-Gas TEG Dehydration Basis

### Process Description

Sweet, hot, water-saturated natural gas from the amine absorbers is cooled in the TEG inlet cooler. The cooled gas blends with cooled water-saturated regeneration gas from the process-gas molecular-sieve regeneration gas scrubber overheads. The combined two-phase stream flows through the TEG inlet filter/coalescer, where free water, aerosols, contaminants, amine carryover, and particulates are removed before the gas enters the TEG contactor.

The TEG contactor removes water from the gas by contact with triethylene glycol. Dehydrated gas flows downstream to the inlet/TEG dehydration cross-exchanger and then to molecular-sieve dehydration. Rich TEG is separated from flash gas in the TEG flash drum, filtered, heated in the lean/rich TEG exchanger, stripped in the still and stripping columns, regenerated in a BKU-type reboiler, cooled, and recirculated.

## UltraTEF Cryogenic Recovery Basis

### Process Description

The UltraTEF cryogenic unit recovers propane and heavier hydrocarbons from molecular-sieve-dried gas while producing sales gas. The unit is designed around a six-pass BAHX, cold separator, J-T valve path, turbo-expander/compressor path, propane absorber, deethanizer, deethanizer reflux system, and reboiler. Expander mode is the normal operating mode at design conditions. J-T mode is used for start-up and off-design operation when the turbo-expander is shut down.

Only one design point is considered for BAHX and turbo-expander design, based on summer conditions. Winter operation is expected performance and must be checked for hydraulics in towers, pumps, and deethanizer reboiler service because of higher operating pressures and increased liquid flows.

### NGL Mercaptan Treating Equipment and Utilities

All caustic treating equipment is installed indoors because caustic freezing and crystallization are risks. Caustic-containing equipment is segregated into the Mercaptan Treating Unit building or immediately adjacent area. The building includes water safety showers; shower quantity and location remain TBD. Shower activation must provide a discrete control-room alert.

The caustic NGL contactor mixes NGL and circulating caustic solution to extract mercaptans. Sweetened NGL flows downstream to filtration and water wash. The NGL contactor caustic outlet filters are independent vessels from the contactor and are provided as 2 x 100%.

Make-up water, if required during upset operation, is supplied from the process water storage tank. Final make-up water routing remains to be confirmed by detailed engineering.

Downstream water wash and coalescing filtration dilute and remove entrained caustic solution from treated NGL to protect the molecular sieve. Water wash recycle pumps are single-stage vertical inline centrifugal pumps with single mechanical seals and 2 x 100% sparing. Water wash is recycled internally and drained on flow control to produced water tanks.

The pressurized caustic drain drum receives high-pressure caustic drains. NGL vapours from the drain drum flow to the stabilizer overheads compressor first-stage suction; remaining caustic solution is level controlled to the spent caustic storage tank. The drain drum is heated and insulated, includes a demister element, and is sized with K factor less than 0.2.

The spent caustic tank is atmospheric, heated, insulated, truck-out capable, connected to the incinerator header, protected against backflash with a flame arrestor, and blanketed with low-pressure fuel gas. The fresh caustic tank is atmospheric, heated, insulated, truck-in capable, provided with fuel-gas blanket, and not connected to the VRU header to avoid contamination by VRU vapours. Fresh caustic tank design specific gravity is 1.75 TBC.

The DSO storage tank is atmospheric, heated, insulated, truck-out capable, connected to the incinerator header, protected against backflash with a flame arrestor, and blanketed with low-pressure fuel gas. DSO tank design specific gravity is 1.75 TBC. Pumping DSO to C5+ product is subject to detailed-engineering review.

### VRU Scrubbing, Cooling, Blowdown, and Controls

Each VRU is provided with a two-phase suction scrubber upstream of each compression stage. Suction scrubber gas capacity must consider off-design compressor operation, including low compression ratio, high package capacity, high suction pressure, and low discharge pressure during initial startup. Capacity range is to be evaluated in detailed engineering.

VRU suction scrubber inlet liquid density is assumed to be at least 0.61 SG and is to be reviewed. Scrubbers include mist pads, not mesh/vane assemblies, and are sized conservatively for possible hydrocarbon condensation at cooler outlet. The sizing basis is K factor 0.25 maximum, Imperial, plus de-rating for operating pressure.

The heavy VRU composition creates hydrocarbon condensation risk. Cooler and temperature-control requirements are to be reviewed. Base design includes manual warm-air recirculation louvers; automatic warm-air recirculation is to be considered to reduce condensation risk. There is no bundle temperature control provision on the VRU.

| Parameter | First stage, deg C | Second stage, deg C |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cooler discharge temperature | 48.9 | 60.0 |
| Dewpoint temperature | 52.7 | 55.8 |

The first-stage cooler discharge temperature is below the dewpoint temperature, so condensation is expected at the first-stage intercooler outlet. Suction scrubber sizing and cooler controls must account for this condition.

VRU package blowdown valves are included and route blowdown to the LP flare under compressor unit control-panel control. Glycol still vapours and stripping gas are recovered using the VRU package after water is condensed upstream. BTEX is mixed with stripping gas and recompressed to the plant inlet; recovered liquids are pumped to produced water tanks. Water and C5+ storage tank vapours are compressed and recovered by the VRU package.

The capacity-control recycle valve routes from second-stage discharge to first-stage suction and is included to maintain first-stage suction pressure. It is sized for 100% flow at minimum driver speed and lowest operating discharge pressure.

The make-up/blanket gas regulator maintains minimum suction pressure at maximum turndown and is supplied from the low-pressure fuel gas system.

The VRU suction header to flare valve is a V-ball control valve that bypasses the VRU system directly to LP flare when the vapour recovery units are not operational. It operates on VRU suction pressure. A check valve on the discharge of the control valve prevents backflow to tanks and must have less than 0.25 psid pressure drop at design conditions. The valve is located in the pipe rack; the header must free-drain without traps or pockets and slope toward the LP flare knock-out.

The VRU suction header to flare valve capacity remains to be confirmed but must at minimum handle the maximum VRU flow condition. The design intent is to minimize the risk of atmospheric tank EPRVs or PVRVs opening. Pressure measurement must include first-stage suction pressure and tank-farm VRU header pressure so VRU and flare PCV controls maintain tank vapour-space pressure despite suction-header pressure drop. Minimizing suction piping fittings and length is critical.

If a compressor package is taken down for maintenance, a manual sweet-gas purge connection is provided at first-stage suction immediately downstream of the inlet manual isolation valve.

The West Doe Deepcut expansion shall be designed so that plant equipment can be operated, isolated, maintained, and packaged without compromising safe pressure containment or controllable facility operation. ## Isolation Requirements

### Multiple Parallel Packages

Multiple parallel packages shall generally be isolated on a unit basis so that the entire unit can be isolated and removed from service for maintenance while remaining units continue to operate. This basis applies to examples including inlet separators, inlet/sales compressors, stabilizer overheads compressors, acid gas compressors, medium-pressure flash feed, inlet stabilizers, mole sieve inlet coalescer/filter service, and amine inlet coalescer/filter service.

For multiple parallel packages:

- additional isolation valves may be included case by case to limit the amount of fluid requiring disposal during maintenance;
- no block valves shall be provided on PSV inlet or outlet piping, and the entire unit shall be drained and depressurized for PSV maintenance;
- remote sensing pilots shall include a root valve at the sensing point so tubing can be isolated and removed for maintenance;
- inlet pressure control valves generally do not require isolation unless they are frequently maintained and subject to seat wear;
- actuated inlet shutoff valves that actuate frequently shall have supplemental upstream manual isolation;
- compressor recycle valves in gas service shall have an outlet manual isolation valve;
- liquid-service control valves shall have bypass, inlet block, and outlet block valves;
- pressure regulators shall have inlet isolation, outlet isolation, and a testing valve immediately downstream of the regulator;
- pilot-sensing regulators shall have root valves at the sensing point where the sensing point is outside the regulator body isolation valves;
- level transmitter bridles shall have root valves;
- direct-to-vessel level switch instrumentation is permitted;
- level instrumentation with tubing connections to a drain header shall have a root valve on the drain header piping;
- spare or standby equipment within a package shall have isolation valves; and
- heat exchangers expected to require frequent cleaning shall have maintenance isolation.

### Common Equipment

Common equipment is equipment where one unit or train serves the facility and the service must be taken out of service for maintenance. Examples include the amine unit, TEG dehydration unit, molecular sieve units, cryogenic module, and NGL caustic treating equipment. Legacy depropanizer or fractionation references do not establish current-scope depropanizer equipment in this section.

For common equipment:

- PSV inlet and outlet block valves shall be provided unless an exception below applies;
- in gas service, a PSV inlet block valve may be omitted where the equipment can be isolated, bypassed, and blown down to satisfy PSV inlet isolation; PSV outlet isolation shall still be provided;
- no block valves shall be provided on PSV inlet or outlet piping for equipment certified under ASME Section I, including the heat medium heater;
- continuous-service control valves shall have inlet block, outlet block, and bypass valves;
- the cryogenic module J-T valve shall not have a manual bypass;
- pressure regulators shall have inlet isolation, outlet isolation, and a testing valve immediately downstream of the regulator;
- pilot-sensing regulators shall have root valves at the sensing point where the sensing point is outside the regulator body isolation valves;
- direct-to-vessel level instrumentation is not preferred;
- direct-to-vessel level switches are permitted only in sweet, clean gas service where equipment can be blown down for maintenance in a limited volume, and this exception shall be used sparingly;
- level transmitter bridles shall have root valves; and
- level instrumentation with tubing connections to a drain header shall have a root valve on the drain header piping.

The PSV isolation basis intentionally differs between multiple parallel packages and common equipment. Multiple parallel packages rely on unit isolation and depressurization for PSV maintenance, while common equipment normally requires PSV inlet and outlet isolation unless an explicit exception applies.

### Skid-Edge and Processing-Unit Isolation

Skid-edge isolation shall be provided at modular processing unit boundaries. The detailed design intent for this requirement contains an incomplete upstream sentence and is therefore partially TBD, but the required isolation features are as follows:

- provide a skid-edge block valve for all services;
- provide a skid-edge spectacle blind for all services except instrument air;
- locate skid-edge isolation outside buildings;
- locate skid-edge isolation in the interconnect piping between the pipe rack and the module, processing unit, or building;
- for typical skid-edge connections other than flare lines, provide a 1 in. vent valve on the pipe-rack side of the skid-edge block valve and spectacle blind;
- for module flare headers that are free-draining to the main header and heat traced, provide a 1 in. vent valve on the pipe-rack side of the skid-edge block valve and spectacle blind;
- for module flare headers trapped relative to the main header and heat traced, provide a 1 in. drain valve on the pipe-rack side of the skid-edge block valve and spectacle blind; and
- remote air coolers that are part of a processing unit but installed away from the module do not require skid-edge isolation.

### Blinds for Vessel Entry and Positive Isolation

Spectacle blinds or equivalent positive isolation shall be installed on vessel nozzles for vessels that can be entered. For filter vessels with quick-open closures where filter element supports prevent full vessel entry, spectacle blind isolation is not required for filter removal.

Where a spool is removed for vessel entry, a spectacle blind is not required; removing the spool and installing a blind flange is an acceptable alternative. Spectacle blinds required for vessel isolation shall be installed at the vessel nozzle. Installation in piping attached to the vessel is not acceptable unless interference makes nozzle installation impractical. Where interference with a building wall or vessel construction prevents practical blind installation at the nozzle, the blind or spacer may be installed less than 2 m from the vessel.

Spectacle blinds should generally be installed vertically, with the face of the blind perpendicular to grade, to support removal and installation. Paddle blinds may be selected where spectacle blinds become impractical due to handling or weight. # SEC-11 - Civil, Buildings, and Miscellaneous Facilities Basis

## Civil Scope

This section defines the civil, structural, site-grading, drainage, road, foundation, building, and miscellaneous-facility basis for the West Doe Deepcut expansion. The basis applies to civil and structural engineering for the facility pad, drainage system, retention pond, roads, foundations, process and utility modules, permanent buildings, and ancillary buildings.

The civil scope includes:

- structural and foundation design requirements for project buildings, equipment, towers, tanks, modules, pipe racks, and related structures;
- geotechnical and topographical input requirements used for foundation, grading, drainage, and road design;
- site grading, ditching, culvert, retention-pond, and surface-control requirements;
- primary and secondary road design criteria;
- foundation concepts for tanks, transformers, compressors, and other structures;
- project building construction requirements, building/module erection basis, and building colors;
- ancillary office/control room and maintenance/warehouse facilities to be installed on site or modified if already existing.

Electrical, controls, and instrumentation implementation details are outside this section except where building or civil support is required for those systems.

## Governing Civil and Structural Basis

Structural and foundation engineering shall be performed using the latest edition of the governing codes and standards listed below.

| Basis Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Building code | National Building Code of Canada |
| Steel design | CAN/CSA-S16 Design of Steel Structures |
| Concrete design | CAN/CSA A23.3 Design of Concrete Structures |

## Site Grading and Surface Water Management

Site grading and drainage shall be designed to prevent off-site surface overflow from entering the expansion facility while directing and containing on-site overflow into a retention pond. Surface-control features shall also be considered within the facility and around selected equipment to prevent on-site releases from discharging outside the facility boundaries. Operations shall have plans and procedures for release monitoring, analysis, and confirmation of non-contaminated surface water before removal or pump-out activities.

General site grading and drainage engineering shall use the following design principles.

| Design Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Main pipe rack grading | High equal-elevation ridges along main pipe racks |
| Facility pad grading | Pad slopes down from pipe racks at 1.5% to each side |
| Reduced pad slope allowance | Pad slope may be reduced to 1.0% where required to maintain reasonable top-of-pile-cap elevations |
| Tank-farm perimeter | Equal elevation around tank farms, with slope or swale out from the exterior of the dike wall as required |
| Maximum grade slope | 3H:1V maximum, including road-fill side slopes, ditches, stockpiles, pond slopes, and similar grade surfaces, unless specifically engineered or mandated otherwise by the geotechnical report |
| Ditch slope | 0.2% minimum |
| Culvert slope | 0.5% minimum; 1.0% preferred |
| Ditch and culvert storm basis | IDF curve for the 1:10 year, 15 minute rainfall event |
| IDF duration | To be confirmed during detailed engineering |
| Facility pad surface | Adequate gravel thickness over the entire pad to provide a driving surface |
| Access-road drainage definition | Only access roads will be defined by ditches on either side |

An on-site retention pond with berm shall be installed to capture natural runoff, which is expected to increase due to reduced drainage through post-construction compacted soil. The pond location will be planned and the pond capacity sized during detailed engineering. Based on initial historical topographical review of the surrounding farmland and vegetation, natural drainage appears to flow from north of the expansion facility toward the south. The approximate retention pond location depends on the plot plan and remains tied to that external deliverable.

For the NGL storage area, provisions for a berm, elevation decline, or other surface-control feature shall be considered for containment of an accidental leak or spill. Grading underneath the NGL bullets shall be sloped, potentially southbound, to redirect NGL away from the pipe rack and process areas and reduce pool-fire exposure.

## Area Classification

Area classification shall be completed in accordance with the governing electrical codes and standards for the project. In general, the facility shall be designed as Class I Zone 2, Gas Groups IIA and IIB. Process modules and process buildings are intended to be classified as Zone 2 hazardous areas based on fugitive emission studies following API RP-505.

The 13.8 kV switchgear bus shall be sized for the full facility scope.

The 13.8 kV switchgear shall be designed, purchased, and installed as the plant main power distribution center. It shall have sufficient capacity to distribute power to the electrical buildings stationed across the facility for the power users. Power from the 13.8 kV switchgear shall be distributed radially through step-down transformers to:

- 4.16 kV Acid Gas/Overheads Compressor Electrical Building.
- 600 V Acid Gas Compressor Electrical Building.
- 600 V Sales/Overheads Compressor Electrical Building.
- 4.16 kV/600 V General Area/Tank Farm/Process Electrical Building.

Power distribution is shared between the 04-25 and 03-25 facilities. Incoming power metering at the facility boundary, protection coordination, and emergency or standby power scope split remain to be confirmed during detailed engineering.

Large oil-filled transformers shall be spaced in accordance with CEC requirements. They will generally be installed on structural steel transformer bases. Secondary containment requirements shall be reviewed, and transformer selection shall avoid or limit containment requirements where practical.

### Motor Control and Motor Specifications

The 6.9 kV motor control center shall provide mechanically latched fused contactors with motor protection relays and an Ethernet communication port for connection to the plant PLC central control panel for data acquisition. Starting VFDs shall be provided for the KM-2150/2250 Inlet/Sales Gas Compressor motors. Power-factor-correction capacitor banks shall not be installed on the MCC-8200 synchronous-transfer bus.

The 4.16 kV motor control center shall provide mechanically latched fused contactors with motor protection relays and an Ethernet communication port for connection to the plant PLC central control panel. VFD and soft-starter requirements for 4.16 kV motors are TBD.

The 600 V MCCs shall be traditional MCCs complete with electronic motor overload relays. A motor local control station shall be installed adjacent to each motor; the default local control station shall be Hand-Off-Auto or On-Off. Local control stations shall be hard-wired to the motor starter circuit of the MCC by the field construction contractor. 600 V VFDs shall be provided as part of the 600 V MCC lineup. Standalone 600 V VFDs are not allowed unless dedicated to large motors. VFD selection shall be based on motor-driven load type and motor full-load current, with motor horsepower used as a general reference.

In general, motors smaller than 3/4 hp shall be 115/200 V. Motors up to and including 250 hp shall be 575 V. Larger motors on this facility shall be 4,000 V or 13.2 kV. VFD-fed motors located in Zone 2 areas shall be marked accordingly and supplied with a temperature code lower than the temperature code specified on the area-classification drawing or fugitive-emissions study.

Anti-condensation space heaters, where required for critical service, shall be connected on motors greater than 100 hp. Outdoor critical-service motors less than 100 hp, including process centrifugal pumps and regeneration gas compressors, shall also be provided with space heaters. Motors 100 hp and larger shall be fed from soft starters or VFDs as required by the process. Air-cooler fan motors shall be provided with VFDs to reduce noise emissions at night and during cooler periods.

### 208/120 V Systems and SCR Heater Controls

The 208/120 V, 3 phase, 4 wire, 60 Hz AC power system shall serve lighting, utility receptacles, building exhaust fans, building heater blower fans, electric heat tracing, and packaged equipment requiring 208/120 V power. The 208/120 V source shall be established using a distribution transformer stepping voltage down from 600 V with the transformer neutral solidly grounded. Each transformer shall feed its own 208/120 V distribution panelboard.

600 V SCR heater-control panels shall be provided in the electrical building to supply heaters requiring process temperature control. SCR panels shall be supplied by feeder breakers in the 600 V MCC.

## Electrical Buildings

Electrical buildings shall be prefabricated, modular buildings located in general purpose areas. They shall house, as required by detailed design, 13.8 kV main switchgear, medium-voltage motor control centers, medium-voltage reduced-voltage soft starters, medium-voltage VFDs, 600 V MCCs, 120 V AC UPS systems with battery banks and distribution panels, 125 V DC UPS systems with battery banks and distribution panels, 600 V to 208/120 V distribution transformers and panelboards, 208/120 V contactor panels, plant PLC control panels, and network racks.

Electrical buildings shall be climate controlled with HVAC sized as an n + 1 system so the cooling system can tolerate failure or maintenance shutdown of one HVAC unit without affecting building heating and cooling.

Electrical buildings shall be designed for bottom entry of incoming and outgoing power cables. Buildings shall be elevated and installed on piles to provide space beneath the building for MCC incoming cables running in trays to the 600 V MCC main incoming section. Outgoing cables from 600 V MCCs to facility 600 V loads shall also be bottom entry.

Electrical buildings shall be wired with TECK and ACIC cables. EMT conduit shall be used for equipment located adjacent to each other, such as control panels to contactor panels. An outdoor GFI receptacle shall be provided for exterior maintenance. Equipment doors shall be sized for, or include removable transom sections to allow, removal of the largest equipment.

Remote Distribution Centres shall be fabricated for 208/120 VAC loads across the plant. Each RDC shall generally consist of a step-down transformer, distribution panel, and wireways for interconnection of remote loads. RDCs reduce long-run cable quantities in plant cable tray systems. RDC quantity, locations, distribution panel sizes, ratings, and specific requirements are TBD and shall be established during detailed engineering. Branch circuit breakers feeding electric heat tracing circuits not controlled by EHT controllers shall be 30 mA GFI type.

## Grounding and Bonding

The BC Hydro utility transformer shall be grounded using a 200 A, 10 s neutral grounding resistor and shall operate as a tripping system. Each 6.9 kV transformer shall be grounded using a 100 A, 10 s neutral grounding resistor and shall operate as a tripping system. Each 600 V transformer shall be grounded by a 5 A continuous high-resistance grounding resistor. The 600 V MCCs shall include power metering and ground/resistor fault detection. Ground-fault protection on 600 V systems shall be alarm-only to maintain continuity of operations.

The plant electrical grounding and bonding system shall use driven piles as ground electrodes interconnected by a main #2/0 green insulated grounding conductor run in the highest-voltage carrying tray, with taps to electrical equipment, buildings, skids, process vessels, and tanks. Pipe racks and skids shall be welded to piles; additional bonding conductors from cable tray down to piles are not required.

All major electrical equipment shall be directly connected to the ground grid at two points. Ground wells at power transformers or electrical buildings shall be provided for maintenance and operational testing, with bolted ground connections at test points in the ground wells. Above-grade grounding conductors shall be green insulated ground wires run in PVC conduit where mechanical protection is required; ground connections shall be compression type.

All distribution transformers, panelboards, and three-phase motors larger than 100 hp shall have a separate copper ground conductor connected directly to ground. This connection shall be sized per CEC requirements and is in addition to the grounding conductor run with power wiring or cables.

Truck loading shall use facility-installed ground studs adjacent to each truck-out piping connection. These studs shall be connected to the facility main ground grid; islanded grounds shall not be used. Each truck shall be equipped with a ground ball or ground bolt and appropriate grounding cable. The driver shall connect and verify grounding to the facility ground stud before proceeding with the truck loading or unloading sequence. Grounding shall be completed before mechanical piping connection.

# SEC-13 - Controls System Basis

## Discipline Scope

This section defines the controls system basis for the West Doe Deepcut expansion, including the operating philosophy, topology levels, physical controls layout, industrial and enterprise network basis, server and virtual-machine architecture, operator and engineering workstations, controller system basis, configuration basis, and Modbus data integration basis.

Metering data and metering-system design values are governed in SEC-03. This section includes only controls-system interfaces and communication restrictions that are part of the mapped controls architecture.

## Operating Philosophy

The controls system is designed for operation from a central operations control room. The control room shall provide the environment for operations personnel to monitor and control the facility while limiting the need for physical interaction in the process facility.

Packaged equipment shall be provided with dedicated control equipment. Process monitoring from packaged equipment shall be available at the central control room.

## Controls Topology

The controls system is based on a multi-level topology from field devices through enterprise networks. Each level or zone shall be designed for reliability, security, and operational effectiveness appropriate to its function.

| Topology Level | Basis |
|---|---|
| Level 0 | Process level or field-level instrumentation devices. |
| Level 1 | Basic control, including control system controllers and processors. |
| Level 2 | Supervisory control, including operator workstations and visual clients. |
| Level 3 | Site operations and control. |
| IDMZ | Industrial demilitarized zone providing firewall and security protection between industrial and enterprise networks. |
| Level 4 | Enterprise machines used for email and internet access. |
| Level 5 | Enterprise network access. |

Levels 4 and 5 are optional in the controls topology because the end-user IT/OT policies govern those levels.

## Physical Layout and Equipment Placement

The controls system shall consist of multiple components distributed throughout the facility. The central control room shall contain operator workstations, engineering workstations, primary servers, and core network switches.

A centrally located motor control center shall house Level 1 components, primarily the control system processors and controllers. The motor control center shall also contain the secondary servers and distribution network switches.

Remote I/O cabinets shall be located in process areas to consolidate Level 0 field devices before communication leaves a module or area. Remote I/O cabinets shall communicate with the controls system over redundant or ring-type ethernet communications. Remote I/O cabinet locations will vary with facility layout and shall be determined during detailed design.

## Network Basis

The controls system shall incorporate multiple networks to manage communications and traffic between systems.

| Network | Design Basis |
|---|---|
| Process Control Network (PCN) | Connects industrial Level 1 equipment to Level 2 and Level 3 networking equipment and servers. Server-to-server communication exists on the PCN. The PCN shall restrict outside access using an IDMZ, support polling and updating of controls data for process control and interfacing, and be designed for continuous operation through redundant installation. Field-mounted network switches shall have dual uplinks forming link aggregation to reduce network interruptions. |
| I/O Network | Provides communication between I/O nodes within a control system. The I/O network shall be segregated from other networks to limit interference and excess traffic, shall be designed for reliable I/O data communication, and shall use redundant ethernet communications. Multiple I/O networks may be required in a single topology. ## Servers and Virtualization Basis

The controls system shall include physical domain-controller servers and physical virtual-host servers. The primary domain controller shall be implemented on a physical server within the controls system and shall handle security authentication within the network domain and host access to domain resources. The secondary domain controller shall also be implemented on a physical server and shall operate if the primary domain controller fails.

The controls system shall use two physical servers for the primary server functions. These servers shall be designed for fault-tolerant operation and shall run multiple virtual machines depending on application requirements. Primary hosts shall be located in the control room and secondary hosts shall be located in the motor control center.

Virtualized machines consist of operating systems running on physical servers controlled and monitored by a hypervisor. The virtualization basis supports reduced installation time, improved disaster recovery reliability, reduced maintenance cost, and multiple virtual machines on one physical server or server pair.

# SEC-14 - Instrumented Protection Basis

## Protection Scope

This section defines the instrumented protection basis for the West Doe Deepcut expansion. The scope includes fire detection, combustible gas detection, H2S detection, methyl mercaptan detection, audible and visual alarm signalling, emergency shutdown push buttons, and local unit shutdown push buttons.

The protection basis applies to buildings, hazardous areas, outdoor rotating equipment, outdoor process areas where the detection criteria apply, large modules without buildings, and local unit control panels. Broader controls-system architecture, electrical power supply, and detailed shutdown logic configuration are addressed outside this section except where required to state the protection interface.

Fire, gas, and H2S detection equipment shall be MSA/General Monitors. Detector display locations, detector coverage, alarm signalling, ESD pushbutton placement, and unit shutdown interfaces shall be finalized in detailed engineering where this section identifies a TBD or confirmation requirement.

## Detection Systems

### Combustible Gas Detection

Gas detector displays are to be located inside buildings. All hazardous-area buildings shall be provided with LEL detection. Additional outdoor LEL detection is to be determined by detailed engineering hazard analysis.

At a minimum, methane LEL detection shall be provided in potentially hazardous areas where LEL detection is required and where hydrocarbons are present in the process. For this basis, potentially hazardous areas are the areas classified by electrical area classification. Buildings that include liquid hydrocarbon service and where volatile liquid hydrocarbons are present shall also be provided with propane LEL detection where LEL detection is required.

### H2S Detection

H2S detector displays are to be located inside their associated buildings. All buildings shall be provided with H2S detection. Additional outdoor H2S detection is to be determined by detailed engineering hazard analysis.

### Methyl Mercaptan Detection

Methyl mercaptan detector displays are to be located inside their associated buildings. Methyl mercaptan detection shall be installed in all buildings and outdoor locations where methyl mercaptans are present in the process. Detailed engineering hazard analysis shall determine the specific methyl mercaptan detector locations required.

### Fire Detection

A dedicated fire alarm protection system is not provided. Fire detection instrumentation shall be connected to the facility Controls System.

UV/IR point fire detectors shall be used in process buildings. Smoke detectors shall be used in general purpose buildings. At minimum, one fire detector shall be installed in every building that contains rotating equipment or heat medium service. Fire detectors shall also be installed near outdoor rotating equipment, and detector coverage shall include rotating equipment seals.

The required quantity and final coverage of fire detectors are to be confirmed during detailed engineering. Additional outdoor fire detection shall be determined by detailed engineering hazard analysis.

## Alarm and Operator Notification Basis

Visual alarm beacons and audible horns shall be installed to alert operators to the current facility alarm status. Beacon groups shall include the applicable facility beacon colours and shall be located outdoors at strategic points on the piperacks. Beacon groups shall also be installed on the control room exterior, on the building containing the main balance of plant control system PLCs, near the NGL Storage Area, and near the atmospheric storage tank area for produced water and condensate tanks.

Horns shall be installed throughout the facility for audible alarm notification. Horns shall be installed at the same locations as piperack beacon groups, on the building containing the main balance of plant control system PLCs, and near the atmospheric storage tank area.

Two horn tones shall be provided. One unique horn tone shall annunciate process alarms and shall sound in conjunction with the amber beacon. One unique horn tone shall annunciate emergency scenarios including fire, LEL, H2S, and ESD trip.

## Shutdown Interfaces

ESD pushbuttons shall be located outside all building exterior doors. ESD pushbuttons shall also be installed in strategic locations on large modules that do not have buildings. Large-module ESD pushbutton locations are TBD during detailed engineering; the current basis identifies cryogenic and molecular sieve modules as examples requiring this detailed-engineering determination.

Push buttons installed on unit control panels, such as compressor control panels, shall trip the local unit emergency shutdown mode.
