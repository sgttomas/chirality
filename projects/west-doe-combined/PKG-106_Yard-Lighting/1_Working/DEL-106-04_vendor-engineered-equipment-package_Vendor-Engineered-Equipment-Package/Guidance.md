# Guidance — DEL-106-04 Vendor Engineered Equipment Package (Yard Lighting)

## Purpose

This deliverable is the Package Vendor's production unit for `PKG-106` Yard Lighting. It exists so that vendor engineering, design, fabrication, and supply of the physical equipment package are produced from the EPC Scope of Work (`DEL-106-01`) and EPC Package Datasheet (`DEL-106-02`) and are governed by an explicit vendor-owned design basis and datasheet set. Downstream, this deliverable supports `DEL-106-05` (vendor document turnover) and is reviewed/accepted in `DEL-106-06` (EPC vendor package review and acceptance).

## Principles

- **Vendor owns the engineered package, EPC owns facility integration.** The package register draws an explicit boundary: vendor engineers, designs, documents, and supplies; EPC integrates into the functional facility, including interfaces, tie-ins, constructability, procurement/construction coordination, and facility-level integration. The vendor design basis must respect this boundary and expose the three declared interfaces (Electrical Power; Grounding / Bonding; Area / Exterior Lighting) cleanly.
- **LED-first.** The DBM is unambiguous that all lighting is LED type, including outdoor/process-area fixtures, to minimize electrical demand and maintenance. Non-LED alternatives should not enter the vendor design without a documented deviation.
- **Light pollution is a regulatory constraint, not a preference.** The DBM names downcast floodlights, prohibition of horizontally aimed floodlights, photocell/switch control, working-area minimization, and mast pole offset from pad edge as the basis to meet regulatory light-pollution requirements. Treat these as hard design rules.
- **Area classification governs fixture selection.** Fixtures must match the area classification in which they are installed. Vendor should require facility hazardous area drawings as an input and call out classifications on the lighting layout.
- **Decomposition routes; sources govern.** The decomposition tells the vendor *what* deliverable to produce. The DBM lighting basis and the (pending) EPC SOW/Datasheet tell the vendor *what to design to*. Where the decomposition narrative and source disagree, source wins (and is recorded in the Conflict Table below).

## Considerations

- **Yard scope vs. building scope.** The package is named "Yard Lighting," which most directly reads as outdoor / area / process-area / exterior lighting (including mast poles). The DBM lighting section covers a broader set (interior building, MCC room flat-panel LED, exit/emergency with battery backup, receptacles). The vendor design basis should declare which subset of the DBM lighting basis it implements and which it explicitly excludes; this should be confirmed against `DEL-106-01` SOW and `DEL-106-02` Package Datasheet when those are issued.
- **Source-extracted package slice is missing.** `26020-Package_Requirements.docx` is in `_Sources` but no PKG-106-specific slice has been copied into this deliverable. Quantitative criteria (lux levels, fixture catalogue, pole heights, foundation loads) that may live in that document are currently TBD and should be resolved before issuing the vendor package.
- **Sibling EPC anchors are not yet drafted.** `DEL-106-01` SOW and `DEL-106-02` Package Datasheet exist as folders with only the PREPARATION fileset. Vendor design that depends on values from those deliverables should treat those inputs as TBD until they are issued.
- **Light-pollution rule citations.** The DBM names regulatory light-pollution requirements but does not cite the specific provincial/federal regulation in the slice read. The vendor design basis should record the specific regulation (and any operator/permit conditions) when EPC SOW/Datasheet provides them; until then, location TBD.
- **Cathodic protection.** Pole foundations and grounding interfaces may interact with the facility cathodic-protection scheme. Cathodic protection engineering is excluded from facility design scope per the DBM; vendor design should expose a clean grounding/bonding interface and not assume CP integration.
- **Cold-weather basis.** The facility is at the West Doe Deepcut (BC) site with a cold-weather electrical envelope. Fixture, driver, and pole hardware selections should be qualified for the site ambient; specific ambient design limits are TBD in the deliverable source set.

## Trade-offs

- **Mast poles vs. building-mounted fixtures.** Mast poles cover large yard areas efficiently but introduce foundation, pole supply, and pad-edge offset constraints (regulatory). Building-mounted fixtures avoid foundations but depend on building layout coverage. Vendor should justify the chosen mix in the design basis.
- **Photocell vs. switch control.** Photocell control gives automatic dusk/dawn behavior and aligns with light-pollution minimization; manual switch control may be preferable for short-occupancy areas. The DBM permits either; vendor should declare and justify per area.
- **Vendor-supplied terminations vs. EPC-built terminations.** Pushing more termination work to the vendor improves shop quality and CEC compliance (especially for shop-built buildings) but increases vendor scope coordination with EPC. The boundary should be explicit in the vendor design basis.
- **Standard fixture catalogue vs. project-specific selection.** Standard catalogue parts simplify spares but may not meet area classification or photometric criteria in every location. Vendor should default to catalogue parts and document exceptions.
