# Guidance: DEL-027-04_vendor-engineered-equipment-package

## Purpose

This guidance supports the Package Vendor production unit (engineering, design, fabrication/supply, and physical equipment) for the Transformer TXP-8301-1 step-down distribution transformer package, `PKG-027` (20/26 MVA, 13.8 kV / 6.9 kV / 0.4 kV per workbook title). The deliverable converts the accepted EPC Scope of Work and EPC Package Datasheet into a vendor-engineered package with a defendable design basis and supplied equipment. EPC integration review is performed downstream (`DEL-027-06`).

## Principles

- **Vendor inputs are EPC outputs.** The vendor design starts from `DEL-027-01` (Scope of Work) and `DEL-027-02` (Package Datasheet). The vendor shall not invent package scope outside those inputs.
- **Responsibility split is preserved.** Package engineering, design, vendor documentation, and physical equipment belong to the Package Vendor. Facility integration, interfaces, tie-ins, constructability, procurement/construction coordination, and facility-level integration belong to the EPC Integrator.
- **Source-anchored values only.** Where accessible source slices (DBM electrical sections, Gate 7 registers, workbook row 29) define a value or constraint, the vendor design shall reflect it. Where source is silent, values remain `TBD` for vendor data to close — not for invention by drafting.
- **Interfaces drive design, not the reverse.** The seven applicable package interfaces (Electrical Power; Grounding / Bonding; Area / Exterior Lighting; I&C / Control Cabling; Communications / Network; Maintenance Access; Structural / Foundations / Supports) are inputs to vendor engineering, not items the vendor may redefine.
- **Facility distribution scheme is the boundary condition.** The 13.8 kV facility backbone, the 6.9 kV process inverter-drive motor service, the 600 V LV service, and the per-class neutral-grounding scheme (200 A 10 s utility; 100 A 10 s per 6.9 kV transformer; 5 A continuous on 600 V) are facility-defined. The vendor designs the transformer to live inside that scheme; the vendor does not redesign the scheme.

## Considerations

- **Voltage class labelling.** Package title is "13.8kV/6.9kV/0.4kV". DBM defines 13.8 kV backbone distribution and 6.9 kV process motor service, but does not describe a 0.4 kV (400 V) facility service. The vendor design shall confirm with EPC whether the 0.4 kV winding represents (a) a tertiary winding for auxiliary loads, (b) a labelling artifact for an LV (e.g., 480/400 V) auxiliary supply, or (c) something else. Until confirmed, treat the 0.4 kV winding as `TBD`.
- **Cooling class.** "20/26 MVA" is a dual rating typical of oil-immersed transformers (e.g., ONAN/ONAF). Vendor data shall confirm cooling class, oil type, radiator configuration, and any forced-cooling controls.
- **Grounding scheme alignment.** Each 6.9 kV transformer is grounded through a 100 A, 10 s NGR operated as a tripping system. The vendor design shall provide a compatible neutral bushing, cubicle, or interface that allows the EPC NGR (or a vendor-supplied NGR, by scope split to be confirmed) to be applied.
- **Foundations and containment.** Large oil-filled transformers in the DBM are supported on precast concrete bearing foundations and/or structural-steel transformer bases, with secondary containment reviewed and minimized where practical. Vendor pad-print and weight data drive the EPC foundation design.
- **Maintenance access.** Vendor layout should preserve access for radiators, bushings, tap changer, sampling, NGR cubicle, oil containment provisions, and CEC clearance between transformer and adjacent equipment.
- **Cable terminations.** Vendor terminations and cable boxes shall accept the facility's medium-voltage TECK cable basis (15 kV / 133% on 13.8 kV side; 8 kV / 100% on 6.9 kV side).

## Trade-offs

- **Standardized transformer vs. project-specific build.** A standard 20/26 MVA distribution transformer simplifies vendor supply but may require adapters (bushing arrangements, cable-box configurations, NGR cubicle interfaces). Project-specific engineering improves integration fit but extends schedule. The trade-off is a vendor + EPC decision at integration review.
- **Outdoor pad vs. electrical-building integration.** DBM describes 13.8 kV switchgear and 6.9 kV electrical buildings; large oil-filled distribution transformers are typically outdoor pad-mounted. Outdoor mounting affects ambient/temperature derating and grounding routing. Indoor (transformer vault or building) mounting affects ventilation, oil-containment, and fire considerations.
- **Secondary-side configuration.** Single secondary (6.9 kV) with a separate small dry-type 0.4 kV auxiliary, versus dual-secondary windings on the same core, has different cost, footprint, and protection implications. This is the central CFL-027-04-001 item.
- **Tap changer (DETC vs. OLTC).** OLTC adds reliability/regulation capability but increases cost, maintenance, and complexity. DETC is typical for distribution transformers without dynamic regulation needs. Source is silent.

## Examples

- A vendor 20/26 MVA, 13.8 kV : 6.9 kV oil-filled step-down transformer with ONAN/ONAF cooling, DETC, conservator or sealed-tank construction, with neutral bushing for an external 100 A 10 s NGR on the 6.9 kV side, supplied on a vendor-designed base for a precast concrete bearing foundation. (ASSUMPTION: representative configuration; actual configuration set by vendor data.)
- A vendor design basis documenting the seven interface positions (Electrical Power; Grounding / Bonding; Area / Exterior Lighting; I&C / Control Cabling; Communications / Network; Maintenance Access; Structural / Foundations / Supports) and the vendor's response to each at the package boundary.
