# Guidance: DEL-031-04_vendor-engineered-equipment-package

## Purpose

This guidance supports the Package Vendor production unit (engineering, design, fabrication/supply, and physical equipment) for the Transformer TXP-8500-1 step-down distribution transformer package (`PKG-031`; 3 MVA, 13.8 kV / 600/347 V). The deliverable converts the accepted EPC Scope of Work and EPC Package Datasheet into a vendor-engineered transformer with a defendable design basis and the supplied equipment. EPC integration review is performed downstream (`DEL-031-06`).

## Principles

- **Vendor inputs are EPC outputs.** The vendor design starts from `DEL-031-01` (Scope of Work) and `DEL-031-02` (Package Datasheet). The vendor shall not invent package scope outside those inputs.
- **Responsibility split is preserved.** Package engineering, design, vendor documentation, and the physical equipment package belong to the Package Vendor. Facility integration, interfaces, tie-ins, constructability, procurement/construction coordination, and facility-level integration belong to the EPC Integrator.
- **Source-anchored values only.** Where accessible source slices (DBM electrical sections, Gate 7 registers, workbook row 33) define a value or constraint, the vendor design shall reflect it. Where source is silent (winding configuration, impedance, cooling class, insulation type, tap arrangement, etc.), values remain `TBD` for vendor data to close — not for invention by drafting.
- **Interfaces drive design, not the reverse.** The seven package interfaces (Electrical Power; Grounding / Bonding; Area / Exterior Lighting; I&C / Control Cabling; Communications / Network; Maintenance Access; Structural / Foundations / Supports) are inputs to vendor engineering, not items the vendor may redefine.
- **Standby power and integration items remain with the EPC.** The vendor designs for compatibility; no 13.8 kV-level emergency-generator interface is to be engineered into this transformer package because the standby-power basis is at the 600 V MCC level with LV standby generators and transfer switches.

## Considerations

- **Step-down role.** The transformer is a 13.8 kV / 600/347 V step-down distribution unit, nameplate 3 MVA. The accessible 03-25 DBM "Incoming Power and Transformers" table is the closest source match (a "13.8 kV to 600V, 3 MVA transformer" feeding the 600 V MCC). Tag association (TXP-8500-1) is consistent with that role but is not explicitly stated by source; treat as a strong working ASSUMPTION pending `DEL-031-02`.
- **Primary side.** 13.8 kV, 3 phase, 3 wire, 60 Hz, low-resistance grounded — same basis as the 04-25 13.8 kV main switchgear bus. The transformer primary is an outgoing feeder from the 13.8 kV switchgear (sub-fed to 03-25 in the working allocation).
- **Secondary side.** 600 V wye (with 347 V available as line-to-neutral) feeding a 600 V MCC; secondary high-resistance grounded with a 5 A continuous resistor and alarm-only ground-fault scheme to preserve continuity of operations.
- **347 V usage.** The 347 V neutral takeoff is consistent with the project's 600/347 V lighting service derivation; specific lighting tie-in is owned outside this package (Area / Exterior Lighting interface).
- **Cabling basis.** Primary 13.8 kV feeder: three-conductor copper TECK 15 kV, 133 percent insulation, shielded. Secondary 600 V feeder from transformer secondary to the 600 V MCC: ACWU cable; avoid single-conductor cables.
- **Grounding application.** Major-equipment grounding (two-point ground grid connection), ground wells at power transformers with bolted ground connections at test points, and CEC-sized separate copper grounding conductors are all DBM-defined design requirements that bound vendor grounding-bus and bonding provisions.
- **Foundations and insulation type.** Source notes large transformers are generally oil-filled (with structural-steel transformer bases or precast concrete foundations and CEC-required spacing) while LACT-area transformers may be dry-type. The DBM does not classify TXP-8500-1; insulation type, foundation design, and any secondary containment are vendor/EPC questions resolved through `DEL-031-02` and `DEL-031-06`.
- **Sound and serviceability.** Sound level, lifting/handling provisions, accessory layout, and maintenance access (incl. radiator/cooling system, bushing access, tap-changer access) are vendor design choices that must respect the Maintenance Access interface.

## Trade-offs

- **Oil-filled vs. dry-type.** Oil-filled units typically offer better efficiency/loss profile and overload capability at lower capital cost; dry-type units reduce fire/spill exposure and may suit indoor or hazardous-adjacent locations. DBM precedent generally favors oil-filled for larger units. Selection driven by `DEL-031-02` and host-building constraints.
- **Cooling class (e.g., ONAN, ONAF, KNAN).** Affects continuous rating, weight, footprint, and serviceability. Source is silent.
- **No-load vs. de-energized vs. on-load tap changer.** Affects voltage regulation, complexity, and reliability. Source is silent.
- **Indoor electrical-building installation vs. outdoor pad.** Indoor installation simplifies environmental exposure but consumes building floor area; outdoor pad requires weather-rated enclosure and security/access provisions. Source does not assign a host location.
- **Protection package depth.** Vendor-internal protection (Buchholz, sudden-pressure, winding/oil temperature, pressure-relief, gas accumulation, dial-type thermometers, neutral CT) versus reliance on upstream/downstream relays. Affects integration with the I&C / Control Cabling and Communications / Network interfaces.
