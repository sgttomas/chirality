# R6 calibration lessons and planner handoff

These are lessons from RUN_D128 and the owner's two supplied feedback perspectives, refined by the R5 evidence recovery. They are run evidence and proposals for later method/planner adoption, not an unapproved workflow amendment.

## What produced the reconciliation cost

Fifty-four deliverables expanded into 3,568 verdict rows, 1,188 remap entries and 25 packets. Of those rows, 1,089 were text out of date and 169 authority conflicts. This is evidence that granularity and uneven carrier propagation drove much of the volume; it is not proof that every stale row was harmless detail. The deliverable corpus had ceased to be the primary implementation work surface while some carriers still received notes, leaving mixed ages within one file.

The run-wide spot check refuted 12 of 338 items. Double-blind agreement varied from 19/35 to 25/31. Deterministic coverage, independent semantic verification and the final spot check served different purposes. A perfectly complete census can still contain a wrong claim interpretation. Final R6 accounting therefore tests its own ability to reject a missing or duplicate key and binds changed blocks to both original and final bytes.

The R5 authority review found that packets sometimes converted an unapplied human act into another question for the same human. D-GOV-43, D-APP-127 and the prior R4-Q6 answer already resolved several central directions. Recording and applying those acts is different from asking the owner to reaccept them. Conversely, caller-asserted HUMAN identity, live-path redaction, real scaffold functionality, unknown native outcomes and explicit D-APP-116–119 reservations remain substantive; lifting prose cannot make them disappear.

## For the next reconciliation

1. Decide the run-level granularity posture before sorting packets. Apply the decision/interface/verification tests to each claim; use default (b) and identify the few genuinely decision-bound mechanisms. Keep borderline judgments and unrelated gates visible.
2. Ask about off-code events at R0: release identity and authorization, signing/notarization, native inspections and manual steps, with their result artifacts. An event without a recovered result is not the same as an event that never happened. The owner corrected that error here.
3. Name recurring forks early and maintain a reading register inherited by later waves. Domain-module reach, build-script reach, SoW conversion, live versus legacy reach and previously applied decisions should not be decided independently by each worker. Preserve original disagreements when normalizing a run-level reading.
4. Check evidence-root sufficiency before creating an Unknown packet. R5 recovered all 16 historical conversion records after a bounded, recorded read expansion to Root. Their mechanical checks and finalization bridge were real. Their old hashes still do not prove current semantic parity or personal human review.
5. Calibrate variance before scale-out. Use independent samples spanning packages and claim classes; inspect recurring refutations before choosing a rerun threshold. A fixed percentage has little meaning without observed inter-reviewer variation.
6. Make runs resumable: preserve exact owner words/hashes, append-only state, source-qualified method identity, bounded briefs, actual native parentage and concise returns. Rotate a manager when accumulated context starts to obscure the bounded subject; the suggested seven-deliverable workload is an operational heuristic, not a new role rule.
7. Design for brief breaches and verifier defects. Record actual read/write enforcement, treat out-of-bound evidence as contested until admitted, and use coverage checks that demonstrably fail. Synthetic subclaims need their own body identity; a stable key alone does not bind text.
8. Run reverse ownership checks across the full potential-owner population and resolve proposed owners before synthesis. The Piping feedback's late routing gaps are relevant calibration input; App's 107 unmapped capabilities still need the §3.1 choice between a real stable claim and evidence for an existing claim.
9. Sequence the decision register before derivative handoffs, or allocate IDs deliberately. Avoid placeholder crosswalks that become another mutable authority-looking surface. Preserve distinct action, record, application and acceptance states.
10. Keep operational limits explicit: placeholder credential strings may trigger secret scans; APP-HOLD takes deliverable IDs; CI cost may favor coherent batch PRs. These are observed working details, not reasons to skip security checks, holds or actual-candidate review.

## For implementation planners

1. Put stable, checkable claims in deliverables from the start, with named verification hooks. Keep incidental filenames, test totals, temporary TBD lists, release snapshots and mechanism descriptions in code/developer evidence. A compact claim is not an excuse for an untestable promise.
2. Keep deliverables and their Remaining sections on the implementation path. If a dependency graph or done declaration substitutes for them, record that direction and its authority at the time. The reconstructed v3 done declaration remains contextual synthesis; it is not blanket acceptance.
3. Apply a ruling to its named carriers when recording it. If propagation is incomplete, record the exact carriers and remaining item in their owning deliverables. Distinguish a ruled choice from an applied choice.
4. Log off-code events with identity, result and candidate basis. Signing/notarization, architecture/minimum-OS inspection and native checks need recoverable outputs, not only a statement that they occurred.
5. Mark retained compatibility code and its claims when a path is superseded; remove it only through justified scope. The 410 mixed live/legacy rows show the recurring cost of unlabelled reach. Do not turn legacy tests into live-Codex qualification evidence.
6. Move formal ownership when scope moves. Runtime extraction and SOW-079's unseated carrier should not be left to an eventual audit. Require an owning stable claim—or a reason the code is implementation detail—when a capability is introduced.
7. Use one accepted decomposition basis and an authoritative register for actual pins. Do not silently repin all SoWs to the latest code or treat a dated reference table as current acceptance.
8. A PR's proposed claim/decision fields should name the claims served and holds settled, with the register application recorded. Merged PR direction may carry actual human intent; an agent summary or merge by itself is not a new human decision.
9. Bind review to claim keys **and** evaluated bytes/revision, with an impact assessment for later edits. Keys improve navigation; they do not replace hash-bound approval or permit new bytes to inherit acceptance automatically. Repeat only checks whose warrants changed.
10. Size implementation slices around finishable scope and split unfinished capability explicitly. Keep Remaining as the current executable residual surface under the adopted kernel; retiring it, as suggested in one feedback perspective, would require a method decision. The immediate repair is truthful maintenance, not a silent replacement queue.
11. Reject unowned carrier notes. Every production assertion should own a stable claim or be clearly dated evidence. Keep repair process prose in run records so reconciliation does not plant the next generation of drift.
12. Reconcile at milestone boundaries and assess affected claims per PR. Small continuing checks at claim granularity are preferable to accumulating another whole-corpus debt. This handoff does not itself edit software-decomp, project-setup, PR templates or any reusable instruction.

## Four perspectives kept together

Ontology keeps claims, decisions, evidence, implementation and lifecycle as different objects with clear owners. Epistemology preserves their different warrants, including testimony, missing records, historical tests and present verification. Praxeology uses bounded delegation, explicit repair manifests, independent re-extraction and live Remaining ownership. Axiology preserves accountable human decisions while reducing redundant reapproval and the documentary detail that obscures meaningful obligations.
