## Cross-lineage cause

The first five repairs progressively hardened authoring supervision: lineage
freshness, durable pacing, allowlisted reads, executable child tools, and
non-circular intake. All five repairs worked in lineage six. The remaining
defect is at a different layer: the salvaged ledger was treated as a complete
semantic command basis even though its clearance contract never executed or
resolved the literal operational binaries embedded in its `exact_command`
column. N1 preserved that meaning faithfully, so structural alignment passed
while executable packet validity failed.

The precise missing gate was a read-only packet-command environment audit
between N1 return and freeze: enumerate literal absolute binaries and shell
builtins in the authored runbook, resolve each on the target shell/host, and
fail before freeze on any mismatch. Such a gate must distinguish commands
that can be harmlessly probed from destructive/runtime forms that may only be
syntax-resolved, and it must define whether correction requires a newly
authorized ledger amendment or a fresh lineage. That choice is owner-class;
this manager does not make it.

No seventh-lineage design or repair is initiated by this analysis.
