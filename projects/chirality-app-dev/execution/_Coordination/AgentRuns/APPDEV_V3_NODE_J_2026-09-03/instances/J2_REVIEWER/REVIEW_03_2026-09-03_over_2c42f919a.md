- Application-basis corroboration: `projects/chirality-app-dev/frontend/electron/main.ts:922-926` and `frontend/electron/desktop-process-policy.ts:75-80`

The controller calls `setsid()` once for itself and launches the command into that group. Teardown inspects and signals only the two recorded PGIDs. That is identity-safe for members that remain in those groups, but POSIX process groups are not inescapable containment. Any launched descendant permitted to call `setsid()` or `setpgid()` can leave the recorded group; it then disappears from `inspect_process_group_members`, is not included in the remaining count, and is not targeted by either group signal.

Reviewer reproduction used the candidate controller unchanged and made its direct child call `os.setsid()` before sleeping. The ready record identified controller/PGID 23936 and child 23939; process-table inspection showed the child had moved to PGID 23939. After TERM and KILL were sent to the anchored group `-23936` and the controller was reaped, `kill -0 23939` still returned success:

```text
controller_pid=23936 controller_pgid=23936
child_pid=23939 child_pgid=23939
TERM target=-23936
KILL target=-23936
ESCAPED_DESCENDANT_AFTER_GROUP_KILL=alive
```

The reviewer then explicitly terminated and reaped the disposable escapee and verified it was absent. No foreign process was signaled.

The accepted retained run does **not** show an actual detached/session-escaping process. Its daemon starts with `activationPolicy:"prohibited"`; the daemon log contains no `runtime.daemon.gui_spawned` event, and the Next log contains no detach indication. Thus this finding does not assert that the recorded run left a known process alive. It establishes that the method cannot prove the stronger claims it records: “recursively launched descendants remain inside” the groups, all descendants are terminated, and cleanup is complete. The retained teardown observes only group membership, socket, and listener state; a detached non-listening descendant is invisible to all three.

The risk is not merely hypothetical in the application basis. The daemon has a default-enabled GUI-spawn policy, and its activation path explicitly executes `spawn(..., { detached: true, stdio: 'ignore' })` followed by `unref()`. That route was prohibited and inactive in this retained run, but it confirms that session escape is a real process behavior in the reviewed application, not an impossible operation.

Impact: DEL-09-01-V3-02 H2-F1 requires recursively signaling every descendant of the started dev server so a wedged descendant cannot survive teardown. The candidate establishes only process-group-member cleanup. It can report group count zero and cleanup success while a controlled descendant remains alive outside the group. The durable recursive-cleanup claims and acceptance evidence are therefore overstated.

Safe remediation direction: do not restore bare-PID signaling or a process-name/ancestry sweep; either would recreate the foreign-process/PID-reuse hazard closed from J2-F1. Instead:

1. Disable every known detach route for this disposable proof before launch (at minimum set and record `CHIRALITY_DAEMON_GUI_SPAWN=0`, even when activation is otherwise prohibited), and fail preflight if the isolation policy is not in force.
2. Make group escape a fail-closed invariant while the controller anchors are alive: enumerate the controller-owned descendant relation and verify every observed descendant remains in the recorded PGID. Do not convert an escaped descendant to “absent”.
3. To claim automatic recursive cleanup, use an OS-backed containment/ownership primitive that remains authoritative across session/group changes and can terminate the owned job without resolving a recycled bare PID. If no such primitive is available on the supported host, narrow the method's supported process contract to “descendants may not detach,” prove that contract for the actual daemon/Next lifecycle, and stop with cleanup failure/manual escalation on any escape rather than signaling an unstable PID.
4. Add a deterministic regression in which a controlled child calls `setsid()`. It must either be prevented/contained and end absent, or make the proof fail truthfully; it must never yield cleanup success with the child alive.
