## Chosen enclosure and independent proof

Accept an adaptive Bernstein convex-hull enclosure instead of root isolation for the scalar objective f=|a|+sqrt(b²+c²). Each input span supplies exact-as-f64 degree-two Bernstein coefficients for the three stress polynomials in local u∈[0,1], independently derived by the caller from statics. Interpolation of three unknown samples is not validation of quadratic statics. The mathematical problem solved is the polynomial represented by those supplied coefficients; no bound on model/discretization/coefficient-derivation error is implied.

The Bernstein weights ((1−u)²,2u(1−u),u²) are nonnegative and sum to one. The vector (a,b,c) lies in the convex hull of its three vector control points. Since |a| and the Euclidean norm of (b,c) are convex, Jensen's inequality gives f(vector(u))≤max_i f(control_i) on the entire span. This bound needs no smoothness of f, hence covers axial zero crossings, zero bending, repeated derivative roots and constant objectives without separate root detection. It also makes clear that maxima at control points are upper bounds, not necessarily attained values.

At a subdivision midpoint, de Casteljau averages produce left controls (P0,(P0+P1)/2,(P0+2P1+P2)/4) and right controls ((P0+2P1+P2)/4,(P1+P2)/2,P2). They represent the same exact quadratic on the two closed halves. Interval arithmetic will enclose the rounding uncertainty of those control vectors; applying the convex bound to each control interval is conservative because |a|≤max(|a_lo|,|a_hi|) and the norm is monotone in the absolute component bounds. Evaluated endpoints/midpoints give interval-enclosed attainable values; a lower endpoint of such an interval is a valid global lower bound. Maintain the best located lower bound and a priority queue of all unresolved interval upper bounds. Pruning only when upper≤best lower preserves enclosure of the global maximum. Success requires an outward-rounded upper−lower gap no larger than the downward-rounded internal tolerance. The returned station is an approximate maximizing witness with a certified value gap, not a claim of the exact unique argmax.

Every supplied span includes both endpoints, with span identity retained, so discontinuous left/right values are both candidates. Adjacent span station values are never deduplicated. The helper does not assert that callers supplied every span or omitted no loads. A dyadic local parameter remains the authoritative station witness on very short spans where a displayed global station rounds to an endpoint.

## Floating-point envelope and bounded termination

Use f64 next-down/next-up around each +,−,×,÷ and sqrt needed by the enclosure; comparisons/maxima/abs of finite floats are exact. Bounds for the norm scale by max(|b|,|c|), applying directed bounds through sqrt(1+(min/max)²); this avoids preventable squaring overflow. Direct sqrt has a correctly-rounded guarantee in Rust's standard f64 documentation, unlike the unspecified precision of hypot; hypot is not used for certified bounds. Finite normal/subnormal operations are covered by adjacent outward representable values. Zero/equal-input identities can be returned exactly; no positive radius/tolerance is fabricated for zero. Any nonfinite enclosure is reported explicitly rather than silently clamped. The proof assumes Rust's ordinary IEEE-754 arithmetic with gradual underflow, no fast-math/flush-to-zero mode or reassociation; an unsupported environment is not certified by these tests.

Subdivision averages use directed half-products and addition, avoiding an overflowing full sum. Enclosures can widen near roundoff and fail to close a tiny requested gap; that must return unresolved. A fixed maximum split count and depth prevent unbounded work. The selected stricter internal acceptance gap is 1e-12 Pa + 1e-12 times the best attained lower bound, three orders tighter in relative terms than the preserved 1e-9 physical oracle. It is an internal numerical stopping rule, not an amendment of that oracle. A work/depth/roundoff failure is an explicit unresolved result with bounds, never a sampled maximum relabeled as certified.
